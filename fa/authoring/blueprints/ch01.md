# FA.1a: Banach Spaces, Dual Spaces and the Dual of Lᵖ

Directory: `src/ch65-banach-spaces-lp-duality/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Opens the Part with Lᵖ duality as decided (decisions.md 9). Proof via Radon–Nikodym ties directly to Part IV; Brezis's uniform-convexity proof appears later in weak-topologies-alaoglu as a second proof. Folland §6.2 is the natural extra reference, but FOL.6 is a Part IV node.*

6. The dual of Lᵖ for 1<p<∞ by Radon–Nikodym, and (L¹)* = L^∞ for σ-finite μ

## Must prove

- B(X,Y) is complete when Y is
- BLT: a bounded operator on a dense subspace extends uniquely with the same norm
- Riesz's lemma; unit ball compact ⇔ finite dimension
- X/M is Banach when M is closed
- (ℓᵖ)* ≅ ℓ^q and c₀* ≅ ℓ¹
- Lᵖ duality for 1<p<∞
- (L¹)* = L^∞ for σ-finite measures

## Key examples and counterexamples

- the L¹ norm on C[0,1] is incomplete (recalled)
- differentiation on C¹ ⊂ C[0,1] is unbounded
- duals of c₀ and ℓ¹
- (L¹)* ≠ L^∞ for a non-σ-finite measure (counting-type example, exercise)
