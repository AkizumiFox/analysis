## Must prove

- the distribution of a random variable is a Borel probability measure, and E g(X) = ∫ g dμ_X
- the Rademacher functions are independent for Lebesgue measure on [0,1]
- E[XY] = E[X]E[Y] for independent integrable X, Y
- both Borel–Cantelli lemmas (the second under independence)
- Kolmogorov's 0–1 law
- the weak law of large numbers under finite variance
- the strong law of large numbers under a finite fourth moment
- Borel's normal numbers theorem
- the central limit theorem for i.i.d. variables with finite variance, by Lindeberg's replacement method

## Key examples and counterexamples

- infinitely many coin flips as the binary digits of x ∈ [0,1]
- pairwise independent events that are not independent
- Cauchy-distributed variables: no mean, and Sₙ/n does not settle down
- the typewriter sequence as random variables: convergence in probability without a.s. convergence
- de Moivre–Laplace for fair coin flips

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Prove the strong law of large numbers under a finite fourth moment
- Prove the central limit theorem by Lindeberg's replacement method
- Show that almost every x ∈ [0,1] is normal in base 2
