# Chapter 61: Infinite Products and Entire Functions

Directory: `src/ch61-entire-functions-products/`. State: not started.

**Part V, Complex Analysis.** Complex differentiability turns out to be so rigid that one integral formula controls everything; the Part runs from Goursat to the Riemann mapping theorem and then through the qualifying-exam second layer (harmonic and subharmonic functions, normal families, products, continuation, Picard), with normal families as the complex spiral node of Arzelà–Ascoli.

*Hadamard is outline-only per curriculum. Runge is kept as an optional section because the general Mittag-Leffler theorem uses it; the main line proves Mittag-Leffler for ℂ only.*

**Page budget:** about 55 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 56 (Cauchy's Theorem and Its First Consequences), Chapter 57 (Laurent Series, Singularities and Residues), Chapter 58 (Winding Numbers, the Global Cauchy Theorem and Counting Zeros), Chapter 59 (Conformal Maps, Normal Families and the Riemann Mapping Theorem).

## Planned sections

1. Infinite products: convergence, absolute convergence, holomorphic products
2. Weierstrass elementary factors and the Weierstrass factorization theorem in ℂ (and in a region, stated)
3. The product for sin πz and the gamma function: Γ as a meromorphic function, 1/Γ entire, reflection formula
4. Mittag-Leffler's theorem: prescribed principal parts; π² / sin² πz as an example
5. Jensen's formula and counting zeros; order and genus of an entire function
6. Hadamard's factorization theorem (outline of proof)
7. Runge's theorem and the general Mittag-Leffler theorem (optional section)

## Must prove

- convergence criterion for Π(1+aₙ)
- Weierstrass factorization theorem in ℂ
- sin πz = πz Π(1−z²/n²)
- Mittag-Leffler's theorem in ℂ
- Jensen's formula
- an entire function of finite order ρ has Σ|aₙ|^{−ρ−ε} < ∞

## Key examples and counterexamples

- an entire function with zeros exactly at √n
- Γ(z)Γ(1−z) = π/sin πz
- e^{z}, cos√z, 1/Γ and their orders
- π cot πz = 1/z + Σ 2z/(z²−n²)

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Construct an entire function with simple zeros at the positive integers and nowhere else, and compute its order
- Use Hadamard to show an entire function of order < 1 with no zeros is constant
