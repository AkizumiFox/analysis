# Blueprint: CA.10 — Möbius Transformations and the Riemann Sphere

**Directory:** `ca/src/ch02-mobius-riemann-sphere/`. **CA, chapter 2** (atlas id CA.10, new chapter, main line, T1). State: S1 written, awaiting S2a.

**Purpose.**
- CA.1 made "holomorphic" mean "conformal where f′ ≠ 0". This chapter studies the simplest conformal maps that are bijections: the maps \( z \mapsto (az+b)/(cz+d) \). They are the only maps the rest of the volume can write down explicitly between discs, half-planes and their complements, and every later normalisation ("move the point to 0", "move the boundary to ℝ") is done with one of them.
- To make these maps bijections one point must be added to ℂ. The chapter builds that point honestly: the extended plane \( \widehat{\nC} \), the chordal metric, and the sphere picture by stereographic projection.
- It proves: Möbius maps form a group acting on \( \widehat{\nC} \), they send circles-or-lines to circles-or-lines, three points determine one, the cross-ratio is their complete invariant on four points, they respect symmetry with respect to a circle, and up to conjugation there are four kinds (elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic, loxodromic).
- It does **not** prove that every conformal automorphism of the disc is a Möbius map (CA.5, from the Schwarz lemma), nor that every meromorphic function on \( \widehat{\nC} \) is rational (CA.3). Both are stated as hooks.

## Named moves (index.md, "Named moves")

- **Check the generators.** A property preserved by composition and true for translations, dilation-rotations and \( z \mapsto 1/z \) holds for every Möbius map.
- **Route through 0, 1, ∞.** To send three points to three points, send each triple to \( (1, 0, \infty) \) and compose one map with the inverse of the other.
- **One test point decides the side.** A Möbius map sends a generalized disc to one of the two sides of the image circle; the image of one interior point says which.
- **The pole is the mirror of the zero.** A Möbius map sending a circle to a circle sends the symmetric point of its zero to its pole.
- **Conjugate to normal form.** Move the fixed points to 0 and ∞ (or one fixed point to ∞), read off the map, and move back.

## Chapter hook, and where it is paid

**Hook (index.md story).** Two questions about shapes that look nothing alike.
- (Q1) Is there a holomorphic bijection of the upper half-plane \( \nH \) onto the unit disc \( \nD \), with holomorphic inverse? A half-plane is unbounded with a straight edge; a disc is bounded and round.
- (Q2) Is there one of ℂ onto \( \nD \)?
- (Q3) If there is one for Q1, how many are there?

**Paid:**
- Q1 **yes**, in §03 (`exm-cayley-map`, \( C(z) = (z-i)/(z+i) \)), and the family of all Möbius ones in §05 (`cor-halfplane-to-disc`).
- Q2 **no**, in CA.2 (Liouville: a bounded entire function is constant). §05 ends by saying that Möbius maps cannot do it (ℂ ∪ {∞} would have to go to a closed disc) and that the full answer needs CA.2.
- Q3 partly in §05 (the Möbius ones form a three-real-parameter family), fully in **CA.5** (Schwarz lemma: every conformal automorphism of \( \nD \) is Möbius). The index says so with a prose forward pointer.

A second, smaller hook opened in §01 and paid in CA.3: "which holomorphic-looking maps extend to continuous maps \( \widehat{\nC} \to \widehat{\nC} \)?" Rational functions do (C item in §02); CA.3 proves they are the only meromorphic ones.

|---|---|---|

| A parabolic map, having one fixed point, moves points "away" | \( z/(z+1) \): every orbit converges to the fixed point 0 | §06 |

## 01-riemann-sphere.md — The extended plane and the Riemann sphere

Budget: ≤ 4,000 main-line words. Major notions: extended plane with its metric (one), circle in \( \widehat{\nC} \) (one).

- **Failure-motivated definition.** \( f(z) = 1/z \) is a holomorphic bijection of \( \nC\setminus\{0\} \) onto itself, but 0 has no image and nothing maps to 0's partner. Try the real-line fix: two points \( \pm\infty \). It fails: \( 1/z \to \)"∞" along every ray into 0, and the rays approach in every direction, so there is no sign to keep. Try "one point at infinity in each direction": then \( 1/z \) would still be undefined at 0, since approaching 0 along a spiral gives no limiting direction. One point is the only choice that makes \( 1/z \) continuous. Slogan: *ℂ has one point at infinity, reached by leaving every bounded set.*
- **`def-extended-plane`.** \( \widehat{\nC} = \nC \cup \{\infty\} \); arithmetic conventions \( a/0 = \infty \) for \( a \neq 0 \), \( a/\infty = 0 \), \( a + \infty = \infty \) for \( a \in \nC \), \( a\cdot\infty = \infty \) for \( a \neq 0 \); **undefined:** \( \infty + \infty \), \( 0\cdot\infty \), \( 0/0 \), \( \infty/\infty \). Warning: ∞ is not a complex number; the conventions are shorthand for limits, and the undefined ones are exactly where limits can be anything (one-line witnesses: \( z\cdot(1/z) \), \( z\cdot(1/z^2) \)).
- **`def-stereographic-projection`.** \( S^2 = \{x \in \nR^3 : \abs x = 1\} \), \( N = (0,0,1) \). For \( x \neq N \), \( \Pi(x) \) is where the line through N and x meets the plane \( x_3 = 0 \), identified with ℂ; \( \Pi(N) = \infty \). Picture (TikZ, cross-section).
- **`prp-stereographic-formulas`.** \( \Pi(x) = \dfrac{x_1 + i x_2}{1 - x_3} \); inverse \( \Pi^{-1}(z) = \dfrac{(2\Re z,\ 2\Im z,\ \abs z^2 - 1)}{\abs z^2 + 1} \). \( \Pi \) is a bijection \( S^2 \to \widehat{\nC} \); the southern hemisphere goes to \( \nD \), the equator to the unit circle, the south pole to 0.
  - *Proof sketch:* parametrize the line \( N + t(x - N) \), solve \( x_3 \)-coordinate = 0, get \( t = 1/(1-x_3) \). For the inverse, the point \( t z + (1-t)N \) on the line through N and \( (z,0) \) has norm 1 iff \( t = 2/(\abs z^2+1) \). Verify by sympy privately.
- **`def-chordal-metric` and `thm-chordal-metric-formula`.** \( \chi(z,w) = \abs{\Pi^{-1}(z) - \Pi^{-1}(w)} \) (Euclidean distance in ℝ³). Formula: \( \chi(z,w) = \dfrac{2\abs{z-w}}{\sqrt{(1+\abs z^2)(1+\abs w^2)}} \) for \( z,w \in \nC \), and \( \chi(z,\infty) = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{1+\abs z^2}} \).
  - It is a metric because it is the restriction of the Euclidean metric through a bijection (one line; see AN2.1, F4.11).
  - *Proof sketch:* for unit vectors, \( \abs{X-Y}^2 = 2 - 2X\cdot Y \); compute \( X\cdot Y \) from the formulas; \( 2 - 2X\cdot Y = 4\abs{z-w}^2/((1+\abs z^2)(1+\abs w^2)) \) is an algebraic identity (sidecar). For ∞, \( Y = N \) and \( 2 - 2x_3 = 4/(1+\abs z^2) \).
  - Sanity values (Quick check material): \( \chi(0,\infty) = 2 \) (a diameter), \( \chi(1,-1) = 2 \), \( \chi(z,w) \le 2 \) always.
- **`prp-chordal-topology`.**
  - (a) On ℂ the chordal and Euclidean metrics have the same open sets and the same convergent sequences: \( \chi(z,w) \le 2\abs{z-w} \), and \( \chi(z,w) \ge 2\abs{z-w}/(1+R^2) \) when \( \abs z, \abs w \le R \).
  - (b) \( z_n \to \infty \) in \( (\widehat{\nC},\chi) \) iff \( \abs{z_n} \to \infty \); the open balls about ∞ are exactly \( \{\abs z > R\} \cup \{\infty\} \) (R ≥ 0) — prove "every ball about ∞ contains such a set and is contained in one".
  - *Proof sketch:* (a) the denominator is ≥ 1 and ≤ 1 + R²; (b) from \( \chi(z,\infty) = 2/\sqrt{1+\abs z^2} \), which is strictly decreasing in \( \abs z \).
  - The two inequalities in (a) are how a later reader converts chordal estimates to Euclidean ones on compact sets (CA2 normal families).
- **`thm-extended-plane-compact`.** \( (\widehat{\nC},\chi) \) is compact; every sequence in ℂ has a subsequence converging in \( \widehat{\nC} \).
  - *Proof:* \( \Pi^{-1} \) is an isometry onto \( S^2 \), which is closed and bounded in ℝ³, hence compact (Heine–Borel, AN2.3 by prose pointer). Second statement: either the sequence is bounded (Bolzano–Weierstrass in ℂ) or a subsequence has \( \abs{z_{n_k}} \to \infty \), and then (b).
  - Remark (one sentence): this is the "one-point compactification"; the general construction is not needed.
- **Failure-motivated definition: circles.** Stereographic projection sends circles on \( S^2 \) to … what? Through N the image is unbounded. A naive "circles go to circles" fails; the correct target class adds lines.
  - **`def-circle-in-extended-plane`.** A circle in \( \widehat{\nC} \) is either a Euclidean circle in ℂ or a set \( L \cup \{\infty\} \) with L a line. Equation form: \( A z\bar z + \bar B z + B \bar z + C = 0 \) with \( A, C \in \nR \), \( B \in \nC \), \( \abs B^2 > AC \); A = 0 gives the lines (then ∞ is included by convention). Check clause by clause: why \( \abs B^2 > AC \) (else empty or a point; witnesses \( z\bar z + 1 = 0 \), \( z\bar z = 0 \)), why A, C real (the left side must be real for all z).
  - Every circle in \( \widehat{\nC} \) is determined by any three of its points (prose; ℂ-geometry from F4.1/F4.9).
- **`thm-stereographic-circles`.** \( \Pi \) maps the circles on \( S^2 \) (intersections of \( S^2 \) with planes meeting it in more than one point) bijectively onto the circles in \( \widehat{\nC} \); the circle passes through N iff its image is a line together with ∞.
  - *Proof sketch:* plane \( a_1x_1 + a_2x_2 + a_3x_3 = a_0 \), \( a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2 = 1 \), \( 0 \le a_0 < 1 \). Substitute \( \Pi^{-1}(z) \), clear \( \abs z^2 + 1 \): \( (a_3 - a_0)\abs z^2 + 2a_1 x + 2a_2 y - (a_3 + a_0) = 0 \), of the equation form with \( \abs B^2 - AC = a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2 - a_0^2 > 0 \). A = 0 iff \( a_3 = a_0 \) iff N lies on the plane. Reverse the computation for surjectivity.
- **`prp-stereographic-conformal`** (stated, proof as the C exercise): \( \Pi^{-1} \) preserves angles between curves, and scales infinitesimal length at z by \( 2/(1+\abs z^2) \). Here "angle" is the angle between tangent vectors in ℝ³. Main-line use: it justifies drawing on the sphere; nothing in CA's proofs depends on it. Weird-examples row "not an isometry, yet conformal".
- **`def-conformal-at-infinity`.** Defined through the chart \( z \mapsto 1/z \): for f defined near ∞ with \( f(\infty) \in \nC \), f is holomorphic (conformal) at ∞ if \( g(w) = f(1/w) \) (with \( g(0) = f(\infty) \)) is holomorphic (with \( g'(0) \neq 0 \)) at 0; if \( f(z_0) = \infty \), use \( 1/f \) near \( z_0 \). Why this definition: the alternative "use the sphere's angles" agrees, by `prp-stereographic-conformal`, but needs differential geometry; the chart costs nothing.
- **Warning.** \( f(z) = z^2 \) is not conformal at ∞ though it is continuous there: \( 1/f(1/w) = w^2 \) has derivative 0 at 0. Angles at ∞ are doubled, just as at 0.
- **Quick checks.** (1) \( \Pi^{-1}(i) = ? \) Answer \( (0,1,0) \). (2) Which point of \( S^2 \) is antipodal to \( \Pi^{-1}(z) \)? Answer \( \Pi^{-1}(-1/\bar z) \) (check with \( z = 1 \): antipode of \( (1,0,0) \) is \( (-1,0,0) = \Pi^{-1}(-1) \)).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A** (one exercise, parts): (a) list the four undefined expressions in \( \widehat{\nC} \); (b) true/false: \( \chi(z,w) \le \abs{z-w} \) for all z, w (false: \( \chi \le 2\abs{z-w} \) is sharp near 0; witness \( z = 0 \), \( w = \varepsilon \): \( \chi = 2\varepsilon/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2} > \varepsilon \)); (c) the image under Π of the circle \( x_3 = 0 \) on \( S^2 \) (unit circle) and of \( x_1 = 0 \) (imaginary axis with ∞); (d) is \( \{\abs z > 3\}\cup\{\infty\} \) open in \( \widehat{\nC} \)? (yes, by `prp-chordal-topology`(b)).
  - **B:**
    - B1. Compute \( \chi(i, -i) \), \( \chi(1, i) \), \( \chi(2,\infty) \). Answers: 2; \( 2\abs{1-i}/2 = \sqrt2 \); \( 2/\sqrt5 \).
    - B2. Find the equation \( Az\bar z + \bar B z + B\bar z + C = 0 \) of the circle \( \abs{z - (1+i)} = 2 \) and of the line \( \Re z = 3 \). Answers: \( A = 1, B = -(1+i), C = -2 \) (since \( \abs{z-c}^2 - r^2 = z\bar z - \bar c z - c\bar z + \abs c^2 - r^2 \), \( \abs c^2 - r^2 = 2 - 4 \)); \( A = 0, B = 1/2, C = -3 \) (from \( z + \bar z = 6 \), i.e. \( \tfrac12 z + \tfrac12\bar z - 3 = 0 \)).
    - B3. Find the plane in ℝ³ whose intersection with \( S^2 \) projects onto the line \( \Re z = 1 \). Answer: \( x_1 + x_3 = 1 \). From the proof's equation, \( A = 0 \) forces \( a_3 = a_0 \); \( 2a_1 x + 2a_2 y - 2a_0 = 0 \) must be \( x = 1 \), so \( a_2 = 0 \) and \( a_1 = a_0 \); normalising gives \( a_1 = a_3 = a_0 = 1/\sqrt2 \).   - **C:**

Marking scheme (10).
    - C2. (a) Show that \( z \mapsto -1/\bar z \) (with \( 0 \leftrightarrow \infty \)) corresponds under Π to the antipodal map \( x \mapsto -x \) of \( S^2 \). ## 02-mobius-group.md — Möbius transformations and their group

Budget: ≤ 4,000 words. Major notion: Möbius transformation (one).

- **Hook (goal stated first).** Find all bijections \( \widehat{\nC} \to \widehat{\nC} \) built from \( z + b \), \( az \) and \( 1/z \). Composites of these have the form \( (az+b)/(cz+d) \); the section shows that every such form is a composite, and what condition makes it a bijection.
- **Failure-motivated definition.** Allow any \( a,b,c,d \): \( (2z+4)/(z+2) = 2 \) for \( z \neq -2 \) and is undefined at \( -2 \). The fraction degenerates exactly when numerator and denominator are proportional, i.e. \( ad - bc = 0 \).
- **`def-mobius-map`.** For \( a,b,c,d \in \nC \) with \( ad - bc \neq 0 \), \( T(z) = (az+b)/(cz+d) \) on \( \widehat{\nC} \), with \( T(-d/c) = \infty \), \( T(\infty) = a/c \) if \( c \neq 0 \); \( T(\infty) = \infty \) if \( c = 0 \). Slogan: *a ratio of two linear functions that are not proportional.* Examples: \( z + b \); \( az \) (\( a \neq 0 \)); \( 1/z \); the Cayley map (forward pointer §03). Degenerate case: the identity. Non-example by minimal change: \( (z+1)/(2z+2) \) (\( ad-bc = 0 \)); \( \bar z \) (not of the form; it will reappear in §04's weird row).
- **`prp-mobius-bijection`.** T is a bijection of \( \widehat{\nC} \) with inverse \( T^{-1}(w) = (dw - b)/(-cw + a) \), again Möbius; T is continuous on \( (\widehat{\nC},\chi) \), hence a homeomorphism.
  - *Proof sketch:* solve \( w = (az+b)/(cz+d) \) for z; handle \( w = a/c \) and \( w = \infty \) by the conventions. Continuity at the two special points by `prp-chordal-topology`(b): \( \abs{T(z)} \to \infty \) as \( z \to -d/c \), and \( T(z) \to a/c \) as \( \abs z \to \infty \); elsewhere by CA.1 (rational functions are continuous) and `prp-chordal-topology`(a).
- **`thm-mobius-group`.** Möbius maps form a group \( \mathrm{M\ddot ob} \) under composition. *Proof:* composition formula computed directly (the product of the coefficient arrays, determinant multiplies); inverse from `prp-mobius-bijection`.
- **`prp-mobius-matrix`.** The map \( A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix} \mapsto T_A \) from invertible 2×2 complex matrices to \( \mathrm{M\ddot ob} \) satisfies \( T_{AB} = T_A \circ T_B \), is onto, and \( T_A = T_B \) iff \( B = \lambda A \) for some \( \lambda \neq 0 \). Every T has a representative with \( ad - bc = 1 \), unique up to sign.
  - *Proof sketch:* \( T_A = \mathrm{id} \) means \( cz^2 + (d-a)z - b = 0 \) for all z, so \( c = b = 0 \), \( a = d \). Homogeneous coordinates \( z = z_1/z_2 \) shown as the reason the matrix product appears (two sentences). Remark: \( \mathrm{M\ddot ob} \cong \mathrm{PGL}_2(\nC) = \mathrm{PSL}_2(\nC) \), cited in prose to the linear-algebra companion; not a dependency.
- **Scratch Work 1** (long division; see list above), then **`prp-mobius-generators`.** Every Möbius map is a composite of translations \( z + b \), dilation-rotations \( az \) (\( a \neq 0 \)) and the inversion \( J(z) = 1/z \). For \( c \neq 0 \): \( T = \tau_{a/c}\circ \mu_{(bc-ad)/c^2} \circ J \circ \tau_{d/c} \), since \( T(z) = \frac ac + \frac{bc-ad}{c^2}\cdot\frac{1}{z + d/c} \) (\( \tau_\beta(z) = z + \beta \), \( \mu_\alpha(z) = \alpha z \)). For \( c = 0 \): \( T = \tau_{b/d}\circ\mu_{a/d} \).
  - Name the move **Check the generators** here.
- **`prp-mobius-conformal`.** A Möbius map is conformal at every point of \( \widehat{\nC} \) (in the sense of CA.1 at finite points and `def-conformal-at-infinity` at ∞ and at the pole). *Proof sketch:* at finite \( z \neq -d/c \), \( T'(z) = (ad-bc)/(cz+d)^2 \neq 0 \); at ∞ and at \( -d/c \) via the charts: \( T(1/w) = (a + bw)/(c + dw) \), derivative at 0 equals \( (bc - ad)/c^2 \neq 0 \) when \( c \neq 0 \); when \( c = 0 \), \( 1/T(1/w) = dw/(a + bw) \), derivative \( d/a \neq 0 \). The pole case is the same computation for \( 1/T \).

## 03-circles-preserved.md — Circles and lines are preserved

Budget: ≤ 4,500 words. Major notion: generalized disc (one, minor-to-major).

- **Hook (Q1 from the index).** To map \( \nH \) onto \( \nD \), the line ℝ must go to the unit circle. First: what do Möbius maps do to lines and circles at all?
- **`thm-mobius-circles` (key proof 1).** A Möbius map sends every circle in \( \widehat{\nC} \) onto a circle in \( \widehat{\nC} \).
  - *Proof:* by **Check the generators** (`prp-mobius-generators`). Translations and dilation-rotations send circles to circles and lines to lines (Euclidean similarity, F4.10). For \( J(z) = 1/z \): if z satisfies \( Az\bar z + \bar Bz + B\bar z + C = 0 \) and \( z \neq 0, \infty \), then \( w = 1/z \) satisfies \( Cw\bar w + Bw + \bar B\bar w + A = 0 \) — the same form with \( (A,B,C) \) replaced by \( (C,\bar B,A) \), and \( \abs{\bar B}^2 > CA \). The points 0 and ∞ are checked separately: the circle passes through 0 iff \( C = 0 \) iff its image is a line (contains ∞); through ∞ iff \( A = 0 \) iff image contains 0. Ontoness from applying the same to \( J^{-1} = J \).

- **Examples** (worked, with folded solutions):

## 04-cross-ratio.md — Fixed points, three points, and the cross-ratio

Budget: ≤ 4,500 words. Major notion: cross-ratio (one).

- **`prp-mobius-fixed-points`.** A Möbius map other than the identity has one or two fixed points in \( \widehat{\nC} \). *Proof:* \( T(z) = z \) iff \( cz^2 + (d-a)z - b = 0 \) for finite z; ∞ is fixed iff \( c = 0 \). Case analysis (c ≠ 0: a genuine quadratic, ∞ not fixed; c = 0, a ≠ d: one finite fixed point plus ∞; c = 0, a = d, b ≠ 0: only ∞). Corollary in the statement: a Möbius map fixing three points is the identity.
- **Failure-motivated question.** Can every triple go to every triple? Two unknown maps agreeing at three points must be equal (\( S^{-1}T \) fixes three points). So a Möbius map has "three complex degrees of freedom" — the scalar ambiguity of `prp-mobius-matrix` removes one of the four.
- **Scratch Work 2** (see list), then **`thm-three-points` (key proof 2, part 1).** Given distinct \( z_2, z_3, z_4 \in \widehat{\nC} \), there is exactly one Möbius S with \( S(z_2) = 1 \), \( S(z_3) = 0 \), \( S(z_4) = \infty \):
  \( S(z) = \dfrac{(z - z_3)(z_2 - z_4)}{(z - z_4)(z_2 - z_3)} \), read with the conventions when one of the \( z_j \) is ∞ (three displayed cases: \( z_2 = \infty \): \( (z - z_3)/(z - z_4) \); \( z_3 = \infty \): \( (z_2 - z_4)/(z - z_4) \); \( z_4 = \infty \): \( (z - z_3)/(z_2 - z_3) \)). Uniqueness from `prp-mobius-fixed-points`. Corollary: for distinct triples \( (z_j) \), \( (w_j) \) there is exactly one T with \( T(z_j) = w_j \): \( T = S_w^{-1}\circ S_z \). Name **Route through 0, 1, ∞.**
- **`def-cross-ratio`.** For \( z_1 \in \widehat{\nC} \) and distinct \( z_2, z_3, z_4 \): \( (z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4) = S(z_1) \) with S from `thm-three-points`. Explain-template steps 1–11 (major notion): situate (invariant of four points), hook (the map S evaluated at the fourth point), slogan *the cross-ratio is where the fourth point lands when the other three are sent to 1, 0, ∞*, examples \( (z, 1, 0, \infty) = z \); \( (0, 1, -1, \infty) \) — compute: \( (0 - (-1))/(1 - (-1)) = \tfrac12 \); degenerate case \( z_1 = z_3 \) gives 0, \( z_1 = z_4 \) gives ∞. Warning: there are several conventions in the literature (the order of the points differs); this volume uses Ahlfors'. Do **not** quote another book's convention table.
- **`thm-cross-ratio-invariant` (key proof 2, part 2).** For every Möbius T and distinct \( z_2, z_3, z_4 \): \( (Tz_1, Tz_2, Tz_3, Tz_4) = (z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4) \). *Proof:* \( S\circ T^{-1} \) sends \( Tz_2, Tz_3, Tz_4 \) to \( 1, 0, \infty \), so by uniqueness it is the S of the image triple; evaluate at \( Tz_1 \). Converse in the statement: if \( (w_1,\dots,w_4) = (z_1,\dots,z_4) \) with each triple distinct, the T with \( T(z_j) = w_j \) (j = 2,3,4) also sends \( z_1 \) to \( w_1 \).

## 05-symmetry.md — Symmetry with respect to a circle

Budget: ≤ 4,500 words. Major notion: symmetric points (one).

- **Hook.** §03's warning: centres are not preserved. Something about a circle and its centre *is* preserved, once ∞ is counted: the centre a and ∞ are "mirror images" in the circle \( \abs{z - a} = R \). This section finds the right notion of mirror image.
- **Failure-motivated definition.** Reflection in a line \( L \) is defined by perpendicular bisection. For a circle, perpendicular bisection makes no sense. Try "the point on the same ray from the centre at the same distance from the circle": it is not preserved by \( J \) (witness in a folded solution: circle \( \abs z = 1 \), \( z = \tfrac12 \) and its "same-distance" partner \( \tfrac32 \); under \( J \) the circle is fixed but \( 2 \) and \( \tfrac23 \) are not "same-distance" partners, since \( 2 - 1 = 1 \neq \tfrac13 \)). Reflection in ℝ, \( z \mapsto \bar z \), is the model; transport it.
- **`lem-real-mobius`.** A Möbius map sends \( \widehat{\nR} \) onto itself iff it has a representative with \( a, b, c, d \in \nR \). *Proof sketch:* if T preserves \( \widehat{\nR} \), pick \( z_j = T^{-1}(1), T^{-1}(0), T^{-1}(\infty) \in \widehat{\nR} \); the formula of `thm-three-points` has real coefficients. Consequently \( T(\bar w) = \overline{T(w)} \).
- **`def-symmetric-points`.** Let Γ be a circle in \( \widehat{\nC} \). Points \( z, z^* \) are symmetric with respect to Γ if \( S(z^*) = \overline{S(z)} \) for some Möbius S with \( S(\Gamma) = \widehat{\nR} \). Well-definedness: independent of S, by `lem-real-mobius` (if \( S_1, S_2 \) both work, \( R = S_2S_1^{-1} \) is real, so \( S_2(z^*) = R(\overline{S_1 z}) = \overline{R S_1 z} \)). Equivalent form (Ahlfors): \( (z^*, z_2, z_3, z_4) = \overline{(z, z_2, z_3, z_4)} \) for distinct \( z_2, z_3, z_4 \in \Gamma \). Slogan: *the mirror image in a circle is reflection in ℝ carried over by any Möbius map that straightens the circle.*
  - Paraphrase; examples: Γ = \( \widehat{\nR} \): \( z^* = \bar z \); Γ a line: Euclidean mirror image; the points of Γ are their own mirror images.
- **`prp-symmetric-point-formula`.** For \( \Gamma = \{\abs{z - a} = R\} \): \( z^* = a + \dfrac{R^2}{\overline{z - a}} \) (with \( a^* = \infty \), \( \infty^* = a \)). Geometric reading: \( z^* \) is on the ray from a through z, and \( \abs{z - a}\,\abs{z^* - a} = R^2 \). Unit circle: \( z^* = 1/\bar z \).
  - *Proof sketch:* reduce to the unit circle by \( z \mapsto (z - a)/R \) (a similarity, which commutes with the definition); for the unit circle use \( S(z) = i(1+z)/(1-z) \), which maps it onto \( \widehat{\nR} \), and verify \( S(1/\bar z) = \overline{S(z)} \) (two-line computation, sidecar).
- **`thm-symmetry-principle`.** If a Möbius T maps Γ onto Γ′, then T maps every pair symmetric with respect to Γ to a pair symmetric with respect to Γ′. *Proof:* if \( S' \) straightens Γ′, then \( S'\circ T \) straightens Γ; apply the definition. (Three lines; the work was in the definition.)
- Name **The pole is the mirror of the zero:** if T sends Γ to a circle Γ′ and \( T(z_0) = 0 \), then \( T(z_0^*) = 0^* \) with respect to Γ′ — which is ∞ when Γ′ is a circle centred at 0.
- **Scratch Work 3** (see list), then **`thm-disc-mobius-automorphisms` (key proof 3).** The Möbius maps T with \( T(\nD) = \nD \) are exactly
  \( T(z) = e^{i\theta}\dfrac{z - a}{1 - \bar a z} \), \( \abs a < 1 \), \( \theta \in \nR \).
  - *Proof:* (⇐) for \( \abs z = 1 \), \( \abs{1 - \bar a z} = \abs{\bar z - \bar a} = \abs{z - a} \), so the unit circle is mapped into, hence onto (`thm-mobius-circles`), itself; test point \( a \mapsto 0 \in \nD \) (`cor-mobius-generalized-discs`). (⇒) Let \( a = T^{-1}(0) \in \nD \). By symmetry \( T(1/\bar a) = \infty \) (for \( a = 0 \): \( T(\infty) = \infty \)). So \( T(z) = \lambda (z - a)/(1 - \bar a z) \) for some λ by the mini-lemma stated just before the theorem: *two Möbius maps \( T_1, T_2 \) with the same zero and the same pole satisfy \( T_1 = \lambda T_2 \)* (proof: \( T_1\circ T_2^{-1} \) fixes 0 and ∞, so it is \( z \mapsto \lambda z \) by the case analysis of `prp-mobius-fixed-points`). \( \abs{T(1)} = 1 \) and \( \abs{(1-a)/(1-\bar a)} = 1 \) give \( \abs\lambda = 1 \).
  - **`exm-disc-automorphism-phi-a`**: \( \varphi_a(z) = (z - a)/(1 - \bar a z) \); \( \varphi_a(a) = 0 \), \( \varphi_a^{-1} = \varphi_{-a} \), \( \varphi_a'(0) = 1 - \abs a^2 \), \( \varphi_a'(a) = 1/(1 - \abs a^2) \). CA.5 (Schwarz) and CA.6 (Poisson kernel) cite these values.
  - **Hook Q3 restated, not paid:** "Are there conformal automorphisms of \( \nD \) that are not Möbius?" Prose forward pointer to CA.5 (Schwarz lemma).
- **`cor-halfplane-to-disc`.** The Möbius maps of \( \nH \) onto \( \nD \) are \( z \mapsto e^{i\theta}(z - a)/(z - \bar a) \) with \( \Im a > 0 \); the Möbius maps of \( \nH \) onto itself are the real ones with \( ad - bc > 0 \) (\( \mathrm{PSL}_2(\nR) \)). *Proof sketch:* compose `thm-disc-mobius-automorphisms` with `exm-cayley-map`; the mirror of a in ℝ is \( \bar a \). For \( \nH \to \nH \): `lem-real-mobius` plus test point \( i \): \( \Im T(i) = (ad - bc)/\abs{ci + d}^2 \). **Q1 fully answered within Möbius maps.**
- **Q2 discussion (prose).** No Möbius map sends ℂ onto \( \nD \): the image of \( \widehat{\nC} \) is \( \widehat{\nC} \), and the image of ℂ omits one point only. Whether *any* holomorphic bijection does: CA.2, Liouville.
- **Remark (outlook).** The quantity \( \abs{dz}/(1 - \abs z^2) \) is invariant under all \( T \) of `thm-disc-mobius-automorphisms` (C exercise); it is the hyperbolic metric of the disc (Needham 6.3; not pursued here). CA.5's Schwarz–Pick lemma is its holomorphic shadow.

## 06-classification.md — Fixed points and the four kinds of Möbius map

Budget: ≤ 4,000 words. Major notion: multiplier/type (one).

- **Hook (failure-motivated).** Iterate \( T(z) = (2z + 1)/(z + 2) \) from \( z = 0 \): \( 0, \tfrac12, \tfrac45, \tfrac{13}{14}, \ldots \) → 1. Iterate \( (z - 1)/(z + 1) \) from 0: \( 0, -1, \infty, 1, 0 \) — period 4. Coefficients do not tell these apart at a glance; fixed points and one number at each do.
- **Scratch Work 4** (the conjugation of \( (2z+1)/(z+2) \): fixed points ±1; \( S(z) = (z-1)/(z+1) \); \( (T z - 1)/(Tz + 1) = (z - 1)/(3(z + 1)) \), so \( STS^{-1}(w) = w/3 \); and \( T'(1) = 3/9 = \tfrac13 \)).
- **`def-multiplier` and `thm-mobius-normal-forms`.** Let T ≠ id.
  - (a) If T has two fixed points \( p \neq q \), and S is Möbius with \( S(p) = 0 \), \( S(q) = \infty \), then \( STS^{-1}(w) = kw \) for a constant \( k \neq 0, 1 \), the **multiplier** at p; k does not depend on S, and \( k = T'(p) \) when p is finite and \( T(p) \) finite (and the multiplier at q is \( 1/k \)).
  - (b) If T has exactly one fixed point p and S sends p to ∞, then \( STS^{-1}(w) = w + \beta \), \( \beta \neq 0 \); choosing S suitably, β = 1.
  - *Proof sketch:* \( STS^{-1} \) fixes 0 and ∞ (resp. only ∞), so is \( kw \) (resp. \( w + \beta \) or \( \alpha w + \beta \) with α ≠ 1 having a second fixed point — excluded). Independence of S: another \( S' \) differs by a map fixing 0 and ∞, i.e. \( w \mapsto \mu w \), which commutes with \( kw \). \( k = T'(p) \): chain rule at p, since \( (S T S^{-1})'(0) = k \) and conjugation preserves derivatives at a fixed point. When \( p = \infty \) the statement defines k through the chart (`def-conformal-at-infinity`): \( k = \) the derivative at 0 of \( 1/T(1/w) \); e.g. for \( T(z) = 3z + 1 \), k = 1/3 at ∞ and 3 at \( -1/2 \).
- **`def-mobius-types`.** T ≠ id is **parabolic** (one fixed point), **elliptic** (\( \abs k = 1 \)), **hyperbolic** (\( k > 0 \)), **loxodromic** (all other k). Convention note: some books call the hyperbolic maps loxodromic too; the four-way split here is Needham's/Conway's (S2a: confirm against Conway III.3 exercises wording without copying).
- **`thm-mobius-trace`.** Normalise \( ad - bc = 1 \) and let \( \tau = (a + d)^2 \) (independent of the sign). Then for T ≠ id: parabolic iff τ = 4; elliptic iff \( 0 \le \tau < 4 \); hyperbolic iff \( \tau > 4 \); loxodromic iff \( \tau \notin [0, \infty) \). *Proof sketch:* τ is invariant under conjugation (trace of conjugate matrices, by direct 2×2 computation); for \( kw \) normalised, \( \mathrm{diag}(\sqrt k, 1/\sqrt k) \) gives \( \tau = k + 2 + 1/k \); for \( w + 1 \), τ = 4. Then \( k + 1/k + 2 \in [0, 4) \) iff \( k = e^{i\theta} \), θ ∉ 2πℤ; \( > 4 \) iff k > 0, k ≠ 1; and \( k + 1/k + 2 = 4 \) iff k = 1. Sidecar: sympy check of \( k + 1/k \in \nR \iff k \in \nR \cup \{\abs k = 1\} \).
- **`prp-mobius-iteration`.** (a) Elliptic: in the normal form, \( T^n \) rotates; \( T \) has finite order iff k is a root of unity. (b) Hyperbolic and loxodromic with \( \abs k < 1 \) at p: \( T^n(z) \to p \) for every \( z \neq q \), uniformly on compact subsets of \( \widehat{\nC}\setminus\{q\} \) in the chordal metric. (c) Parabolic: \( T^n(z) \to p \) for every z. *Proof sketch:* conjugate; \( k^n w \to 0 \) for \( w \neq \infty \); \( w + n \to \infty \); transport by the homeomorphism \( S^{-1} \) (`prp-mobius-bijection`). The uniformity in (b): S2a decide whether to keep it (useful in CA2; cheap with `thm-extended-plane-compact`).
- **Worked examples** (one per type, all verified by sidecar):
  - hyperbolic: \( (2z+1)/(z+2) \), τ = 16/3, k = 1/3 at 1, 3 at −1;
  - parabolic: \( z/(z+1) \), fixed point 0 double, conjugate by J to \( w + 1 \), \( T^n(z) = z/(nz + 1) \);
  - **`exm-order-four-mobius`** (elliptic): \( (z - 1)/(z + 1) \), \( ad - bc = 2 \), τ = 4/2 = 2, fixed points ±i, \( k = T'(i) = 2/(i+1)^2 = -i \), \( T^2 = -1/z \), \( T^4 = \mathrm{id} \);
  - loxodromic: \( 2iz \) (τ = \( (\sqrt{2i} + 1/\sqrt{2i})^2 = 2i + 2 + 1/(2i) = 2 + \tfrac{3i}2 \notin \nR \)).
- **Weird rows** (finite order; repelling point stays put; parabolic attracts).
- **Picture** (one TikZ per type in the normal form: rays and circles through 0 for \( kw \); horizontal lines for \( w + 1 \)), with a `::: {.print}` fallback; no animation.
- **Quick check.** Classify \( 3z \), \( -z \), \( z + i \), \( 1/z \). Answers: hyperbolic, elliptic (k = −1), parabolic, elliptic (fixed ±1, \( T'(1) = -1 \); τ: representative \( \begin{pmatrix}0 & i\\ i & 0\end{pmatrix} \) with det 1, τ = 0).
