# CA.1: Holomorphic Functions and Power Series

Directory: `src/ch55-holomorphic-functions/`. State: not started.

**Part V, Complex Analysis.** Complex differentiability turns out to be so rigid that one integral formula controls everything; the Part runs from Goursat to the Riemann mapping theorem and then through the qualifying-exam second layer (harmonic and subharmonic functions, normal families, products, continuation, Picard), with normal families as the complex spiral node of Arzelà–Ascoli.

*Complex numbers themselves are assumed from earlier Parts; this chapter recalls, it does not re-teach. Möbius maps are postponed to conformal-riemann-mapping, where they are used.*

## Must prove

- Cauchy–Riemann equations are necessary, and sufficient when the partials are continuous
- a power series is holomorphic inside its disc of convergence with the termwise derivative
- ∮_γ f′ = 0 for closed γ when f has a primitive
- the ML-inequality
- f holomorphic on a connected open set with f′ ≡ 0 is constant

## Key examples and counterexamples

- z̄ is nowhere complex-differentiable although smooth
- |z|² is complex-differentiable only at 0
- ∮_{|z|=1} dz/z = 2πi, so 1/z has no primitive on ℂ∖{0}
- principal branch of log on ℂ∖(−∞,0]
- e^{1/z} near 0 (preview of an essential singularity)

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- If f is holomorphic on a region and |f| (or Re f) is constant, then f is constant
- Compute the radius of convergence of Σ z^{n!} and Σ nⁿ zⁿ/n! and justify termwise differentiation
