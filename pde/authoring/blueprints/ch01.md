# PDE.1: Distributions

Directory: `src/ch73-distributions/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Locally convex spaces are used for 𝒟 and 𝒮 only (curriculum b); the inductive-limit topology is handled through sequential convergence plus a short appendix-style section. Tensor products and convolution of two general distributions (FJ ch. 4, 5.3) are cut. ΔΦ = δ uses the divergence theorem and Green's identities of surfaces-divergence-theorem (Part IV).*

4. Operations: differentiation, multiplication by smooth functions; weak derivatives; u′ = 0 in 𝒟′ ⇒ u constant
5. Convergence in 𝒟′ and sequential completeness (UBP again); approximations of δ
6. Support; ℰ′ as the dual of C^∞; distributions supported at a point
7. Convolution with test functions; regularization and density of C_c^∞ in 𝒟′
8. Fundamental solutions: Δ in ℝⁿ and the heat operator

## Must prove

- a distribution is determined locally (sheaf property via partitions of unity)
- p.v. 1/x is a distribution of order 1
- u′ = 0 in 𝒟′(ℝ) implies u is constant
- 𝒟′ is sequentially complete
- a distribution supported at {0} is a finite combination of derivatives of δ
- u*φ is smooth with ∂(u*φ) = (∂u)*φ
- ΔE = δ for the Newtonian/logarithmic potential E

## Key examples and counterexamples

- δ and δ′
- p.v. 1/x and its derivative
- d/dx log|x| = p.v. 1/x
- n sin(nx) → 0 in 𝒟′
- Σ δ_n is a distribution of infinite support but order 0
- log|x| as fundamental solution in ℝ²
