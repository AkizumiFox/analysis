## Purpose

- AN3.5 defined manifolds **inside** some ℝᴺ. Many spaces that look locally like ℝⁿ are not handed to us inside any ℝᴺ: the space of lines through the origin (ℝPⁿ), a torus built by gluing the sides of a square, the phase space of an ODE whose state is an angle. This chapter defines a smooth manifold **intrinsically**, by charts and smooth transition maps, and checks that nothing is lost: every submanifold of ℝᴺ is one, and every compact one is diffeomorphic to a submanifold (easy Whitney, §3).
- Without an ambient space there are no arrows, so tangent vectors are redefined as **derivations** (what a vector does to functions). They agree with AN3.5's tangent spaces for submanifolds.
- Vector fields become sections of the tangent bundle, and AN3.3's flow of an autonomous ODE becomes the **flow of a vector field** on a manifold: existence, uniqueness (which needs Hausdorff), smoothness, completeness on compact manifolds.
- The chapter closes with three uses of flows: straightening a non-vanishing field, the Lie bracket as the derivative of one field along the flow of another (commuting fields ⇔ commuting flows), and the **hairy ball theorem**, which pays the chapter's hook.

**Out of scope** (said in prose, once, in index.md): manifolds with boundary in the abstract (only submanifolds with boundary of ℝᴺ from AN3.7 are used, in §6), Riemannian metrics, Lie groups and their Lie algebras, Sard's theorem, the strong Whitney embedding theorem (stated in §3 without proof), time-dependent flows, first-order PDE by characteristics (pointer to PDE), forms and Stokes on abstract manifolds (pointer: the AN3.6–AN3.7 proofs transfer chart by chart with partitions of unity; Lee ISM 14–16).

## Named moves (index.md, "Named moves")

1. **Check it in a chart, then check chart-independence.** Define a notion through one chart; prove that a second chart gives the same answer because transition maps are smooth. (§2; Scratch Work at first appearance: smoothness of a map.)
2. **Localize with a bump.** Multiply by a bump function that is 1 near p to replace a global object by a local one or to extend a local object globally. (§2–§3; Scratch Work at first appearance: derivations are local.)
3. **Hausdorff closes the agreement set.** Two solutions agree on a set that is open (local uniqueness) and closed (Hausdorff), hence on an interval. (§5; Scratch Work at first appearance.)
4. **Uniform time from compactness, then the group law.** Get one ε that works at every point of a compact set; then compose flows to reach any time. (§5; Scratch Work at first appearance.)
5. **Straighten, then compute.** Put a non-vanishing field into the form ∂/∂x¹ and do the computation in those coordinates; handle the zeros of X by continuity or by triviality. (§6.)

Fading: move 1 is worked in full (§2, Scratch Work + proof), then in §3 with the chart-independence step left as a Quick check, then in §4 (tangent bundle) with the whole check as a B exercise. Move 2 is worked in §3, half-faded in §4 (extension of a vector to a global field), left to the reader in §6 (C exercise). ## Hook and payoff

- **Hook (§1, opening of index.md and §1).** At every instant, the horizontal wind on the Earth is a tangent vector at each point of a sphere. Must there be a calm point somewhere? On a planet shaped like a doughnut there need not be: the field that blows "around the hole" never vanishes. On a sphere of odd dimension S³ ⊂ ℝ⁴ there need not be either. The question is about vector fields on a manifold, and the difference between S² and the torus is not visible locally.
- **Paid in §6.5**, `thm-hairy-ball`: every smooth vector field on Sⁿ, n even, vanishes somewhere (the continuous version is a C exercise), and S^{2k+1} carries the nowhere-vanishing field of `exm-rotation-field-odd-sphere`, whose flow is a matrix exponential (AN3.3). The torus field is `exm-irrational-flow-torus`'s field.
- **Second, smaller hook (§5 lead-in).** AN3.3's x′ = x² escapes to infinity in finite time. Can that happen on a compact manifold? Paid in the same section by `cor-compact-manifold-complete`.

## 01-topological-manifolds.md — Spaces that are locally Euclidean

**Budget:** ≤ 3,000 main-line words.

- **Lead-in.** Three spaces not given inside ℝᴺ: ℝPⁿ (lines through 0 in ℝⁿ⁺¹), the torus as a square with opposite sides glued, the configuration space of a planar rod pinned at one end (a circle, but described by an angle, not by points of ℝ²). Each has a notion of "nearby" and looks like ℝⁿ near each point.
- **Quotient topology, restated** (not a dependency on AN2.7 §8): for a surjection q: X → Y, U ⊂ Y is open iff q⁻¹(U) is open. One sentence of motivation, one example (ℝ → S¹, t ↦ (cos 2πt, sin 2πt) identifies ℝ/ℤ with S¹: by AN2.7's compact-to-Hausdorff theorem applied to [0,1]/(0 ∼ 1); stated in prose, proof as B exercise).
- **Failure-motivated definition.** First attempt: "every point has a neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open subset of ℝⁿ". Two failures:
  - `exm-line-two-origins`: ℝ × {0, 1} with (x, 0) ∼ (x, 1) for x ≠ 0. Locally Euclidean; the two origins cannot be separated, and a sequence 1/k converges to both. §5 shows the real damage: integral curves are not unique there.
  - `exm-uncountable-lines`: ℝ × D with D an uncountable discrete set. Locally Euclidean and Hausdorff, but it has no countable basis; §2 shows the damage: no exhaustion by compact sets, so the partition-of-unity construction fails at the first step. (The long line is mentioned in one sentence as a connected example, no proof.)
- **`def-topological-manifold`.** A topological n-manifold is a topological space M that is Hausdorff, second countable (has a countable basis), and locally Euclidean of dimension n: every p ∈ M has an open neighbourhood U and a homeomorphism φ: U → φ(U) onto an open subset of ℝⁿ. Also `def-second-countable` (AN2.7 has bases but not this word) and `def-chart` (the pair (U, φ), "coordinate chart", φ = (x¹, …, xⁿ)).
- **Remark (no proof): invariance of dimension.** A non-empty open subset of ℝⁿ is not homeomorphic to one of ℝᵐ for m ≠ n (Brouwer). Not proved here and not used: the smooth version (`cor-smooth-invariance-dimension`, §3) is all the chapter needs.
- **Examples** (`exm-topological-manifolds`): open subsets of ℝⁿ; graphs of continuous functions; Sⁿ (charts by projection to coordinate hyperplanes); any submanifold of ℝᴺ (AN3.5); products (`thm-product-manifold-top`, one-line proof: products of charts, product of countable bases, product of Hausdorff).
- **`thm-rpn-topological-manifold`.** ℝPⁿ := (ℝⁿ⁺¹ ∖ {0})/∼, x ∼ λx (λ ≠ 0), with the quotient topology, is a topological n-manifold.
  - *Hypotheses:* n ≥ 1 (n = 0 gives a point, which is fine but uninteresting; say so).
  - *Proof sketch.* (i) q is open: q⁻¹(q(V)) = ⋃_{λ≠0} λV is open. Hence images of a countable basis form a countable basis. (ii) Hausdorff: the map [x] ↦ xxᵀ/|x|² into symmetric (n+1)×(n+1) matrices is well defined, continuous (universal property of the quotient: its composite with q is continuous) and injective (xxᵀ/|x|² is orthogonal projection onto the line ℝx); a continuous injection into a Hausdorff space forces Hausdorff. (iii) Charts Uᵢ = {[x] : xᵢ ≠ 0}, φᵢ[x] = (x₀/xᵢ, …, x̂ᵢ, …, xₙ/xᵢ), inverse u ↦ [u₀, …, 1, …, uₙ] (1 in place i); both continuous.
- **`thm-compact-exhaustion`.** Every topological manifold M has a countable basis of precompact coordinate balls, and an exhaustion by compact sets K₁ ⊂ int K₂ ⊂ K₂ ⊂ int K₃ ⊂ ⋯ with ⋃ Kⱼ = M.
  - *Hypotheses:* Hausdorff (precompact sets have compact closure that is closed; needed for "closure of a coordinate ball is compact" to make sense in M) and second countable (countability).
  - *Proof sketch.* Each p has a chart; the balls B with closure B̄ ⊂ φ(U) and rational centre and radius give coordinate balls φ⁻¹(B) whose closures φ⁻¹(B̄) are compact (continuous image of a compact set) and closed in M (Hausdorff). A standard lemma: every basis contains a countable sub-basis when the space is second countable (one-paragraph proof). Exhaustion: list the countable basis B₁, B₂, … of precompact sets; K₁ = B̄₁; given Kⱼ compact, cover it by finitely many Bᵢ and set K_{j+1} = closure of (B₁ ∪ ⋯ ∪ B_{m_j}) with m_j ≥ j + 1 large enough to cover Kⱼ.
  - Weird example check: `exm-uncountable-lines` has no exhaustion (each Kⱼ meets only finitely many lines, so ⋃ Kⱼ meets countably many).
- **Warning.** "Locally Euclidean" is local; Hausdorff and second countable are global conditions and do not follow from it (the two examples above).
- **Quick check.** Is the figure eight {(x, y) : (x² + y²)² = x² − y²} ⊂ ℝ² a topological manifold? (No: every neighbourhood of 0 minus 0 has four components, while a punctured interval has two; any small connected neighbourhood of 0 would be homeomorphic to an interval.)
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Which of the three conditions fails: (a) the line with two origins; (b) ℝ × D, D uncountable discrete; (c) [0, ∞) at the point 0. *Answer:* (a) Hausdorff; (b) second countable; (c) locally Euclidean of dimension 1 fails at 0: a connected open neighbourhood [0, ε) of 0 stays connected when 0 is removed, while an open interval minus any point is disconnected, and a homeomorphism would preserve this.
  - **A2.** Write the chart φ₀ of ℝP² and its inverse explicitly.
  - **B1.** Prove that ℝ/ℤ (quotient of ℝ by x ∼ x + k) is homeomorphic to S¹. *Answer:* t ↦ e^{2πit} induces a continuous bijection [0,1]/(0∼1) → S¹, compact to Hausdorff (AN2.7).
  - **B2.** Prove that a topological manifold is locally path-connected and that its components are open and countable in number. *Answer:* coordinate balls are path-connected; components are unions of basis elements, disjoint, so each contains a basis element of a countable basis.
  - **C1.** Show that ℝPⁿ is compact. *Answer:* q restricted to Sⁿ is surjective and continuous.
  - **C2.** Show that the line with two origins is second countable and locally Euclidean, and that the sequence 1/k converges to both origins. *Answer:* direct.

## 02-smooth-structures.md — Charts, atlases and smooth maps

**Budget:** ≤ 4,500 main-line words.

- **Failure-motivated definition.** A topological manifold has no notion of a differentiable function: f: M → ℝ "smooth if f ∘ φ⁻¹ is smooth" depends on the chart. `exm-cube-root-chart`: on ℝ, the identity chart and ψ(x) = x³ are both homeomorphisms; f(x) = x is smooth in the first chart and f ∘ ψ⁻¹(u) = u^{1/3} is not differentiable at 0 in the second. A smooth structure is a choice of charts that agree about smoothness.
- **Definitions.** `def-smoothly-compatible` (transition ψ ∘ φ⁻¹: φ(U ∩ V) → ψ(U ∩ V) is a C^∞ diffeomorphism; vacuous if U ∩ V = ∅); `def-smooth-atlas` (charts covering M, pairwise compatible); `def-smooth-structure` (a maximal smooth atlas); `def-smooth-manifold` (a topological manifold with a smooth structure). Charts in the maximal atlas are "smooth charts".
- **`thm-atlas-determines-structure`.** Every smooth atlas 𝒜 on a topological manifold is contained in exactly one maximal smooth atlas: the set 𝒜̄ of all charts compatible with every chart of 𝒜.
  - *Proof sketch.* 𝒜̄ ⊇ 𝒜. Two charts (U, φ), (V, ψ) of 𝒜̄ are compatible: smoothness of ψ ∘ φ⁻¹ is local, and near φ(p) it equals (ψ ∘ χ⁻¹) ∘ (χ ∘ φ⁻¹) for a chart (W, χ) ∈ 𝒜 at p. Maximality: a chart compatible with 𝒜̄ is compatible with 𝒜, hence in 𝒜̄. Uniqueness: any maximal atlas containing 𝒜 is contained in 𝒜̄, hence equals it.
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance of move 1)**, set apart before the proof: show how "smooth at a point" is checked through an intermediate chart and why only the overlap near φ(p) matters.
- **`thm-smooth-chart-lemma`** (build a manifold from a set and charts). Let M be a set, and suppose given subsets Uₐ ⊂ M (a ∈ A) and injections φₐ: Uₐ → ℝⁿ such that: (i) each φₐ(Uₐ) is open in ℝⁿ; (ii) each φₐ(Uₐ ∩ U_b) is open; (iii) each φ_b ∘ φₐ⁻¹: φₐ(Uₐ ∩ U_b) → φ_b(Uₐ ∩ U_b) is smooth; (iv) countably many Uₐ cover M; (v) for p ≠ q in M, either some Uₐ contains both, or some Uₐ ∋ p and U_b ∋ q are disjoint. Then M has a unique topology and smooth structure making each (Uₐ, φₐ) a smooth chart.
  - *Proof sketch.* Topology: the basis {φₐ⁻¹(V) : V ⊂ ℝⁿ open}; check it is a basis using (ii)–(iii) (intersection of two basis sets is φₐ⁻¹ of an open set). Each φₐ is a homeomorphism onto its image. Hausdorff from (v); second countable from (iv) (countably many Uₐ, each with a countable basis); the atlas is smooth by (iii).
  - Used for: the tangent bundle (§4); optionally for ℝPⁿ (B exercise: re-derive `thm-rpn-topological-manifold` from the lemma).
- **Examples** (`exm-smooth-manifolds`):
  - ℝⁿ with the identity chart; open subsets of a smooth manifold (`thm-open-submanifold`, restrict charts); finite-dimensional real vector spaces (any linear iso to ℝⁿ; two choices differ by a linear map, so the structure is canonical); GL(n, ℝ) as an open subset of ℝ^{n²}.
  - Sⁿ with **stereographic charts** σ_N, σ_S; transition σ_S ∘ σ_N⁻¹(u) = u/|u|² on ℝⁿ ∖ {0}, which is its own inverse and smooth. Compatible with the projection charts of §1 (Quick check for one pair).
  - ℝPⁿ with the charts of §1; transitions φⱼ ∘ φᵢ⁻¹ are rational with non-vanishing denominators (write one, i = 0, j = 1).
  - Products (`thm-product-smooth-manifold`).
  - `exm-cube-root-chart`, completed: {(ℝ, ψ)} is a smooth atlas whose maximal atlas differs from the standard one.
- **`thm-submanifold-smooth-manifold`** (the bridge). Let M ⊂ ℝᴺ be a k-dimensional C^∞ submanifold in the sense of AN3.5. The inverses of AN3.5's local parametrizations form a smooth atlas on M (with the subspace topology). The resulting smooth structure is the only one for which (a) the inclusion M ↪ ℝᴺ is smooth with injective differential everywhere, and the maps called smooth in AN3.5 are exactly the smooth maps of this section.
  - *Proof sketch.* Hausdorff and second countable are inherited from ℝᴺ. Transition maps are smooth by AN3.5's result that the inverse of a parametrization is locally the restriction of a smooth map on an open subset of ℝᴺ (projection to suitable coordinates composed with the inverse given by the inverse function theorem, AN3.2). The "smooth maps agree" clause: unwind both definitions in a parametrization. **Drafter's note:** prove the existence half in full; state the uniqueness clause (a) and prove it as a C exercise after §3, since it uses differentials. S2a list item 3.
- **`def-smooth-map`.** F: M → N is smooth if for every p there are smooth charts (U, φ) at p and (V, ψ) at F(p) with F(U) ⊂ V and ψ ∘ F ∘ φ⁻¹ smooth. `thm-smooth-map-chart-independent` (move 1, worked in full: if it holds for one pair of charts, it holds for every pair, by smoothness of transitions; smooth maps are continuous). `def-smooth-function` (N = ℝ), C^∞(M). `def-diffeomorphism`. Composition of smooth maps is smooth (`thm-composition-smooth`).
- **Weird example, completed.** (ℝ, standard) and (ℝ, ψ-structure) are **different smooth structures but diffeomorphic** via F(x) = x^{1/3}: ψ ∘ F ∘ id⁻¹ = id and id ∘ F⁻¹ ∘ ψ⁻¹ = id. One sentence in prose: there exist topological manifolds with non-diffeomorphic smooth structures (exotic spheres) and ones with none; neither is proved.
- **Bump functions and partitions of unity.**
  - `lem-smooth-bump-manifold`: for K ⊂ U ⊂ M with K compact and U open there is ψ ∈ C^∞(M) with 0 ≤ ψ ≤ 1, ψ = 1 on a neighbourhood of K, supp ψ ⊂ U. *Sketch:* cover K by finitely many precompact coordinate balls inside U; transplant AM.1's bump on ℝⁿ to each, extend by 0 (smooth because the support is a compact subset of the ball, which is closed in M by Hausdorff); combine finitely many by 1 − Π(1 − ψᵢ). Note that "extend by 0" is where Hausdorff is used: in the line with two origins a bump around one origin extended by 0 is not continuous at the other.
  - **`thm-smooth-partition-of-unity`.** For every open cover {Uₐ}_{a∈A} of a smooth manifold M there is a smooth partition of unity {ψₐ}_{a∈A} subordinate to it: ψₐ ∈ C^∞(M), 0 ≤ ψₐ ≤ 1, supp ψₐ ⊂ Uₐ, the supports form a locally finite family, Σₐ ψₐ = 1.
    - *Proof sketch.* Exhaustion `thm-compact-exhaustion` (K₀ = K₋₁ = ∅). For each j cover the compact Kⱼ ∖ int K_{j−1} by finitely many precompact coordinate balls each inside some Uₐ and inside int K_{j+1} ∖ K_{j−2}; take bumps fᵢ ≥ 0 positive on these balls. The family of all balls is locally finite (a point of int Kⱼ meets only balls from stages ≤ j + 1), so f = Σ fᵢ is smooth and positive. Set gᵢ = fᵢ/f, then group the gᵢ by a chosen index a(i) with ball ⊂ U_{a(i)}: ψₐ = Σ_{a(i)=a} gᵢ (locally finite sum, support ⊂ Uₐ because a locally finite union of closed sets is closed).
    - Name AM.1 as the Euclidean case and say exactly what is new: the exhaustion replaces the Euclidean one by closed balls.
  - `cor-exhaustion-function`: there is a smooth f: M → [0, ∞) with every sublevel set f⁻¹([0, c]) compact (f = Σ j ψⱼ for a partition subordinate to {int K_{j+1} ∖ K_{j−1}}). Used in §5 (C exercise: a vector field can be rescaled to be complete).
- **Warning.** A smooth structure is extra data: "M is a smooth manifold" always means a chosen maximal atlas. Two atlases can give the same structure (stereographic vs projection charts on Sⁿ) or different but diffeomorphic structures (`exm-cube-root-chart`).
- **Quick checks.** (1) Is ψ(x) = x³ smoothly compatible with the identity chart? (No: the inverse transition is not smooth at 0.) (2) Verify σ_S ∘ σ_N⁻¹(u) = u/|u|² for n = 1 directly from the formulas. (3) Why does the definition of a smooth map need F(U) ⊂ V? (So that ψ ∘ F ∘ φ⁻¹ is defined on all of φ(U); continuity of F is what lets one shrink U to get it.)
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Show that the charts φ₀, φ₁ of ℝP¹ are compatible and compute the transition. *Answer:* u ↦ 1/u on ℝ ∖ {0}.
  - **A2.** Show that f: Sⁿ → ℝ, f(x) = x_{n+1}, is smooth using stereographic charts. *Answer:* f ∘ σ_N⁻¹(u) = (|u|² − 1)/(|u|² + 1).
  - **B1.** Prove that ℝPⁿ satisfies the hypotheses of `thm-smooth-chart-lemma` with its standard charts. ## 03-tangent-vectors.md — Tangent vectors as derivations

**Budget:** ≤ 4,500 main-line words.

- **Failure-motivated definition.** AN3.5's TₚM is a subspace of ℝᴺ; for ℝPⁿ there is no ℝᴺ. Attempt 1: velocities of curves, with γ₁ ~ γ₂ iff (φ ∘ γ₁)′(0) = (φ ∘ γ₂)′(0) in one chart. It works, but the vector space structure must be imported from a chart and checked chart-independent (one paragraph, then dropped; it returns as `thm-velocity-every-vector`). Attempt 2: a tangent vector is determined by what it does to functions: v ↦ (f ↦ D_v f(a)). On ℝⁿ, D_v satisfies the product rule at a. Take that as the definition.
- **`def-tangent-space-derivations`.** A derivation at p ∈ M is a linear map v: C^∞(M) → ℝ with v(fg) = f(p) v(g) + g(p) v(f). TₚM is the vector space of derivations at p.
- **`lem-derivation-basic`.** (a) v(c) = 0 for constants; (b) if f(p) = g(p) = 0 then v(fg) = 0; (c) **locality:** if f = g on a neighbourhood of p then v(f) = v(g).
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance of move 2)** for (c): choose a bump ψ = 1 near p supported where f = g; then ψ(f − g) = 0, and Leibniz gives 0 = v(ψ(f − g)) = ψ(p) v(f − g) + (f − g)(p) v(ψ) = v(f − g).
- **`thm-tangent-space-euclidean`.** For a ∈ ℝⁿ, the map ℝⁿ → Tₐℝⁿ, w ↦ D_w|ₐ (D_w|ₐ f = Σ wⁱ ∂ᵢf(a)) is a linear isomorphism; the ∂/∂xⁱ|ₐ form a basis.
  - *Hypotheses:* functions are C^∞. **Weird example in the table:** the proof uses that gᵢ below is smooth, which costs one derivative; for C^k, 1 ≤ k < ∞, the corresponding space of derivations at a point is not n-dimensional (stated without proof; S2a list item 4: confirm the statement and phrase it as "is infinite-dimensional" only if a reference is pinned, otherwise "the proof breaks").
  - *Proof sketch.* Injective: D_w xʲ = wʲ. Surjective: given v, set wʲ = v(xʲ). For f ∈ C^∞(ℝⁿ), Taylor with integral remainder on the ball (or all of ℝⁿ): f(x) = f(a) + Σᵢ (xⁱ − aⁱ) gᵢ(x), gᵢ(x) = ∫₀¹ ∂ᵢf(a + t(x − a)) dt, gᵢ smooth by differentiation under the integral sign (AN3.4 / AN2's result for continuous integrands on compact parameter sets; cite the exact label at S2a, item 1), gᵢ(a) = ∂ᵢf(a). Apply v and `lem-derivation-basic`(a): v(f) = Σ v(xⁱ) gᵢ(a) = D_w f(a).
- **`def-differential`.** For smooth F: M → N, dFₚ: TₚM → T_{F(p)}N, dFₚ(v)(f) = v(f ∘ F). **`thm-differential-properties`:** linear; d(G ∘ F)ₚ = dG_{F(p)} ∘ dFₚ; d(id_M)ₚ = id; a diffeomorphism has invertible differentials.
- **`cor-smooth-invariance-dimension`.** If a non-empty smooth m-manifold is diffeomorphic to a smooth n-manifold, then m = n. (Via an open set of ℝᵐ diffeomorphic to an open set of ℝⁿ, plus `thm-tangent-space-euclidean`.) **Also:** `thm-tangent-space-open-subset`: for U ⊂ M open, dι_p: TₚU → TₚM is an isomorphism (locality + bump extension). Hence dim TₚM = n for every p of a smooth n-manifold (`cor-dimension-tangent-space`).
- **Coordinates.** For a chart (U, φ = (xⁱ)), ∂/∂xⁱ|ₚ := (dφₚ)⁻¹(∂/∂xⁱ|_{φ(p)}); `thm-coordinate-basis`: a basis of TₚM, with ∂/∂xⁱ|ₚ f = ∂ᵢ(f ∘ φ⁻¹)(φ(p)) and v = Σ v(xⁱ) ∂/∂xⁱ|ₚ.
  - `thm-differential-in-coordinates`: the matrix of dFₚ in coordinate bases is the Jacobian of ψ ∘ F ∘ φ⁻¹ at φ(p).
  - `thm-change-of-coordinates-vectors`: ∂/∂xⁱ|ₚ = Σⱼ (∂x̃ʲ/∂xⁱ)(p) ∂/∂x̃ʲ|ₚ, so components transform by the Jacobian of the transition. **Fading step for move 1:** the chart-independence of "the matrix of dFₚ is the Jacobian" is a Quick check, not proved.
  - Worked example `exm-polar-coordinate-vectors`: on ℝ² ∖ (−∞, 0], ∂/∂r = cos θ ∂/∂x + sin θ ∂/∂y, ∂/∂θ = −r sin θ ∂/∂x + r cos θ ∂/∂y.
- **Velocity.** `def-velocity`: γ′(t₀) = dγ_{t₀}(d/dt|_{t₀}) for smooth γ: J → M, J an interval. `thm-velocity-in-coordinates`: γ′(t₀) = Σ (γⁱ)′(t₀) ∂/∂xⁱ|_{γ(t₀)}; γ′(t₀) f = (f ∘ γ)′(t₀). `thm-velocity-every-vector`: every v ∈ TₚM is γ′(0) for some γ (a straight line in a chart). `thm-velocity-chain-rule`: (F ∘ γ)′(t) = dF(γ′(t)). This is the bridge back to Attempt 1.
- **`thm-tangent-space-submanifold-agrees`.** Let M ⊂ ℝᴺ be a k-dimensional submanifold (AN3.5) with the smooth structure of `thm-submanifold-smooth-manifold`, and ι the inclusion. Under Tₚℝᴺ ≅ ℝᴺ (`thm-tangent-space-euclidean`), dιₚ is injective and its image is AN3.5's tangent space TₚM ⊂ ℝᴺ.

- **`thm-easy-whitney`** (answer to "is anything new?"). Every compact smooth n-manifold M admits a smooth embedding into some ℝᴺ; its image is a submanifold of ℝᴺ (AN3.5) and M is diffeomorphic to it.

  - *Proof sketch.* Finitely many charts (Uᵢ, φᵢ), i = 1…m, and bumps ρᵢ with supp ρᵢ ⊂ Uᵢ such that the sets {ρᵢ = 1} cover M (`lem-smooth-bump-manifold` with a shrinking of the cover: compact Kᵢ ⊂ Uᵢ covering M — one-line lemma, by compactness). ## 04-tangent-bundle-vector-fields.md — The tangent bundle and vector fields

**Budget:** ≤ 4,000 main-line words.

- **Lead-in.** The wind on the sphere is a choice of TₚS² for each p, varying smoothly. "Varying smoothly" needs all tangent spaces assembled into one manifold.
- **`def-tangent-bundle`.** TM = ⊔_{p∈M} TₚM, π: TM → M. **`thm-tangent-bundle-manifold`:** TM is a smooth 2n-manifold and π is smooth, with charts (π⁻¹(U), φ̃), φ̃(Σ vⁱ ∂/∂xⁱ|ₚ) = (x¹(p), …, xⁿ(p), v¹, …, vⁿ).
  - *Proof sketch* via `thm-smooth-chart-lemma`: transition (x, v) ↦ (x̃(x), D(x̃ ∘ x⁻¹)(x) v) is smooth by `thm-change-of-coordinates-vectors`; (iv) from countably many charts; (v) two points in the same fibre lie in one π⁻¹(U), points in different fibres are separated by π⁻¹ of disjoint chart domains (Hausdorff of M). **Fading of move 1:** this whole verification is B1 in the main text's words, "checked like the atlas of §2".
  - `thm-global-differential`: dF: TM → TN, (p, v) ↦ dFₚ(v), is smooth. `exm-tangent-bundle-sphere`: TSⁿ ≅ {(x, v) ∈ ℝⁿ⁺¹ × ℝⁿ⁺¹ : |x| = 1, x · v = 0}, a submanifold of ℝ²ⁿ⁺² (via `thm-tangent-space-submanifold-agrees`; the level-set description from AN3.5).
- **`def-vector-field`.** A vector field is a section X: M → TM, π ∘ X = id; smooth if smooth as a map. Notation 𝔛(M); X_p := X(p). **`thm-smooth-vector-field-criteria`:** for a rough section X, TFAE: (a) X smooth; (b) in every smooth chart the component functions Xⁱ (X = Σ Xⁱ ∂/∂xⁱ) are smooth; (c) for every f ∈ C^∞(M), the function Xf: p ↦ X_p f is smooth. *Sketch:* (a)⇔(b) by the bundle charts; (b)⇒(c): Xf = Σ Xⁱ ∂(f∘φ⁻¹)/∂uⁱ ∘ φ; (c)⇒(b): apply to bump-modified coordinate functions (move 2, half-faded: the reader supplies the bump, as in §2 B2).
- **Vector fields as derivations.** X: C^∞(M) → C^∞(M) is linear and X(fg) = f Xg + g Xf. `thm-derivations-are-vector-fields`: every such derivation D: C^∞(M) → C^∞(M) is f ↦ Xf for a unique smooth X (set X_p f = (Df)(p); X_p is a derivation at p; smoothness by (c)).
- **Examples** (`exm-vector-fields`):
  - Euler field Σ xⁱ ∂/∂xⁱ on ℝⁿ; coordinate fields; on ℝ² ∖ {0}, ∂/∂θ = −y ∂/∂x + x ∂/∂y extends to all of ℝ².
  - **`exm-rotation-field-odd-sphere`:** on S^{2k+1} ⊂ ℝ^{2k+2}, X(x) = (−x₂, x₁, −x₄, x₃, …, −x_{2k+2}, x_{2k+1}) = Jx, J block-diagonal with blocks [[0, −1], [1, 0]]. Jx · x = 0 (tangent, by `exm-tangent-bundle-sphere`) and |Jx| = |x| = 1 (nowhere zero). k = 0 gives ∂/∂θ on S¹.
  - The torus T² = S¹ × S¹ ⊂ ℝ⁴ with X = (∂/∂θ₁, α ∂/∂θ₂) (α ∈ ℝ): nowhere zero (used in `exm-irrational-flow-torus`).
  - The hook restated precisely, as `thm-hairy-ball`'s statement with a forward pointer "proved in §6.5"; the reader is invited to try to write a nowhere-zero field on S² and see where it fails (stereographic pictures of the "dipole" field with one zero).
- **`lem-extend-vector`** (half-faded move 2): every v ∈ TₚM is X_p for some X ∈ 𝔛(M) (constant components in a chart times a bump). Proof left as B2 with the bump named.
- **F-related fields.** `def-f-related`: X ∈ 𝔛(M), Y ∈ 𝔛(N) are F-related if dF_p(X_p) = Y_{F(p)} for all p. `thm-f-related-criterion`: iff X(f ∘ F) = (Yf) ∘ F for all f ∈ C^∞(N). `def-pushforward`: for a diffeomorphism F, F_*X := dF ∘ X ∘ F⁻¹, the unique field F-related to X. Example: under polar coordinates the field ∂/∂θ on (0, ∞) × (−π, π) pushes forward to −y ∂/∂x + x ∂/∂y.
- **Lie bracket, failure-motivated.** Composite XY: f ↦ X(Yf) is a second-order operator, not a vector field: on ℝ, X = Y = d/dx, XY(x · x) = 2 at 0 while x(0)·XY(x) + x(0)·XY(x) = 0, so Leibniz fails. The second-order terms cancel in XY − YX.
  - **`def-lie-bracket`:** [X, Y]f = X(Yf) − Y(Xf). **`thm-lie-bracket-properties`:** [X, Y] ∈ 𝔛(M) (it is a derivation of C^∞(M); apply `thm-derivations-are-vector-fields`); coordinate formula [X, Y] = Σⱼ (X Yʲ − Y Xʲ) ∂/∂xʲ; bilinear over ℝ, antisymmetric, Jacobi identity; [fX, gY] = fg[X, Y] + f(Xg)Y − g(Yf)X; naturality: if Xᵢ is F-related to Yᵢ (i = 1, 2) then [X₁, X₂] is F-related to [Y₁, Y₂].
  - Worked example `exm-bracket-dx-xdy`: [∂/∂x, x ∂/∂y] = ∂/∂y on ℝ². Kept for §6.4, where the two flows fail to commute by exactly this.
  - Coordinate fields commute: [∂/∂xⁱ, ∂/∂xʲ] = 0 (equality of mixed partials, AN3.1).
- **Warning.** A vector field is not a function M → ℝⁿ unless M ⊂ ℝⁿ is open; its "components" depend on the chart. And [X, Y] is not "XY − YX evaluated pointwise": it uses derivatives of the components.

## 05-integral-curves-flows.md — Integral curves and flows

**Budget:** ≤ 5,000 main-line words.

- **Lead-in (second hook).** AN3.3: the solution of x′ = x² through x₀ > 0 is x₀/(1 − t x₀) and leaves every compact set before t = 1/x₀. Two questions: what is the flow of a vector field on a manifold, and can an integral curve on a compact manifold "escape"? Answer at the end of the section: no.
- **`def-integral-curve`.** γ: J → M (J an open interval containing 0) with γ′(t) = X_{γ(t)} for all t. In a chart: (γⁱ)′(t) = Xⁱ(γ(t)), an autonomous system as in AN3.3.
- **Examples.** ∂/∂x on ℝ² (lines); −y ∂/∂x + x ∂/∂y (circles); Euler field (rays, γ(t) = eᵗ p); x² d/dx on ℝ (escape; `exm-escape-quadratic`).
- **`thm-integral-curve-local`.** Local existence and uniqueness: for each p there is ε > 0 and an integral curve γ: (−ε, ε) → M with γ(0) = p; two integral curves through p agree near 0. *Proof:* in a chart, AN2.4 (Picard–Lindelöf; X's components are C¹ hence locally Lipschitz), and `thm-velocity-in-coordinates`.
- **`thm-integral-curve-uniqueness`.** If γ: J → M and η: J′ → M are integral curves of X with γ(t₀) = η(t₀) for some t₀ ∈ J ∩ J′, then γ = η on J ∩ J′.
  - **Scratch Work (move 3, first appearance)**, set apart: S = {t ∈ J ∩ J′ : γ(t) = η(t)}. Non-empty. Open by `thm-integral-curve-local` (translated in time). Closed in J ∩ J′ because S is the preimage of the diagonal of M × M under (γ, η), and the diagonal is closed **because M is Hausdorff**. J ∩ J′ is an interval, hence connected.
  - **Weird example (Hausdorff matters).** In the line with two origins, the field "d/dx" (well defined since the two charts differ by the identity) has two different integral curves through (−1, 0): one passes through each origin. They agree on (−∞, 1) ∩ … up to time 1, and then differ. Which step of the Scratch Work fails? (Closedness of S.) This is the section's one **find-the-error** item, as a Quick check.
- **Maximal integral curves.** `def-maximal-integral-curve`; `cor-maximal-integral-curve`: each p has a unique maximal integral curve θ⁽ᵖ⁾: 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾ → M starting at p (union of all integral curves starting at p; well defined by uniqueness).
- **`def-flow`.** A flow domain 𝒟 ⊂ ℝ × M is an open set such that each 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾ = {t : (t, p) ∈ 𝒟} is an open interval containing 0. A (local) flow is a continuous θ: 𝒟 → M with θ(0, p) = p and θ(t, θ(s, p)) = θ(s + t, p) whenever s ∈ 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾ and t ∈ 𝒟^{(θ(s,p))} (which, by the theorem below, holds iff s + t ∈ 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾). θₜ(p) := θ(t, p), Mₜ = {p : (t, p) ∈ 𝒟}. A **global flow** (one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms) has 𝒟 = ℝ × M.
- **`thm-fundamental-theorem-flows`.** Let X be a smooth vector field on a smooth manifold M. There is a unique smooth maximal flow θ: 𝒟 → M whose infinitesimal generator is X: for each p, t ↦ θ(t, p) is the maximal integral curve of X starting at p. Moreover: (a) if s ∈ 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾ then 𝒟^{(θ(s,p))} = 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾ − s and θ(t, θ(s, p)) = θ(t + s, p) for t ∈ 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾ − s; (b) each Mₜ is open and θₜ: Mₜ → M₋ₜ is a diffeomorphism with inverse θ₋ₜ; (c) 𝒟 is open.
  - *Hypotheses:* X smooth (C^∞). Say where each piece of smoothness comes from.
  - *Proof route (the chapter's longest; Idea paragraph + proof in steps; no Scratch Work — graduate taper).*
    1. Uniqueness and (a): from `thm-integral-curve-uniqueness` (t ↦ θ(t + s, p) is an integral curve starting at θ(s, p)).
    2. **Local smoothness in ℝⁿ** (`lem-local-flow-smooth`): for X smooth on open W ⊂ ℝⁿ and p₀ ∈ W there are ε > 0 and a neighbourhood U₀ of p₀ with the flow defined and C^∞ on (−ε, ε) × U₀. AN3.3 gives C¹ dependence on the initial point, with the derivative Y(t) = ∂ₚθ(t, p) solving the variational equation Y′ = DX(θ) Y, Y(0) = I. **Bootstrap:** the pair (θ, Y) solves the autonomous system (x, Y)′ = (X(x), DX(x)Y) on W × ℝ^{n×n}, whose right-hand side is C^∞; by AN3.3 its flow is C¹ in the initial point, so ∂ₚθ is C¹, so θ is C² in p; induction on k gives C^k for all k. Joint smoothness in t follows from ∂ₜθ = X(θ).
    3. **Globalize** (Lee's argument, in the book's words): for p ∈ M let t₀ = sup of t ≥ 0 in 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾ such that 𝒟 contains a product neighbourhood (−δ, t + δ) × U of [0, t] × {p} on which θ is smooth. Suppose t₀ is in 𝒟⁽ᵖ⁾. Apply step 2 at q₀ = θ(t₀, p) in a chart; use the group law θ(t, x) = θ(t − t₁, θ(t₁, x)) with t₁ < t₀ close to t₀ to extend the product neighbourhood past t₀; contradiction. Same for t ≤ 0. This proves (c) and smoothness.
    4. (b): θₜ maps Mₜ into M₋ₜ by (a); θ₋ₜ ∘ θₜ = id on Mₜ by (a); both smooth; Mₜ open as the slice of the open 𝒟.
  - Drafter: this is a key proof (§11.2). The full proof may exceed the section budget; keep the bootstrap of step 2 in full and step 3 in full, steps 1 and 4 in a paragraph each. S2a list item 6.
- **Escape lemma.** **`lem-escape`.** If γ: (a, b) → M is a maximal integral curve with b < ∞, then for every t₀ ∈ (a, b) the set γ([t₀, b)) is not contained in any compact subset of M. *Sketch:* if it were in a compact K, then by `lem-local-flow-smooth` and compactness there is one ε > 0 such that the flow from any point of K exists for |t| < ε (move 4 appears here first — Scratch Work set apart: the cover of K by the U₀ from step 2, a finite subcover, and the minimum of finitely many ε); then γ extends past b by θ(t − (b − ε/2), γ(b − ε/2)).
- **Completeness.** `def-complete-vector-field` (maximal flow is global). **`thm-compact-support-complete`:** a smooth vector field with compact support is complete. *Sketch (move 4 again, with Scratch Work omitted):* off supp X the integral curves are constant; uniform ε on the support; then for any t, write t = kε/2 + r and compose θ_{ε/2} k times (group law). **`cor-compact-manifold-complete`:** every smooth vector field on a compact manifold is complete; the flow is a one-parameter group of diffeomorphisms. **Second hook paid:** x² d/dx escapes on ℝ, which is not compact. Identify ℝ with S¹ ∖ {N} by stereographic projection. In the chart at N, u = 1/x, the field becomes x² d/dx = −d/du (Quick check), which is smooth at u = 0 and **non-zero** there: the field extends to a smooth field on S¹ without zeros, whose flow is complete and periodic; the solution that "escaped to infinity" in finite time on ℝ simply passes through N.
- **Examples of flows** (`exm-flows`):

## 06-flows-at-work.md — Straightening, brackets, and the hairy ball

**Budget:** ≤ 5,000 main-line words. This section carries the chapter's **R group** (last section).

### 6.1 The canonical form near a regular point

- **`thm-canonical-form-vector-field`.** Let X ∈ 𝔛(M) with X_p ≠ 0. There is a smooth chart (U, (xⁱ)) at p in which X = ∂/∂x¹ on U.
  - *Proof sketch (move 5 introduced).* Choose a chart centred at p with X_p = ∂/∂x¹|ₚ (linear change). Let S be the slice {x¹ = 0} near 0, parametrized by y = (y², …, yⁿ). ψ(t, y) = θ(t, (0, y)) is smooth on a neighbourhood of 0 (`thm-fundamental-theorem-flows`), dψ₀ sends ∂/∂t to X_p = ∂/∂x¹|ₚ and ∂/∂yʲ to ∂/∂xʲ|ₚ, so it is invertible; by `thm-inverse-function-manifolds` ψ⁻¹ is a chart. ψ_*(∂/∂t) = X by the group law: ψ(t + s, y) = θₛ(ψ(t, y)).
  - Consequence (prose): near a point where X ≠ 0, every flow looks like translation; all the geometry of a flow lives near its zeros and in its global behaviour.
  - Example: −y ∂/∂x + x ∂/∂y at (1, 0): polar coordinates (θ, r) are such a chart.

### 6.2 Lie derivative = bracket

- **`def-lie-derivative`.** ℒ_X Y|ₚ = d/dt|_{t=0} d(θ₋ₜ)_{θₜ(p)}(Y_{θₜ(p)}) (a derivative of a curve in the vector space TₚM; defined because for small |t| the point p lies in Mₜ and θₜ(p) in M₋ₜ).
- **`thm-lie-derivative-bracket`.** ℒ_X Y = [X, Y] for all X, Y ∈ 𝔛(M).
  - *Proof sketch (move 5).* Three kinds of points. (i) X_p ≠ 0: in canonical coordinates X = ∂/∂x¹, θₜ is translation by t e₁, d(θ₋ₜ) is the identity on components, so ℒ_X Y|ₚ = Σ ∂Yʲ/∂x¹(p) ∂/∂xʲ|ₚ = [∂/∂x¹, Y]ₚ by the coordinate formula. (ii) p in the interior of {X = 0}: θₜ = id near p, both sides 0. (iii) p ∈ supp X with X_p = 0: p is a limit of points of type (i) (the support is the closure of {X ≠ 0}); both sides are continuous in p (ℒ_X Y is continuous by smooth dependence of θ on (t, p); state it as part of the lemma that ℒ_X Y is smooth). Every point is of one of the three types.

### 6.3 Commuting fields and commuting flows

- **`thm-commuting-flows`.** Let X, Y ∈ 𝔛(M) be complete with flows θ, η. Then [X, Y] = 0 iff θₜ ∘ ηₛ = ηₛ ∘ θₜ for all s, t ∈ ℝ. (The local version, for non-complete fields on the domains where both sides are defined, is stated in a remark, proof as C exercise.)
  - *Proof sketch.* (⇐) Differentiate θₜ ∘ ηₛ ∘ θ₋ₜ = ηₛ in s at 0: (θₜ)_*Y = Y; then differentiate in t: ℒ_X Y = 0 = [X, Y]. (⇒) Fix p; V(t) = d(θ₋ₜ)(Y_{θₜ(p)}) ∈ TₚM. Its derivative at t equals d(θ₋ₜ)(ℒ_X Y|_{θₜ(p)}) (group law), which is 0, so V is constant: (θₜ)_*Y = Y. A diffeomorphism that preserves Y maps integral curves of Y to integral curves of Y (`thm-velocity-chain-rule`), so θₜ(ηₛ(p)) = ηₛ(θₜ(p)) by uniqueness.
  - **Worked example and failure** (`exm-noncommuting-flows`): X = ∂/∂x, Y = x ∂/∂y on ℝ² (bracket ∂/∂y by `exm-bracket-dx-xdy`). θₜ(x, y) = (x + t, y), ηₛ(x, y) = (x, y + sx). θₜηₛ(x, y) = (x + t, y + sx) and ηₛθₜ(x, y) = (x + t, y + s(x + t)): they differ by st in the y-direction. The loop "flow X for t, Y for s, X for −t, Y for −s" ends st away from its start in the direction of [X, Y]. (Prose: why a car can be parallel-parked although it cannot move sideways; no figure of a car needed, a diagram of the four legs suffices.)
  - Corollary (`cor-commuting-frame`, stated, proof as C exercise): if X₁, …, Xₖ are pointwise independent at p and pairwise commuting, there is a chart with Xᵢ = ∂/∂xⁱ (i ≤ k) near p. It generalizes 6.1 (k = 1).

### 6.4 Hairy ball — the hook paid

- **`thm-hairy-ball`.** For n ≥ 1, the sphere Sⁿ ⊂ ℝⁿ⁺¹ carries a smooth vector field with no zero if and only if n is odd.
  - *If:* `exm-rotation-field-odd-sphere`.
  - *Only if (n even):* suppose X ∈ 𝔛(Sⁿ) has no zero; replace X by v = X/|X| (|·| from ℝⁿ⁺¹ via `exm-tangent-bundle-sphere`), so v(x) ⊥ x and |v(x)| = 1. Define H: [0, 1] × Sⁿ → Sⁿ, H(t, x) = cos(πt) x + sin(πt) v(x) (on Sⁿ because x ⊥ v(x), both unit). H₀ = id, H₁ = −id =: a. Let ω be the n-form on ℝⁿ⁺¹ ∖ {0} of AM.2 that is closed and has ∫_{Sⁿ} ω ≠ 0 (orientation of Sⁿ as the boundary of the unit ball, AN3.7), ω = |x|^{−(n+1)} Σᵢ (−1)ⁱ xᵢ dx₀ ∧ ⋯ ∧ dx̂ᵢ ∧ ⋯ ∧ dxₙ. Then:
    1. **Homotopy invariance** (`lem-homotopy-invariance-integral`, proved here): for smooth H: [0, 1] × Sⁿ → ℝⁿ⁺¹ ∖ {0} and closed ω, ∫_{Sⁿ} H₁^*ω = ∫_{Sⁿ} H₀^*ω. *Proof:* [0, 1] × Sⁿ is a compact oriented (n+1)-submanifold with boundary of ℝ^{n+2}; Stokes (AN3.7) gives 0 = ∫ H^*(dω) = ∫ d(H^*ω) = ∫_{∂} H^*ω = ±(∫ H₁^*ω − ∫ H₀^*ω) with the boundary orientations. (H extends smoothly to an open neighbourhood in ℝ^{n+2} by AN3.5's extension result or by using the same formula for x ∈ ℝⁿ⁺¹ ∖ {0} with v extended homogeneously; drafter picks the cleaner, S2a list item 7.)
    2. **The antipodal map** pulls ω back to (−1)^{n+1} ω (each term has n + 1 sign changes: n from the dx's and one from xᵢ; the factor |x|^{−(n+1)} is even) and hence ∫_{Sⁿ} a^*ω = (−1)^{n+1} ∫_{Sⁿ} ω directly — **no** argument about whether a preserves orientation is needed, because the pulled-back form is integrated over Sⁿ with its fixed orientation.
    3. Hence ∫_{Sⁿ} ω = (−1)^{n+1} ∫_{Sⁿ} ω; with ∫ ω ≠ 0, n + 1 is even, n odd.
  - Answer to the hook in prose: the wind on the Earth (n = 2) has a calm point at every instant; on S³ or on the torus it need not.
  - Remark (one paragraph, no proof): the flow version. A nowhere-zero field on a compact manifold gives a fixed-point-free diffeomorphism θ_ε isotopic to the identity; the Euler characteristic obstruction (Poincaré–Hopf) is the general answer, outside this book.
  - Continuous version: C exercise.
