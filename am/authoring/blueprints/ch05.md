# Blueprint: AM.2 — Closed and Exact Forms: Poincaré Lemma and de Rham Cohomology of Punctured ℝⁿ

**Directory:** `am/src/ch05-closed-and-exact-forms/`. **AM chapter 5** (graduate, main line, T2; math-atlas spec/08 §D3, row 5). Reading order AM.1, AN3.5, AN3.6, AN3.7, **AM.2**, AM.3. State: S1 blueprint, not frozen.

**Purpose.**
- AN3.6 defined closed and exact forms (`def-closed-exact`) and showed that the angle form ω_θ is closed but not exact on ℝ² ∖ {0} (`thm-angle-form-not-exact`). AN3.7 showed why: an exact form integrates to zero over every compact oriented manifold without boundary (`cor-stokes-closed-manifold`, `thm-exact-integral-zero`), and ∫_{S¹}ω_θ = 2π. It also planted the solid-angle form σ on ℝ³ ∖ {0} (`exm-solid-angle-not-exact`). This chapter asks the converse question. When is a closed form exact, and what measures the failure?
- Locally the answer is always yes (the Poincaré lemma on star-shaped sets). Globally it depends on the holes of the domain, and the de Rham groups Hᵏ(U) = closed/exact count them.
- The chapter computes Hᵏ for star-shaped sets (zero for k ≥ 1) and for ℝⁿ ∖ {0} (ℝ in degrees 0 and n − 1, zero otherwise), with homotopy invariance as the tool that lets dimensions drop. It then uses the computation: a second, cohomological proof of AN3.7's no-retraction theorem, reflections are not homotopic to the identity on punctured space, and vector calculus on ℝ³ ∖ {0} sorts into "always a gradient" and "a curl only if the flux vanishes".
- Out of scope: abstract manifolds (AM.3); the hairy ball theorem (AM.3 §6.4, which uses this chapter's σₙ through a proposed b AM.2); the general Mayer–Vietoris sequence and zig-zag lemma (a pointer to Tu 25–26 only; here the two-set argument is done by hand for one cover); the de Rham theorem; compactly supported cohomology; degree theory beyond the solid-angle integral. The Brouwer fixed-point theorem is mentioned in one remark and pointed to the FX branch (see edge only; it is not proved here and no exercise proves it).

## Chapter hook and where it is paid

- **Hook received.** AN3.6 Hook 1 left open the question "what does the 2π measure, and is ω_θ essentially the only closed form that is not exact?". AN3.7 answered the first half (the 2π is ∫_{S¹}ω_θ, the same for every loop around 0) and planted σ on ℝ³ ∖ {0}. This chapter's index names both and takes up the second half.
- **Chapter hook (index.md and §01 opening).** On every slit plane ω_θ is d of an angle function, so it is a derivative near every point, yet on ℝ² ∖ {0} it is d of nothing. The chapter's question: *on a given domain, how many independent closed forms fail to be exact, and what do they count?*
- **Paid in three places.**
  1. §04 `thm-h1-punctured-plane`: every closed 1-form on ℝ² ∖ {0} is c·ω_θ + df for a unique c = (1/2π)∮_{S¹}ω. One hole, one dimension of failure. This also closes AN3.6's Hook 1.
  2. §05 `thm-cohomology-punctured-rn`: in ℝⁿ ∖ {0} the single hole is invisible to closed k-forms for 1 ≤ k ≤ n − 2 and is seen exactly once, in degree n − 1, by the solid-angle form σₙ. This closes AN3.7's σ teaser.
  3. §06 `prp-no-retraction-cohomology`: the hook's invariant (∫_{Sⁿ⁻¹}σₙ ≠ 0) together with the Poincaré lemma on the ball gives the no-retraction theorem again, without AN3.7's determinant argument.
- **Secondary hook (end of §02).** "Star-shaped is enough. Is it necessary?" A C-shaped annular sector is not star-shaped, yet every closed form on it is exact. Paid in §03 (`cor-contractible-acyclic` and §03 C1): what matters is the homotopy type, not the shape.

## Named moves (index.md, "Named moves")

1. **Integrate along rays (the cone construction).** To find a primitive on a star-shaped set, pull the form back along the radial homotopy x ↦ p + t(x − p) and integrate in t. First appearance §02, with Scratch Work.
2. **The homotopy formula.** To show two maps act the same way on closed forms, produce an operator K with F₁*ω − F₀*ω = dKω + Kdω. First appearance §03 (the cone of §02 revisited as a special case), with Scratch Work.

## 01-cohomology.md — Exactness of 1-forms, and de Rham cohomology

*Budget: ≤ 3,000 main-line words.*

- **Hook.** Recall ω_θ, `thm-angle-form-not-exact` and `exm-stokes-singular-form`: closed, not exact, and the witness is a loop. Fix the two slit planes U₋ ≔ ℝ² ∖ {(x, 0) : x ≤ 0} and U₊ ≔ ℝ² ∖ {(x, 0) : x ≥ 0}; on them ω_θ = d Arg (Arg ∈ (−π, π)) and ω_θ = dθ₊ (θ₊ ∈ (0, 2π)) respectively (AN3.6 `exm-angle-form`, `exm-angle-form-polar`). Is a loop integral the only possible witness in degree 1?
- `thm-exact-iff-zero-circulation`: for U open and connected and ω ∈ Ω¹(U), ω is exact ⇔ ∮_γ ω = 0 for every loop γ in U.
  - *Proof sketch.* (⇒) ∮_γ df = 0 by the fundamental theorem of calculus on each C¹ piece. (⇐) Fix x₀; connected open ⇒ polygonally path-connected (AN2.3). Define f(x) ≔ ∫_γ ω along any path from x₀ to x; zero circulation ⇒ independent of the path. For ∂ᵢf(x), append the segment from x to x + heᵢ; the difference quotient is the average of ωᵢ over the segment, which tends to ωᵢ(x). So f is C¹ with df = ω, hence C^∞.
  - Remark: this characterizes exactness of 1-forms with no hypothesis "closed". Closedness is what makes the circulation depend only on the homotopy class of the loop (C2), which is §03's theme in degree 1.
- **Failure-motivated definition of cohomology.** The closed forms form an infinite-dimensional space even on ℝⁿ, so "how many closed forms are not exact" is not a count of forms. Adding an exact form changes neither the answer to "is it exact?" nor any integral over a compact boundaryless M (AN3.7 `cor-stokes-closed-manifold`). So identify forms that differ by an exact form:
  - `def-de-rham-cohomology`: Zᵏ(U) ≔ closed k-forms, Bᵏ(U) ≔ exact k-forms (B⁰ ≔ {0}), Hᵏ(U) ≔ Zᵏ(U)/Bᵏ(U) (LA1.4 quotient; Bᵏ ⊆ Zᵏ by AN3.6 `prp-exact-implies-closed`). Class [ω]. Hᵏ(U) = 0 for k > n. The standard name, de Rham cohomology, is given in one sentence.
  - The question of the chapter now reads: compute Hᵏ(U).
- `prp-h0-locally-constant`: H⁰(U) = Z⁰(U) = locally constant functions, ≅ ℝᶜ when U has c components (c finite). *Proof:* df = 0 ⇒ f is constant on each component (open and connected).
- `prp-wedge-cohomology` (stated; proof is B3): closed ∧ closed is closed; exact ∧ closed is exact. So [ω]·[η] ≔ [ω ∧ η] is well defined. One sentence: this product is not used later in the chapter.
- **Worked example.** Decide exactness of ω = (x dx + y dy)/(x² + y²) and of ω_θ on ℝ² ∖ {0}: the first is d(½ log(x² + y²)); the second has circulation 2π. Both are closed; only a global integral separates them.
- **Quick checks.**
  - Q1. Is every closed 0-form exact? *Answer:* no. B⁰ = {0} by convention, so a non-zero constant is closed and not exact, and H⁰(U) ≠ 0 for U ≠ ∅.
  - Q2. ω is closed on U and exact on each of two open sets covering U. Must ω be exact on U? *Answer:* no: ω_θ on U₋ ∪ U₊.
- **Warning.** Hᵏ(U) is a vector space of classes, not of forms: "[ω] = 0" means "ω is exact", not "ω = 0".
- **Non-example.** A closed form on U is not determined by its class: x dx and 0 have the same class on ℝ.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Show ω = 2xy dx + (x² + 3y²) dy on ℝ² is exact and find a primitive. *Answer:* the circulation of ω around any loop is 0 because f = x²y + y³ satisfies df = ω; f is found by integrating 2xy in x and matching the y-derivative.
  - A2. Compute H⁰(ℝ ∖ {0, 1}) and H⁰ of the open set {(x, y) : xy ≠ 0} ⊆ ℝ². *Answer:* ℝ³; ℝ⁴ (four quadrants).
  - A3. Show that [ω] = [ω′] in H¹(U) iff ω − ω′ = df for some f. *Answer:* definition of the quotient.
  - B1. Let ω be a closed 1-form on ℝ² ∖ {0}. Show that ∮ over the circle of radius r about 0 does not depend on r. *Answer:* AN3.7 `thm-stokes` on the closed annulus {r₁ ≤ |x| ≤ r₂}: ∫_{∂A} ω = ∫_A dω = 0, and ∂A is the outer circle minus the inner one.
  - B2. Show that if U is connected and f, g are primitives of the same 1-form, then f − g is constant. Deduce that the potential in `thm-exact-iff-zero-circulation` is unique up to an additive constant. *Answer:* d(f − g) = 0 and `prp-h0-locally-constant`.
  - B3. Prove `prp-wedge-cohomology`. *Answer:* d(ω ∧ η) = dω ∧ η ± ω ∧ dη = 0. If ω = dα with α ∈ Ωᵏ⁻¹, then d(α ∧ η) = dα ∧ η + (−1)^{k−1} α ∧ dη = ω ∧ η.
  - C1. Show, without computing any integral, that ω_θ is not exact on ℝ² ∖ {0}: suppose df = ω_θ, compare f with Arg ∈ (−π, π) on U₋ and with the angle θ₊ ∈ (0, 2π) on U₊, and derive a contradiction. *Answer:* f − Arg = a on U₋ and f − θ₊ = b on U₊ (constants; both sets are connected). On the upper half-plane Arg = θ₊, so a = b. On the lower half-plane Arg = θ₊ − 2π, so b = a − 2π. Contradiction.
  - C2. Let ω be a closed 1-form on an open U ⊆ ℝⁿ and γ_s a smooth family of loops in U (fixed parameter interval [a, b], γ_s(a) = γ_s(b) for all s ∈ [0, 1]). Show that ∮_{γ_s}ω does not depend on s. *Answer:* let Γ(s, t) = γ_s(t); Γ*ω is a closed 1-form near [0, 1] × [a, b]. By `thm-stokes` on the rectangle, ∮_{γ₁} − ∮_{γ₀} plus the two side integrals equals 0; the side integrals are along the same path s ↦ γ_s(a) = γ_s(b) in opposite directions and cancel.

## 02-poincare-lemma.md — The Poincaré lemma on star-shaped sets

*Budget: ≤ 4,500 main-line words.*

- **Hook.** ω_θ fails globally but succeeds on the slit plane U₋. What does U₋ have that ℝ² ∖ {0} lacks? Every point of U₋ can be joined to (1, 0) by a segment inside U₋. Convexity is too strong (U₋ is not convex), and it is exactly the slit plane that §04 needs.
- **Failure-motivated definition.** Convex sets fail to include the slit planes; so:
  - `def-star-shaped`: U is star-shaped about p ∈ U if the segment [p, x] ⊆ U for every x ∈ U.
  - Examples: convex sets; U₋ (about (1, 0); not about the origin, which is not in U₋); `exm-plus-sign-star` (union of two open rectangles crossing in a square: star-shaped about each point of the square, not convex). Non-examples: the annulus, ℝ² ∖ {0}.
- **Degree 1 first** (named move 1, first appearance).
  - **Scratch Work.** "We want f with df = ω and f(p) = 0. Theorem `thm-exact-iff-zero-circulation` says f(x) must be ∫ ω along any path from p; on a star-shaped set there is a canonical path, the segment. So guess f(x) = ∫₀¹ ω_{p+t(x−p)}(x − p) dt and check df = ω by differentiating under the integral; the check will use ∂ᵢωⱼ = ∂ⱼωᵢ at exactly one point." Then the proof.
  - `lem-ray-potential`: U star-shaped about p, ω ∈ Z¹(U) ⇒ f(x) ≔ ∫₀¹ Σⱼ ωⱼ(p + t(x − p))(xⱼ − pⱼ) dt is C^∞ and df = ω.
  - *Proof sketch.* p = 0 by translation. Differentiate under the integral (AN2.11): the integrand is C^∞ on the open set {(t, x) : tx ∈ U}, which contains [0, 1] × U because U is star-shaped about 0; for fixed x choose r with B̄(x, r) ⊆ U and apply the theorem on the compact set [0, 1] × B̄(x, r). ∂ᵢf = ∫₀¹ [ωᵢ(tx) + Σⱼ t xⱼ ∂ᵢωⱼ(tx)] dt; closedness turns ∂ᵢωⱼ into ∂ⱼωᵢ, and the integrand becomes d/dt [t ωᵢ(tx)]. Integrate: ωᵢ(x).
- **All degrees.** Write ι_x for contraction with the position vector (define `def-interior-product` here if AN3.6 does not).
  - `def-cone-operator`: for U star-shaped about 0, K: Ωᵏ(U) → Ωᵏ⁻¹(U), (Kω)_x ≔ ∫₀¹ t^{k−1} ι_x(ω_{tx}) dt (coefficientwise). For k = 1 it is the f of `lem-ray-potential`.
  - `lem-cone-formula`: for k ≥ 1 and every ω ∈ Ωᵏ(U), ω = dKω + Kdω.
  - *Proof sketch.* Linear in ω, so take ω = g dx_I. Compute both sides coefficientwise; the sum of the two integrands is d/dt[tᵏ g(tx)] times dx_I. The full computation is in §03, where the formula is derived from the pullback along H(x, t) = tx; here the proof is the direct coordinate check, with the bookkeeping for ι_x (dx_I) written out once for k = 2 and stated for general k.
  - `thm-poincare-lemma`: U star-shaped ⇒ Hᵏ(U) = 0 for k ≥ 1, H⁰(U) ≅ ℝ.
  - `cor-convex-poincare`: same for convex U (PMA's form).
  - `cor-locally-exact`: every closed form on an open U ⊆ ℝⁿ is exact on some ball around each point.
- **Worked example.** ω = y dx ∧ dz + x dy ∧ dz on ℝ³: check dω = 0, compute Kω = (2xy dz − yz dx − xz dy)/3, verify dKω = ω, and observe Kω − xy dz = −d(xyz/3): primitives differ by a closed, hence exact, form.
- **Weird examples** (rows for the multivariable table, collected in §06):
  - `exm-plus-sign-star` (star-shaped, not convex).
  - The secondary hook: `exm-c-sector`, the annular sector {1 < r < 2, 0 < φ < 3π/2}: not star-shaped, yet (by §03) acyclic.
- **Warning.** Star-shapedness depends on the centre. U₋ is star-shaped, but not about the origin (which is not in U₋), and the cone operator must be built from a valid centre.
- **Non-example.** The annulus: for p in the annulus, −p is in the annulus and the segment from p to −p passes through 0, so no point is a centre.
- **Quick checks.**
  - Q1. For what centres is U₋ star-shaped? *Answer:* every point (a, 0) with a > 0 (the open positive x-axis); no other point. (Points off the axis see the slit's far side blocked.)
  - Q2. Compute K(dx ∧ dy) on ℝ². *Answer:* ½(x dy − y dx); its d is dx ∧ dy.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Use the ray formula to find a primitive of y dx + x dy on ℝ². *Answer:* ∫₀¹ (ty·x + tx·y) dt = xy.
  - A2. Which are star-shaped: the open annulus; ℝ² minus a closed ray; the open set {(x, y) : |y| < 1 + |x|}? *Answer:* no (the segment between two antipodal points passes through the hole, and every point has an antipode in the annulus); yes, about any point of the opposite open ray; yes, about 0, because for t ∈ [0, 1], |ty| < t(1 + |x|) ≤ 1 + t|x|.
  - A3. Show directly that K(dy ∧ dz) = ½(y dz − z dy) on ℝ³ and that d of it is dy ∧ dz.
  - B1. Show that if ω has polynomial coefficients, homogeneous of degree m, and ω is a k-form with k ≥ 1, then Kω = (1/(m + k)) ι_x ω. *Answer:* ω_{tx} has coefficients tᵐ times those of ω_x, so the integral is ∫₀¹ t^{m+k−1} dt.

## 03-invariance.md — Pullbacks, diffeomorphisms and homotopy invariance

*Budget: ≤ 4,000 main-line words.*

- **Hook.** §05 must compare ℝⁿ ∖ (x_n-axis), which is (ℝⁿ⁻¹ ∖ {0}) × ℝ, with ℝⁿ⁻¹ ∖ {0}. They are not diffeomorphic (different dimensions), yet a closed form on X × ℝ "should not care" about the ℝ factor. Diffeomorphism invariance is not enough; a weaker equivalence is needed.
- `prp-induced-map`: for smooth F: U → V, F*: Hᵏ(V) → Hᵏ(U), [ω] ↦ [F*ω] is well defined and linear; (G∘F)* = F*G*, id* = id. *Proof:* F*d = dF* (AN3.6).
- `cor-diffeo-invariance`: a diffeomorphism induces isomorphisms in every degree. Example: ℝ² ∖ {0} and the open annulus 1 < |x| < 2 have isomorphic cohomology (B2).
- **Failure-motivated definition.** No diffeomorphism X × ℝ → X; but the projection π and the inclusion i(x) = (x, 0) satisfy π∘i = id and i∘π is "deformable" to id through (x, t) ↦ (x, st). Name what that deformation is:
  - `def-smooth-homotopy`: F₀, F₁: U → V are smoothly homotopic if there is a C^∞ map H: U × J → V, J an open interval containing [0, 1], with H(·, 0) = F₀, H(·, 1) = F₁.
- **The homotopy formula** (named move 2, first appearance).
  - **Scratch Work.** "Look back at the cone: it pulled ω back along H(x, t) = tx to U × J and integrated the dt-component over [0, 1]. Do the same for any H. Write H*ω = α_t + dt ∧ β_t with α_t, β_t free of dt. Then Kω ≔ ∫₀¹ β_t dt. Compute H*dω = d(H*ω) by splitting d = d_x + dt ∧ ∂_t, and read off the dt-component. Expect: K dω = α₁ − α₀ − dKω." Then the clean statement:
  - `lem-homotopy-formula`: with K as above, F₁*ω − F₀*ω = dKω + Kdω for every ω ∈ Ωᵏ(V), k ≥ 0 (for k = 0, K = 0 and the formula reads f∘F₁ − f∘F₀ = K(df), the fundamental theorem of calculus).
  - *Proof sketch.* d(dt ∧ β_t) = −dt ∧ d_xβ_t; dα_t = d_xα_t + dt ∧ ∂_tα_t. So the dt-component of H*dω is ∂_tα_t − d_xβ_t; integrate over [0, 1], differentiating under the integral (AN2.11) to commute d_x with ∫. Finally α_j = F_j*ω (restrict to the slice t = j).
  - Remark: for H(x, t) = tx on a set star-shaped about 0, F₀ is constant, F₀*ω = 0 for k ≥ 1, and K is the cone operator. `lem-cone-formula` is the special case; the coordinate check of §02 is now redundant.
- `thm-homotopy-invariance`: smoothly homotopic maps induce the same map on cohomology.
- `cor-cylinder-invariance`: π: X × ℝ → X induces isomorphisms Hᵏ(X) ≅ Hᵏ(X × ℝ) for X ⊆ ℝᵐ open. *Proof:* π∘i = id; i∘π ≃ id via (x, t, s) ↦ (x, st).
- `cor-contractible-acyclic`: if id_U is smoothly homotopic to a constant map, Hᵏ(U) = 0 for k ≥ 1. Pays the secondary hook: `exm-c-sector` is diffeomorphic to a rectangle through polar coordinates, so acyclic, although not star-shaped (C1).
- **Worked example.** On A ≔ ℝⁿ ∖ {0}, the radial map r(x) = x/|x| is homotopic to id_A through (1 − s)x + s x/|x| (never zero: a positive combination of x and a positive multiple of x). So r* = id on H*(A): a closed form on A is cohomologous to r*ω, which depends only on ω along the unit sphere. This explains in advance why the sphere integral detects everything in §05.
- **Warning.** Homotopic maps induce the same map on cohomology, not the same pullback on forms: F₁*ω and F₀*ω differ by the exact form dKω (for closed ω).
- **Non-example.** Not every diffeomorphism of a domain is homotopic to the identity: the reflection of ℝⁿ ∖ {0} (`prp-reflection-not-homotopic`, §06).
- **Quick checks.**
  - Q1. Is the antipodal map a(x) = −x on ℝ² ∖ {0} homotopic to the identity? *Answer:* yes, rotation by sπ, s ∈ [0, 1]. Consistent with a*ω_θ = ω_θ (check directly).
  - Q2. Why can K not be defined for a continuous (non-smooth) homotopy here? *Answer:* H*ω needs derivatives of H. (Pointer: continuous homotopies suffice after smoothing, Lee 17; not used.)

  - B1.   - B2. Show that φ(x) = (1 + |x|/(1 + |x|)) x/|x| is a diffeomorphism of ℝ² ∖ {0} onto {1 < |x| < 2}. *Answer:* ρ ↦ 1 + ρ/(1 + ρ) is a smooth increasing bijection (0, ∞) → (1, 2) with positive derivative; φ is radial with that profile; inverse radial with the inverse profile.

## 04-punctured-plane.md — The punctured plane: one hole, one dimension

*Budget: ≤ 3,500 main-line words.*

- **Hook.** Return to ω_θ. AN3.6 and AN3.7 showed [ω_θ] ≠ 0. Is it the only kind of non-exactness on ℝ² ∖ {0}, up to scalars and exact forms?
- `lem-slit-plane-primitives`: U₋ and U₊ are star-shaped (about (1, 0) and (−1, 0)); U₋ ∩ U₊ = upper half-plane ⊔ lower half-plane; U₋ ∪ U₊ = ℝ² ∖ {0}.
- **Example** `exm-slit-primitive-jump`: Arg on U₋ and θ₊ on U₊ are both primitives of ω_θ; their difference is 0 on the upper half-plane and −2π on the lower half-plane. The jump is the circulation.
- **Glue across the overlap** (named move 3, first appearance, degree 1).
  - **Scratch Work.** "Given closed ω on ℝ² ∖ {0}, the Poincaré lemma gives f on U₋ and g on U₊ with df = ω = dg. On each half-plane f − g is constant: c_up and c_down. If c_up = c_down we replace g by g + c_up and glue. So the only obstruction is the number c_up − c_down. Express it as an integral: walk around the circle, using f on the right half and g on the left half." Then the proof.
  - `thm-h1-punctured-plane`: the map H¹(ℝ² ∖ {0}) → ℝ, [ω] ↦ (1/2π)∮_{S¹}ω is an isomorphism with inverse c ↦ c[ω_θ].
  - *Proof (worked in full: faded triple, step 1).* Well defined and linear (AN3.7 `cor-stokes-closed-manifold` on S¹). Surjective: ω_θ ↦ 1. Injective: if ∮_{S¹}ω = 0, show c_up = c_down via the explicit computation of ∮_{S¹}ω as (f(top) − f(bottom)) on the right semicircle (in U₋) plus (g(bottom) − g(top)) on the left semicircle (in U₊), which equals c_up − c_down; then glue as in the scratch work.
  - `cor-circulation-criterion`: a closed 1-form on ℝ² ∖ {0} is exact iff ∮_{S¹}ω = 0; then ∮_γ ω = 0 for every loop γ (`thm-exact-iff-zero-circulation`).
- **Pointer to CA (prose, revisits CA.4).** For a loop γ in ℂ ∖ {0}, (1/2π)∮_γ ω_θ is the winding number of CA.4; the theorem says the winding number is the only invariant of closed 1-forms on the punctured plane.
- **Worked example.** ω = (x dy − y dx)/(x² + 4y²): closed (one line), circulation π around the ellipse x = 2cos t, y = sin t, which is homotopic in ℝ² ∖ {0} to the unit circle (straight-line homotopy of the parametrizations never hits 0), so [ω] = ½[ω_θ].
- **Weird example** `exm-two-punctures` (exercise C1 below): on ℝ² ∖ {p, q} the form ω_p − ω_q (ω_c ≔ the angle form centred at c, the pullback of ω_θ by x ↦ x − c) has zero circulation around a large circle enclosing both points, yet is not exact. One loop is not enough when there are two holes.
- **Warning.** A single loop integral decides exactness on the punctured plane, not on other domains: with two punctures it does not (`exm-two-punctures`).
- **Non-example.** A closed 1-form on ℝ² ∖ {0} with circulation 2π that is not a multiple of ω_θ: ω_θ + dx (it is cohomologous to ω_θ, not equal to it).
- **Quick checks.**
  - Q1. What is (1/2π)∮_{S¹} of ω_θ + 3 dx? *Answer:* 1; dx is exact.
  - Q2. Suppose U₋ ∪ V = ℝ² ∖ {0} with V star-shaped. Can U₋ ∩ V be connected? *Answer:* No: if it were, the gluing argument would give H¹(ℝ² ∖ {0}) = 0, contradicting [ω_θ] ≠ 0. The disconnected overlap is where the hole lives.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Compute (1/2π)∮_{S¹} of (x dy − y dx)/(x² + y²) + 3 dx, and the class of this form. *Answer:* 1; [ω_θ].
  - A2. Give explicit primitives of ω_θ on U₋ and on U₊ and their difference on U₋ ∩ U₊. *Answer:* Arg ∈ (−π, π) and θ₊ ∈ (0, 2π); Arg − θ₊ = 0 on y > 0 and −2π on y < 0.
  - A3. Give a closed 1-form on ℝ² ∖ {0}, invariant under the antipodal map, that is not exact. *Answer:* ω_θ itself (a*ω_θ = ω_θ).
  - B1. For the closed form ω = (x dy − y dx)/(x² + 4y²), find c and f with ω = c ω_θ + df. *Answer:* c = ½ (worked example). Locally ω = ½ dΘ₂, where Θ₂ is an angle function of (x, 2y), and ω_θ = dΘ₁ with Θ₁ an angle of (x, y). The difference Θ₂ − Θ₁ is the argument of (x + 2iy)/(x + iy), whose real part (x² + 2y²)/(x² + y²) is positive, so it is the single-valued smooth function arctan(xy/(x² + 2y²)). Hence f = ½ arctan(xy/(x² + 2y²)).
  - B2. Let f be holomorphic on ℂ ∖ {0}. Show that the real and imaginary parts of f(z) dz are closed real 1-forms, and that f(z) dz = dF for some holomorphic F on ℂ ∖ {0} iff the residue of f at 0 is 0. *Answer:* Cauchy–Riemann gives closedness; ∮_{S¹} f dz = 2πi Res₀ f (CA.3); apply `cor-circulation-criterion` to real and imaginary parts; F = u + iv with du, dv the primitives is holomorphic since dF = f dz.
  - B3. Show that `thm-h1-punctured-plane` holds with ℝ² ∖ {0} replaced by the open annulus {1 < |x| < 2}. *Answer:* B2 of §03 and `cor-diffeo-invariance`; the isomorphism is ∮ over the circle |x| = 3/2.
  - C1. Let p = (−1, 0), q = (1, 0), W = ℝ² ∖ {p, q}. Cover W by U ≔ {x < ½} ∖ {p} and V ≔ {x > −½} ∖ {q}; U ∩ V is the strip |x| < ½, convex. Show ω ↦ ((1/2π)∮_{|z−p|=½}ω, (1/2π)∮_{|z−q|=½}ω) is an isomorphism H¹(W) → ℝ². *Answer:* U and V are each diffeomorphic to a punctured plane (a half-plane is diffeomorphic to ℝ² by a map fixing a neighbourhood of the puncture) so H¹(U) ≅ ℝ ≅ H¹(V) via the small circles; glue across the overlap: if both circulations vanish, ω|U = df, ω|V = dg, f − g constant on the connected strip, adjust and glue (injective); ω_p and ω_q map to (1, 0) and (0, 1) (surjective). Then `exm-two-punctures`: ω_p − ω_q ↦ (1, −1) ≠ 0 but has zero circulation around |z| = 3.
  - C2. Show H¹(ℝ² ∖ (ℤ × {0})) is infinite-dimensional. *Answer:* the forms ω_{(m,0)}, m ∈ ℤ, are closed; the circulation around the circle of radius ½ about (j, 0) is 2πδ_{jm}; a finite linear combination that is exact has all circulations zero, so all coefficients zero.

## 05-punctured-rn.md — Punctured ℝⁿ: the hole is seen in one degree

*Budget: ≤ 5,000 main-line words.*

- **Hook.** In ℝ³ ∖ {0}, is there a closed 1-form that is not exact? The loops around the origin can all be shrunk to a point without crossing it, so `thm-exact-iff-zero-circulation` with C2 of §01 suggests no. But the Coulomb flux form has flux 4π through the sphere. The hole of ℝⁿ ∖ {0} hides from 1-forms and shows up in degree n − 1.
- **The solid-angle form.**
  - `def-solid-angle-form`: σₙ ≔ |x|⁻ⁿ Σᵢ (−1)^{i−1} xᵢ dx₁ ∧ … ∧ \widehat{dxᵢ} ∧ … ∧ dxₙ on A ≔ ℝⁿ ∖ {0}. σ₂ = ω_θ.
  - `prp-solid-angle-closed`: dσₙ = 0. *Proof:* Σᵢ ∂ᵢ(xᵢ|x|⁻ⁿ) = n|x|⁻ⁿ − n|x|⁻ⁿ⁻² Σ xᵢ² = 0.
  - `prp-solid-angle-integral`: ∫_{Sⁿ⁻¹} σₙ = vol(Sⁿ⁻¹) > 0 (σₙ restricts to the volume form of the unit sphere with the outward orientation; AN3.7). Hence [σₙ] ≠ 0 in Hⁿ⁻¹(A) (AN3.7 `thm-exact-integral-zero`). For n = 3 this is AN3.7 `exm-solid-angle-not-exact`, cited.
- **The cover.** `lem-two-star-cover`: with e = eₙ, U ≔ ℝⁿ ∖ {te : t ≥ 0} and V ≔ ℝⁿ ∖ {te : t ≤ 0} are star-shaped (about −e and e), U ∪ V = A, and U ∩ V = ℝⁿ ∖ (xₙ-axis), which is diffeomorphic to (ℝⁿ⁻¹ ∖ {0}) × ℝ. *Proof sketch:* a segment from −e to x ∈ U meets the axis only at its start unless x lies on the axis, where it stays in the negative half.
- **Dimension shift** (named move 4, first appearance).
  - **Scratch Work.** "Take closed ω on A, k ≥ 1. On U and V, ω = dα and ω = dβ (Poincaré). On U ∩ V, d(α − β) = 0, so α − β is a closed (k − 1)-form on U ∩ V. If it is exact, glue (move 3) and ω is exact. So the obstruction to exactness in degree k on A lives in degree k − 1 on U ∩ V, which by `cor-cylinder-invariance` is degree k − 1 on ℝⁿ⁻¹ ∖ {0}: one degree and one dimension lower. Run it until n = 2 (or n = 1)." Then the clean lemma:
  - `lem-mv-step`: let {φ_U, φ_V} be a partition of unity on A subordinate to {U, V} (AM.1). Define δ: Hᵏ(A) → Hᵏ⁻¹(U ∩ V) by δ[ω] ≔ [α − β]. Then:
    (a) for k ≥ 2, δ is a well-defined isomorphism;
    (b) for k = 1, δ induces an isomorphism H¹(A) ≅ H⁰(U ∩ V)/{(a − b)|_{U∩V} : a, b constants}.
  - *Proof sketch.* Well defined: changing α by a closed (k−1)-form on U changes it by an exact one if k − 1 ≥ 1 (Poincaré on U); for k = 1 by a constant, which is quotiented out. Injective (glue across the overlap, general form): if α − β = dγ, set γ_U ≔ φ_V γ extended by 0 to U and γ_V ≔ φ_U γ extended by 0 to V (smooth: supp φ_V ∩ U is closed in U and lies in U ∩ V); η ≔ α − dγ_U on U, β + dγ_V on V agree on U ∩ V; dη = ω. Surjective: given closed τ on U ∩ V, ω ≔ d(φ_V τ) on U and −d(φ_U τ) on V agree on U ∩ V because d((φ_U + φ_V)τ) = dτ = 0; ω is closed, and δ[ω] = [φ_V τ + φ_U τ] = [τ].
  - Pointer: this is one piece of the Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence (Tu 25–26); the chapter needs only this piece and proves it for this cover.
- `thm-cohomology-punctured-rn`: for n ≥ 2, Hᵏ(ℝⁿ ∖ {0}) ≅ ℝ for k = 0 and k = n − 1, and 0 otherwise. For n ≥ 2, [σₙ] spans Hⁿ⁻¹.
  - *Proof sketch.* H⁰: A connected. H¹: by (b), U ∩ V ≅ (ℝⁿ⁻¹ ∖ {0}) × ℝ has 2 components for n = 2 (quotient ℝ²/diagonal ≅ ℝ) and 1 for n ≥ 3 (quotient 0). Hᵏ, k ≥ 2: by (a) and `cor-cylinder-invariance`, Hᵏ(ℝⁿ ∖ {0}) ≅ Hᵏ⁻¹(ℝⁿ⁻¹ ∖ {0}); induct on n from n = 2 (where Hᵏ⁻¹(ℝ ∖ {0}) = 0 for k ≥ 2 since ℝ ∖ {0} is two intervals). The spanning statement: `prp-solid-angle-integral` and dimension 1.
  - Remark: n = 2 recovers §04 by a second route; §04's proof is the special case where the correction is a constant.
- `cor-sphere-integral-criterion`: a closed (n − 1)-form ω on ℝⁿ ∖ {0} is exact iff ∫_{Sⁿ⁻¹}ω = 0; in general ω − (∫_{Sⁿ⁻¹}ω / vol Sⁿ⁻¹) σₙ is exact.
- **Worked example.** Gauss's law for σ₃: for a compact 3-manifold with boundary M ⊆ ℝ³ with 0 ∉ ∂M, ∫_{∂M} σ₃ = 4π if 0 ∈ int M and 0 if 0 ∉ M (Stokes on M, or on M minus a small ball).
- **Weird examples.**
  - `exm-coulomb-flux` (pays the section hook): E = x/|x|³ on ℝ³ ∖ {0}. Its work form E·dx = −d(1/|x|) is exact (as every closed 1-form on ℝ³ ∖ {0} must be, H¹ = 0), but its flux form is σ₃, not exact.
  - `exm-dirac-string`: σ₃ is exact on V = ℝ³ ∖ {(0, 0, t) : t ≤ 0} (star-shaped about e₃, `lem-two-star-cover`) with primitive (1 − z/|x|) ω_θ, ω_θ = (x dy − y dx)/(x² + y²). The primitive is smooth across the positive z-axis and blows up along the negative one. No primitive extends to all of ℝ³ ∖ {0}, since σ₃ is not exact there.
  - `exm-wire-field`: B = (−y, x, 0)/(x² + y²) on ℝ³ ∖ (z-axis): curl-free, circulation 2π around the axis, not a gradient. ℝ³ ∖ line ≅ (ℝ² ∖ {0}) × ℝ has H¹ ≅ ℝ: removing a line is a hole for 1-forms, removing a point is not.
- **Warning.** σₙ is not d of a global "solid angle function" on ℝⁿ ∖ {0}; no such function exists, exactly as with ω_θ.
- **Non-example.** Removing a line instead of a point: the wire field (`exm-wire-field`) shows ℝ³ minus a line has H¹ ≠ 0, so the vanishing of H¹ in §05 is special to point holes.
- **Quick checks.**
  - Q1. List Hᵏ(ℝ⁴ ∖ {0}) for k = 0, …, 4. *Answer:* ℝ, 0, 0, ℝ, 0.
  - Q2. Why does the induction start at n = 2 and not use n = 1 for H¹? *Answer:* H¹ is handled by (b) for every n; (a) is used only for k ≥ 2, where the base case needs Hᵏ⁻¹(ℝ ∖ {0}) = 0 for k − 1 ≥ 1.

## 06-applications.md — Retractions, reflections, potentials and fluxes

*Budget: ≤ 3,000 main-line words.*

- **Hook.** AN3.7 proved that the closed ball does not retract smoothly onto its boundary sphere (`thm-smooth-no-retraction`), by showing det Dr = 0. Now that the hole of ℝⁿ ∖ {0} has a name (the class [σₙ]), the same theorem becomes one line: the identity of Sⁿ⁻¹ would have to factor through a star-shaped set, which kills every class.
- `prp-no-retraction-cohomology`: let W ⊇ B̄ⁿ be open and g: W → ℝⁿ ∖ {0} smooth with g(x) = x on Sⁿ⁻¹. This is impossible (n ≥ 2).
  - *Proof sketch.* g*σₙ is a closed (n − 1)-form on W; on an open ball B(0, 1 + ε) ⊆ W (star-shaped) it is exact (`thm-poincare-lemma`), so ∫_{Sⁿ⁻¹} g*σₙ = 0 (`thm-exact-integral-zero`). But g = id on Sⁿ⁻¹, so g*σₙ and σₙ agree on the tangent spaces of Sⁿ⁻¹, and the integral is vol(Sⁿ⁻¹) ≠ 0 (`prp-solid-angle-integral`).
  - Remark: a retraction r as in AN3.7 is such a g; conversely g/|g| is a retraction. So this is AN3.7's theorem (for C^∞ maps), with the determinant argument replaced by Poincaré.
- `rem-brouwer-pointer`: one paragraph. The no-retraction theorem is the engine of the Brouwer fixed-point theorem. The deduction, the passage from smooth to continuous maps, and the applications live in the FX branch (see edge only). No proof, no exercise.
- `prp-reflection-not-homotopic`: the reflection R(x) = (x₁, …, xₙ₋₁, −xₙ) of ℝⁿ ∖ {0} is not smoothly homotopic to the identity (n ≥ 2), although it is a diffeomorphism.
  - *Proof sketch.* R*σₙ = −σₙ (the term with xₙ changes sign through xₙ, every other term through dxₙ). If R ≃ id, then `thm-homotopy-invariance` gives [σₙ] = −[σₙ], so 2σₙ is exact, contradicting `prp-solid-angle-integral`.
  - Contrast (one sentence): the antipodal map satisfies a*σₙ = (−1)ⁿσₙ (§05 A3), so the same argument shows a ≄ id when n is odd; AM.3 turns this into the hairy ball theorem.
- **Vector calculus on ℝ³**, in AN3.6's dictionary (ω_F work form, η_F flux form, `exm-grad-curl-div`):
  - `cor-gradient-criterion`: on a star-shaped open set, curl F = 0 ⇒ F = ∇f. On ℝ³ ∖ {0}, curl F = 0 ⇒ F = ∇f (H¹ = 0). On ℝ³ minus a line, curl F = 0 ⇒ F = ∇f iff the circulation around the line vanishes.
  - `cor-flux-criterion`: on a star-shaped set, div G = 0 ⇒ G = curl F. On ℝ³ ∖ {0}, div G = 0 ⇒ G = curl F iff the flux of G through S² vanishes.

- **Worked example.** Decide which fields are gradients or curls: F = (yz, xz, xy) on ℝ³ (gradient of xyz); the wire field (not a gradient); x/|x|³ on ℝ³ ∖ {0} (gradient of −1/|x|, not a curl).

- **Non-example.** The closed annulus does retract onto its outer circle (Q1).
- **Quick checks.**
  - Q1. Does the closed annulus {1 ≤ |x| ≤ 2} retract smoothly onto its outer circle? *Answer:* yes, x ↦ 2x/|x|. The theorem is about the ball, whose boundary is the whole sphere and which has no hole.
  - Q2. Is the rotation by π of ℝ² ∖ {0} homotopic to the identity? Is R? *Answer:* yes (rotate by sπ); no (`prp-reflection-not-homotopic`, n = 2: R*ω_θ = −ω_θ).
- **Exercises.**

  - A2. Show that the wire field (−y, x, 0)/(x² + y²) is curl-free and compute its circulation around the unit circle in the xy-plane. *Answer:* 2π.
  - A3. Is x/|x|³ divergence-free on ℝ³ ∖ {0}? Is it a curl there? *Answer:* yes; no (flux 4π, `cor-flux-criterion`).
  - B1. Prove Gauss's law for a ball not centred at 0: ∫_{∂B(c, ρ)}σ₃ = 4π if |c| < ρ and 0 if |c| > ρ (0 ∉ ∂B). *Answer:* if |c| > ρ, σ₃ is exact on a ball slightly larger than B(c, ρ) that misses 0 (star-shaped), so the integral is 0; if |c| < ρ, `thm-stokes` on B̄(c, ρ) minus a small open ball around 0 gives the integral over a small sphere about 0, which is 4π (σ₃ is invariant under x ↦ λx, λ > 0).
  - B2. Let G be smooth and divergence-free on ℝ³ ∖ {0}, with flux Φ through S². Show that G − (Φ/4π) x/|x|³ is a curl on ℝ³ ∖ {0}. *Answer:* `cor-sphere-integral-criterion` in vector language.
  - B3. Show that R*ω_θ = −ω_θ for R(x, y) = (x, −y), and deduce directly from `thm-h1-punctured-plane` that R ≄ id. *Answer:* direct substitution; R* acts as −1 on H¹ ≅ ℝ, while id* = 1.

Show F*[σₙ] = d_F [σₙ] for a unique real number d_F, that d_F depends only on the smooth homotopy class of F, and compute d_F for F = id, R and a. *Answer:* Hⁿ⁻¹ is one-dimensional spanned by [σₙ] (`thm-cohomology-punctured-rn`), so F*[σₙ] is a multiple; homotopy invariance; d_id = 1, d_R = −1, d_a = (−1)ⁿ.
