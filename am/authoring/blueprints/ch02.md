# Blueprint: AN3.5 — Submanifolds of ℝⁿ and Tangent Spaces

**Directory:** `am/src/ch02-submanifolds-tangent-spaces/`. **AM chapter 2** (reading order AM.1, AN3.5, AN3.6, AN3.7, AM.2, AM.3; math-atlas spec/08 §D3). Main line; the seg B mark of the old AN3 placement is dropped. Atlas id stays AN3.5. State: blueprint (S1), not drafted.

**Purpose.**
- A *k*-dimensional smooth object in ℝⁿ can be handed to us in three ways: as a graph, as a level set, or as the image of a parametrization. Each naive version admits monsters (a cusp, a crossing, a cone). The chapter fixes one definition (local graph over some *k* standard coordinates), proves that the regular versions of the other two descriptions give exactly the same sets, and extracts from that proof the two tools every later chapter of AM uses: **charts with smooth transition maps** and **the tangent space T_pM**.
- Everything is done inside ℝⁿ. Abstract manifolds wait for AM.3, where the transition-map theorem of §3 becomes a definition.
- The chapter also sets up **manifolds with boundary** in ℝⁿ (§6), because AN3.7 integrates over them and needs their boundary to be a manifold of one dimension less, with an outward normal.
- Volume of a *k*-manifold (Gram determinant), orientation and integration are **not** here; they open AN3.7.

**House manner (spec/08 §C0, §C5; graduate taper).** Apostol-style breadth on the skeleton of spec/08 §D3; Cummings devices for motivation: the chapter hook is planted in the index and paid in §5; definitions enter through a failure; Scratch Work, kept apart from the proof, appears only at the first use of a technique (four places, listed below); one faded triple, for the chapter's main move; a weird-examples table closes §1 and is extended in §6; the chapter ends with a List of Results. No footnote jokes, no historical anecdote footnotes, no first person.

## index.md

- **Hook (planted here, paid in §5, `exm-rotation-velocity`).** A rigid body spinning about a fixed point is, at each instant, a rotation matrix R(t) ∈ SO(3). Physics speaks of its angular velocity, a single vector ω. Where does a vector come from? SO(3) sits in ℝ⁹ cut out by six polynomial equations RᵀR = I, so its "dimension" should be 9 − 6 = 3, and the velocity R′(0) at R(0) = I should range over a 3-dimensional space of matrices. The chapter makes both statements precise: SO(3) is a 3-dimensional submanifold of ℝ⁹ (`thm-orthogonal-group`), its tangent space at I is the space of skew-symmetric 3 × 3 matrices, and a skew-symmetric Ω acts by Ωv = ω × v. That is where ω comes from.
- **Two hooks planted for later chapters** (each one sentence in the index, each repeated where it arises):
  - The Möbius band (`exm-moebius-band`, §3) is a perfectly good 2-submanifold of ℝ³; does it admit a continuous unit normal field? Paid in AN3.7 (orientation).
  - `thm-transition-smooth` (§3) is a theorem here because M sits in ℝⁿ. Paid in AM.3, where smoothness of transition maps becomes the definition of a smooth manifold and the submanifolds of this chapter become the first examples.
- **What you need:** AN3.2 (inverse, implicit and rank theorems), AN3.1 (chain rule, C¹), metric compactness and connectedness (AN2.1, AN2.3), and from linear algebra kernels, images and rank–nullity (LA1.2).

- **Named moves** (STYLE §6 list):

## 01-three-descriptions.md — Graphs, level sets, parametrizations, and their monsters

Budget: ≈ 3,800 main-line words.

- **Opening frame.** The unit circle is {x² + y² = 1}, the graph y = ±√(1 − x²) in pieces, and the image of t ↦ (cos t, sin t). Each description generalizes. Try each generalization naively and watch it fail.
- **Failure 1: images of smooth maps** (`exm-cusp-curve`). t ↦ (t², t³) is C^∞, injective, and its image {y² = x³} has a cusp at 0. The trouble is φ′(0) = 0. Repair: ask for an *immersion* (`def-immersion`: Dφ(a) injective). Then `exm-figure-eight`: φ(t) = (sin 2t, sin t), t ∈ (−π, π), is an injective immersion (check: φ′ = (2 cos 2t, cos t) never vanishes; injectivity from sin t = sin s ⇒ s ∈ {t, π − t}), yet the image crosses itself at the origin as t → ±π. A second repair is needed (homeomorphism onto the image); it is postponed to `def-coordinate-patch` in §3, and stated here as a forward pointer.
- **Failure 2: zero sets of smooth functions** (`exm-double-cone`). {x² + y² − z² = 0} is the zero set of a polynomial and is a double cone with a vertex. Remark (prose only, no proof, no edge): every closed subset of ℝⁿ is the zero set of some C^∞ function, so "zero set of a smooth function" alone means nothing. Repair: ask DF(p) to be surjective at every point of the level set (`def-regular-value`, `def-submersion` at a point).
- **Failure 3: graphs over a fixed axis** (`exm-cube-root-graph`). The graph of x ↦ x^{1/3} is the graph of the smooth y ↦ y³ over the *other* axis. So the definition must allow the graph to be over any k of the standard coordinates, chosen near each point.
- **`def-local-graph` and `def-submanifold`.** M ⊆ ℝⁿ is a k-dimensional (smooth) submanifold if every p ∈ M has an open neighbourhood U ⊆ ℝⁿ such that, after a permutation of the coordinates, M ∩ U = {(x, g(x)) : x ∈ W} for some open W ⊆ ℝᵏ and smooth g : W → ℝ^{n−k}. In symbols as well (STYLE §10). k = n: open subsets of ℝⁿ; k = 0: discrete sets (every point isolated). Explain template in full for this definition (major notion).
- **`lem-coordinate-plane-projection`.** If V ⊆ ℝⁿ is a k-dimensional subspace, some projection onto k standard coordinates restricts to an isomorphism V → ℝᵏ. *Proof Idea:* the n × k matrix of a basis has rank k, so k of its rows are independent (LA1.2). This is why fixing standard coordinates loses nothing: a rotated graph is still a graph over some coordinate plane locally (§2 proves it in the needed form).
- **`thm-graph-is-submanifold`.** The graph of a smooth g : W → ℝ^{n−k}, W open in ℝᵏ, is a k-submanifold (one U = W × ℝ^{n−k} serves every point). One line.
- **Worked example: the circle and the sphere by graphs.** S¹ needs four graphs (y = ±√(1 − x²), x = ±√(1 − y²)); S^{n−1} needs 2n. Count them as an exercise.
- **Warning.** "Locally a graph" is a statement about M ∩ U for a *neighbourhood U in ℝⁿ*, not about a piece of M chosen by hand. The figure-eight is locally a graph along each of its two strands, but no neighbourhood of the crossing point meets the image in a single strand.
- **Quick check.** Is the x-axis together with the y-axis a 1-submanifold of ℝ²? Answer: no; near 0, the set with 0 removed has four components, a graph of a continuous function over an interval with one point removed has two.
- **Weird-examples table** (multivariable table of AN3 continued; extended in §6). Columns: example; which naive description it satisfies; which condition fails; where it returns.

  | Example | Satisfies | Fails | Returns |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `exm-cusp-curve` (t², t³) | smooth injective image | immersion at t = 0 | §2 B-ex |
  | (t³, t³) | smooth image, non-immersion | (nothing: the image is a line) | §1 A2 |
  | `exm-figure-eight` | injective immersion | homeomorphism onto image | §3 |
  | `exm-dense-winding` (§2 C2) | injective immersion into a torus in ℝ⁴ | image is dense in the torus | AM.3 |

- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** For each set decide whether it is, near the origin, a graph of a smooth function over some choice of standard coordinates: (a) z = x² − y²; (b) x = y³ + z; (c) z² = x² + y². *Answer:* (a) yes, over (x, y); (b) yes, over (y, z); (c) no: the cone is two-sheeted at 0, and over no coordinate plane is it single-valued and smooth (over (x, y) it is z = ±√(x² + y²), two-valued; over (x, z) it is y = ±√(z² − x²), defined only where |z| ≥ |x|).
  - **A2.** Is t ↦ (t³, t³) an immersion? Is its image a submanifold? *Answer:* not an immersion at 0 (derivative 0); the image is the line y = x, a submanifold. Regularity of a parametrization is sufficient, not necessary.
  - **B1.** Show that the graph of x ↦ x^{1/3} in ℝ² is a 1-submanifold although x^{1/3} is not differentiable at 0. *Answer:* it equals {(y³, y) : y ∈ ℝ}, the graph of the smooth y ↦ y³ over the second coordinate.
  - **B2.** Show that {y² = x³} is not a 1-submanifold at the origin. *Answer:* near 0, over the x-axis it is two-valued for x > 0 (y = ±x^{3/2}); over the y-axis it is x = |y|^{2/3}, which is not differentiable at 0, and a local graph over the y-axis is unique, so no smooth one exists.
  - **B3.** Let n ≥ 2. Cover S^{n−1} by 2n local graphs, and show that a single graph cannot cover S^{n−1}. *Answer:* the hemispheres x_i > 0 and x_i < 0, as graphs of ±√(1 − Σ_{j≠i} x_j²) over the other coordinates. A single graph over an open W ⊆ ℝ^{n−1} is homeomorphic to W (projection and its inverse); if it were all of S^{n−1}, W would be compact, hence closed, and open and nonempty in the connected ℝ^{n−1}, so W = ℝ^{n−1}, which is not compact.
  - **C1.** Show that the union of the coordinate axes in ℝ³ is not a 1-submanifold. *Answer:* same component count as the Quick check: six components against two.
  - **C2.** Is the graph of x ↦ |x| a C¹ 1-submanifold of ℝ²? *Answer:* no. Over the x-axis the unique graph function is |x|, not differentiable; over the y-axis it is not a graph (two x for each y > 0). It is homeomorphic to ℝ, so the failure is of smoothness, not of topology.

## 02-local-descriptions.md — The local equivalence theorem

Budget: ≈ 4,500 main-line words.

- **Lead-in.** The repaired level sets and the repaired parametrizations of §1 should give the same sets as local graphs. The inverse function theorem is the bridge, entered through one move.
- **`def-submersion`** (DF(p) surjective) and **`def-regular-value`** (c is a regular value of F : U → ℝᵐ if DF(p) is surjective for every p ∈ F⁻¹(c); an empty level set counts).
- **`thm-regular-level-set`.** Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be open, F : U → ℝ^{n−k} smooth, c a regular value. Then F⁻¹(c) is a k-submanifold. Hypotheses: U open (else no derivative); surjectivity at every point of the level set (else `exm-double-cone`); n − k ≤ n.
  - **Scratch Work (move 1, first appearance).** We want coordinates in which F⁻¹(c) is flat. DF(p) is (n − k) × n of rank n − k; pick n − k independent columns and permute them to the end, writing x = (u, v) with D_vF(p) invertible. The map F alone goes ℝⁿ → ℝ^{n−k}; to use the inverse function theorem we need ℝⁿ → ℝⁿ, so keep the u-coordinates: Φ(u, v) = (u, F(u, v)). DΦ = [[I, 0], [D_uF, D_vF]] is invertible. Under Φ, the level set becomes {(u, c)}, flat.
  - **Proof.** Write the steps cleanly: Φ is a local diffeomorphism at p (`thm-inverse-function`, `thm-inverse-regularity`); F⁻¹(c) ∩ U′ = Φ⁻¹((ℝᵏ × {c}) ∩ V′); writing Φ⁻¹(u, c) = (u, g(u)) shows F⁻¹(c) ∩ U′ is the graph of g. (Equivalently, cite `thm-implicit-function` directly; say that the implicit function theorem is this move done once and for all, and that the move is the part to remember because it also gives the straightening.)
- **`thm-submanifold-local-descriptions`.** For M ⊆ ℝⁿ, p ∈ M, and 0 ≤ k ≤ n, the following are equivalent:
  1. (graph) M is a local graph over some k standard coordinates near p;
  2. (level set) there are an open U ∋ p and a smooth F : U → ℝ^{n−k} with DF(q) surjective for q ∈ M ∩ U and M ∩ U = F⁻¹(0);
  3. (patch) there are an open W ⊆ ℝᵏ, an open U ∋ p and a smooth immersion φ : W → ℝⁿ that is a homeomorphism of W onto M ∩ U;
  4. (straightening) there are open U ∋ p, V ⊆ ℝⁿ and a diffeomorphism Φ : U → V with Φ(M ∩ U) = (ℝᵏ × {0}) ∩ V.
  - *Route.* 1 ⇒ 4: Φ(x, y) = (x, y − g(x)), inverse (x, z) ↦ (x, z + g(x)), written out. 4 ⇒ 2: F = last n − k coordinates of Φ. 2 ⇒ 1: `thm-regular-level-set` (localized). 1 ⇒ 3: φ(x) = (x, g(x)), inverse = coordinate projection, continuous. 3 ⇒ 1: Dφ(a) has rank k; by `lem-coordinate-plane-projection` pick k coordinates π with π ∘ Dφ(a) invertible; π ∘ φ is a local diffeomorphism at a (`thm-inverse-function`) with inverse ψ; then φ(W₀) is the graph of (complementary coordinates) ∘ φ ∘ ψ over the open set π(φ(W₀)). **The homeomorphism clause enters exactly here:** φ(W₀) is open in M (because φ is a homeomorphism onto M ∩ U), so φ(W₀) = M ∩ U₀ for some open U₀; without it, M ∩ U₀ could contain other strands (`exm-figure-eight`). Mark the line.
  - Proof Idea paragraph names move 1 again (no Scratch Work: second appearance).
- **`cor-straightening`.** Every k-submanifold is, near each point, carried by a diffeomorphism of ℝⁿ onto a flat piece of ℝᵏ × {0}. Name it: *slice chart* (Lee's term may be mentioned in prose as the standard name, without quoting).
- **Remark (regularity).** Replace "smooth" by Cʳ, r ≥ 1, throughout; nothing changes, since the inverse function theorem preserves Cʳ.
- **Warning (regularity is sufficient, not necessary).** F(x, y) = y² has 0 as a critical value and F⁻¹(0) is the x-axis, a submanifold. A critical value says nothing; to *disprove* submanifoldness, use topology (component counts) or uniqueness of local graphs.
- **Worked example `exm-sphere-three-ways`.** S² near (0, 0, 1): as a graph z = √(1 − x² − y²) over the open unit disc; as F⁻¹(1) for F = x² + y² + z², DF = 2(x, y, z) ≠ 0 on S²; as the patch (x, y) ↦ (x, y, √(1 − x² − y²)); straightening Φ(x, y, z) = (x, y, z − √(1 − x² − y²)) on {z > 0, x² + y² < 1}.
- **Quick check.** For which c is {x² + y² − z² = c} a 2-submanifold? Answer: c ≠ 0 by `thm-regular-level-set` (the only critical point of F is 0, which lies on F⁻¹(0) alone); for c = 0, not at the vertex (B1 below).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Write the hyperbola {xy = 1} as a level set, a graph and a patch, and give a straightening diffeomorphism near (1, 1). *Answer:* F = xy, DF = (y, x) ≠ 0 on the set; graph y = 1/x over x > 0; patch t ↦ (t, 1/t); Φ(x, y) = (x, y − 1/x) on {x > 0}.
  - **A2.** Which implication in `thm-submanifold-local-descriptions` uses the inverse function theorem? *Answer:* 2 ⇒ 1 and 3 ⇒ 1. The others are explicit formulas.
  - **B1.** Show that the cone {x² + y² = z²} is not a 2-submanifold at 0. *Answer:* a small ball around 0 meets the cone minus the vertex in two components (z > 0 and z < 0); a graph over an open disc minus one point is connected.
  - **B2.** Show the image of the figure-eight φ(t) = (sin 2t, sin t), t ∈ (−π, π), is not a 1-submanifold at 0. *Answer:* in a small ball, the image minus 0 has four components (the two strands through 0, with tangent directions (2, 1) at t = 0 and (2, −1) as t → ±π), against two for a graph.
  - **B3.** Show that S² ∩ {z = x} is a 1-submanifold of ℝ³ (a circle). *Answer:* F = (x² + y² + z² − 1, z − x); rows (2x, 2y, 2z) and (−1, 0, 1) are dependent only when y = 0 and x = −z, which with z = x forces x = z = 0 and then 0 + 0 + 0 = 1 fails. So 0 is a regular value and the set is a 1-submanifold.
  - **C1 (faded, last step blank; move 1).** Let φ : (a, b) → ℝⁿ be smooth with φ′(t₀) ≠ 0. Show that φ restricted to a small interval around t₀ has image equal to a graph over one coordinate axis. Given: pick i with φᵢ′(t₀) ≠ 0; φᵢ is a local diffeomorphism of a small interval I onto an interval J by the one-variable inverse function theorem; let ψ : J → I be its inverse. *Name the move, then complete it.* *Answer:* φ(I) = {(… , s, …) : s ∈ J}, the i-th coordinate equal to s and the others given by φⱼ ∘ ψ(s), j ≠ i; it is the graph of (φⱼ ∘ ψ)_{j≠i} over the i-th axis. (Padding is trivial here: the move is to invert the one good coordinate.) Add: this shows φ(I) is a 1-submanifold, but not that φ((a, b)) is (`exm-figure-eight`).
  - **C2 (`exm-dense-winding`).** Let α be irrational and φ(t) = (cos t, sin t, cos αt, sin αt) ∈ ℝ⁴. Show φ is an injective immersion whose image is not a 1-submanifold. *Answer:* |φ′| = √(1 + α²); φ(t) = φ(s) forces t − s ∈ 2πℤ and α(t − s) ∈ 2πℤ, so t = s. The tangent vector at p = φ(0) is (0, 1, 0, α), with nonzero second entry, so a local graph at p would be over the second coordinate, and the points of M near p with second coordinate 0 would be p alone. But φ(2πm) = (1, 0, cos 2παm, sin 2παm), and by the pigeonhole principle (among the fractional parts of 0, α, …, Nα two lie within 1/N) there are m ≠ 0 with αm within 1/N of an integer, so φ(2πm) ≠ p is arbitrarily close to p with second coordinate 0. Contradiction.

## 03-charts-and-smooth-maps.md — Coordinate patches, transition maps and smooth maps

Budget: ≈ 3,800 main-line words.

- **Failure-motivated definition.** The figure-eight showed that an injective immersion can still glue a strand onto another point. **`def-coordinate-patch`:** a smooth immersion φ : W → ℝⁿ, W ⊆ ℝᵏ open, that is a homeomorphism of W onto an open subset of M. By `thm-submanifold-local-descriptions` (3 ⇔ 1), M is a k-submanifold iff it is covered by the images of coordinate patches. Quick contrast with Munkres' definition (which starts from patches): the two agree by the theorem; say so in one sentence.
- **`thm-transition-smooth`.** If φ : W → M and ψ : W′ → M are coordinate patches with overlapping images, then ψ⁻¹ ∘ φ : φ⁻¹(ψ(W′)) → ψ⁻¹(φ(W)) is a diffeomorphism between open subsets of ℝᵏ.
  - *Route.* Continuity and openness of the domains: φ and ψ are homeomorphisms onto open subsets of M. Smoothness near a point: take a straightening Φ at q = φ(a) (`cor-straightening`); write Φ ∘ ψ = (ψ̃, 0); ψ̃ is a smooth immersion between open subsets of ℝᵏ, hence a local diffeomorphism (`thm-inverse-function`); then ψ⁻¹ ∘ φ = ψ̃⁻¹ ∘ (first k coordinates of Φ ∘ φ), smooth. Symmetric argument for the inverse.
  - **Why it matters** (one paragraph): it is the only reason "smooth function on M" and, in AN3.7, "integral over M" do not depend on the chart. **Forward hook (AM.3):** for an abstract manifold this theorem becomes the definition.
- **`exm-stereographic-circle`.** Patches φ_N(u) = (2u, u² − 1)/(u² + 1) and φ_S(u) = (2u, 1 − u²)/(u² + 1) (projections from (0, 1) and (0, −1)). Transition φ_S⁻¹ ∘ φ_N(u) = 1/u on u ≠ 0. *Drafter: verify with sympy; σ_N(x, y) = x/(1 − y), σ_S(x, y) = x/(1 + y).*
- **`def-smooth-on-submanifold`.** f : M → ℝᵐ is smooth if f ∘ φ is smooth for every patch φ; by `thm-transition-smooth` it suffices to check one patch around each point.
- **`prp-smooth-iff-local-extension`.** f : M → ℝᵐ is smooth iff each p ∈ M has an open U ∋ p in ℝⁿ and a smooth F : U → ℝᵐ with F = f on M ∩ U. *Route.* ⇐: f ∘ φ = F ∘ φ. ⇒: with a straightening Φ and the patch φ = Φ⁻¹(·, 0), set F = (f ∘ φ) ∘ π ∘ Φ, π the projection to the first k coordinates. (Global extension needs partitions of unity; one sentence pointing back to AM.1 in prose, not used.)
- **`def-smooth-map-submanifolds`.** f : M → N (M ⊆ ℝⁿ, N ⊆ ℝᵐ submanifolds) is smooth if it is smooth as a map into ℝᵐ; a diffeomorphism if bijective, smooth, with smooth inverse.
- **Warning.** A smooth homeomorphism need not be a diffeomorphism: x ↦ x³ on ℝ. And the identity of ℝ as a set can carry two different "chart structures" (ℝ with patch x ↦ x versus x ↦ x³) that are diffeomorphic but not equal; this is noted only to prepare AM.3, without the terminology.
- **`exm-moebius-band`.** φ(s, t) = ((2 + t cos(s/2)) cos s, (2 + t cos(s/2)) sin s, t sin(s/2)), s ∈ ℝ, |t| < 1. Two patches (s ∈ (0, 2π) and s ∈ (−π, π)) cover the band; show they are immersions and homeomorphisms onto open subsets. The transition map on one of the two overlap components is (s, t) ↦ (s − 2π, −t). **Plant the AN3.7 hook here:** the determinant of the transition derivative is +1 on one component of the overlap and −1 on the other. Is there a continuous unit normal? Question left open; answered in AN3.7.
  *Drafter: check both components with sympy; on s ∈ (0, π) the transition is the identity, on s ∈ (π, 2π) it is (s, t) ↦ (s − 2π, −t).*
- **Quick check.** Can S¹ be covered by a single coordinate patch? Answer: no; the domain W would be homeomorphic to S¹, hence compact, nonempty, open and closed in ℝ, so W = ℝ, which is not compact.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Show that the restriction of a smooth map ℝⁿ → ℝᵐ to a submanifold M is smooth, and that if it maps M into a submanifold N it is smooth as a map M → N. *Answer:* the restriction extends itself (`prp-smooth-iff-local-extension`).
  - **A2.** For the patch φ(θ) = (cos θ, sin θ), θ ∈ (0, 2π), of S¹, which point is missed, and is φ a coordinate patch? *Answer:* (1, 0); yes (inverse is a branch of the argument, continuous on S¹ ∖ {(1, 0)}).
  - **B1.** Prove that the squaring map S¹ → S¹, (x, y) ↦ (x² − y², 2xy), is smooth, and write it in the patch of A2. *Answer:* restriction of a polynomial map of ℝ² that maps S¹ to S¹ (|z²| = |z|²); in the patch it is θ ↦ 2θ (mod 2π, piecewise on the domain).
  - **B2.** Verify that the two Möbius patches are coordinate patches and compute both transition components. *Answer:* as in the drafter's note; Dφ has rank 2 since ∂φ/∂t = (cos(s/2) cos s, cos(s/2) sin s, sin(s/2)) is a unit vector orthogonal to the horizontal part of ∂φ/∂s at t = 0 and independence persists for |t| < 1 (drafter: write the 2 × 2 Gram determinant and show it is positive).
  - **B3.** Show that if f : M → ℝ is smooth and γ : (−ε, ε) → ℝⁿ is a smooth curve with values in M, then f ∘ γ is smooth. *Answer:* f ∘ γ = F ∘ γ near each t, with F a local extension (`prp-smooth-iff-local-extension`).
  - **C1.** Show that no compact k-submanifold with k ≥ 1 can be covered by a single coordinate patch. *Answer:* as the Quick check, with ℝᵏ connected.

## 04-tangent-spaces.md — Tangent spaces and differentials

Budget: ≈ 4,500 main-line words.

- **Failure-motivated definition.** "The tangent plane to the graph of g is the graph of Dg" depends on which coordinates the graph is over, and "the tangent space of F⁻¹(c) is ker DF" depends on F. A definition must not depend on the description. Velocities of curves do not.
- **`def-tangent-space`.** T_pM = {γ′(0) : γ : (−ε, ε) → ℝⁿ smooth, γ(t) ∈ M for all t, γ(0) = p}. Note: a subset of ℝⁿ, a priori not a subspace.
- **`thm-tangent-space-descriptions`.** Let M be a k-submanifold and p ∈ M. Then:
  1. if φ : W → M is a patch with φ(a) = p, T_pM = Dφ(a)(ℝᵏ);
  2. if M ∩ U = F⁻¹(0) with DF surjective on M ∩ U, T_pM = ker DF(p);
  3. if M ∩ U is the graph of g over coordinates x (after permutation), with p = (x₀, g(x₀)), T_pM = {(v, Dg(x₀)v) : v ∈ ℝᵏ};
  in particular T_pM is a k-dimensional subspace (`cor-tangent-space-dimension`).
  - **Scratch Work (move 3, first appearance).** If γ stays in M then F ∘ γ ≡ 0, so DF(p)γ′(0) = 0 by the chain rule: T_pM ⊆ ker DF(p). Conversely we need a curve with prescribed velocity; patches produce curves: t ↦ φ(a + tw) has velocity Dφ(a)w. So Dφ(a)(ℝᵏ) ⊆ T_pM ⊆ ker DF(p). The two ends are k-dimensional (Dφ(a) injective; DF(p) surjective onto ℝ^{n−k} and rank–nullity). Squeeze (move 4).
  - **Proof.** The chain Dφ(a)ℝᵏ ⊆ T_pM ⊆ ker DF(p) and dimension count; item 3 is item 1 with φ(x) = (x, g(x)). One subtlety to state: item 1 needs some patch at p and item 2 some level function at p; both exist by `thm-submanifold-local-descriptions`, so the squeeze applies to *any* patch and *any* level function (for the squeeze use a fixed F with any φ, then a fixed φ with any F).
- **`def-normal-space`.** N_pM = (T_pM)^⊥; for a level set it is spanned by ∇F₁(p), …, ∇F_{n−k}(p) (row space of DF(p) = (ker DF(p))^⊥).
- **`def-differential-submanifold` and `prp-chain-rule-submanifolds`.** For smooth f : M → N, df_p(γ′(0)) = (f ∘ γ)′(0). Well defined and linear: equals DF(p)|_{T_pM} for any local extension F (`prp-smooth-iff-local-extension`), and lands in T_{f(p)}N because f ∘ γ is a curve in N. Chain rule d(g ∘ f)_p = dg_{f(p)} ∘ df_p, from the ambient chain rule (`thm-chain-rule`) applied to extensions.
- **`thm-constrained-critical-point`.** If f is smooth on an open set containing M and f|_M has a local extremum at p, then Df(p) vanishes on T_pM, that is, ∇f(p) ∈ N_pM. With item 2 this is the Lagrange condition ∇f(p) = Σ λᵢ∇Fᵢ(p) of `thm-lagrange-multipliers` (AN3.2), re-derived in three lines: say so, and say that the tangent-space form is the one that survives to abstract manifolds (AM.3).
- **Worked example.** M = S² ∩ {z = x} (§2 B3), p = (0, 1, 0). DF(p) has rows (0, 2, 0) and (−1, 0, 1); ker: v₂ = 0, v₃ = v₁, so T_pM = span (1, 0, 1). Check against the patch θ ↦ (cos θ/√2, sin θ, cos θ/√2) at θ = π/2: velocity (−1/√2, 0, −1/√2), parallel. ✓
- **Worked example (affine tangent plane).** {x² + y² − z² = 1} at p = (1, 1, 1): ∇F = (2, 2, −2), T_pM = {v₁ + v₂ − v₃ = 0}, affine plane x + y − z = 1.
- **Warning.** T_pM is a subspace through 0, not the plane touching M at p; the touching plane is p + T_pM. Readers who conflate them get "T_pS² = {x · p = 1}", which is not a subspace.
- **Quick check.** What is T_pM for an open subset M of ℝᵏ × {0} ⊆ ℝⁿ? Answer: ℝᵏ × {0}, at every p.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Show T_pS^{n−1} = p^⊥. *Answer:* F(x) = |x|², DF(p) = 2pᵀ.
  - **A2.** For the graph z = x² − y², find T_{(1,1,0)}M. *Answer:* {(v₁, v₂, 2v₁ − 2v₂)}.
  - **B1.** Find the maximum of x + 2y + 2z on S² using `thm-constrained-critical-point`. *Answer:* ∇f = (1, 2, 2) must be normal, i.e. parallel to p; p = ±(1, 2, 2)/3; maximum 3 at (1, 2, 2)/3 (S² compact, so the maximum exists).
  - **B2.** For the squaring map f of §3 B1, compute df_{(1,0)}. *Answer:* T_{(1,0)}S¹ = span (0, 1); the extension (x, y) ↦ (x² − y², 2xy) has derivative 2I at (1, 0); df(0, 1) = (0, 2) ∈ T_{(1,0)}S¹. So df_{(1,0)} is multiplication by 2.
  - **C1.** Prove: v ∈ T_pM iff dist(p + tv, M) = o(t) as t → 0. *Answer:* ⇒: take γ with γ′(0) = v; |p + tv − γ(t)| = o(t). ⇐: let Φ be a straightening at p; Φ is bi-Lipschitz on a small ball (C¹ with invertible derivative, `thm-inverse-function`), so dist(Φ(p + tv), ℝᵏ × {0}) ≤ L·dist(p + tv, M ∩ U) for small t (drafter: check that the nearest points stay in U for small t, which holds since dist(p + tv, M) ≤ |tv|). Since Φ(p + tv) = Φ(p) + tDΦ(p)v + o(t) and Φ(p) ∈ ℝᵏ × {0}, the last n − k components of DΦ(p)v are 0. Hence DΦ(p)v ∈ ℝᵏ × {0} = DΦ(p)(T_pM), and v ∈ T_pM.
  - **C2 (find the error).** A "proof" that T_pM ⊆ ker DF(p) implies equality "because T_pM and ker DF(p) are both subspaces". *Answer:* inclusion of subspaces does not give equality; the missing step is the dimension count, which needs DF(p) surjective (move 4). Give F(x, y) = y² on the x-axis: T_p = x-axis, ker DF(p) = ℝ² (DF = 0 on the axis).

## 05-matrix-groups-and-examples.md — Matrix groups, products and tori

Budget: ≈ 4,000 main-line words.

- **`prp-open-subset-submanifold`.** An open subset of a k-submanifold is a k-submanifold; GL(n, ℝ) is an n²-submanifold of ℝ^{n²} (open by `thm-gl-open`) with T_A GL(n) = ℝ^{n×n}.
- **`prp-product-submanifold`.** M × N is a (k + l)-submanifold of ℝ^{n+m}, with T_{(p,q)} = T_pM × T_qN (exercise B4). Route: products of local graphs are local graphs (after interleaving coordinates).
- **`thm-orthogonal-group`.** O(n) = {A ∈ ℝ^{n×n} : AᵀA = I} is a compact submanifold of dimension n(n − 1)/2, with T_I O(n) = Skew(n) and T_A O(n) = A·Skew(n). SO(n) = O(n) ∩ {det > 0} is open in O(n) (det = ±1 on O(n), continuous), hence a submanifold of the same dimension, with the same tangent space at I.
  - The target of F(A) = AᵀA is Sym(n) ≅ ℝ^{n(n+1)/2} (F takes symmetric values; using ℝ^{n×n} as target would make I a critical value; say this as the reason for the choice of target, it is a common trap).
  - DF(A)H = AᵀH + HᵀA.
  - **Scratch Work (move 2, first appearance).** Surjectivity is needed only at A ∈ O(n). Given S ∈ Sym(n), look for H with AᵀH + HᵀA = S. The two terms are transposes of each other, so it is enough to make AᵀH = S/2; with AᵀA = I, H = AS/2 works.
  - **Proof.** I is a regular value of F : ℝ^{n×n} → Sym(n); dimension n² − n(n + 1)/2 = n(n − 1)/2. Tangent space: ker DF(I) = {H : H + Hᵀ = 0}; ker DF(A) = {H : AᵀH skew} = A·Skew(n). Compactness: closed (preimage of a point) and bounded (each column a unit vector, so the Frobenius norm is √n).
- **`exm-rotation-velocity` (hook paid).** n = 3: dim SO(3) = 3. A skew Ω = [[0, −ω₃, ω₂], [ω₃, 0, −ω₁], [−ω₂, ω₁, 0]] satisfies Ωv = ω × v. For R(t) in SO(3) with R(0) = I, R′(0) = Ω ∈ T_I SO(3), and the velocity of a body point v is R′(0)v = ω × v. At time t₀ the same holds with R′(t₀)R(t₀)ᵀ ∈ Skew(3) (from T_A O(n) = A·Skew(n) written as Skew(n)·A, A ∈ O(n): Skew(n)·A = A·(AᵀSkew(n)A) = A·Skew(n)). Close the hook in two sentences; no physics beyond this. Prose pointer: the curve t ↦ e^{tΩ} realizes each Ω (AN3.3, seg B; not used).
- **`thm-special-linear-group`.** SL(n, ℝ) = det⁻¹(1) is a submanifold of dimension n² − 1, with T_I SL(n) = {H : tr H = 0}. Route: D det(I)H = tr H (from det(I + tH) = 1 + t tr H + O(t²), Leibniz formula); D det(A)H = det A · tr(A⁻¹H) (from det(A + tH) = det A det(I + tA⁻¹H)); at A ∈ SL(n), D det(A)(A) = n ≠ 0, so the derivative onto ℝ is surjective (move 2, second appearance: guessed preimage A/n · s for target s, no Scratch Work).
- **`exm-torus-surface`.** T = {(√(x² + y²) − 2)² + z² = 1} on U = ℝ³ minus the z-axis. ∇F = 2(r − 2)(x/r, y/r, 0) + (0, 0, 2z), r = √(x² + y²); it vanishes only where z = 0 and r = 2, where F = 0 ≠ 1, so 1 is a regular value. T does not meet the z-axis (F = 1 needs 1 ≤ r ≤ 3). Patch (s, t) ↦ ((2 + cos t) cos s, (2 + cos t) sin s, sin t). T is also diffeomorphic to S¹ × S¹ ⊆ ℝ⁴ (prose; exercise C2).
- **Warning.** dim = (number of variables) − (number of equations) only when the equations are independent at every point of the set. AᵀA = I is n² equations, of which only n(n + 1)/2 are independent; counting n² gives a negative dimension for n ≥ 2.
- **Quick check.** dim SO(4)? dim SL(3, ℝ)? Answer: 6; 8.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Show T_I SL(n) has dimension n² − 1 directly. *Answer:* the trace is a nonzero linear functional on ℝ^{n×n}; its kernel has dimension n² − 1.
  - **A2.** Show det = ±1 on O(n). *Answer:* det(AᵀA) = (det A)² = 1.
  - **B1.** Show that the set of 2 × 2 matrices of rank exactly 1 is a 3-submanifold of ℝ⁴, and that {det = 0} is not a submanifold at 0. *Answer:* det(a, b; c, d) = ad − bc, ∇det = (d, −c, −b, a), nonzero unless A = 0; the rank-1 matrices are det⁻¹(0) ∖ {0}, an open subset of ℝ⁴ minus {0} on which 0 is a regular value. At 0, {det = 0} is a cone: with a = u₁ + w₁, d = u₁ − w₁, b = w₂ + u₂, c = w₂ − u₂ one gets ad − bc = |u|² − |w|², so {det = 0} = {|u| = |w|}. Removing 0, the cone in ℝ⁴ is connected (it is ≅ (0, ∞) × S¹ × S¹), so the component count of §1 does not work; use instead: if it were a 3-submanifold at 0, T₀ would be a 3-dimensional subspace containing every line through 0 in the cone (each such line is a curve in the set), but those lines span ℝ⁴. Contradiction.
  - **B2.** Show T_A O(n) = A·Skew(n). *Answer:* in the proof; write it.
  - **B3.** Is O(n) connected? Show it is not. *Answer:* det : O(n) → {±1} is continuous and onto.
  - **B4.** Let M ⊆ ℝⁿ be a k-submanifold and N ⊆ ℝᵐ an l-submanifold. Show T_{(p,q)}(M × N) = T_pM × T_qN. *Answer:* with patches φ, ψ, the product patch (u, w) ↦ (φ(u), ψ(w)) has derivative Dφ(a) ⊕ Dψ(b), whose image is T_pM × T_qN (`thm-tangent-space-descriptions` item 1).
  - **C1.** Let J = diag(1, −1) and O(1, 1) = {A ∈ ℝ^{2×2} : AᵀJA = J}. Show it is a 1-submanifold, and find T_I. *Answer:* F(A) = AᵀJA into Sym(2) ≅ ℝ³; DF(A)H = AᵀJH + HᵀJA; given S, H = ½ J A^{−T} S gives AᵀJH = ½ S, so DF(A)H = S. Dimension 4 − 3 = 1. T_I = {X : XᵀJ + JX = 0} = span [[0, 1], [1, 0]]. (Its points include the hyperbolic rotations [[cosh u, sinh u], [sinh u, cosh u]].)
  - **C2.** Show that the torus surface T of `exm-torus-surface` is diffeomorphic to S¹ × S¹ ⊆ ℝ⁴. *Answer:* f((cos s, sin s), (cos t, sin t)) = ((2 + cos t) cos s, (2 + cos t) sin s, sin t) is the restriction of a polynomial map of ℝ⁴, a bijection onto T; its inverse (x, y, z) ↦ ((x, y)/r, (r − 2, z)) is the restriction of a smooth map on U. Both smooth by `prp-smooth-iff-local-extension`.

## 06-manifolds-with-boundary.md — Manifolds with boundary in ℝⁿ

Budget: ≈ 4,000 main-line words. Last section: carries the R group and the List of Results.

- **Failure-motivated definition.** The closed unit disc is what Green's theorem integrates over, and it is not a 2-submanifold: at a point of the rim, no neighbourhood in ℝ² meets it in an open set of a plane. AN3.7 needs objects with an edge.
- **`def-half-space`.** ℍᵏ = {x ∈ ℝᵏ : x_k ≥ 0}, ∂ℍᵏ = {x_k = 0}. A map on an open subset of ℍᵏ is smooth if it extends to a smooth map on an open subset of ℝᵏ (Munkres' convention; its derivative at boundary points is then determined, since an open subset of ℍᵏ has nonempty interior near each point: one sentence of proof).
- **`def-manifold-with-boundary`.** M ⊆ ℝⁿ is a k-manifold with boundary if each p ∈ M lies in the image of a patch φ : W → ℝⁿ, W open in ℝᵏ or in ℍᵏ, smooth, Dφ of rank k, homeomorphism onto an open subset of M.
- **`def-boundary-interior-points`.** p ∈ ∂M if some patch sends a point of ∂ℍᵏ to p; otherwise p ∈ int M.
- **`thm-boundary-well-defined`.** If φ(a) = p with a ∈ ∂ℍᵏ for one patch, then ψ⁻¹(p) ∈ ∂ℍᵏ for every patch ψ at p.
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance of the invariance argument).** Suppose ψ⁻¹(p) = b lies in the interior of ℍᵏ. The transition φ⁻¹ ∘ ψ (smooth, as in `thm-transition-smooth`, with extensions) has invertible derivative at b, so near b it is a local diffeomorphism of open subsets of ℝᵏ by `thm-inverse-function`; it maps a small open ball around b onto an *open* set containing a, and this open set lies in the domain of φ, a subset of ℍᵏ. But no open subset of ℝᵏ containing a point of ∂ℍᵏ lies inside ℍᵏ.
  - **Proof.** The clean version, citing `thm-transition-smooth` adapted to ℍᵏ (state in one sentence that its route works verbatim with extensions).
  - Remark: this is the smooth case of invariance of domain, whose topological version is much harder and not needed.
- **`thm-boundary-is-manifold`.** ∂M is a (k − 1)-submanifold without boundary (k ≥ 1), and int M is a k-submanifold. Route: restrict boundary patches to ∂ℍᵏ ≅ ℝ^{k−1}; the restriction is an immersion and a homeomorphism onto an open subset of ∂M by `thm-boundary-well-defined`.
- **`prp-regular-sublevel-set`.** (a) If U ⊆ ℝⁿ is open, F : U → ℝ smooth and c a regular value, then {F ≤ c} is an n-manifold with boundary F⁻¹(c). (b) More generally, if M is a k-submanifold and h : M → ℝ is smooth with dh_p ≠ 0 whenever h(p) = 0, then {h ≥ 0} is a k-manifold with boundary h⁻¹(0). Route for (b): at p with h(p) = 0, use a patch φ of M at p and pad h ∘ φ to a local diffeomorphism of ℝᵏ, (u₁, …, u_{k−1}, h ∘ φ) after reordering (move 1, third appearance in the chapter; this is the setting of C2's faded item).
- **`prp-tangent-space-at-boundary` and `def-outward-normal`.** At p ∈ ∂M, T_pM := Dφ(a)(ℝᵏ) is k-dimensional and independent of the patch (as in §4); T_p∂M is a hyperplane in it; the image of {v_k < 0} is the set of *outward* vectors, independent of the patch because the transition maps ∂ℍᵏ to itself and so its derivative maps ∂ℍᵏ to ∂ℍᵏ and preserves the sign of the last coordinate (the last component of D(φ⁻¹∘ψ)(b)e_k is ≥ 0 since the map sends ℍᵏ to ℍᵏ, and ≠ 0 since the derivative is invertible and preserves ∂ℍᵏ). **`def-outward-normal`:** the outward unit vector n(p) is the unique unit vector in T_pM that is orthogonal to T_p∂M and outward (exists and is unique: T_p∂M is a hyperplane of T_pM, and of its two unit normals inside T_pM exactly one is outward). For k = n it is the outward unit normal of M; for {F ≤ c} it is ∇F(p)/|∇F(p)|; for k = 1 it is the outward unit tangent at an endpoint of a curve. AN3.7's divergence theorem uses exactly this.
- **Examples.** `exm-closed-ball`: B̄ⁿ = {|x|² ≤ 1}, boundary S^{n−1}, n(p) = p. The closed upper hemisphere of S² ({z ≥ 0} on S², by (b)), boundary the equator. The solid torus {F ≤ 1} with F of `exm-torus-surface`. The annulus {1 ≤ |x| ≤ 2} as {(|x|² − 1)(|x|² − 4) ≤ 0}: check regularity (F = (s − 1)(s − 4), s = |x|²; DF = (2s − 5)·2xᵀ, zero only at x = 0 (F = 4) or s = 5/2 (F = −9/4), neither on the level set F = 0).
