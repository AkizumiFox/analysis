# Blueprint: AM.1 — Partitions of Unity, Improper Integrals, and Change of Variables

**Directory:** `am/src/ch01-partitions-of-unity-change-of-variables/`. **AM, chapter 1** (graduate, T2, main line; math-atlas spec/08 §D3). State: S1 blueprint, not frozen.

**Purpose.**
- AN3.4 proved the *linear* change of variables and stated the nonlinear formula without proof (math-atlas `chapter-constraints.yaml`, AN3.4 of 2026-10-25). This chapter proves the nonlinear formula for a C¹ diffeomorphism of open sets in ℝⁿ, with the integral taken in the extended sense over open sets.
- The proof has two halves, and each one needs a new tool. **Locally**, a diffeomorphism factors into maps that move one coordinate at a time, and each of those is one-variable substitution inside Fubini. **Globally**, the local pieces are glued with a partition of unity. The open sets on which diffeomorphisms naturally live (ℝⁿ, a punctured plane, the image of polar coordinates) are unbounded or have bad boundaries. So the integral has to be extended to open sets first.
- The same two tools (partitions of unity, and the extended integral computed through one) are what AN3.7 uses to define ∫_M over a manifold and to show it does not depend on the charts. AM.3 reuses smooth partitions of unity on abstract manifolds.

**House manner** (spec/08 §C0, §C5; graduate setting). The skeleton is the spec/08 §D3 order. Motivation uses Cummings' devices: the chapter hook is paid in §06; Scratch Work, kept apart from the proof, appears **only** at a technique's first appearance (three places, marked below); support fades after that (later proofs get an Idea line at most); definitions are introduced by a failure; the multivariable weird-examples table that AN3 starts is extended (§03, completed in §06); the chapter ends with a List of Results. No footnote jokes, no historical-anecdote footnotes, no first person.

**Named moves** (in `index.md`, "Named moves"):
1. **Patch with a partition of unity.** Prove a statement for functions supported in small open sets, write a general f as Σ φᵢ f, and add up. Local finiteness makes every sum finite on a compact set.
2. **Exhaust and take the monotone limit.** Compute an integral over an open set as the limit over an increasing sequence of compact Jordan sets whose interiors cover it. Do it with |f| first; only then is the limit independent of the exhaustion.
3. **One coordinate at a time.** Factor a diffeomorphism locally into primitive maps (each changes one coordinate), so that Fubini reduces the claim to one variable.
4. **Discard a null set.** Closed sets of measure zero can be removed from the domain of an extended integral; this is how polar and spherical coordinates, which fail to be diffeomorphisms on a null set, become usable.

The main new named move is move 1. It gets a faded triple (STYLE §11.4): worked in full in `thm-change-of-variables-open` step 2 (§05); last step blank in `exr-pou-and-null-sets-b2` (§03); last half blank in `exr-proof-of-change-of-variables-c1` (§05). Each blank carries the prompt "*Name the move, then complete it.*"

## 01-bump-functions-and-partitions-of-unity.md — Bump functions and partitions of unity

Budget: ≤ 3,500 main-line words.

- **Failure that motivates the section.** Suppose a statement about ∫ f is known whenever f vanishes outside a small cube, and we want it for every f. Cutting f into pieces f·1_{Qₖ} along a grid fails twice: the pieces are discontinuous (so the C¹ tools of §05 do not apply to them), and when the domain is an open set U, cubes inside U never cover U up to its boundary with finitely many pieces. Weights φₖ ≥ 0 with Σ φₖ = 1, each smooth and vanishing outside a small set, fix both. First we need one smooth function that vanishes on a half-line and nowhere else.
- **A second failure (one paragraph).** Polynomials, exp, sin, and everything given by a convergent power series cannot do this: a power series that vanishes on an interval vanishes on its whole interval of convergence (AN1.10, cite the identity statement). So the function must be smooth without being analytic.
- **`lem-flat-function`.** h(t) = e^{−1/t} for t > 0, h(t) = 0 for t ≤ 0, is C^∞ on ℝ, h^{(k)}(0) = 0 for all k, and h > 0 exactly on (0, ∞).
  - *Proof sketch.* Induction: for t > 0, h^{(k)}(t) = pₖ(1/t) e^{−1/t} with pₖ a polynomial (p₀ = 1, p_{k+1}(s) = s²(pₖ(s) − pₖ′(s))). For each m, s^m e^{−s} → 0 as s → ∞ (from e^s ≥ s^{m+1}/(m+1)!), so t^{−m}e^{−1/t} → 0 as t → 0⁺. Then h^{(k)}(0) = 0 by the difference quotient (h^{(k)}(t)/t → 0 from the right, 0 from the left).
  - Into the weird-examples table: a C^∞ function whose Taylor series at 0 is identically 0 but which is not 0 near 0. If AN1/AN2 already has this example, recall it with `@ref` and give the proof only as "recall"; the drafter checks.
- **`cor-smooth-step`.** s(t) = h(t)/(h(t) + h(1 − t)) is C^∞, 0 for t ≤ 0, 1 for t ≥ 1, strictly between on (0, 1). (The denominator is never 0.)
- **`cor-bump-on-cube`.** For an open cube (or open rectangle) Q = Π(aᵢ, bᵢ), ψ(x) = Π h(xᵢ − aᵢ) h(bᵢ − xᵢ) is C^∞ on ℝⁿ, positive on Q, zero off Q; its support is the closed cube Q̄.
- **`def-support`, `def-compact-support`.** For f: U → ℝ with U ⊆ ℝⁿ open, supp f is the closure *in U* of {f ≠ 0}; f has compact support in U if supp f is compact. C_c(U) is the space of continuous functions with compact support in U.
  - **Warning.** "Closure in U" matters: on U = (0, 1), the function x ↦ x(1 − x) has supp = (0, 1), which is closed in U but not compact. A function in C_c(U) vanishes near ∂U.
- **`lem-compact-exhaustion`.** Every open U ⊆ ℝⁿ is a union of compact sets D₁ ⊆ D₂ ⊆ ⋯ with Dⱼ ⊆ int D_{j+1}. Take Dⱼ = {x : |x| ≤ j, d(x, ℝⁿ ∖ U) ≥ 1/j} (drop the second condition if U = ℝⁿ).
  - *Proof sketch.* Closed and bounded; {x : |x| < j + 1, d(x, ℝⁿ∖U) > 1/(j+1)} is open (d is continuous), contains Dⱼ, and lies in D_{j+1}; every x ∈ U has d(x, ℝⁿ∖U) > 0 because ℝⁿ∖U is closed.
  - The Dⱼ need not be Jordan. §02 needs Jordan exhaustions; the remark there replaces each Dⱼ by a finite union of closed cubes (`thm-extended-exhaustion`, remark).
- **`def-partition-of-unity`.** Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be open and 𝒰 = {U_α} an open cover of U. A (smooth) partition of unity on U subordinate to 𝒰 is a sequence (φᵢ) of C^∞ functions U → [0, 1] such that
  (P1) each supp φᵢ is compact and lies in some U_α;
  (P2) the family is locally finite: each point of U has a neighbourhood meeting supp φᵢ for only finitely many i;
  (P3) Σᵢ φᵢ(x) = 1 for every x ∈ U.
  **Continuous variant** (`def-continuous-partition-of-unity`, a clause of the same block): the same with "continuous" for "C^∞". §03 and §05 need only the continuous variant, because composing a smooth partition with a C¹ map gives a continuous one; say so here so the reader sees why the variant is stated.
  - Give the definition in symbols for (P2) (STYLE §10: ∀x ∈ U ∃W ∋ x open, {i : W ∩ supp φᵢ ≠ ∅} finite).
- **`exm-shells-accumulate`** (non-example by minimal change and the reason for (P2) being relative to U). On U = (0, 1), bumps supported in (1/(k+2), 1/k) and normalized form a partition of unity on U subordinate to {(0, 1)}: locally finite in U, not near 0, which is not in U. No *finite* partition of unity with compact supports exists on (0, 1), since a finite sum of functions in C_c((0,1)) vanishes near 0 (this is the Quick check).
- **Scratch Work (first appearance of move 1: building a locally finite family).** Before the proof of `thm-partition-of-unity`, a boxed Scratch Work: draw the shells Bⱼ = Dⱼ ∖ int D_{j−1}; explain that covering each compact shell by finitely many small cubes, each kept inside the "thick shell" int D_{j+1} ∖ D_{j−2}, makes the whole family locally finite automatically, because a point of int Dⱼ meets only cubes coming from shells j − 2, …, j + 2. Then normalizing (divide by the sum) is harmless because the sum is positive and locally a finite sum of smooth functions. The proof that follows is written without the picture.
- **`thm-partition-of-unity`.** For every open cover 𝒰 of an open set U ⊆ ℝⁿ there is a smooth partition of unity on U subordinate to 𝒰.
  - *Proof sketch.* Exhaustion Dⱼ from `lem-compact-exhaustion`, D₀ = D₋₁ = ∅. Bⱼ = Dⱼ ∖ int D_{j−1} is compact and lies in the open set Wⱼ = int D_{j+1} ∖ D_{j−2}. For each x ∈ Bⱼ choose an open cube Q_x ∋ x with Q̄_x ⊆ Wⱼ ∩ U_α for some α; finitely many cover Bⱼ (compactness). List all chosen cubes over all j as Q₁, Q₂, …, with bumps ψₖ from `cor-bump-on-cube`. Local finiteness: a point x ∈ Bⱼ has the neighbourhood int D_{j+1}, which is disjoint from every cube chosen for a shell Bₖ with k ≥ j + 3 (those cubes lie outside D_{k−2} ⊇ D_{j+1}); so it meets only cubes from shells 1, …, j + 2, a finite set. ψ = Σψₖ is C^∞ (locally a finite sum) and > 0 on U (each x lies in some Bⱼ, which the Q's cover). φₖ = ψₖ/ψ.
- **`cor-smooth-urysohn`.** If K ⊆ U is compact and U ⊆ ℝⁿ open, there is a C^∞ function φ: ℝⁿ → [0, 1] with φ = 1 on a neighbourhood of K and supp φ compact in U.
  - *Proof sketch.* Choose a compact K′ ⊂ U with K ⊆ int K′ (a finite union of closed cubes). Take a partition of unity (φᵢ) on ℝⁿ subordinate to {U, ℝⁿ ∖ K′}. Let φ be the sum of the φᵢ whose support meets K′: finitely many, by (P2) and compactness; each has support in U (a support inside ℝⁿ ∖ K′ cannot meet K′). Every other φᵢ vanishes on K′, so φ = 1 on K′ ⊇ int K′ ⊇ K, and supp φ is a finite union of compact subsets of U. Used by AN3.5, AN3.7 and AM.2.
- **Quick check.** Why is there no finite partition of unity on (0, 1) with compact supports? *Answer:* each of finitely many compact supports in (0, 1) lies in [δ, 1 − δ] for some δ > 0, so the sum is 0 on (0, δ).
- **Warning.** A partition of unity is not a partition of U into pieces: the supports overlap, and at a point several φᵢ are positive. Integrals add because Σφᵢ = 1, not because the supports are disjoint.
- **Hook (forward, prose only).** "Nothing in the proof used more about U than that it is a union of open sets on which we can build bumps. In Chapter 4 this is what lets an integral be defined on a set that is only locally like ℝⁿ."
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Show that ψ(x, y) = h(x)h(1 − x)h(y)h(1 − y) vanishes outside (0, 1)² and is positive inside. *Answer:* each factor is positive exactly on the stated open interval, by `lem-flat-function`.
  - **A2.** Find supp f on U = ℝ ∖ {0} for f(x) = max(0, 1 − |x|). Is it compact in U? *Answer:* [−1, 0) ∪ (0, 1]; not compact (not closed in ℝ), so f ∉ C_c(U).
  - **B1.** Prove that for a < b there is a C^∞ function on ℝ equal to 1 on [a, b] and 0 outside (a − 1, b + 1), using only `cor-smooth-step`. *Answer:* s(x − a + 1)·s(b + 1 − x).
  - **B2.** Prove that a locally finite sum of C^∞ functions on U is C^∞, and that a locally finite family of compact sets has only finitely many members meeting a given compact set. *Answer:* each point has a neighbourhood on which the sum is a finite sum; cover the compact set by finitely many such neighbourhoods.
  - **C1.** Let F ⊆ ℝⁿ be closed. Construct a C^∞ function f ≥ 0 on ℝⁿ with F = {f = 0}. *Answer:* write ℝⁿ∖F as a countable union of open balls Bₖ; take bumps ψₖ ≥ 0 positive exactly on Bₖ; let Mₖ = max of all partial derivatives of ψₖ of order ≤ k (finite, compact support); f = Σ 2^{−k} ψₖ/(1 + Mₖ). Every derivative series converges uniformly by the M-test, so f is C^∞ by term-by-term differentiation (AN1.9); f > 0 exactly off F.

## 02-the-extended-integral.md — The extended integral over an open set

Budget: ≤ 3,500 main-line words.

- **Opening frame (spiral).** Recall from AN3.4 the integral of a bounded function over a bounded Jordan set, and its definition through a rectangle and extension by zero. One sentence on what changes: the domain becomes an arbitrary open set and the function may be unbounded; what survives: the value on compact Jordan sets.
- **`exm-three-failures`** (failure-motivated definition; all three appear before the definition):
  1. f(x, y) = (xy)^{−1/2} on (0, 1)²: unbounded, so no Riemann integral exists, yet every square [δ, 1 − δ]² gives (2√(1 − δ) − 2√δ)² → 4.
  2. `exm-fat-cantor-open-set` (its own block): let C ⊆ [0, 1] be a closed nowhere dense set of positive measure (built as in AN2/AN3.4's fat Cantor set, removing middle intervals of lengths 4^{−k} at stage k, so the removed length is 1/2), and U = (0, 1) ∖ C. U is a disjoint union of countably many open intervals of total length 1/2, so its "volume" is plainly 1/2. But ∂U ⊇ C has positive measure, so U is not Jordan and ∫_U 1 does not exist in AN3.4's sense. (In ℝⁿ use U × (0, 1)ⁿ⁻¹.)
  3. The Gaussian on ℝ² from the hook.
  - **The common fix:** never integrate over the whole set at once; integrate over compact Jordan subsets, where AN3.4 works, and take a supremum.
- **`def-extended-integral`.** Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be open and f: U → ℝ continuous.
  - If f ≥ 0: f is *integrable over U* if the set {∫_D f : D ⊆ U compact and Jordan} is bounded above, and then ∫_U f is its supremum.
  - In general: f is integrable over U if f₊ = max(f, 0) and f₋ = max(−f, 0) are, and ∫_U f = ∫_U f₊ − ∫_U f₋. Equivalently, |f| is integrable (one line: f± ≤ |f| = f₊ + f₋).
  - State in words what is being defined: an *absolute* integral. A function with ∫|f| = ∞ is not integrable over U, however its integrals over particular sets behave.
  - Remark (one sentence): the definition extends verbatim to f locally bounded and continuous off a closed set of measure zero; the chapter does not need it.
- **`thm-extended-bounded`** (agreement with AN3.4). Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be a bounded open set and f: U → ℝ bounded and continuous. Then f is integrable over U. If moreover ∂U has measure zero (so U is Jordan), the AN3.4 integral of f over U exists and equals the extended one.
  - *Proof sketch.* Existence: with |f| ≤ M and U inside a box R, ∫_D f₊ ≤ M vol(R). Agreement: given ε > 0, a partition of R whose subrectangles meeting ∂U have total volume < ε (U Jordan, AN3.4) yields a compact Jordan D_ε ⊆ U (the union of closed subrectangles inside U) with vol(U ∖ D_ε) < ε; for every compact Jordan D with D_ε ⊆ D ⊆ U, the AN3.4 integrals satisfy |∫_U f − ∫_D f| ≤ Mε. Apply to f₊ and f₋ separately and take suprema. Uses: the AN3.4 integral is additive over Jordan sets and monotone for non-negative integrands.
  - Consequence for notation: one symbol ∫_U f is safe.
- **`exm-fat-cantor-open-set`, finished.** The extended integral of 1 over U exists and equals 1/2: every compact D ⊆ U is covered by finitely many of the component intervals (an open cover by disjoint sets), so ∫_D 1 ≤ their total length ≤ 1/2; finite unions of closed subintervals of the components are compact Jordan subsets of U with lengths approaching 1/2. So the extension is strict: it integrates over sets AN3.4 could not.

- **`thm-extended-exhaustion`.** Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be open, f: U → ℝ continuous, and D₁ ⊆ D₂ ⊆ ⋯ compact Jordan sets with U = ⋃ int Dⱼ. Then f is integrable over U iff sup_j ∫_{Dⱼ} |f| < ∞, and in that case ∫_U f = lim_j ∫_{Dⱼ} f.
  - *Proof sketch.* For g ≥ 0 continuous: a compact Jordan D ⊆ U is covered by the increasing open sets int Dⱼ, so D ⊆ Dⱼ for some j (AN2.3), and ∫_D g ≤ ∫_{Dⱼ} g; hence the supremum over all D equals sup_j ∫_{Dⱼ} g = lim_j (monotone). Apply to |f|, f₊, f₋.
  - **Remark: Jordan exhaustions exist.** For every open U there are such Dⱼ: in `lem-compact-exhaustion`, replace Dⱼ by the union of the finitely many closed dyadic cubes of side 2^{−j−m} (m chosen large) that meet Dⱼ and lie in int D_{j+1}. (Drafter: a finite union of closed cubes is Jordan; check the containments; S2a item.)
- **`prp-extended-linear`.** For f, g integrable over U and c ∈ ℝ: f + g and cf are integrable with the expected integrals; f ≤ g implies ∫f ≤ ∫g; if |f| ≤ g with g integrable and f continuous, f is integrable (comparison test); if U = U₁ ⊔ U₂ (disjoint open), ∫_U f = ∫_{U₁} f + ∫_{U₂} f.
  - *Proof sketch.* All from `thm-extended-exhaustion` with one Jordan exhaustion and the AN3.4 properties on each Dⱼ; for linearity use the limit formula, which is legitimate once |f| and |g| are integrable.

## 03-pou-and-null-sets.md — Computing with partitions of unity; discarding null sets

Budget: ≤ 3,000 main-line words.

- **Lead-in.** Exhaustions need nested compact Jordan sets, and change of variables does not map one exhaustion to another nicely (images of cubes are not cubes). A partition of unity is the replacement: it can be pulled back along a homeomorphism, and each piece is a compactly supported continuous function, which AN3.4 already integrates.
- **`thm-extended-via-pou`.** Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be open, f: U → ℝ continuous, and (φᵢ) a *continuous* partition of unity on U (no subordination needed beyond (P1)'s compact supports). Then f is integrable over U iff Σᵢ ∫_U φᵢ|f| < ∞; in that case Σᵢ ∫_U φᵢ f converges absolutely and equals ∫_U f.
  - Each ∫_U φᵢ f is the AN3.4 integral of a continuous function with compact support (extend by zero to a box), equal to its extended integral by `thm-extended-bounded`.
  - *Proof sketch.* (⇒ and the inequality ≤) For N fixed, K = supp φ₁ ∪ ⋯ ∪ supp φ_N is compact in U, so K ⊆ Dⱼ for a Jordan exhaustion and large j; Σ_{i≤N} ∫ φᵢ|f| = ∫_{Dⱼ} (Σ_{i≤N} φᵢ)|f| ≤ ∫_{Dⱼ} |f| ≤ ∫_U |f|. (⇐ and ≥) For compact Jordan D ⊆ U only finitely many φᵢ are non-zero on D (P2 and compactness), so ∫_D |f| = Σᵢ ∫_D φᵢ|f| ≤ Σᵢ ∫_U φᵢ|f|. Equality of values: apply both inequalities to f₊ and f₋ (φᵢf₊ = (φᵢf)₊) and subtract; absolute convergence from |φᵢ f| = φᵢ|f|.
  - **Remark.** Spivak takes this formula as the definition of the integral over an open set (CoM 3-12). Here it is a theorem, and the remark says that the two definitions agree by this theorem (meta-claim: checkable against the statement). In Chapter 4 this formula, with charts, is the definition of ∫_M (prose, for the promise table).
- **`thm-extended-delete-null`.** Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be open, E ⊆ U closed in U with measure zero, and f: U → ℝ continuous. Then f is integrable over U iff it is integrable over U ∖ E (an open set), and the integrals agree.
  - *Proof sketch.* Work with |f| and then f±. Compact Jordan subsets of U ∖ E are compact Jordan subsets of U, giving ∫_{U∖E} ≤ ∫_U. Conversely, for compact Jordan D ⊆ U, W = int D ∖ E is a bounded open subset of U ∖ E with ∂W ⊆ ∂D ∪ (E ∩ D), which has measure zero. By `thm-extended-bounded`, the AN3.4 integral of |f| over W equals the extended one, which is ≤ ∫_{U∖E} |f|; and it equals ∫_D |f| because the two integrands differ only on ∂D ∪ (E ∩ D) (AN3.4: integrable functions equal off a null set have equal integrals). Hence ∫_U |f| ≤ ∫_{U∖E} |f|. (S2a: check "E ∩ D has measure zero" and "∂W ⊆ ∂D ∪ (E ∩ D)".)
  - **Warning.** "Closed in U" cannot be dropped: E = ℚⁿ ∩ U has measure zero but U ∖ E is not open, so the right side is not even defined. And measure zero, not just "small": removing the fat Cantor set C from (0, 1) changes nothing about integrability of 1 but removes measure 1/2, so the integrals differ (1 versus 1/2).

## 04-diffeomorphisms-and-the-theorem.md — Diffeomorphisms and the statement of the theorem

Budget: ≤ 3,500 main-line words.

- **Opening frame (spiral).** Recall AN3.4's linear formula and its statement of the nonlinear one, with `@ref`. What survives: |det| is the local volume factor. What changes: the factor varies from point to point, so the proof must be local and then glued.
- **`def-diffeomorphism`.** U, V ⊆ ℝⁿ open; g: U → V is a C^r diffeomorphism (r ≥ 1) if it is a bijection and g, g⁻¹ are C^r.
- **`exm-cube-not-diffeo`** (failure; into the table). x ↦ x³ on ℝ is a C^∞ bijection with C⁰ inverse that is not differentiable at 0. The polar map P(r, θ) = (r cos θ, r sin θ) on (0, ∞) × ℝ has invertible derivative everywhere but is not injective. Two ways to fail, two hypotheses.
- **`thm-injective-local-diffeo`.** If g: U → ℝⁿ is C^r on an open U, injective, and det Dg(x) ≠ 0 for all x ∈ U, then g(U) is open and g: U → g(U) is a C^r diffeomorphism.
  - *Proof sketch.* The inverse function theorem (AN3.2) gives, around each point, an open neighbourhood mapped C^r-diffeomorphically onto an open set; so g(U) is open and the set inverse agrees near each point with a local C^r inverse.
  - Apply at once: P is a C^∞ diffeomorphism of (0, ∞) × (0, 2π) onto ℝ² minus the closed ray {(x, 0) : x ≥ 0} (det DP = r).
- **`lem-c1-preserves-null-sets`.** If U ⊆ ℝⁿ is open and g: U → ℝⁿ is C¹, then g maps sets of measure zero to sets of measure zero.
  - *Proof sketch.* U is a countable union of closed cubes Cₖ ⊆ U; on Cₖ, ‖Dg‖ ≤ Mₖ, so |g(x) − g(y)| ≤ Mₖ|x − y| (mean value inequality on the convex Cₖ, AN3.1). A cube of side s inside Cₖ has image of diameter ≤ Mₖ√n s, hence inside a cube of side 2Mₖ√n s, of volume (2Mₖ√n)ⁿ sⁿ. Cover E ∩ Cₖ by cubes of total volume < ε, intersect with Cₖ and subdivide the resulting rectangles into cubes, at most multiplying total volume by a fixed constant (S2a: drafter states the constant or uses "rectangles with side ratio ≤ 2"). A countable union of null sets is null.
  - Same dimension is essential: say so, and refer to the Cantor function (AN2.9) in the table: continuous, maps the null Cantor set onto [0, 1]. Continuity does not suffice; Lipschitz does.
- **`thm-diffeo-jordan`.** Let g: U → V be a C¹ diffeomorphism of open sets and D ⊆ U compact Jordan. Then g(D) is compact and Jordan, g(int D) = int g(D), and g(∂D) = ∂g(D).
  - *Proof sketch.* g is a homeomorphism of U onto V, so it carries interiors and boundaries relative to U to those relative to V; for a compact D ⊆ U, the boundary in ℝⁿ equals the boundary relative to U. ∂D has measure zero, so g(∂D) does (`lem-c1-preserves-null-sets`).
- **`thm-change-of-variables-open`** (the main theorem; stated here, proved in §05). Let g: U → V be a C¹ diffeomorphism of open subsets of ℝⁿ and f: V → ℝ continuous. Then f is integrable over V iff (f ∘ g)·|det Dg| is integrable over U, and in that case
  ∫_V f = ∫_U (f ∘ g)|det Dg|.
  - Say explicitly that the absolute value is not optional: with g(x) = −x on (0, 1) → (−1, 0) the signed version gives the wrong sign (Quick check below). The oriented version returns in Chapter 4 with forms; prose pointer only, promise listed.
- **Scratch Work (first appearance of move 3, and the plan of the proof).** Boxed, before `prp-cov-reductions`: work n = 2 and g(x, y) = (x, y + x²) (a shear along a parabola) by hand: on each vertical line, t ↦ t + x² is a translation, so Fubini plus one-variable substitution proves the formula for this g in two lines. A general g is not of this form, but near each point it is a composition of maps of this form, after renaming coordinates. That suggests: (a) the formula is stable under composition; (b) it is enough to prove it near each point; (c) it holds for maps that change one coordinate. (a) and (b) are proved now; (c) and the local factorization are §05.
- **`prp-cov-reductions`.** Write CV(g) for the statement "∫_{g(U)} F = ∫_U (F ∘ g)|det Dg| for every F ∈ C_c(g(U))" (g a C¹ diffeomorphism of U onto an open set).
  (R1) *Composition.* If CV(g) for g: U → V and CV(k) for k: V → W, then CV(k ∘ g). Chain rule and det(AB) = det A det B (`@la:`), applied with F′ = (F ∘ k)|det Dk| ∈ C_c(V).
  (R2) *Locality.* If every a ∈ U has an open neighbourhood Uₐ ⊆ U with CV(g|_{Uₐ}), then CV(g). Proof: given F ∈ C_c(V), take a smooth partition of unity on V subordinate to {g(Uₐ)}; only finitely many φᵢ are non-zero on supp F; F = Σ φᵢF (finite), each φᵢF ∈ C_c(g(U_{a(i)})); apply CV on each piece and add. (A second, finite instance of move 1; say so in one clause.)
  (R3) *Linear and affine maps.* CV(T) for invertible linear T and for translations: AN3.4's linear change of variables, recalled with `@ref`.
  - Note (one sentence): CV(g) is the compactly supported version; §05 proves it for all g and then upgrades it to `thm-change-of-variables-open` with `thm-extended-via-pou`.
- **Remark (alternative route).** Pugh (§5.7) proves the formula by comparing vol g(Q) with |det Dg(x)| vol Q on small cubes, uniformly; Spivak (3-13) inducts on n directly. Two sentences, no detail; coverage only.

## 05-proof-of-change-of-variables.md — Primitive diffeomorphisms and the proof

Budget: ≤ 4,000 main-line words.

- **Lead-in.** By `prp-cov-reductions`, CV(g) follows once every diffeomorphism is, near each point, a composition of linear maps, translations and maps for which CV is known. This section supplies the last kind and the factorization. Support is reduced: Idea lines only, no further Scratch Work.
- **`def-primitive-diffeomorphism`.** A C¹ diffeomorphism h: U → V of open sets in ℝⁿ is *primitive* if, for some i, it changes only the i-th coordinate: hⱼ(x) = xⱼ for j ≠ i. Then det Dh = ∂hᵢ/∂xᵢ.
- **`lem-cov-primitive`.** CV(h) for every primitive diffeomorphism h.
  - *Idea.* Fubini along the i-th coordinate turns it into one-variable substitution on each line.
  - *Proof sketch.* Conjugating with a permutation of coordinates (linear, |det| = 1, (R1) and (R3)), assume i = n. Write x = (x′, t). For F ∈ C_c(V), extend F and G = (F ∘ h)|∂ₙhₙ| by zero to continuous functions on ℝⁿ (G ∈ C_c(U), since supp G = h⁻¹(supp F) is compact). Choose a box R′ × [−L, L] containing both supports. Fubini (AN3.4, continuous integrands on a box): ∫ G = ∫_{R′} (∫ G(x′, t) dt) dx′. Fix x′. The slice U_{x′} = {t : (x′, t) ∈ U} is open; t ↦ hₙ(x′, t) is C¹ with non-zero derivative and injective on U_{x′} (h is injective and keeps x′), and it maps U_{x′} onto V_{x′}. supp G ∩ slice is compact, so it meets finitely many component intervals of U_{x′}; on each, t ↦ hₙ(x′, t) is a C¹ diffeomorphism onto a component interval of V_{x′}, and one-variable substitution (with |·| absorbing a decreasing map) gives ∫ G(x′, t) dt = ∫ F(x′, s) ds. Integrate in x′.
  - S2a: check that every component of V_{x′} meeting supp F is the image of a component of U_{x′} (yes: h restricted to the slice is a homeomorphism of U_{x′} onto V_{x′}).
- **`lem-local-primitive-factorization`.** Let g: U → ℝⁿ be C¹ with Dg(a) invertible. Then there is an open neighbourhood U₀ of a on which g = τ ∘ L ∘ h_n ∘ ⋯ ∘ h₁, where each h_k is a primitive diffeomorphism between open sets, L is a permutation of coordinates (a product of transpositions, possibly the identity), and τ is a translation. With translations at both ends allowed: g = τ ∘ L ∘ h_n ∘ ⋯ ∘ h₁ ∘ τ′ in general (state it so).
  - *Idea.* Straighten one coordinate at a time. If the first m − 1 coordinates of g are already xᵢ, invertibility of the derivative forces some later component to depend on x_m at a; swap it into slot m and use it as the new m-th coordinate. That change of coordinates is primitive and, by the inverse function theorem, a local diffeomorphism.
  - *Proof sketch (induction on m, Rudin 10.7 route, in our words).* Translations reduce to a = 0, g(0) = 0. Put F₁ = g. Suppose that, near 0, F_m is a C¹ map with F_m(0) = 0, invertible derivative at 0, and components x₁, …, x_{m−1}, α_m(x), …, α_n(x). Then ∂α_k/∂x_m(0) ≠ 0 for some k ≥ m (the first m − 1 rows of DF_m(0) are (I_{m−1} 0), so if every ∂α_k/∂x_m(0) with k ≥ m vanished, the m-th column would be zero). Swap coordinates m and k (a transposition B_m). Define p_m(x) = x + (α_k(x) − x_m)e_m, primitive, with ∂(p_m)_m/∂x_m(0) = ∂α_k/∂x_m(0) ≠ 0, so by the inverse function theorem and `thm-injective-local-diffeo` it is a diffeomorphism near 0. Then F_{m+1} = B_m ∘ F_m ∘ p_m⁻¹ fixes the first m coordinates near 0, and F_{n+1} is the identity near 0. Unwinding: g = F₁ = B₁ ∘ ⋯ ∘ B_n ∘ p_n ∘ ⋯ ∘ p₁ near 0 (each Bₖ is its own inverse), which is the stated form with L = B₁ ⋯ B_n. Domains shrink at each step: keep the neighbourhoods explicit.
- **`thm-cov-compact-support`.** CV(g) for every C¹ diffeomorphism g: U → V.
  - *Proof sketch.* By (R2) it suffices to prove CV near each a ∈ U; there `lem-local-primitive-factorization` writes g as a composition of maps for which CV holds (`lem-cov-primitive`, (R3)), each a diffeomorphism between open sets; (R1) finishes.
- **Proof of `thm-change-of-variables-open`** (the worked instance of move 1 in the faded triple; label the steps).
  - *Step 1.* `thm-cov-compact-support`.
  - *Step 2 (patch).* Take a smooth partition of unity (φᵢ) on V with compact supports (`thm-partition-of-unity` with the cover {V}). Then (φᵢ ∘ g) is a continuous partition of unity on U with compact supports (§03 Quick check). By Step 1 applied to φᵢ|f| ∈ C_c(V): ∫_V φᵢ|f| = ∫_U (φᵢ ∘ g)(|f| ∘ g)|det Dg|. So the two series of `thm-extended-via-pou` (for f on V, and for (f ∘ g)|det Dg| on U) have the same terms; one converges iff the other does. Repeat with φᵢ f (absolutely convergent) to get equal values.
  - Point at the line where continuity rather than smoothness of φᵢ ∘ g is all that is used (meta-claim checkable on the page).
- **Quick check.** Where in the proof of `lem-cov-primitive` is injectivity of h used? *Answer:* to know that t ↦ hₙ(x′, t) is injective on the slice, so that one-variable substitution applies on each component without double counting.

## 06-applications.md — Polar coordinates, the Gaussian integral, and volumes of balls

Budget: ≤ 4,000 main-line words.

- **Lead-in.** The hook is now one paragraph away. Polar and spherical coordinates fail to be diffeomorphisms only on null sets, and null sets can be discarded.
- **`cor-cov-null-exceptions`.** Let V ⊆ ℝⁿ be open, F ⊆ V closed in V with measure zero, U′ ⊆ ℝⁿ open, g: U′ → V ∖ F a C¹ diffeomorphism, and f: V → ℝ continuous. Then f is integrable over V iff (f ∘ g)|det Dg| is integrable over U′, and then ∫_V f = ∫_{U′} (f ∘ g)|det Dg|. (For polar coordinates: V = ℝ², F the closed ray {(x, 0) : x ≥ 0}, U′ = (0, ∞) × (0, 2π).)
  - *Proof.* `thm-extended-delete-null` on V, then `thm-change-of-variables-open`. Three lines.
- **`prp-polar-coordinates`.** Let 0 ≤ a < b ≤ ∞, V = {a < |x| < b} ⊆ ℝ² (V = ℝ² ∖ {0} for a = 0, b = ∞; add the origin back by `thm-extended-delete-null`), and f: V → ℝ continuous. Then f is integrable over V iff (r, θ) ↦ f(r cos θ, r sin θ) r is integrable over (a, b) × (0, 2π), and the integrals agree. Proof: `cor-cov-null-exceptions` with F = V ∩ the closed positive x-axis.
- **`exm-gaussian-integral`** (pays the hook; name the three obstacles and their removers, as listed under "Chapter hook").
  - (a) e^{−r²}r is integrable over (0, ∞) × (0, 2π) with integral π, by the exhaustion [1/j, j] × [1/j, 2π − 1/j]: (2π − 2/j)(e^{−1/j²} − e^{−j²})/2 → π.
  - (b) So e^{−x²−y²} is integrable over ℝ² with integral π (`prp-polar-coordinates`).
  - (c) By `thm-extended-exhaustion` with the squares [−j, j]² and Fubini on each: ∫_{ℝ²} e^{−x²−y²} = lim (∫_{−j}^{j} e^{−x²} dx)² = I². Hence I = √π.
  - Corollary in prose: Γ(1/2) = √π by the one-variable substitution t = x² in Γ(1/2) = ∫_0^∞ t^{−1/2}e^{−t} dt (AN2.11). If AN2.11 already proved Γ(1/2) = √π by another route, say "a second proof"; the drafter checks (S2a item).
- **`exm-sine-of-radius-squared`** (weird; into the table). On discs D_R, ∫_{D_R} sin(x² + y²) = π(1 − cos R²), which has no limit as R → ∞; so sin(x² + y²) is not integrable over ℝ² (consistent: |sin(x² + y²)| has ∫ over the annulus √(kπ) < r < √((k+1)π) equal to 2π, by polar coordinates, summing to ∞). Remark, plainly marked as not proved here and used nowhere: over the squares [−R, R]² the integrals tend to π (Fresnel integrals). Two exhaustions of ℝ², two behaviours, the 2D version of `exm-exhaustion-dependence`.
- **`prp-spherical-coordinates`.** In ℝ³, S(ρ, φ, θ) = (ρ sin φ cos θ, ρ sin φ sin θ, ρ cos φ) is a C^∞ diffeomorphism of (0, ∞) × (0, π) × (0, 2π) onto ℝ³ minus the closed half-plane {y = 0, x ≥ 0}, with |det DS| = ρ² sin φ. Statement with the injectivity check sketched; the formula by cofactor expansion.
- **`exm-ball-volume`** (worked example; ωₙ = vol of the unit ball Bⁿ).
  - ω₁ = 2, ω₂ = π (polar). Recursion: split ℝⁿ = ℝ² × ℝ^{n−2}; Fubini (AN3.4, region between graphs; drafter checks the version AN3.4 proves suffices, or uses `thm-extended-exhaustion` with the open ball and continuous integrands) and the linear scaling vol(ρBᵏ) = ρᵏωₖ (AN3.4) give ωₙ = ∫_{x²+y²<1} ω_{n−2}(1 − x² − y²)^{(n−2)/2} dx dy = 2π ω_{n−2} ∫_0^1 (1 − r²)^{(n−2)/2} r dr = (2π/n) ω_{n−2}.
  - Table: ω₃ = 4π/3, ω₄ = π²/2, ω₅ = 8π²/15; ω_{2k} = πᵏ/k!. Sidecar script checks the table and the recursion.
  - Remark: ωₙ = π^{n/2}/Γ(n/2 + 1) (by induction from the recursion and Γ(s + 1) = sΓ(s), with Γ(1/2) = √π from `exm-gaussian-integral`); and ωₙ → 0 as n → ∞, a short C-level surprise that goes into the table ("the unit ball has volume tending to 0").
- **`exm-power-singularity`** (worked; move: scaling, a fourth use of the linear formula). ∫_{0<|x|<1} |x|^{−p} dx < ∞ iff p < n, in every dimension, without spherical coordinates: on the shells S_k = {2^{−k−1} ≤ |x| < 2^{−k}} (use their interiors and `thm-extended-delete-null` for the spheres), the linear map x ↦ 2^{−k}x gives vol S_k = 2^{−kn} vol S₀ and 2^{kp} ≤ |x|^{−p} ≤ 2^{(k+1)p} on S_k, so the integral is comparable to Σₖ 2^{k(p−n)}. Similarly ∫_{|x|>1} |x|^{−p} < ∞ iff p > n. Closing sentence: an exact formula ∫ = nωₙ/(n − p) needs the area of the sphere, which comes in Chapter 4 (prose pointer, promise listed only if AN3.7's blueprint delivers it; see "Hooks this chapter opens").
