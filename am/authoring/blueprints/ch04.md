# Blueprint: AN3.7 — Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem

**Purpose.**
- Make one theorem, ∫_M dω = ∫_{∂M} ω, out of four separate theorems the reader already half-knows: the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green, Gauss and the classical Stokes theorem.
- Three things have to be built first. Integrals of forms over a single chart are already the change of variables formula from AM.1. A sign convention (orientation) makes them independent of the chart. A gluing device (partitions of unity, AM.1) lets them leave the chart.
- The proof itself is short: the fundamental theorem of calculus in one variable on a half-space, then a partition of unity. The chapter is built so that this shortness is visible.
- Everything lives in ℝⁿ: compact k-manifolds with boundary, as in AN3.5, and forms defined on an open set of ℝⁿ that contains M, as in AN3.6. Abstract manifolds wait for AM.3.

## index.md

- **Story (chapter hook).** F(x, y, z) = (−y, x, 0). Compute the flux of curl F = (0, 0, 2) upward through three surfaces with the same rim, the unit circle in the plane z = 0: the flat disk, the upper hemisphere and the paraboloid z = 1 − x² − y², z ≥ 0. All three give 2π, and so does the work of F around the circle. Why should the answer forget the surface? A second question: the Möbius band also has a single circle as its rim. Is there a fourth equal answer for it?
  - **Paid:** the Möbius question in §2 (`exm-mobius-nonorientable`: there is no "upward", so the flux is undefined); the surfaces in §5 (`exm-hook-hemisphere`: each flux equals the circle integral by `thm-stokes-surface`); and the closing remark of §6 (the closed surface hemisphere ∪ disk bounds a solid, and d(d(…)) = 0 makes the difference zero).
- **Hook planted for AM.2.** The form (x dy − y dx)/(x² + y²) has d = 0 on ℝ² ∖ {0}, but its integral over the unit circle is 2π. §4 and §6 show this does not contradict Stokes: the form is not defined on the disk. AM.2 turns this into the de Rham group of the punctured plane.
- **What you need:** Chapter 1 (partitions of unity, change of variables), Chapter 2 (manifolds with boundary in ℝⁿ), Chapter 3 (forms, d, pullback); AN3.4 (Fubini); the one-variable FTC.
- **Roadmap:** §1 integrates over one chart; §2 fixes the sign; §3 glues; §4 proves Stokes; §5 recovers Green, Gauss and Stokes; §6 gives first consequences, the weird-examples table and the List of Results.

## 01-forms-over-parametrized-manifolds.md — Integrating a form over a chart (budget ≤ 3,000 main-line words)

- **Opening frame (spiral):** a line integral ∫_C P dx + Q dy and a flux integral ∫∫ F·N dA from vector calculus are both "pull the integrand back to the parameter domain, then integrate". Chapter 3's pullback is exactly that operation.
- **`def-form-integral-parametrized`.** Let A ⊆ ℝᵏ be open, or open in Hᵏ, let α: A → ℝⁿ be C^∞ with rank k, and let ω be a k-form defined on an open set containing α(A). Write α*ω = h dx₁∧⋯∧dx_k. Define ∫_{Y_α} ω := ∫_A h whenever the integral exists (in this chapter: h is continuous and has compact support in A, or the extended integral of AM.1 exists).
- **Scratch Work (first appearance of "pull back and integrate"; kept apart from the examples).** For ω = x dy − y dx and α(t) = (cos t, sin t) on (0, 2π): compute α*x, α*dy = cos t dt, and so on, and collect α*ω = dt. Then state the move in one line.
- **Worked examples:**
  - `exm-circle-two-ways`: α(t) = (cos t, sin t) gives 2π. β(t) = (cos t, −sin t), the same set traversed the other way, gives −2π.
  - `exm-paraboloid-flux`: α(u, v) = (u, v, 1 − u² − v²) on the open unit disk and ω = z dx∧dy. Then α*ω = (1 − u² − v²) du∧dv, and the integral is π/2, the volume under the paraboloid.
  - `exm-circle-twice`: α(t) = (cos 2t, sin 2t) on (0, 2π) gives 4π. A map that is not injective counts the set twice. Patches are injective for this reason, and `def-form-integral-manifold` uses only patches.
- **`thm-reparametrization-sign`.** Let g: B → A be a C^∞ diffeomorphism of open sets in ℝᵏ (or of sets open in Hᵏ), with det Dg of constant sign, and let β = α∘g. If α*ω has compact support in A (or the extended integral exists), then ∫_{Y_β} ω = ∫_{Y_α} ω when det Dg > 0, and = −∫_{Y_α} ω when det Dg < 0.
  - *Proof sketch:* β*ω = g*(α*ω) = (h∘g)·det Dg dy₁∧⋯∧dy_k (cited from Chapter 3); then the change of variables theorem (cited from Chapter 1) gives ∫_B (h∘g)|det Dg| = ∫_A h. The sign of det Dg is the only difference.
  - *Remark:* on a connected B, det Dg never vanishes, so it has constant sign automatically (IVT along paths). On a disconnected B, split it into components.
- **Failure that motivates §2.** The integral over a chart is invariant only up to sign, and `exm-circle-two-ways` shows the sign really changes. To speak of "the integral over the circle" one must choose a direction. This choice is §2.
- **Quick check.** α(t) = (t, t²) on (0, 1) and ω = y dx. What is ∫_{Y_α} ω, and what is it for β(s) = (1 − s, (1 − s)²)? Answer: 1/3 and −1/3.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** α(t) = (t², t³) on (0, 1): compute α*(x dy) and its integral. Answer: 3t⁴ dt, 3/5.
  - **A2 (proof comprehension):** in `thm-reparametrization-sign`, which line uses det Dg > 0, and what does the proof give if det Dg changes sign on a disconnected B? Answer: the step |det Dg| = det Dg; the sum over components of ± (integral over the corresponding part of A).
  - **B1:** helicoid α(u, v) = (u cos v, u sin v, v), A = (0, 1) × (0, 2π). Compute ∫_{Y_α} dx∧dy and ∫_{Y_α} z dx∧dy. Answer: α*(dx∧dy) = u du∧dv, so π; α*(z dx∧dy) = uv du∧dv, so (1/2)(2π²) = π².
  - **B2:** α_m(t) = (cos t, sin t) on (0, 2πm). Show ∫ (x dy − y dx) = 2πm, and say which patch condition α_m violates for m ≥ 2. Answer: injectivity.
  - **C1:** deduce the case det Dg < 0 of `thm-reparametrization-sign` from the case det Dg > 0 by composing with the reflection ρ(x₁, …, x_k) = (−x₁, x₂, …). Answer: ρ*(dx₁∧⋯∧dx_k) = −dx₁∧⋯∧dx_k, and ρ preserves integrals (change of variables with |det| = 1).

## 02-orientation.md — Orientation (budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words)

- **Failure-motivated definition.** §1 ended with a sign we cannot control. The fix is to fix the sign once at every point, coherently.
- **`def-orientation-vector-space`.** Two ordered bases of a k-dimensional space V have the same orientation if the change-of-basis matrix has positive determinant (cited from LA2.7). An orientation is one of the two classes. For ℝᵏ, the standard orientation is the class of (e₁, …, e_k). A 0-dimensional space gets an orientation by a choice of sign, ±1.
- **Scratch Work (first appearance of sign bookkeeping for frames).** Compute the sign of (−e_k, e₁, …, e_{k−1}) against (e₁, …, e_k): move −e_k to the end past k − 1 vectors, which gives (−1)^{k−1}·(−1) = (−1)ᵏ. Keep the computation; §4 meets it again.
- **`def-orientation-manifold`.** Let M be a k-manifold in ℝⁿ with k ≥ 1. Two patches α: U → M, β: V → M *overlap positively* if det D(β⁻¹∘α) > 0 on α⁻¹(β(V)). An *orienting atlas* is a family of patches that cover M and overlap positively in pairs. M is *orientable* if it has one. An *orientation* is a maximal orienting atlas, equivalently a choice of orientation of each T_pM such that every patch in the atlas carries the standard orientation of ℝᵏ to it (state the equivalence; prove one direction, the other is A-level). `def-orientation-preserving-patch`: a patch in the maximal atlas. A 0-manifold (a finite set, when compact) is oriented by a function to {±1}.
- **`thm-two-orientations`.** A connected orientable k-manifold, k ≥ 1, has exactly two orientations.
  - *Proof Idea:* given two orientations, the set of points where they agree is open, and so is the set where they disagree (the sign of a continuous non-vanishing determinant is locally constant). Connectedness finishes.
- **`thm-orientation-unit-normal`.** Let M be an (n − 1)-manifold in ℝⁿ, n ≥ 2, with or without boundary. Orientations of M correspond one-to-one to continuous unit normal fields N on M. The correspondence: a basis (a₁, …, a_{n−1}) of T_pM is positive iff (N(p), a₁, …, a_{n−1}) is positive in ℝⁿ.
  - *Proof sketch:* given N, the patches α with det[N∘α, Dα] > 0 form an orienting atlas. If a patch on a connected domain has det < 0, compose it with a reflection of one coordinate. Conversely, given an orientation, N(p) is the unique unit vector orthogonal to T_pM with det[N, Dα] > 0. It is continuous because it is given by the generalized cross product of the columns of Dα, a polynomial formula, divided by its length.
- **`exm-mobius-nonorientable` (pays the Möbius question of the hook).** The band B(u, v) = ((2 + v cos(u/2)) cos u, (2 + v cos(u/2)) sin u, v sin(u/2)), u ∈ ℝ, |v| ≤ 1/2. Along the centre circle, n(u) = B_u × B_v at v = 0 satisfies n(u + 2π) = −n(u), while B(u + 2π, 0) = B(u, 0). A continuous unit normal N would give N(B(u, 0)) = s(u) n(u)/|n(u)| with s: ℝ → {±1} continuous, hence constant, and then the same point would have two opposite normals. By `thm-orientation-unit-normal`, the band is not orientable. Write out B_u and B_v at v = 0 (the sympy sidecar checks them).
- **Induced orientation on the boundary.**
  - `lem-boundary-transition-positive`: let k ≥ 2, and let α, β be patches about p ∈ ∂M with domains open in Hᵏ that overlap positively. Then their restrictions to ∂Hᵏ = ℝᵏ⁻¹ × {0} overlap positively as patches of ∂M.
    - *Proof sketch:* g = β⁻¹∘α maps ∂Hᵏ into ∂Hᵏ and keeps x_k ≥ 0 (cited from Chapter 2: boundary points go to boundary points). So at a boundary point ∂g_k/∂x_j = 0 for j < k and ∂g_k/∂x_k ≥ 0. Dg is block upper triangular with corner entry c ≥ 0, and det Dg = c·det A > 0 forces c > 0 and det A > 0, where A = D(g restricted to the boundary).
  - `def-induced-boundary-orientation`: if M is oriented and k ≥ 2, ∂M is oriented by the restricted patches α(x′, 0) when k is even, and by those restrictions composed with a reflection of x₁ when k is odd. (This is the (−1)ᵏ of the Scratch Work; §4 explains why this choice is forced.) For k = 1, each boundary point p gets the sign −1 if the positive direction of M at p points into M, and +1 if it points out.
  - `thm-boundary-orientation-outward` (usable form, the move "outward normal first"): a basis (b₁, …, b_{k−1}) of T_p∂M is positive for the induced orientation iff (n_out(p), b₁, …, b_{k−1}) is positive for T_pM. *Proof sketch:* check it on Hᵏ, where n_out = −e_k, with the Scratch Work's sign, then carry it over by an orientation-preserving patch, whose derivative maps the outward vector to an outward vector (Chapter 2).
  - `exm-boundary-interval`: M = [a, b] with the standard orientation gives ∂M = {b} with +1 and {a} with −1. That is "f(b) − f(a)".
  - Corollary in prose: for an n-manifold M in ℝⁿ with the standard orientation, ∂M is oriented by the outward unit normal in the sense of `thm-orientation-unit-normal`.
- **Warning.** The boundary of a non-orientable manifold can be orientable: the closed Möbius band has a single circle as boundary (exercise C1). "∂M orientable" says nothing about M.
- **Quick check.** Give the unit disk in ℝ² the standard orientation. Is the induced orientation of the unit circle counterclockwise? Answer: yes. At (1, 0), n_out = e₁, and (e₁, e₂) is positive, so e₂ is positive.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** is ((1, 1), (1, −1)) positively oriented in ℝ²? Answer: no, det = −2.
  - **A2:** orient S¹ counterclockwise. Is α(t) = (sin t, cos t) orientation-preserving? Answer: no. At t = 0, α′ = (1, 0) at (0, 1), which is clockwise there.
  - **B1:** the cylinder M = {x² + y² = 1, 0 ≤ z ≤ 1}, oriented by N = (x, y, 0). Find the induced orientations of the two boundary circles, viewed from above. Answer: bottom counterclockwise, top clockwise. At (1, 0, 0) with n_out = −e_z, det[e_x, −e_z, e_y] = +1.
  - **B2:** orient S^{n−1} by N(x) = x. Show that the antipodal map is orientation-preserving iff n is even. Answer: the frame (−w) at −x is compared using det[−x, −w₁, …, −w_{n−1}] = (−1)ⁿ det[x, w].
  - **C1:** show that the boundary of the closed Möbius band is a single circle (hence orientable) although the band is not. Answer: as u runs over [0, 4π], v = 1/2 traces the whole boundary, because (u + 2π, 1/2) and (u, −1/2) give the same point.
  - **C2:** prove `lem-boundary-transition-positive` in full, including the claim ∂g_k/∂x_k ≥ 0.

## 03-integrating-over-oriented-manifolds.md — Gluing: integrals over compact manifolds (budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words)

- **Failure that motivates gluing.** A single patch cannot cover S². A patch domain homeomorphic to S² would be a compact, open, non-empty subset of ℝ² (or of H²), which is impossible because ℝ² is connected. So integrals over S² need several charts and a way to avoid counting overlaps twice.
- **Scalar integrals first (orientation-free).** Define V(Dα) = √det(DαᵀDα), the k-volume factor (cited from LA2.7 for Gram determinants). Define `def-scalar-integral-manifold`: for M compact and f continuous on M, ∫_M f dV := Σᵢ ∫_{Uᵢ} (φᵢ f)∘αᵢ · V(Dαᵢ), with {φᵢ} a partition of unity (cited from Chapter 1) dominated by patch neighbourhoods. Well-definedness is the same proof as below with |det| in place of det, so it is stated and left as B1. Also define *measure zero in M*: each patch pulls the set back to a measure-zero set (AN3.4). **If Chapter 2 already defines these (Munkres §21–22, §25), recall them instead and shrink this subsection to one paragraph** (coordinator-notes).
- **`def-form-integral-manifold`.** Let M be a compact oriented k-manifold in ℝⁿ (k ≥ 1), and let ω be a k-form on an open set containing M. If supp ω ∩ M lies in the image of one orientation-preserving patch α: U → M, set ∫_M ω := ∫_U α*ω. In general, choose a partition of unity {φᵢ} on M dominated by images of orientation-preserving patches (finitely many non-zero near M, by compactness), and set ∫_M ω := Σᵢ ∫_M φᵢ ω. For k = 0 and M = {p₁, …, p_m} with signs εⱼ, ∫_M f := Σ εⱼ f(pⱼ).
- **`lem-form-integral-one-patch`.** The one-patch value does not depend on the orientation-preserving patch. *Proof:* `thm-reparametrization-sign` (from §1) with det D(β⁻¹∘α) > 0, applied on the overlap, where the support lies.
- **`thm-form-integral-well-defined` (key proof 1).** The value does not depend on the partition of unity or on the orientation-preserving patches. *Proof:* for a second partition {ψⱼ}, Σᵢ ∫φᵢω = Σᵢ Σⱼ ∫ψⱼφᵢω = Σⱼ ∫ψⱼω, using linearity in the one-patch case and finiteness. This is the first full run of **localize, prove in a chart, glue**: faded triple, item 1 (worked).
- **`thm-form-integral-properties`.** The integral is linear in ω; ∫_{−M} ω = −∫_M ω; and if M = M₁ ∪ M₂ with M₁ ∩ M₂ of measure zero in M, both compact k-manifolds with the restricted orientation, then the integrals add. (State the last item only in the form needed in §5. Its proof is C2.)
- **`thm-form-integral-as-scalar` (Munkres §36, our own route).** For p ∈ M, let λ_ω(p) := ω(p)(a₁, …, a_k), where (a₁, …, a_k) is any positive orthonormal basis of T_pM. Then λ_ω is well defined and continuous, and ∫_M ω = ∫_M λ_ω dV.
  - *Proof sketch:* two positive orthonormal bases differ by T ∈ SO(k), and ω(Ta) = det T·ω(a) (cited from Chapter 3), so λ_ω is well defined. In a patch, Gram–Schmidt applied to the columns of Dα gives a continuous positive orthonormal frame. Then α*ω = (λ_ω∘α)·V(Dα) dx₁∧⋯∧dx_k, because ω applied to the columns of Dα equals λ_ω times the determinant of the triangular Gram–Schmidt matrix, which is V(Dα) > 0.
  - *Corollary in prose:* for a hypersurface oriented by N and the flux form ι_F := Σᵢ (−1)^{i−1} Fᵢ dx₁∧⋯∧(dxᵢ omitted)∧⋯∧dx_n, λ = ⟨F, N⟩. This is proved in §5, where the Scratch Work for it sits.
- **`thm-form-integral-practical`.** Let M be compact and oriented, and let αⱼ: Aⱼ → M (j = 1, …, m) be orientation-preserving patches with Aⱼ open in ℝᵏ, pairwise disjoint images, and M ∖ ∪ⱼ αⱼ(Aⱼ) of measure zero in M. Then ∫_M ω = Σⱼ ∫_{Aⱼ} αⱼ*ω, the right side being extended integrals (cited from Chapter 1), which exist.
  - *Proof sketch:* reduce to the scalar statement with `thm-form-integral-as-scalar`. The scalar statement follows from partitions of unity and the fact that sets of measure zero do not change extended integrals. If Chapter 2 proves the scalar version, cite it.
- **Worked examples:**
  - `exm-sphere-area-form`: ω = x dy∧dz + y dz∧dx + z dx∧dy on S² with outward orientation. With spherical coordinates α(φ, θ) = (sin φ cos θ, sin φ sin θ, cos φ) on (0, π) × (0, 2π), check that α is orientation-preserving (α_φ × α_θ = sin φ·α, outward), and that α*ω = sin φ dφ∧dθ. The integral is 4π. The complement of the image is a half great circle, of measure zero.
  - `exm-torus-area`: the torus ((2 + cos v) cos u, (2 + cos v) sin u, sin v) on (0, 2π)², with V = 2 + cos v. The area is 8π².
- **Quick check.** Why does `def-form-integral-manifold` need M compact? Answer: so that only finitely many φᵢ are non-zero near M, and each φᵢω has compact support in its patch.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** compute ∫_{S²} z dx∧dy, outward orientation, using λ. Answer: λ = z·N_z = z², so 4π/3.

## 04-generalized-stokes.md — The generalized Stokes theorem (budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words)

- **Opening frame (spiral):** ∫_a^b f′ = f(b) − f(a) (AN2), read with `exm-boundary-interval`, is ∫_M df = ∫_{∂M} f for M = [a, b]. That is the case k = 1 of the theorem of this section.
- **Scratch Work (first appearance of "one variable at a time on the model", kept apart from the proof).** On H², take η = f dx₁ + g dx₂ with compact support. Then dη = (∂₁g − ∂₂f) dx₁∧dx₂. Integrate ∂₁g in x₁ first: 0. Integrate ∂₂f in x₂ first, from 0 to ∞: −f(x₁, 0), so −∂₂f contributes +∫f(x₁, 0) dx₁. The boundary term carries the sign (−1)² = +1 against the restriction x₁ ↦ (x₁, 0). Say what to look for in k dimensions: only j = k survives, with the sign (−1)ᵏ.
- **`lem-stokes-half-space` (key proof 2).** Let k ≥ 1, let U be open in Hᵏ, and let η be a C¹ (k − 1)-form on U with compact support. Then ∫_U dη = (−1)ᵏ ∫_{ℝᵏ⁻¹} b*η, where b(x′) = (x′, 0), and the right side is read as 0 if supp η misses ∂Hᵏ. If U is open in ℝᵏ, then ∫_U dη = 0.
  - *Proof:* write η = Σⱼ fⱼ dx₁∧⋯∧(dxⱼ omitted)∧⋯∧dx_k, so dη = Σⱼ (−1)^{j−1} ∂ⱼfⱼ dx₁∧⋯∧dx_k. Extend by 0 to a box Q = [−c, c]ᵏ⁻¹ × [0, c] whose relative boundary misses the support. By Fubini (AN3.4) integrate the j-th term in xⱼ first. For j < k the FTC gives fⱼ at two points outside the support, so 0. For j = k it gives −f_k(x′, 0). So ∫ dη = (−1)^{k−1}(−∫ f_k(x′, 0) dx′) = (−1)ᵏ ∫ b*η, since b*η = f_k(x′, 0) dx₁∧⋯∧dx_{k−1}.
  - *Self-explanation prompt:* "*Self-explain why only j = k survives.*"
- **Why the induced orientation is defined as it is (pays the forward note of §2).** b is orientation-preserving for ∂Hᵏ exactly when (−1)ᵏ = +1, so the (−1)ᵏ is absorbed. The definition in §2 is the one that makes the theorem sign-free.
- **`thm-stokes` (key proof 3; the registry label `thm-stokes`, statements.yaml).** Let k ≥ 1. Let M be a compact oriented k-manifold in ℝⁿ of class C^∞, give ∂M the induced orientation, and let ω be a C^∞ (k − 1)-form on an open set containing M. Then ∫_M dω = ∫_{∂M} ω, and ∫_M dω = 0 if ∂M = ∅.
  - *Proof:* choose a partition of unity {φᵢ} dominated by patch neighbourhoods, finitely many non-zero near M, with Σφᵢ = 1 on an open set W ⊇ M. Then dω = Σᵢ d(φᵢω) on W, because Σᵢ dφᵢ = d(Σᵢφᵢ) = 0 on W. For each i, α*(d(φᵢω)) = d(α*(φᵢω)) (cited from Chapter 3), and `lem-stokes-half-space` together with §2's sign gives ∫_M d(φᵢω) = ∫_{∂M} φᵢω. Sum. For k = 1, use the convention of §2 directly.
  - *Self-explanation prompt:* "*Before reading on: where is Σφᵢ = 1 near M used, and why is 'near' needed, not just 'on'?*"
  - *Remark (smoothness):* the proof uses only α ∈ C² (so that α* commutes with d) and ω ∈ C¹. Say so in one sentence. MT.6 revisits the theorem with less smoothness.
- **Hypotheses, each with a witness (STYLE §10):**
  - *Compactness:* `exm-stokes-noncompact`: M = [0, ∞), f = arctan. Then ∫_M df = π/2, but ∫_{∂M} f = −f(0) = 0.
  - *Orientation:* on the Möbius band neither side is defined (§2).
  - *ω defined on a neighbourhood of all of M:* `exm-stokes-singular-form`: ω = (x dy − y dx)/(x² + y²) and M the closed unit disk. ω is not defined at 0, ∫_{∂M} ω = 2π, and dω = 0 wherever ω is defined. On the annulus 1/2 ≤ r ≤ 1 the theorem applies: 2π − 2π = 0. Forward pointer: AM.2.
- **`cor-stokes-closed-manifold`.** If ∂M = ∅ and ω = dη for a (k − 1)-form η on an open set containing M, then ∫_M ω = 0.
- **`cor-ftc-line-integral`.** If C is a compact oriented 1-manifold with ∂C = {q} (+1) ∪ {p} (−1), and f is C¹ near C, then ∫_C df = f(q) − f(p). A closed curve gives 0.
- **Worked example:** verify `thm-stokes` for the closed unit ball in ℝ³ and ω from `exm-sphere-area-form`: dω = 3 dx∧dy∧dz, so the left side is 4π, matching §3.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1 (proof comprehension, key proof 2):** (a) why do the terms j < k vanish? (b) run the proof for k = 1, U = [0, ∞), η = f. Answer: (a) the FTC in xⱼ, with the endpoint values outside the support; (b) ∫_0^∞ f′ = −f(0) = (−1)¹ f(0).
  - **A2 (proof comprehension, key proof 3):** (a) where is compactness of M used? (b) why does Σφᵢ = 1 only on M not suffice? Answer: (a) finitely many φᵢ, and compact supports inside patches; (b) dω needs derivatives, so the identity Σdφᵢ = 0 must hold on an open set.
  - **B1 (faded triple, item 2):** the global step of `thm-stokes` with the last step blank. *Name the move, then complete it.* Answer: localize, prove in a chart, glue; sum the per-patch identities.
  - **B2:** verify `thm-stokes` for the upper unit hemisphere (normal pointing up) and ω = x dy. Answer: ∫ dx∧dy = ∫ N_z dA = π. The boundary circle is counterclockwise (at (1, 0, 0), det[e_x, −e_z, e_y] > 0), and ∮ x dy = π.
  - **C1 (proof ordering, STYLE §11.4):** eight shuffled lines of `lem-stokes-half-space` for k = 2, plus two distractors: "∫_0^c ∂₂f dx₂ = f(x₁, 0)" (the wrong sign) and "the j = 2 term vanishes because f has compact support in U" (support in U does not avoid ∂H²). The solution gives the admissible orders and why each distractor fails.
  - **C2 (find the error, true conclusion, STYLE §11.3):** "∫ over the circle |z − (3, 0)| = 1 of ω = (x dy − y dx)/(x² + y²) is 0. Proof: ω = dθ, where θ is the polar angle on ℝ² ∖ {0}, so by `cor-ftc-line-integral` the integral of a closed curve is 0." The conclusion is true. The failing step: θ is not a C¹ function on ℝ² ∖ {0}; there is no such global function. A counterexample to the step: on the unit circle the same argument would give 0, but the value is 2π. A correct proof: on the half-plane x > 0, θ = arctan(y/x) is C^∞ and ω = dθ there, and the circle lies in that half-plane.
  - **C3:** prove `cor-stokes-closed-manifold`. Then show that ω = (x dy − y dx)/(x² + y²) is closed on ℝ² ∖ {0} but not exact there. Answer: dω = 0 by computation; ∫_{S¹} ω = 2π ≠ 0, so ω ≠ df by `cor-ftc-line-integral`.

## 05-green-gauss-stokes.md — Green, Gauss and Stokes (budget ≤ 4,000 main-line words)

- **Opening frame:** the dictionary of Chapter 3 (work forms ω_F = Σ Fᵢ dxᵢ, flux forms ι_F, d ω_F ↔ curl F in ℝ³, d ι_F = (div F) dx₁∧⋯∧dx_n) turns `thm-stokes` into the three classical theorems. The only new ingredient is §3's λ.
- **Scratch Work (first appearance: reading a flux form as ⟨F, N⟩).** For M a hypersurface oriented by N and a positive orthonormal basis (a₁, …, a_{n−1}) of T_pM, ι_F(a₁, …, a_{n−1}) = det[F, a₁, …, a_{n−1}] (cofactor expansion along the first column). Write F = ⟨F, N⟩N + tangential part. The tangential part makes the determinant vanish, and det[N, a] = 1. So λ = ⟨F, N⟩. For a curve with positive unit tangent T, λ_{ω_F} = ⟨F, T⟩.
- **`thm-green`.** Let D ⊆ ℝ² be a compact 2-manifold with boundary (standard orientation, ∂D induced), and let P, Q be C¹ on an open set containing D. Then ∫_D (∂_xQ − ∂_yP) dA = ∫_{∂D} P dx + Q dy.
- **`thm-divergence-manifold`.** Let M ⊆ ℝⁿ be a compact n-manifold with boundary, N the outward unit normal on ∂M, and F a C¹ vector field on an open set containing M. Then ∫_M div F dV = ∫_{∂M} ⟨F, N⟩ dV. (The label differs from MT.6's `thm-divergence`; coordinator-notes.)
- **`thm-stokes-surface`.** Let S ⊆ ℝ³ be a compact 2-manifold with boundary, oriented by a unit normal N, with ∂S oriented by `thm-boundary-orientation-outward` (unit tangent T), and let F be C¹ near S. Then ∫_S ⟨curl F, N⟩ dA = ∫_{∂S} ⟨F, T⟩ ds.
- *Proofs:* each is `thm-stokes` plus the dictionary plus `thm-form-integral-as-scalar` with the Scratch Work. Prove the divergence theorem in full; the other two are A/B items.
- **`exm-hook-hemisphere` (pays the chapter hook).** F = (−y, x, 0), curl F = (0, 0, 2). The flux through the disk, the hemisphere and the paraboloid each equals ∮ −y dx + x dy = 2π by `thm-stokes-surface`. The flat disk confirms it directly: 2·π. The rim's orientation is counterclockwise for the upward normal in all three cases (B2 of §4).
- **`exm-gauss-law`.** F = x/|x|³ on ℝ³ ∖ {0}, div F = 0. The flux through any sphere centred at 0 is 4π (direct computation, λ = 1/R²). For a sphere not enclosing 0 it is 0 (divergence theorem on the ball). For one enclosing 0 it is 4π (divergence theorem on the region between it and a small sphere about 0; the small sphere carries the opposite orientation as part of the boundary).
- **`exm-ellipse-area`.** Area = (1/2)∮ x dy − y dx = πab for x²/a² + y²/b² ≤ 1.
- **Remark (corners; Rudin's chains; Lee's corners):** the square [0, 1]² is not a 2-manifold with boundary in our sense, because of its corners, yet Green holds on it (C1). Rudin proves Stokes for chains of simplices, and Lee for manifolds with corners. Our version suffices for the targets of this chapter.
- **Quick check.** For F = (P, Q, R), which form's integral over a surface is the flux of F, and which form's integral over a curve is the work? Answer: P dy∧dz + Q dz∧dx + R dx∧dy; P dx + Q dy + R dz.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** derive `thm-green` from `thm-stokes` (write d(P dx + Q dy)).
  - **A2:** compute the area of the ellipse with `thm-green`. Answer: πab.
  - **B1:** F = (x³, y³, z³) on the closed unit ball: compute both sides of `thm-divergence-manifold`. Answer: div F = 3r², ∫ = 12π/5; ∫_{S²} (x⁴ + y⁴ + z⁴) dA = 3·(4π/5) = 12π/5.
  - **B2:** F = (z, x, y), S = upper unit hemisphere with upward normal. Compute both sides of `thm-stokes-surface`. Answer: curl F = (1, 1, 1), ∫ (x + y + z) dA = ∫ z dA = π; ∮ z dx + x dy + y dz = ∮ x dy = π.
  - **B3:** the flux of x/|x|³ through the sphere of radius 1 about (3, 0, 0), and through the sphere of radius 4 about (3, 0, 0). Answer: 0; 4π.
  - **C1:** prove Green on the square [0, 1]² directly from Fubini and the FTC, for P, Q of class C¹ near the square.
  - **C2:** derive the normal form of Green, ∫_{∂D} ⟨F, n⟩ ds = ∫_D div F dA, from `thm-green`, with n the outward normal in the plane.
  - **C3:** compute the flux of curl(−y, x, 0) through the paraboloid z = 1 − x² − y², z ≥ 0, directly with α(u, v) = (u, v, 1 − u² − v²), and compare it with the hook. Answer: α*(2 dx∧dy) = 2 du∧dv, so 2π.

## 06-consequences.md — First consequences, and when Stokes fails (budget ≤ 3,500 main-line words)

- **`thm-exact-integral-zero`** (restating `cor-stokes-closed-manifold` as a test): if M is a compact oriented k-manifold without boundary and ∫_M ω ≠ 0, then ω is not exact on any open set containing M. This is the named move "a nonzero integral certifies non-exactness".
- **`exm-solid-angle-not-exact` (the hook planted for AM.2).** σ = (x dy∧dz + y dz∧dx + z dx∧dy)/|x|³ on ℝ³ ∖ {0}. It is closed (dσ = 0, a computation), ∫_{S²} σ = 4π, so σ is not exact. Prose: AM.2 shows that on ℝ³ ∖ {0} this is essentially the only obstruction.
- **`thm-smooth-no-retraction`.** There is no C² map r from an open set containing the closed unit ball Bⁿ (n ≥ 1) into ℝⁿ with r(Bⁿ) ⊆ S^{n−1} and r = id on S^{n−1}.
  - *Proof sketch:* η = x₁ dx₂∧⋯∧dx_n has ∫_{S^{n−1}} η = vol(Bⁿ) > 0 by `thm-stokes`. But r*η = η on S^{n−1} (because r = id there, and r ∈ C² gives r*(dη) = d(r*η)), and ∫_{S^{n−1}} r*η = ∫_B r*(dη) = ∫_B det Dr dV = 0, since |r|² = 1 on B gives Drᵀr = 0 in the interior, so rank Dr ≤ n − 1 there, and by continuity on B. Note in one sentence that r*η = η on S^{n−1} holds as forms restricted to the tangent spaces of S^{n−1}, which is all ∫ sees (A3 of §3). For n = 1, the statement is that [−1, 1] does not retract onto {±1}, by the IVT.
  - Prose only: the fixed-point theorem that follows from this belongs to a later branch (no label, no edge).
- **`thm-green-identities`.** For f, g of class C² near a compact n-manifold M ⊆ ℝⁿ: ∫_M (f Δg + ⟨∇f, ∇g⟩) dV = ∫_{∂M} f ∂_N g dV, and ∫_M (f Δg − g Δf) dV = ∫_{∂M} (f ∂_N g − g ∂_N f) dV. *Proof:* `thm-divergence-manifold` with F = f∇g.
- **Closing remark (pays the last part of the hook):** the hemisphere and the disk together bound the solid half-ball H. Their fluxes of curl F differ by ∫_H div curl F = 0, which is d∘d = 0 read through the dictionary. The boundary of a boundary is empty, and d∘d = 0 is the same fact seen through Stokes (prose).
- **Weird-examples table "When Stokes fails"** (continues the AM table; each row: the example, the hypothesis it breaks, where it appears):
  - `exm-circle-twice`: a non-injective parametrization counts twice (§1);
  - `exm-mobius-nonorientable`: no orientation, so neither side is defined (§2);
  - `exm-stokes-noncompact`: [0, ∞) with arctan breaks compactness (§4);
  - `exm-stokes-singular-form`: ω undefined at one point of the disk (§4; AM.2);
  - the square: corners, yet the formula holds (§5 C1);
  - `exm-solid-angle-not-exact`: closed, not exact, detected by Stokes (§6; AM.2);
  - `exm-gauss-law`: the singularity inside versus outside (§5).
- **List of Results:** every def, thm, lem and cor of the chapter, in order, with one-line statements.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** "∫_{S²} ω = 0 for every 2-form ω, since S² has no boundary." Correct the claim, with a witness. Answer: only for exact ω. For ω = x dy∧dz, ∫ = ∫ x N_x dA = 4π/3 = ∫_B dx dy dz.
  - **A2:** check dσ = 0 for `exm-solid-angle-not-exact` (sympy sidecar).
  - **B1:** derive both `thm-green-identities` from `thm-divergence-manifold`.
  - **B2:** prove vol(M) = (1/n)∫_{∂M} ⟨x, N⟩ dV for a compact n-manifold M ⊆ ℝⁿ, and check it on the unit ball in ℝ³. Answer: div x = n; (1/3)·4π = 4π/3.
  - **C1 (faded triple, item 3):** prove `cor-stokes-closed-manifold` directly from `lem-stokes-half-space` without quoting `thm-stokes`, with the second half blank. *Name the move, then complete it.* Answer: localize, prove in a chart, glue.
  - **C2 (exit check):** prove `thm-smooth-no-retraction` in full for n = 2. Marking outline: the choice of η and ∫_{S¹} η = π (2); r*η = η on S¹ (2); Stokes on B (2); det Dr = 0 with the reason (3); conclusion (1). Fatal: using r*η = η on all of B.
  - **C3:** a harmonic function u (Δu = 0) near the closed unit ball with u = 0 on the sphere vanishes on the ball. Answer: the first identity with f = g = u gives ∫_B |∇u|² = 0.
  - **R. Review:**
    - **R1 (Chapter 3):** compute d and the pullback by spherical coordinates of z dx∧dy. Answer: d = dx∧dy∧dz; the pullback is cos φ · sin φ cos φ dφ∧dθ = sin φ cos² φ dφ∧dθ.
    - **R2 (Chapter 1, three back):** use the change of variables theorem in polar coordinates to compute ∫∫_{x²+y²≤1} (x² + y²) dA. Answer: π/2.
    - **R3 (spiral, AN2):** "You proved ∫_a^b f′ = f(b) − f(a). State it as `thm-stokes` for a 1-manifold, and say what the orientation of ∂[a, b] contributes." Answer: `exm-boundary-interval`, the signs ±1.
    - **R4 (no hint):** ∮ (x dy − y dx)/(x² + y²) over the ellipse x²/4 + y² = 1, counterclockwise. Answer: 2π.
