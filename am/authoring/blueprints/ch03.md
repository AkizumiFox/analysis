**Purpose.**
- Give the integrand of AN3.7 a meaning. In AN3.7 the thing integrated over a k-dimensional manifold in ℝⁿ is a k-form, and Stokes' theorem is one sentence, ∫_M dω = ∫_{∂M} ω. This chapter builds both symbols on that line, d and ω, and nothing about integration.
- Everything here lives on **open subsets of ℝⁿ**. Forms are fields of alternating multilinear functionals on the tangent spaces T_pℝⁿ = {p} × ℝⁿ. AN3.5's tangent spaces of submanifolds are not used; AN3.7 restricts forms to them.
- The pointwise algebra is LA4.15's exterior algebra, read on the dual space. The chapter does not rebuild Λᵏ. It proves one bridge theorem that identifies Λᵏ(V*) with alternating k-linear forms on V, and inherits associativity and graded commutativity from it.
- The analytic content is three theorems: the exterior derivative exists and is unique (with d∘d = 0 from Schwarz), pullback is natural (commutes with ∧ and d), and the top-degree pullback is multiplication by det Dα **with its sign**.

## Chapter hook and where it is paid

- **Hook 1 (index.md, then §04): the angle form.** On the punctured plane, ω_θ = (−y dx + x dy)/(x² + y²). Every point has a neighbourhood on which ω_θ = dθ for a branch of the angle, so ω_θ "is the derivative of the angle". §04 shows dω_θ = 0 and proves that ω_θ is **not** d of any function on ℝ² ∖ {0}, by the one-variable FTC along the unit circle (the would-be primitive climbs by 2π around a closed loop). The question left open: *what does the 2π measure, and is ω_θ essentially the only closed form that is not exact?*
  - **Paid in AN3.7** (the 2π is ∫_{S¹} ω_θ, and Stokes explains why it is the same for every loop around 0) and **AM.2** (H¹(ℝ² ∖ {0}) is one-dimensional, spanned by [ω_θ]; its higher analogue on ℝⁿ ∖ {0} is the solid-angle form introduced in §04's exercises).
- **Hook 2 (§05): the missing absolute value.** AN3.4 and AM.1's change of variables carry |det Dα|; the top-degree pullback of §05 carries det Dα with no absolute value. The sign is not an error. **Paid in AN3.7** (orientation: for orientation-preserving parametrizations the two agree, and that is why forms, not functions, are what one integrates over oriented manifolds).
- **Hooks paid here:** AN3.1's Schwarz counterexample returns in §04 as the reason d∘d = 0 needs C² (weird-examples row W4). LA4.15's "determinant is the top exterior power" returns as the top-degree pullback formula (§05).

## 01-alternating-forms.md — Alternating multilinear forms

Budget: ≤ 4,000 main-line words.

- **Hook: area is not linear, signed area is.** The area of the parallelogram on v, w ∈ ℝ² is |v₁w₂ − v₂w₁|. It fails additivity: area(v, w + w') ≠ area(v, w) + area(v, w') for v = e₁, w = e₂, w' = −2e₂ (1 + 2 ≠ 1). Dropping the absolute value repairs it, at the price of a sign that records orientation (`exm-signed-area`). **Failure-motivated definition:** what survives is multilinearity plus "degenerate input gives 0".
- `def-tensor-k`: a k-tensor on V is a k-linear map V^k → ℝ (LA4.15 `def-multilinear-map` with W = ℝ); tensor product (S ⊗ T)(v₁, …, v_{k+l}) = S(v₁..v_k)T(v_{k+1}..v_{k+l}).
- `def-alternating-form`: a k-tensor that vanishes whenever two inputs are equal (LA4.15 `def-alternating-multilinear-map`). 𝒜ᵏ(V).
- `prp-alternating-sign-rule`: for alternating T and σ ∈ S_k, T(v_{σ(1)}, …, v_{σ(k)}) = sgn σ · T(v₁, …, v_k); T vanishes on dependent inputs; 𝒜ᵏ(V) = 0 for k > dim V. *Proof sketch:* cite LA4.15 `lem-alternating-map-properties` for the transposition case and LA2.7 for sgn of products; dependent inputs: expand one input as a combination of the others.
- `exm-tensor-not-alternating`: φ₁ ⊗ φ₂ on ℝ² is not alternating ((φ₁⊗φ₂)(e₁, e₂) = 1, (φ₁⊗φ₂)(e₂, e₁) = 0). Motivates §02.
- `def-elementary-alternating-form`: for V = ℝⁿ with dual basis φ₁..φ_n and increasing I, φ_I(v₁, …, v_k) = det of the k × k matrix [φ_{i_a}(v_b)] (rows I of the matrix with columns v_b).
- **`thm-alternating-dual-exterior` (key proof 1).** Let V be finite-dimensional over ℝ, k ≥ 1. The map Ψ: Λᵏ(V*) → 𝒜ᵏ(V), Ψ(λ₁ ∧ ⋯ ∧ λ_k)(v₁, …, v_k) = det[λ_a(v_b)], is a well-defined linear isomorphism.
  - *Proof sketch.* (i) For fixed v's, (λ₁, …, λ_k) ↦ det[λ_a(v_b)] is alternating k-linear in the λ's, so LA4.15 (E1) gives a linear map on Λᵏ(V*); letting v vary gives Ψ, and det[λ_a(v_b)] is alternating k-linear in the v's, so Ψ lands in 𝒜ᵏ(V). (ii) With a basis e_j of V and dual basis φ_j, Ψ(φ_I) = φ_I (the elementary form), and φ_I(e_{j₁}, …, e_{j_k}) = δ_{IJ} for increasing J. So the images of the basis φ_I of Λᵏ(V*) (LA4.15 `thm-exterior-power-basis`) are linearly independent. (iii) The φ_I span 𝒜ᵏ(V): for T ∈ 𝒜ᵏ(V), T − Σ_I T(e_I)φ_I vanishes on every increasing tuple of basis vectors, hence by `prp-alternating-sign-rule` on every tuple of basis vectors, hence by multilinearity everywhere.
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of "evaluate on basis tuples, then use the sign rule"): kept apart; shows how to guess the coefficients T(e_I) before writing (iii).
- `cor-alternating-basis`: {φ_I} is a basis of 𝒜ᵏ(ℝⁿ); dim = C(n, k); T = Σ_I T(e_{i₁}, …, e_{i_k}) φ_I.
- `def-linear-pullback`: for linear L: U → V and T ∈ 𝒜ᵏ(V), (L*T)(u₁..u_k) = T(Lu₁, …, Lu_k). L* is linear and (ML)* = L*M*.
- `prp-top-degree-det`: for L: V → V with dim V = n and T ∈ 𝒜ⁿ(V), L*T = (det L) T. *Proof sketch:* 𝒜ⁿ(V) is a line; transport LA4.15 `thm-det-is-top-exterior` through Ψ, or evaluate on a basis and use the Leibniz expansion.
- **Warning.** "Alternating" is not "antisymmetric under one particular swap": a bilinear form with T(e₁, e₂) = −T(e₂, e₁) but T(e₁, e₁) ≠ 0 is not alternating.
- **Quick check.** Compute φ_{13}(v, w) for v = (1, 2, 0), w = (0, 1, 3) in ℝ³. *Answer:* det [[1, 0], [0, 3]] = 3.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1** (comprehension, key proof 1): (a) in step (iii), why does vanishing on increasing tuples imply vanishing on all tuples of basis vectors, including tuples with repeats? *Answer:* repeats give 0 by alternation; a non-increasing tuple without repeats is a permutation of an increasing one, and the sign rule turns its value into ± the value on the increasing one. (b) Where is finite dimension used? *Answer:* existence of the dual basis φ_j with φ_i(e_j) = δ_ij spanning V*, and the basis theorem for Λᵏ(V*).
  - **A2:** which are alternating on ℝ³: T₁(v, w) = v₁w₂; T₂(v, w) = v₁w₂ − v₂w₁ + v₃w₃; T₃(u, v, w) = det[u v w]. *Answer:* only T₃ (T₁(e₁,e₂)≠−T₁(e₂,e₁); T₂(e₃,e₃) = 1).
  - **B1:** expand T(v, w) = v₁w₃ − v₃w₁ + 2(v₂w₃ − v₃w₂) in the basis φ_I of 𝒜²(ℝ³). *Answer:* φ_{13} + 2φ_{23}.
  - **B2:** dim 𝒜ᵏ(ℝ⁵) for k = 0, …, 6. *Answer:* 1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1, 0.
  - **B3:** for L(x, y) = (x + 2y, 3y) on ℝ², compute L*φ_{12}. *Answer:* 3φ_{12} (det L = 3).
  - **C1:** show that every T ∈ 𝒜^{n−1}(ℝⁿ) has the form T(v₁, …, v_{n−1}) = det[a v₁ ⋯ v_{n−1}] for a unique a ∈ ℝⁿ. *Answer sketch:* both sides are linear in the coefficient data; dimension n on each side; injectivity: if det[a v₁..] = 0 for all v's then a = 0 (choose v's completing a to a basis). This is the "flux" identification used in §03.
  - **C2:** let L: ℝⁿ → ℝᵐ be linear with m < n. Show L*: 𝒜ⁿ(ℝᵐ) → 𝒜ⁿ(ℝⁿ) is zero, and give the reason in one sentence without coordinates. *Answer:* 𝒜ⁿ(ℝᵐ) = 0 since n > m. Follow-up: is L*: 𝒜ᵐ(ℝᵐ) → 𝒜ᵐ(ℝⁿ) zero? *Answer:* only if rank L < m; if L is onto, L*φ_{1..m} ≠ 0 (evaluate on vectors mapping to e₁..e_m).

## 02-wedge-product.md — The wedge product

Budget: ≤ 4,000 main-line words.

- **Hook (failure-motivated definition).** `exm-tensor-not-alternating`: the tensor product of alternating forms is not alternating. We want a product of a k-form and an l-form that is a (k + l)-form, with φ_i "times" φ_j equal to φ_{ij} for i < j. LA4.15 already has it on Λ(V*).
- `def-wedge-forms`: for α ∈ 𝒜ᵏ(V), β ∈ 𝒜ˡ(V), α ∧ β := Ψ(Ψ⁻¹α ∧ Ψ⁻¹β); 𝒜⁰ = ℝ acts by scalars.
- `thm-wedge-properties`: bilinear; associative; (α ∧ β) = (−1)^{kl}(β ∧ α); α ∧ α = 0 for k odd; for 1-forms λ_i, (λ₁ ∧ ⋯ ∧ λ_k)(v₁, …, v_k) = det[λ_a(v_b)]. *Proof sketch:* transported from LA4.15 `def-exterior-algebra` and `thm-exterior-algebra-graded-commutative`; the last item is the definition of Ψ.
- `cor-elementary-is-wedge`: φ_I = φ_{i₁} ∧ ⋯ ∧ φ_{i_k}. (Immediate; names the bridge between §01's determinant definition and the product.)
- **`thm-wedge-shuffle-formula`.** (α ∧ β)(v₁, …, v_{k+l}) = Σ_σ sgn σ · α(v_{σ(1)}, …, v_{σ(k)}) β(v_{σ(k+1)}, …, v_{σ(k+l)}), the sum over (k, l)-shuffles (σ increasing on {1..k} and on {k+1..k+l}).
  - *Proof sketch:* both sides are bilinear in (α, β) and (k + l)-linear alternating in the v's (alternation of the right side: a transposition of two equal v's pairs shuffles with opposite signs). By move 1 it suffices to check α = φ_I, β = φ_J and v's = basis vectors e_K, K increasing of size k + l; both sides are 0 unless I ∪ J = K with I ∩ J = ∅, and then the right side has exactly one non-zero term, with the sign of the shuffle that sorts (I, J). That is the generalized Laplace expansion; cite LA4.15 `cor-laplace-from-wedge` for the k = 1 case and note the general case is the same count.
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of move 1): written apart, on k = l = 1 in ℝ³ first, then the general bookkeeping.
- `prp-wedge-independence`: 1-forms λ₁..λ_k are linearly independent iff λ₁ ∧ ⋯ ∧ λ_k ≠ 0 (LA4.15 `thm-wedge-nonzero-iff-independent` through Ψ).
- `prp-linear-pullback-wedge`: L*(α ∧ β) = L*α ∧ L*β. *Proof sketch:* by move 1, on 1-forms it is det[λ_a(Lv_b)] = det[(L*λ_a)(v_b)]; extend by bilinearity and associativity.
- `exm-cross-product-wedge`: on ℝ³, (a·φ) ∧ (b·φ) = (a × b)₁ φ_{23} + (a × b)₂ φ_{31} + (a × b)₃ φ_{12}, with φ_{31} = −φ_{13}. Explicit components: (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂), (a₃b₁ − a₁b₃), (a₁b₂ − a₂b₁).
- `exm-symplectic-square` (weird row W1): on ℝ⁴, α = φ_{12} + φ_{34} has α ∧ α = 2φ_{1234} ≠ 0. Even-degree forms need not square to zero, so α is not a wedge of two 1-forms (if α = λ ∧ μ then α ∧ α = 0).
- `rem-wedge-conventions` (weird row W2): some differential-geometry texts normalize the product without the shuffle count, so that their (φ₁ ∧ φ₂)(e₁, e₂) = 1/2. This book uses the determinant convention (Munkres, Spivak, Lee, Tu agree), under which φ_I(e_I) = 1. Formulas copied across conventions differ by k!l!/(k+l)!. One paragraph; no history.
- **Warning.** α ∧ α = 0 is for odd degree only (W1). And φ_{13} ∧ φ_2 = −φ_{123}, not φ_{123}: count the transpositions (move 4).
- **Quick check.** Simplify φ₃ ∧ φ₁ ∧ φ₂ in 𝒜³(ℝ³). *Answer:* φ₁₂₃ (two transpositions).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** sort and sign: φ₂ ∧ φ₁; φ₂ ∧ φ₃ ∧ φ₁; φ₁ ∧ φ₂ ∧ φ₁. *Answer:* −φ₁₂; φ₁₂₃; 0.
  - **A2:** degree bookkeeping: if α ∈ 𝒜²(ℝ⁵) and β ∈ 𝒜⁴(ℝ⁵), what is α ∧ β? *Answer:* 0 (degree 6 > 5).
  - **B1** (faded use of move 1, last step blank): prove (−1)^{kl} graded commutativity directly from the shuffle formula for α = φ_I, β = φ_J, with all steps up to "the shuffle sorting (J, I) differs from the one sorting (I, J) by kl transpositions" given. *Answer:* complete by counting: moving each of the l indices of J past the k indices of I.
  - **B2:** on ℝ⁴ compute (φ₁ + φ₂) ∧ (φ₃ − φ₄) ∧ φ₂. *Answer:* expand; terms with φ₂ twice vanish: φ₁ ∧ φ₃ ∧ φ₂ − φ₁ ∧ φ₄ ∧ φ₂ = −φ₁₂₃ + φ₁₂₄.
  - **B3:** are λ = φ₁ + φ₂ − φ₃, μ = 2φ₁ − φ₃, ν = φ₂ + φ₃ independent in (ℝ³)*? *Answer:* λ ∧ μ ∧ ν = det [[1,1,−1],[2,0,−1],[0,1,1]] φ₁₂₃ = −3 φ₁₂₃ ≠ 0 (expand along the first row: 1·(0·1 − (−1)·1) − 1·(2·1 − 0) + (−1)·(2·1 − 0) = 1 − 2 − 2 = −3); independent.
  - **C1** (Plücker): for α = Σ_{i<j} a_{ij} φ_{ij} ∈ 𝒜²(ℝ⁴), show α ∧ α = 2(a₁₂a₃₄ − a₁₃a₂₄ + a₁₄a₂₃) φ₁₂₃₄, and deduce that α is decomposable only if a₁₂a₃₄ − a₁₃a₂₄ + a₁₄a₂₃ = 0. (The converse holds; prove it for a₁₂ ≠ 0: α = (φ₁ − (a₂₃/a₁₂)φ₃ − (a₂₄/a₁₂)φ₄) ∧ (a₁₂φ₂ + a₁₃φ₃ + a₁₄φ₄); S2a: verify by sympy.)
  - **C2:** give an α ∈ 𝒜²(ℝ³) that is not decomposable, or prove none exists. *Answer:* none exists. Write α = c₁φ₂₃ + c₂φ₃₁ + c₃φ₁₂. If c₃ ≠ 0, then α = (φ₁ − (c₁/c₃)φ₃) ∧ (c₃φ₂ − c₂φ₃) (expand: c₃φ₁₂ − c₂φ₁₃ − c₁φ₃₂, and −φ₁₃ = φ₃₁, −φ₃₂ = φ₂₃); the cases c₃ = 0 follow by permuting coordinates, and α = 0 is 0 ∧ φ₁. Contrast with W1 on ℝ⁴.

## 03-differential-forms.md — Differential forms on open sets of ℝⁿ

Budget: ≤ 3,500 main-line words.

- **Hook (failure-motivated).** Calculus books write f(x) dx and dy = f'(x) dx with dx "an infinitely small number". That reading collapses at the first test: dx dy versus dy dx in a double integral after a change of variables gives the same positive number, while the polar computation of §05 will need dx ∧ dy = −dy ∧ dx. The repair: dx is a linear functional on displacement vectors, one at each point.
- `def-tangent-space-rn`: T_pℝⁿ = {p} × ℝⁿ with vector operations in the second slot; (p; v). One paragraph says why the base point is kept: AN3.7 restricts to subspaces T_pM ⊆ T_pℝⁿ that vary with p (AN3.5, prose pointer).
- `def-differential-form`: a k-form on open U ⊆ ℝⁿ assigns to each p ∈ U an ω_p ∈ 𝒜ᵏ(T_pℝⁿ). 0-forms are functions.
- `thm-standard-presentation`: every k-form is uniquely ω = Σ_I f_I dx_I, f_I: U → ℝ, with f_I(p) = ω_p(e_{i₁}, …, e_{i_k}) (vectors based at p). *Proof:* `cor-alternating-basis` pointwise.
- `def-form-class`: ω is of class C^r iff every f_I is C^r; Ωᵏ_r(U). Well defined by uniqueness of the presentation. Wedge of forms pointwise; class C^r is preserved.
- `def-differential-of-function`: for f ∈ C¹(U), df_p(p; v) = Df(p)v. In particular the coordinate function x_i gives d(x_i)_p(p; v) = v_i, so **dx_i is d of the function x_i**, not a new symbol, and dx_I = dx_{i₁} ∧ ⋯ ∧ dx_{i_k} is the field p ↦ φ_I.
- `prp-df-coordinates`: df = Σ_i ∂_i f dx_i; f ∈ C^r ⇒ df ∈ Ω¹_{r−1}. *Proof:* evaluate on e_i; AN3.1 (Df(p)e_i = ∂_if(p)).
- `exm-work-flux-density` (vector calculus dictionary on U ⊆ ℝ³): for F = (F₁, F₂, F₃), the work form ω_F = F₁dx + F₂dy + F₃dz satisfies (ω_F)_p(v) = F(p)·v; the flux form η_F = F₁ dy∧dz + F₂ dz∧dx + F₃ dx∧dy satisfies (η_F)_p(v, w) = det[F(p) v w]; the density form ρ_g = g dx∧dy∧dz. Link: §01 C1.
- `exm-not-a-differential` (weird row W3): x dy on ℝ² is not df for any C² f: ∂_x f = 0 and ∂_y f = x would give ∂_x∂_y f = 1 ≠ 0 = ∂_y∂_x f. (Foreshadows "closed" in §04 without the word.)

## 04-exterior-derivative.md — The exterior derivative

Budget: ≤ 4,500 main-line words.

- **Hook.** The angle form ω_θ (`exm-angle-form`): computed in both coordinates. We want an operator that extends f ↦ df to all degrees and detects the obstruction "x dy is not a differential" of §03 (W3) by a computation, not an argument.
- **Failure-motivated definition.** Try "differentiate the coefficients": for ω = Σ f_I dx_I set Dω := Σ_I Σ_j ∂_jf_I · (something). Demand: (a) agrees with df on 0-forms; (b) Leibniz d(α ∧ β) = dα ∧ β + (−1)ᵏ α ∧ dβ; (c) d(df) = 0. Show by the one-line computation d(x dy) under (a)–(c) that the only candidate is dx ∧ dy, so the "something" is ∧ dx_j in front: d(f dx_I) = df ∧ dx_I.
- **`thm-exterior-derivative` (key proof 2).** Let U ⊆ ℝⁿ be open. There is a unique family of maps d: Ωᵏ_1(U) → Ωᵏ⁺¹_0(U), k ≥ 0, such that (D1) d is ℝ-linear; (D2) on 0-forms d is the differential of `def-differential-of-function`; (D3) d(α ∧ β) = dα ∧ β + (−1)ᵏ α ∧ dβ for α ∈ Ωᵏ_1, β ∈ Ωˡ_1; (D4) d(df) = 0 for every f ∈ C²(U). Moreover d maps Ωᵏ_r to Ωᵏ⁺¹_{r−1} (r ≥ 1), and **d(dω) = 0 for every ω ∈ Ωᵏ_2(U)**.
  - *Proof route.* Uniqueness: by (D1) reduce to f dx_I; by (D3) and (D4) applied to the coordinate functions, d(f dx_I) = df ∧ dx_I + f d(dx_I) and d(dx_I) = 0 (induction on |I| with (D3)). Existence: define d by `prp-d-coordinate-formula` d(Σ f_I dx_I) = Σ df_I ∧ dx_I (well defined by `thm-standard-presentation`); (D1), (D2) are immediate; (D3) by move 2 on α = f dx_I, β = g dx_J, using d(fg) = g df + f dg (AN3.1, product rule) and dx_I ∧ dg = (−1)ᵏ dg ∧ dx_I (move 4); for (D4) and d∘d = 0: d(d(f dx_I)) = Σ_{i,j} ∂_i∂_j f dx_i ∧ dx_j ∧ dx_I = 0 because ∂_i∂_jf = ∂_j∂_if (Schwarz, AN3.1, uses C²) and dx_i ∧ dx_j = −dx_j ∧ dx_i.
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of move 2): the reduction to one term f dx_I, with the sign check in (D3) done on k = 1 first.
  - Hypotheses: `exm-peano-dd` shows what fails without C² in d∘d = 0. The text claims no counterexample to uniqueness without (D4); it says only that (D4) is used in the uniqueness proof at d(dx_i) = 0 (S2a item 4).
- `exm-peano-dd` (weird row W4): f(x, y) = xy(x² − y²)/(x² + y²), f(0, 0) = 0, is C¹ on ℝ² with ∂_y∂_x f(0, 0) = −1 and ∂_x∂_y f(0, 0) = 1 (AN3.1's counterexample, cited). df is a C⁰ 1-form, so d(df) is not defined; applying the coordinate formula anyway at the origin, where the partials of the coefficients exist, gives 2 dx∧dy at 0 and 0 elsewhere. The C² hypothesis in d∘d = 0 is doing work.
- `exm-grad-curl-div`: on U ⊆ ℝ³, df = ω_{∇f}, dω_F = η_{curl F}, dη_F = (div F) dx∧dy∧dz. Then d∘d = 0 says curl ∇f = 0 and div curl F = 0 (for C² data). One table, three lines.
- `def-closed-exact`: ω ∈ Ωᵏ_1(U) is closed if dω = 0; ω ∈ Ωᵏ_0(U) is exact if ω = dη for some η ∈ Ωᵏ⁻¹_1(U).
- `prp-exact-implies-closed`: if ω = dη with η ∈ Ωᵏ⁻¹_2(U), then ω is closed. (Hypothesis: η of class C²; flag that with η only C¹ the statement needs care, and keep C² in the statement.)
- `exm-angle-form` (Hook 1 paid in part): ω_θ = (−y dx + x dy)/(x² + y²) on U = ℝ² ∖ {0} is C^∞ and closed: ∂_x(x/(x²+y²)) = (y² − x²)/(x²+y²)² = ∂_y(−y/(x²+y²)). On the slit plane ℝ² ∖ {(x, 0) : x ≤ 0}, ω_θ = dθ with θ ∈ (−π, π) the principal angle ; verified in §05 (`exm-angle-form-polar`), and the text says so here.
- **`thm-angle-form-not-exact`.** There is no C¹ function f on ℝ² ∖ {0} with df = ω_θ.
  - *Proof sketch.* Let γ(t) = (cos t, sin t), t ∈ [0, 2π]. If df = ω_θ, then by the chain rule (AN3.1) (f∘γ)'(t) = df_{γ(t)}(γ'(t)) = (ω_θ)_{γ(t)}(γ'(t)) = sin²t + cos²t = 1, so f(γ(2π)) − f(γ(0)) = 2π by the FTC (AN1/AN2), contradicting γ(2π) = γ(0).
  - Remark: the same computation for any closed C¹ loop in ℝ² ∖ {0} gives 2π times an integer; that integer, and why only closedness matters, is AN3.7's and AM.2's business (prose pointer; Hook 1 left open).
- **Warning.** "Closed" here is not "closed set", and the closed/exact question depends on the domain: ω_θ is exact on the slit plane and not on ℝ² ∖ {0}.
- **Quick check.** d(xy dz) on ℝ³. *Answer:* y dx∧dz + x dy∧dz.
- **Exercises.**

## 05-pullback.md — Pullback and naturality

Budget: ≤ 4,500 main-line words. Last section: carries the R group, the weird-examples table and the List of Results.

- **Hook (failure-motivated).** Functions can be pushed forward along a map only when it is a bijection; forms pull back along every C¹ map, because a form eats tangent vectors and Dα carries tangent vectors forward. Hook 2 (the missing absolute value) is stated here.
- `def-pullback-form`: for α: U → V of class C¹ (U ⊆ ℝᵐ, V ⊆ ℝⁿ open) and ω a k-form on V, (α*ω)_p(v₁, …, v_k) = ω_{α(p)}(Dα(p)v₁, …, Dα(p)v_k), i.e. (α*ω)_p = Dα(p)*(ω_{α(p)}) in the sense of `def-linear-pullback`. For k = 0, α*f = f∘α.
- **`thm-pullback-properties`.** Let α: U → V be C^{r+1} and ω, η forms on V of class C^r (r ≥ 0). Then (i) α*ω is of class C^r; (ii) α* is linear and α*(ω ∧ η) = α*ω ∧ α*η; (iii) α*(dy_i) = dα_i; (iv) for β: V → W of class C^{r+1}, (β∘α)* = α*β*. *Proof sketch:* (ii) pointwise from `prp-linear-pullback-wedge`; (iii) Dα(p)v has i-th component dα_i(v); (iv) chain rule D(β∘α)(p) = Dβ(α(p))Dα(p) and (ML)* = L*M*; (i) from the explicit formula of move 3: α*(Σ f_I dy_I) = Σ (f_I∘α) dα_{i₁} ∧ ⋯ ∧ dα_{i_k}, whose coefficients are sums of products of f_I∘α (C^r) and first partials of α (C^r).
- **Worked example (move 3 in full)** `exm-polar-pullback`: α(r, θ) = (r cos θ, r sin θ): α*(dx ∧ dy) = (cos θ dr − r sin θ dθ) ∧ (sin θ dr + r cos θ dθ) = r dr ∧ dθ.
- `exm-angle-form-polar` (finishes §04's verification): α*ω_θ = dθ on (0, ∞) × ℝ. Consequence: on the slit plane, where α restricts to a C^∞ diffeomorphism from (0, ∞) × (−π, π), ω_θ = d(θ∘α⁻¹).
- **`cor-pullback-top-degree`.** For α: U → V with U, V ⊆ ℝⁿ, α*(f dy₁ ∧ ⋯ ∧ dy_n) = (f∘α) · det Dα · dx₁ ∧ ⋯ ∧ dx_n. *Proof:* pointwise `prp-top-degree-det`. **Hook 2 restated:** det, not |det|; AN3.7 explains.
- **`thm-pullback-commutes-d` (key proof 3).** If α: U → V is C² and ω ∈ Ωᵏ_1(V), then α*(dω) = d(α*ω).
  - *Proof route.* k = 0: d(f∘α) = α*(df) is the chain rule (AN3.1). General k: by linearity, ω = f dy_I. Then α*ω = (f∘α) dα_{i₁} ∧ ⋯ ∧ dα_{i_k}; apply (D3) and d(dα_i) = 0 (needs α_i ∈ C², the reason for the C² hypothesis) to get d(α*ω) = d(f∘α) ∧ dα_I = α*(df) ∧ α*(dy_I) = α*(df ∧ dy_I) = α*(dω).
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of "induct with Leibniz and kill d(dα_i)"): on k = 1 in ℝ² first, then the general line.
  - Hypothesis check: α only C¹ makes d(α*ω) undefined in general (coefficients involve Dα, only C⁰).
- `cor-d-coordinate-free`: if α: U → V is a C^∞ diffeomorphism, the coordinate formula for d in the y-coordinates and in the x-coordinates give the same operator: d = (α⁻¹)* ∘ d ∘ α*. One paragraph: this is what lets AM.3 define d on abstract manifolds chart by chart.
- `exm-fold-pullback` (weird row W5): α(x) = x² on ℝ: α*(dy) = 2x dx vanishes at 0 although dy vanishes nowhere. Pullback can create zeros; it never creates non-zero top forms out of nothing when m > n (weird row W6: α: ℝ³ → ℝ², α*(any 3-form) = 0 since Ω³(ℝ²) = 0, yet α*(dy₁ ∧ dy₂) ≠ 0 when α is a submersion).
- **Warning.** α*ω uses ω at α(p), not at p: forgetting to substitute y = α(x) into the coefficients is the standard slip (see B1).
- **Quick check.** For α(u, v) = (uv, u + v), compute α*(dy₁ ∧ dy₂). *Answer:* det [[v, u], [1, 1]] du∧dv = (v − u) du∧dv.
