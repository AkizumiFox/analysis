**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

## Must prove

- sequential characterization of closed sets and of closure
- arbitrary unions / finite intersections of open sets are open
- open subsets of ℝ are countable disjoint unions of open intervals
- compact ⇔ closed and bounded (sequential version, via BW)
- Heine–Borel in ℝ: closed bounded ⇒ every open cover has a finite subcover, and conversely
- nested nonempty compact sets have nonempty intersection
- Lebesgue number lemma for a compact subset of ℝ
- a nonempty perfect set is uncountable
- a subset of ℝ is connected iff it is an interval

## Key examples and counterexamples

- the Cantor set
- a fat Cantor set
- ℚ: neither open nor closed, closure ℝ
- (0,1) covered by (1/n,1) with no finite subcover
- {1/n} ∪ {0} compact, {1/n} not

## Exit checks (C-exercises)
