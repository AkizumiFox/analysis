# Chapter 15: Limits of Functions and Continuity

Directory: `src/ch15-limits-and-continuity/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

*sin, cos, exp appear as provisional examples (flag them; they are constructed in power-series). Quantifier-order drill: continuity at every point vs uniform continuity is set up here and resolved in the next chapter.*

## Planned sections

1. Why continuity needs a definition: Dirichlet's and Thomae's functions
2. Limits of functions at a limit point: ε-δ definition, sequential criterion, divergence criterion, algebra of limits
3. One-sided limits, limits at infinity and infinite limits

5. Classifying discontinuities; monotone functions have only jump discontinuities, and countably many
6. Thomae's function is continuous exactly at the irrationals; the set of discontinuities is an F_σ (optional); no function is continuous exactly on ℚ (optional, via Baire)

## Must prove

- sequential criterion for functional limits
- algebraic limit theorem for functions
- composition of continuous functions is continuous
- f continuous on ℝ iff preimages of open sets are open
- a monotone function has one-sided limits everywhere and at most countably many discontinuities
- Thomae's function is continuous at each irrational and discontinuous at each rational

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Dirichlet function: nowhere continuous
- Thomae function
- x·sin(1/x) and sin(1/x) at 0
- x times the Dirichlet function: continuous only at 0
- a monotone function whose discontinuities are exactly ℚ

## Exit checks (C-exercises)
