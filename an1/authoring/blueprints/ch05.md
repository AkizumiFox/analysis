## Must prove

- convergent ⇒ Cauchy; a Cauchy sequence with a convergent subsequence converges
- ℝⁿ complete (from ℝ)
- closed subset of complete space is complete; complete subspace is closed
- Cantor intersection theorem
- C_b(X) is complete in the sup metric (3ε argument)
- ℓ² is complete
- the L¹ distance on C[0,1] is incomplete
- unique extension of uniformly continuous maps from a dense subset into a complete space
- every metric space has a completion, unique up to isometry fixing X
- the Cauchy-sequence model of ℚ's completion is a complete ordered field with the LUB property

## Key examples and counterexamples

- ℚ, (0,1) incomplete; ℤ and discrete spaces complete
- ℝ with d(x,y)=|arctan x − arctan y| is incomplete though homeomorphic to ℝ
- fₙ piecewise-linear steps: Cauchy in L¹ distance on C[0,1] with no continuous limit
- nested closed balls with empty intersection in a complete space when radii do not shrink (GO.12)
- polynomials are not complete under sup on [0,1]
