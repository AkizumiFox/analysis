# Chapter 18: The Riemann–Darboux Integral

Directory: `src/ch18-riemann-darboux-integral/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

*Placed before sequences-of-functions (Rudin's order), so 'integration commutes with uniform limits' is proved in the next chapter; this crosses Abbott's ABB.6 → ABB.7 edge only through §7.4, see exemptions. Stage S1's 'full Riemann theory'. The Riemann integral on ℝⁿ stays in Part II (decisions.md 10). Lebesgue's criterion is stated and proved in outline; it seeds the 'what the Riemann integral lacks' list that Part III completes. The sup/inf description of the integral is a long-range descendant of the least-upper-bound chapter: recall it.*

## Planned sections

## Must prove

- refinement lemma (L(f,P) ≤ L(f,P′) ≤ U(f,P′) ≤ U(f,P) for P ⊂ P′)
- integrability criterion (∀ε ∃P: U − L < ε)
- continuous ⇒ integrable (via Heine–Cantor)
- monotone ⇒ integrable
- linearity, additivity and monotonicity of the integral
- if f is integrable and φ continuous then φ∘f is integrable
- FTC part 1: F(x) = ∫ₐˣ f is continuous, and differentiable at points where f is continuous
- FTC part 2: ∫ₐᵇ F' = F(b) − F(a) for integrable F'
- integration by parts and substitution
- integral test

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Dirichlet function: not integrable
- Thomae function: integrable with integral 0
- indicator of the Cantor set: integrable (discontinuities of measure zero)
- a bounded derivative that is not Riemann integrable (Volterra; statement only)
- ∫₁^∞ sin x / x converges but not absolutely

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- prove Thomae's function is integrable with integral 0, and the Dirichlet function is not
