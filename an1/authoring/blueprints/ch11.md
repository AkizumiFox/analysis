# AN1.11: The Forms of Completeness

Directory: `src/ch21-forms-of-completeness/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

## Must prove

- LUB ⇒ MCT ⇒ BW ⇒ (Cauchy complete + Archimedean) ⇒ (nested intervals + Archimedean) ⇒ LUB, all in an arbitrary ordered field
- Archimedes follows from LUB but not from Cauchy completeness
- IVT (for all continuous f on intervals of the field) ⇒ LUB
- Heine–Borel ⇒ LUB

## Key examples and counterexamples

- ℚ: Archimedean, not complete
- ℝ(x): non-Archimedean ordered field
- a Cauchy-complete non-Archimedean field (Levi-Civita / GO 1.7)
- the hyperreals: every Cauchy sequence eventually constant, yet no LUB

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- prove BW ⇒ LUB in an arbitrary ordered field
- show that Cauchy completeness alone does not give LUB, and say exactly where Archimedes is used in the converse
- the Stage S1 capstone set: uniform continuity twice, limsup two ways, the pointwise-limit question
