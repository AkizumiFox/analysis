# Chapter 17: The Derivative and the Mean Value Theorem

Directory: `src/ch17-derivative-and-mean-value/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

*Taylor with Lagrange remainder is proved here (Stage S1 (c)); the integral remainder and Taylor series come later. Elementary functions remain provisional examples. The continuous nowhere-differentiable function is deferred to sequences-of-functions, where the M-test is available.*

## Key examples and counterexamples

- x² sin(1/x): differentiable everywhere, derivative discontinuous at 0
- x² sin(1/x) + x/2: f'(0) > 0 but not increasing near 0
- |x| and x^{1/3} at 0
- a function with a jump cannot be a derivative (Darboux)
- g_n(x) = x^n sin(1/x) and how many derivatives exist
