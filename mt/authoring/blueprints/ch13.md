**Volume.** *Measure, Integration, and Real Analysis* (prefix `mt`), on the skeleton of Axler's MIRA 1–10. This chapter is the first half of MIRA 4 (the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function and derivatives of integrals); MT.10 is the second half (BV, AC and the fundamental theorem). Following D4, everything is done concretely on ℝᵈ in the Stein–Shakarchi manner, with balls, before any abstract differentiation of measures (MT.11).

**Purpose.**
- Answer the question every reader of HA.9 and MT.2 has been carrying: *for f ∈ L¹_loc(ℝ), is (d/dx)∫ₐˣ f = f(x) for almost every x?* In ℝᵈ: do the ball averages A_r f(x) converge to f(x) as r → 0, almost everywhere?
- The answer is yes, and the proof is the chapter's one big idea: **prove it on a dense class, then pass to the limit with a single inequality for the supremum over all scales** (the maximal inequality). The covering lemma is the geometry that makes the inequality true.
- Consequences: Lebesgue points, the density theorem (a measurable set is "all or nothing" at almost every point), and a.e. convergence of approximate identities at every Lebesgue point.
- Not here: which functions F are indefinite integrals (MT.10), differentiation of monotone functions (MT.10, which uses this chapter's fine-cover theorem), differentiation of a measure with respect to m and the Radon–Nikodym derivative as a limit of ratios (MT.11), the strong maximal function and Calderón–Zygmund theory (out of scope; one remark).

## Named moves (index.md)

1. **Greedy selection by size** (Vitali): from finitely many balls, repeatedly pick the largest ball disjoint from those already picked; every discarded ball lies in the 3-fold enlargement of a picked one.
2. **Covering to weak type**: to bound m({Mf > λ}), cover a compact subset by balls on which the average exceeds λ, select disjoint ones, and add up.
3. **Dense class plus maximal inequality** (the chapter's main move; faded triple): for an operator family T_r with T_r g → g for g in a dense class, write limsup|T_r f − f| ≤ sup_r|T_r(f − g)| + |g − f| and control the first term by a weak-type maximal bound.
4. **Split at height λ/2** (Marcinkiewicz): f = f·1_{|f|>λ/2} + f·1_{|f|≤λ/2}; the bounded piece cannot push Mf above λ/2, so only the tall piece matters; integrate over λ with the layer-cake formula.
5. **Majorise a kernel by averages**: a radially decreasing integrable kernel is a positive combination (an integral) of normalised ball indicators, so its convolution is dominated by ‖Φ‖₁·Mf.

## 01-averages.md — Averages and the differentiation question (budget ≤ 3,000 main-line words)

- **Opening frame.** The hook; the ℝ statement F'(x) = f(x) rewritten as "(1/2h)∫_{x−h}^{x+h} f → f(x)" (symmetric version) and then as ball averages in ℝᵈ.
- `def-ball-average` A_r f(x). Explain: ℝᵈ has no "∫ₐˣ", but balls are its intervals.
- `prp-average-continuity-points`: f locally integrable and continuous at x ⇒ A_r f(x) → f(x). Proof: |A_r f(x) − f(x)| ≤ sup_{B(x,r)} |f − f(x)|. This is HA.9's FTC proof in ℝᵈ dress; say so.
- `prp-average-continuous-in-x` (fixed r, f ∈ L¹_loc ⇒ x ↦ A_r f(x) continuous; via m(B(x,r) Δ B(y,r)) → 0 and absolute continuity of the integral) and `prp-average-l1-contraction` (‖A_r f‖₁ ≤ ‖f‖₁ by Tonelli; the kernel is 1_{B(0,r)}/(v_d rᵈ), an approximate identity of MT.7).
- `prp-average-l1-convergence`: A_r f → f in L¹ as r → 0 (MT.7's approximate-identity theorem with the normalised ball indicator). Corollary in prose: along some sequence r_k → 0, A_{r_k} f → f a.e. (MT.8).
- **The obstruction (failure that motivates §03).** A subsequence is not enough: the question is about *every* r → 0 at once. Try the dense-class argument with only the fixed-r bound ‖A_r h‖₁ ≤ ‖h‖₁: with g ∈ C_c, ‖f − g‖₁ < δ,
  limsup_{r→0}|A_r f − f| ≤ limsup_{r→0}|A_r(f − g)| + 0 + |g − f|,
  and the middle term is a limsup over uncountably many r of functions each of which is small *in L¹*. No bound on a single A_r controls the limsup. What is needed is a bound on sup_r |A_r h| in terms of ‖h‖₁. Name it now (the maximal function), define it in §03.
- **Scratch Work** (first appearance of the dense-class template): the three-term split written out with the hole marked "need: m({sup_r A_r|h| > α}) ≤ C‖h‖₁/α". The proof proper is in §04.
- **Warning.** "A_r f → f in L¹" says nothing pointwise; the typewriter sequence of MT.8 is the reminder.
- **Quick check.** f = 1_[0,∞) on ℝ: compute A_r f(0) for each r. *Answer:* ½ for every r; so lim A_r f(0) = ½ ≠ f(0) = 1, on a null set.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Compute A_r f(x) for f(x) = x² on ℝ and every x, r; check lim_{r→0} = f(x). *Answer:* (1/2r)∫_{x−r}^{x+r} t² dt = x² + r²/3.
  - A2. Prove `prp-average-continuity-points` for ℝᵈ. *Answer:* as in the text, with the sup over B(x,r) tending to 0 by continuity.
  - B1. Prove `prp-average-continuous-in-x`. *Answer:* |A_r f(x) − A_r f(y)| ≤ (v_d rᵈ)^{−1}∫_{B(x,r) Δ B(y,r)} |f|, and m(B(x,r) Δ B(y,r)) → 0 as y → x (DCT on indicators), so absolute continuity of the integral over the compact B(x, r + 1) finishes.
  - B2. Prove ‖A_r f‖₁ ≤ ‖f‖₁ with equality when f ≥ 0. *Answer:* Tonelli: ∫∫ 1_{|x−y|<r} |f(y)| dy dx/(v_d rᵈ) = ‖f‖₁.
  - C1. Let f ∈ L¹(ℝᵈ). Show there are r_k ↓ 0 with A_{r_k} f → f a.e., and explain in two sentences why this does not prove that A_r f → f a.e. *Answer:* `prp-average-l1-convergence` plus MT.8's subsequence theorem; the exceptional null set depends on the sequence, and there are uncountably many ways for r to tend to 0, so the union of exceptional sets is not controlled.

## 02-covering.md — Covering lemmas (budget ≤ 4,000 main-line words)

- **Opening frame.** To bound m({sup_r A_r|f| > λ}) we will know, around each point of the set, *some* ball on which |f| has a large average. The balls overlap arbitrarily; summing over them overcounts. A covering lemma extracts disjoint balls that still carry a fixed fraction of the total measure.
- `lem-vitali-finite` (3ᵈ lemma). Finitely many balls B₁,…,B_N in ℝᵈ contain a disjoint subfamily B_{i_1},…,B_{i_k} with ⋃ B_j ⊂ ⋃ 3B_{i_l}, hence m(⋃ B_j) ≤ 3ᵈ Σ m(B_{i_l}).
  - **Scratch Work** (greedy selection, first appearance): picture in ℝ with three intervals; why "largest first" matters (a small ball picked first can block a large one that it does not engulf); the triangle inequality step: if B meets a picked ball B' of radius r' ≥ r, then B ⊂ 3B'. Then the clean proof.
  - Remark: 3 cannot be replaced by 2 for balls in ℝ (exercise A2).
- `lem-vitali-countable` (5r lemma, for MT.10/MT.11 and the fine-cover theorem): any family of balls with sup of radii < ∞ has a countable disjoint subfamily 𝒢 with ⋃_{all} B ⊂ ⋃_{B∈𝒢} 5B. Proof sketch: sort by dyadic radius classes (R/2^{k+1}, R/2^k], choose maximal disjoint subfamilies class by class (countability: disjoint open sets in ℝᵈ), the factor 5 comes from radius ratio ≤ 2. Within a class, a maximal disjoint subfamily is chosen inductively (disjoint open sets in ℝᵈ form a countable family, so no choice principle beyond countable choice is needed). S2a: decide whether to give the full proof or a proof for families of balls whose union has finite measure, which is all that is used.
- **Failure-motivated definition.** For differentiating monotone functions (MT.10) and measures (MT.11) we cannot choose the radius; the balls are handed to us, one family of arbitrarily small balls at each point. `def-fine-cover` (Vitali cover): a family 𝒱 of closed balls such that every x ∈ E lies in balls of 𝒱 of arbitrarily small radius.
- `thm-vitali-covering-fine`: if m*(E) < ∞ and 𝒱 is a fine cover of E, there are countably many disjoint B_j ∈ 𝒱 with m(E ∖ ⋃ B_j) = 0; moreover for every ε > 0 finitely many with m*(E ∖ ⋃_{j≤N} B_j) < ε. Proof sketch: put E inside an open U of finite measure (MT.1), discard balls not inside U, apply `lem-vitali-countable`; if the tail Σ_{j>N} m(B_j) is small, every point of E not covered by B₁…B_N lies in a ball of 𝒱 disjoint from them, which must meet some B_j with j > N of comparable radius, so it lies in ⋃_{j>N} 5B_j, of measure ≤ 5ᵈ Σ_{j>N} m(B_j) → 0.
- `exm-eccentric-rectangles` (weird example): in ℝ² the crosses H_n = [−n,n]×[−1/n,1/n], V_n its transpose, n = 1,…,N. Every two meet at the origin, so a disjoint subfamily has one member, of area 4; the union has area at least 4H_N ≥ 4 log N (H_N the harmonic number). No covering lemma with a fixed constant holds for rectangles of unbounded eccentricity. Remark (no proof): this is why differentiation along arbitrary shrinking rectangles fails for some f ∈ L¹(ℝ²), and why the theory below uses balls or sets comparable to balls (`def-regular-family`).
- **Warning.** The finite lemma is about finitely many balls; the countable lemma needs bounded radii (exercise B2).
- **Quick check.** Run the greedy selection on (0,2), (1,3), (2,4), (2.5, 3). *Answer:* all of the first three have radius 1; pick (0,2), discard (1,3); pick (2,4); (2.5,3) meets (2,4). Check (1,3) ⊂ 3·(0,2) = (−2,4) and (2.5,3) ⊂ (0,6).
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Show that the 3-fold enlargement of a selected ball contains every ball of no larger radius that meets it. *Answer:* triangle inequality: |y − x'| ≤ |y − x| + |x − z| + |z − x'| < r + r + r' ≤ 3r' for z in the intersection.
  - A2. Show that factor 2 does not suffice in ℝ: take B₁ = (−1,1), B₂ = (0.9, 2.9), selection picks B₁ (equal radii, either may be picked; the ordering is part of the data). *Answer:* 2B₁ = (−2,2) misses (2, 2.9) ⊂ B₂; 3B₁ = (−3,3) contains B₂.
  - B1. Prove `lem-vitali-countable` assuming the union of the family has finite measure. *Answer:* sketch above: dyadic classes, at each stage a maximal disjoint subfamily of balls disjoint from those picked; finite measure makes each stage's selection countable (in fact the tail of radii summable), radius ratio ≤ 2 gives factor 5.
  - B2. The family B(0,n), n ≥ 1, in ℝᵈ: show no disjoint subfamily 𝒢 has ⋃_n B(0,n) ⊂ ⋃_{B∈𝒢} cB for any c. *Answer:* 𝒢 has one ball B(0,N); cB(0,N) = B(0,cN) misses B(0,cN+1).
  - B3. Compute the area of ⋃_{n≤N} H_n in `exm-eccentric-rectangles` exactly. *Answer:* for 1/(k+1) ≤ |y| < 1/k the widest strip has half-width k, so the area is 4Σ_{k=1}^{N−1} k(1/k − 1/(k+1)) + 4 = 4H_N.
  - C1. Prove `thm-vitali-covering-fine`. *Answer:* the sketch above in full; the key line is m*(E ∖ ⋃_{j≤N} B_j) ≤ 5ᵈ Σ_{j>N} m(B_j).
  - C2. Prove the finite Vitali lemma with axis-parallel cubes in place of balls and constant 3ᵈ. *Answer:* identical: a cube of side s meeting a cube of side s' ≥ s lies in the concentric cube of side 3s'.

## 03-maximal-function.md — The Hardy–Littlewood maximal function (budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words)

- **Opening frame.** The hole left in §01's Scratch Work: a bound for sup_r A_r|h|.
- `def-maximal-function` Mf; `def-uncentred-maximal-function` M̃f. **Failure-motivated:** §01 showed every fixed-r bound is useless for limits as r → 0; the supremum over all scales is the smallest object that sees all of them at once.
- `prp-maximal-basic`: M(f + g) ≤ Mf + Mg, M(cf) = |c|Mf, Mf ≤ ‖f‖_∞, and Mf(x) ≥ |f(x)| at every point where A_r|f|(x) → |f(x)| (used after §04). `prp-uncentred-comparable`: Mf ≤ M̃f ≤ 2ᵈMf.
- `prp-maximal-lsc`: {Mf > λ} is open (each A_r|f| is continuous in x by `prp-average-continuous-in-x`; a sup of continuous functions is lower semicontinuous). So Mf is Borel measurable.
- `exm-maximal-indicator-interval` (weird example): f = 1_[0,1] on ℝ. Mf = 1 on (0,1), ½ at 0 and 1, and Mf(x) = 1/(2x) for x ≥ 1, 1/(2(1 − x)) for x ≤ 0 (optimise the ratio over r: it increases in r until the ball reaches the far end). So Mf ∉ L¹(ℝ).
- `prp-maximal-not-integrable`: if f ∈ L¹(ℝᵈ) and f ≠ 0 on a set of positive measure, then Mf(x) ≥ c|x|^{−d} for |x| large; so M is never bounded L¹ → L¹. **Failure-motivated definition**: since strong (1,1) fails, measure size by the distribution function. `def-weak-type`: T is of weak type (1,1) if m({|Tf| > λ}) ≤ C‖f‖₁/λ (recall MT.8's weak L¹; Chebyshev shows strong ⇒ weak).
- `thm-maximal-weak-11` (Hardy–Littlewood maximal inequality): m({Mf > λ}) ≤ 3ᵈ‖f‖₁/λ.
  - **Scratch Work** (covering to weak type, first appearance): we cannot cover the open set {Mf > λ} by finitely many balls, but by inner regularity (MT.1) it suffices to bound m(K) for compact K ⊂ {Mf > λ}; each x ∈ K has a ball B_x with ∫_{B_x}|f| > λ m(B_x); compactness gives finitely many; `lem-vitali-finite` gives disjoint ones; add up.
  - Proof in that order; then remark the same proof with M̃ and the countable lemma.
- `thm-maximal-lp`: for 1 < p ≤ ∞, ‖Mf‖_p ≤ C_{d,p}‖f‖_p with C_{d,p} = 2(3ᵈp/(p − 1))^{1/p} for p < ∞.
  - **Scratch Work** (split at height λ/2, first appearance): f₁ = f·1_{|f|>λ/2}; Mf ≤ Mf₁ + λ/2, so {Mf > λ} ⊂ {Mf₁ > λ/2}; apply weak (1,1) to f₁ ∈ L¹ (Hölder or Chebyshev show f₁ ∈ L¹ when f ∈ Lᵖ); then layer cake (MT.8) and Tonelli: ‖Mf‖_p^p = p∫₀^∞ λ^{p−1} m({Mf > λ}) dλ ≤ p∫₀^∞ λ^{p−2}·2·3ᵈ ∫_{|f|>λ/2}|f| dλ, swap, integrate λ^{p−2} over (0, 2|f|).
  - Note the constant blows up as p → 1, as `exm-maximal-indicator-interval` forces.
- `thm-marcinkiewicz-diagonal` (stated and proved, short): a sublinear T that is weak (1,1) and bounded on L^∞ is bounded on Lᵖ, 1 < p < ∞. The proof is the Scratch Work above with M replaced by T; `thm-maximal-lp` becomes a corollary. Forward pointer (prose): the off-diagonal version and Riesz–Thorin are outside this volume.
- **Remark** (FA.4, see): uniform boundedness for a family of operators is the norm-level analogue of what a maximal inequality does for a.e. convergence.
- **Warning.** Mf is not Lebesgue-integrable even for the nicest nonzero f; a draft that writes ∫Mf has gone wrong.
- **Quick check.** For f = 1_[0,1] compute m({Mf > λ}) for 0 < λ < ½ and compare with 3/λ. *Answer:* {Mf > λ} = (1 − 1/(2λ), 1/(2λ)), of length 1/λ − 1 < 3/λ.

## 04-lebesgue-differentiation.md — The Lebesgue differentiation theorem (budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words)

- **Opening frame.** The §01 template with the hole filled.
- `thm-lebesgue-differentiation`: for f ∈ L¹_loc(ℝᵈ), A_r f(x) → f(x) as r → 0 for a.e. x.
  - **Faded triple, item 1 (worked in full).** Reduce to f ∈ L¹ (multiply by 1_{B(0,N)}; locality of A_r for r < 1). Set Ωf(x) ≔ limsup_{r→0}|A_r f(x) − f(x)|. For g ∈ C_c, Ωg = 0 (`prp-average-continuity-points`) and Ωf ≤ Ω(f − g) ≤ M(f − g) + |f − g|. So m({Ωf > 2α}) ≤ m({M(f−g) > α}) + m({|f − g| > α}) ≤ (3ᵈ + 1)‖f − g‖₁/α, arbitrarily small. Hence Ωf = 0 a.e.
  - Name the move in a box: **dense class plus maximal inequality**.
- `cor-indefinite-integral-derivative` (hook paid): for f ∈ L¹_loc(ℝ) and F(x) = ∫ₐˣ f, F'(x) exists and equals f(x) for a.e. x. Proof: one-sided quotients (1/h)∫_x^{x+h} f are averages over intervals inside B(x,|h|) of half its length; apply `thm-differentiation-regular-families` (or prove directly from the Lebesgue-point statement of §05 — choose at S2a; the cleanest order is to state this corollary after `thm-differentiation-regular-families` within this section, using the Lebesgue-point form proved in §05 only by forward pointer? **Decision:** prove `thm-differentiation-regular-families` here from `thm-lebesgue-differentiation` applied to |f − q| for rational q, which is the Lebesgue-point argument; §05 then names it). Close with the 1_K example of the hook: F' = 1_K a.e.
- `def-regular-family` (failure-motivated: the FTC needs one-sided intervals, which are not centred balls; `exm-eccentric-rectangles` shows "any shrinking sets" is too much). A family {E_r} shrinks regularly to x if E_r ⊂ B(x,r) and m(E_r) ≥ c·m(B(x,r)) for a fixed c > 0.
- `thm-differentiation-regular-families`: for f ∈ L¹_loc, at a.e. x, (1/m(E_r))∫_{E_r}|f − f(x)| → 0 for every family shrinking regularly to x. Proof: the bound (1/m(E_r))∫_{E_r}|f − f(x)| ≤ c^{−1} A_r|f − f(x)|(x), plus the a.e. statement A_r|f − f(x)|(x) → 0 (proved here via rationals: apply `thm-lebesgue-differentiation` to |f − q| for each q ∈ ℚ, take the countable union of null sets, approximate f(x) by q).
- `cor-vanishing-on-cubes`: if ∫_Q f = 0 for every cube Q, then f = 0 a.e.
- **Planted for MT.10.** The Cantor function c: c' = 0 a.e., c(1) − c(0) = 1. So "d/dx then ∫" fails; which F satisfy F(x) − F(a) = ∫ₐˣ F'? Stated, not answered.
- **Planted for MT.11.** For ν = f·m, A_r f(x) = ν(B(x,r))/m(B(x,r)). Compute this ratio for ν = δ₀ at x = 0 (→ ∞) and x ≠ 0 (→ 0). Stated, not answered.
- **Warning.** The theorem is about a.e. x; at a given point anything can happen (the A1 example).
- **Quick check.** Where does lim A_r f(x) = f(x) fail for f = 1_[0,1] on ℝ? *Answer:* exactly at 0 and 1 (limit ½).
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. For f = 1_ℚ on ℝ, compute lim_{r→0} A_r f(x) for every x and identify the exceptional set. *Answer:* A_r f ≡ 0, so the limit is 0; exceptional set ℚ, null.
  - A2. Deduce `cor-vanishing-on-cubes` from `thm-differentiation-regular-families`. *Answer:* cubes centred at x of side 2r/√d lie in B(x,r) with volume ratio depending only on d; averages are 0, so f(x) = 0 a.e.
  - B1. ★ (Faded triple, item 2: last step blank.) Let f ∈ L¹(ℝ). Show (1/h)∫_x^{x+h} f → f(x) a.e. as h ↓ 0, directly by the template: the dense class C_c, the bound sup_{h>0}(1/h)∫_x^{x+h}|g| ≤ 2Mg(x), and the split are given; *Name the move, then complete it.* *Answer:* m({Ω'f > 2α}) ≤ m({2M(f−g) > α}) + m({|f−g| > α}) ≤ (2·3 + 1)‖f−g‖₁/α.
  - B2. Show that if f ∈ L¹_loc(ℝ) and F(x) = ∫₀ˣ f is identically 0, then f = 0 a.e. *Answer:* F' = f a.e. by `cor-indefinite-integral-derivative`.
  - B3. For f ∈ L¹(ℝᵈ), show |f(x)| ≤ Mf(x) for a.e. x. *Answer:* A_r|f|(x) → |f(x)| a.e. and A_r|f| ≤ Mf.
  - C1. ★ Prove `thm-lebesgue-differentiation` from `thm-maximal-weak-11`, writing out the reduction from L¹_loc to L¹. *Answer:* as in the text.
  - C2. Let K ⊂ [0,1] be a fat Cantor set and F(x) = ∫₀ˣ 1_K. Show F is non-decreasing, constant on each complementary interval of K, not constant on any interval meeting K in positive measure, and F'(x) = 1 for a.e. x ∈ K. *Answer:* the integrand vanishes on the gaps; F' = 1_K a.e. by `cor-indefinite-integral-derivative`.
  - C3. Prove that the conclusion of `thm-lebesgue-differentiation` holds with the uncentred family of all balls containing x of radius < r. *Answer:* those balls shrink regularly to x with c = 2^{−d}.

## 05-lebesgue-points.md — Lebesgue points and density (budget ≤ 4,000 main-line words)

- **Opening frame.** §04 proved more than it stated: the averages of |f − f(x)|, not just of f − f(x), go to 0. That stronger property is what later chapters use.
- `def-lebesgue-point`, `def-lebesgue-set`: x is a Lebesgue point of f if A_r|f − f(x)|(x) → 0. **Failure-motivated:** `exm-symmetric-average-not-lebesgue-point`: for 1_[0,∞) at 0, A_r f(0) = ½ for all r, so "averages converge to a value" holds; but A_r|f − c|(0) = (|1 − c| + |c|)/2 ≥ ½ for every c, so no redefinition makes 0 a Lebesgue point. Symmetric convergence is fragile (a one-sided average gives 1 or 0); the Lebesgue-point condition is what survives one-sided averages and kernels (§06).
- `prp-continuity-points-lebesgue`; `thm-lebesgue-points-ae` (a.e. point is a Lebesgue point; the rational-q argument of §04 named).
- `def-density`, `def-density-point`: D_E(x) ≔ lim m(E ∩ B(x,r))/m(B(x,r)) when it exists.
- `thm-lebesgue-density`: for measurable E, D_E = 1 a.e. on E and D_E = 0 a.e. on ℝᵈ ∖ E. Proof: Lebesgue points of 1_E. Littlewood's first principle (MT.1: a measurable set is nearly a finite union of cubes) in pointwise form: near almost every point a measurable set looks full or empty.
- `exm-density-oscillates` (weird example): E = ⋃_{k≥0}[4^{−k}/2, 4^{−k}] ⊂ ℝ. At r = 4^{−j} the ratio is 1/3; at r = 4^{−j}/2 it is 1/6. So D_E(0) does not exist; the theorem allows this since {0} is null.
- `exm-fat-cantor-density` (hook paid for AN1.4): a fat Cantor set K contains no interval, yet D_K = 1 at a.e. point of K.
- `cor-no-half-dense-set`: no measurable E ⊂ ℝᵈ satisfies δ·m(B) ≤ m(E ∩ B) ≤ (1 − δ)m(B) for all balls B (some δ > 0).
- `cor-approximate-continuity`: a measurable f is approximately continuous at a.e. x: for every ε > 0, {y : |f(y) − f(x)| < ε} has density 1 at x. (Proof for f ∈ L¹_loc from Lebesgue points and Chebyshev; general measurable f by composing with arctan.)
- **Warning.** A Lebesgue point is a property of the function, not of its a.e.-class, at a given point; the Lebesgue set of two a.e.-equal functions can differ, but only on a null set.
- **Quick check.** What is D_E(0) for the open quadrant E = {x > 0, y > 0} ⊂ ℝ²? *Answer:* ¼ for every r, so D_E(0) = ¼; 0 is in neither "a.e." clause because it is a single point.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Prove `prp-continuity-points-lebesgue`. *Answer:* A_r|f − f(x)|(x) ≤ sup_{B(x,r)}|f − f(x)|.
  - A2. Verify the two ratios in `exm-density-oscillates`. *Answer:* m(E ∩ (0, 4^{−j})) = Σ_{k≥j} 4^{−k}/2 = (2/3)4^{−j}, divided by 2·4^{−j}; m(E ∩ (0, 4^{−j}/2)) = (2/3)4^{−j−1}, divided by 4^{−j}.
  - A3. Show that 1_ℚ has every point of ℝ ∖ ℚ as a Lebesgue point and no point of ℚ. *Answer:* A_r|1_ℚ − c|(x) = |c|, which is 0 iff c = 0.
  - B1. Prove `thm-lebesgue-points-ae` in detail. *Answer:* for q ∈ ℚ, A_r|f − q|(x) → |f(x) − q| off a null set N_q; off ⋃ N_q and given ε pick q with |f(x) − q| < ε; then limsup A_r|f − f(x)| ≤ 2ε.
  - B2. Show the density point set of E differs from E by a null set, and deduce: if E is measurable and D_E(x) exists and lies in (0,1) at every x of a set A, then m(A) = 0.
  - B3. Prove `cor-approximate-continuity` for f ∈ L¹_loc. *Answer:* m({y ∈ B(x,r): |f(y) − f(x)| ≥ ε}) ≤ ε^{−1}∫_{B(x,r)}|f − f(x)| = o(m(B(x,r))) at Lebesgue points.
  - C1. ★ Prove `cor-no-half-dense-set`. *Answer:* by the density theorem D_E ∈ {0,1} a.e.; the hypothesis forces D_E (where it exists) into [δ, 1 − δ]; if m(E) > 0 contradiction on E, if m(E) = 0 the lower bound fails for any ball.
  - C2. Steinhaus via density: if m(E) > 0 then E − E contains a ball around 0. *Answer:* choose a density point x and r with m(E ∩ B(x,r)) > ¾m(B(x,r)); for |h| small, m(B(x,r) ∩ (B(x,r) + h)) > ½·… then m(E ∩ B) + m((E ∩ B) + h) > m(B ∪ (B + h)) once m(B ∪ (B + h)) < 3/2·m(B), so the two sets meet and h ∈ E − E.

## 06-approximate-identities.md — Approximate identities at Lebesgue points (budget ≤ 3,500 main-line words)

- **Opening frame.** MT.7 proved f * K_ε → f in Lᵖ norm and postponed the pointwise question. Ball averages are the special kernel 1_{B(0,1)}/v_d; the question is which other kernels behave.
- `def-radial-majorant`: K ∈ L¹(ℝᵈ), ∫K = 1, |K| ≤ Φ with Φ radial, decreasing in |x|, integrable.
- `lem-radial-majorant-maximal`: sup_{ε>0}|f * K_ε(x)| ≤ ‖Φ‖₁·Mf(x).
  - **Scratch Work** (majorise a kernel by averages, first appearance): first for Φ a finite positive combination of ball indicators Σ a_j 1_{B(0,r_j)}: then |f|*Φ_ε(x) = Σ a_j v_d r_jᵈ·A_{εr_j}|f|(x) ≤ (Σ a_j v_d r_jᵈ)Mf(x) = ‖Φ‖₁Mf(x). A radial decreasing Φ is an increasing limit of such combinations (layer-cake in the radial variable); MCT.
- `thm-approx-identity-lebesgue-points`: if K has a radial majorant and x is a Lebesgue point of f ∈ Lᵖ (1 ≤ p ≤ ∞), then f * K_ε(x) → f(x).
  - **Faded triple, item 3 (last half blank).** The text gives: |f*K_ε(x) − f(x)| ≤ ∫|f(x − y) − f(x)|Φ_ε(y) dy; split the integral into |y| < δ and |y| ≥ δ; *Name the move, then complete it* (the inner part by the Lebesgue-point condition with the radial layer-cake of the Scratch Work; the outer part by ‖Φ‖_{L¹(|y|≥δ/ε)} → 0 and the size of f). The full proof is the C1 answer.
- `cor-approx-identity-ae`: f * K_ε → f a.e.
- `exm-heat-kernel`: G(x) = (4π)^{−d/2}e^{−|x|²/4}; G_{√t} * f solves the heat equation and tends to f a.e. as t → 0. `exm-poisson-half-space`: P(x) = c_d(1 + |x|²)^{−(d+1)/2}; u(x,y) = P_y * f → f(x) at every Lebesgue point as y → 0 (vertical approach only; non-tangential approach as a remark). Pointers (see): PDE branch, HA.10 (Fourier inversion by Gauss summability).
- **Remark (see HA.2).** On 𝕋 the Fejér and Poisson kernels have periodic radial majorants; the same proof gives a.e. convergence of Fejér and Abel means of f ∈ L¹(𝕋). The exercise below is self-contained; HA.2 is not needed.
- **Warning.** The theorem gives convergence at Lebesgue points, not at every point of continuity of K_ε * f's limit; and it needs a majorant, not just ∫|K| < ∞ (remark only: for general L¹ kernels a.e. convergence can fail; no proof).
- **Quick check.** Is K = ½·1_{[−1,1]} covered by the theorem? What is f * K_ε then? *Answer:* yes (it is its own radial majorant); f * K_ε = A_ε f, so the theorem contains `thm-lebesgue-differentiation` on ℝ.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Check that the heat kernel G has ∫G = 1 and is its own radial majorant. *Answer:* Gaussian integral in each coordinate (MT.4/MT.5); e^{−|x|²/4} decreases in |x|.
  - A2. Check that P(x) = 1/(π(1 + x²)) on ℝ has ∫P = 1, and write P_y. *Answer:* arctan; P_y(x) = y/(π(x² + y²)).
  - B1. Prove `lem-radial-majorant-maximal`. *Answer:* the Scratch Work with MCT.
  - B2. (Self-contained Fejér exercise; see HA.2.) Given the bound 0 ≤ F_N(t) ≤ min{N + 1, π²/((N + 1)t²)} for |t| ≤ π and (1/2π)∫F_N = 1, show σ_N f(x) → f(x) at every Lebesgue point of f ∈ L¹(𝕋). *Answer:* the majorant Φ_N(t) = min{N+1, π²/((N+1)t²)} is a dilate (ε = 1/(N+1)) of an even decreasing integrable function on the line; apply the proof of `thm-approx-identity-lebesgue-points` on (−π, π).
  - B3. For the boundary function f = 1_[0,1] on ℝ, compute the Poisson integral u(x,y) and check u(x,y) → f(x) as y → 0 for x ∉ {0,1}, and → ½ at x = 0. *Answer:* u = (1/π)[arctan((1 − x)/y) + arctan(x/y)].
  - C1. ★ Prove `thm-approx-identity-lebesgue-points` (completes faded item 3). *Answer:* with ψ(r) ≔ ∫_{B(0,r)}|f(x − y) − f(x)|dy = o(rᵈ), integrate by parts in the radial variable against Φ_ε for the inner part; the outer part is ≤ (‖f‖_p‖Φ_ε 1_{|y|≥δ}‖_{p'} + |f(x)|‖Φ 1_{|y|≥δ/ε}‖₁) → 0.
  - C2. Prove that for f ∈ Lᵖ(ℝ), 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞, the Poisson integral u(x,y) tends to f(x) as y ↓ 0 for a.e. x. *Answer:* `cor-approx-identity-ae` with P.
