**Part IV, Measure and Integration.** Starting from Part III's list of what the Riemann integral lacks, build Lebesgue measure and the Lebesgue integral on ℝᵈ, then abstract measures, products (Fubini–Tonelli and change of variables in full), surfaces and the divergence theorem, Lᵖ, modes of convergence, differentiation, and signed measures up to Radon–Nikodym, ending with probability on measure spaces (the laws of large numbers and the central limit theorem). Lusin and the density of C_c use only the metric Urysohn and Tietze theorems of Part II.

## Planned sections

1. Uniqueness of translation-invariant Radon measures on ℝᵈ; m(TE) = |det T| m(E) for linear T
2. Lipschitz and C¹ maps send null sets to null sets and measurable sets to measurable sets
3. The C¹ change-of-variables theorem: full proof (local linearization on small cubes, then monotone convergence)
4. Polar coordinates: the surface measure σ on S^{d−1} with m = ρ × σ; volume of the unit ball; the Gaussian integral
5. Applications: integrability of |x|^{−α} near 0 and at ∞, radial functions, recovering Part II's Riemann change of variables
6. (Optional) The Brunn–Minkowski inequality

## Key examples and counterexamples

- |x|^{−α} ∈ L¹(B₁) iff α < d
- a continuous injective image of a null set with positive measure (Cantor function variant) showing C¹/Lipschitz is needed
- spherical coordinates in ℝ³
