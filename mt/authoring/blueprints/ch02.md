## Chapter hook and where it is paid

- **Hook (index.md).** HA.9 ended with a list. Its first item: the indicator of the rationals in [0, 1] is an increasing limit of indicators of finite sets, each with Riemann integral 0, and the limit has no Riemann integral at all. Every sensible answer says "0, because ℚ ∩ [0, 1] has length 0". HA.9's Arzelà theorem rescues the case where the limit happens to be Riemann integrable, and says nothing otherwise.
  - The chapter asks two questions. **(Q1)** What does "length" (volume in ℝᵈ) mean for a set as ragged as ℚ ∩ [0, 1] or the fat Cantor set of HA.9, so that it is countably additive? **(Q2)** Which functions can such a length integrate?
  - **Paid:** ℚ ∩ [0, 1] is null in §01 (`exm-countable-null`); countable additivity in §02 (`thm-countable-additivity`); the fat Cantor set's measure in §03 (`exm-fat-cantor-measure`); Q2's answer (measurable functions) in §05; the full payment of Arzelà is **MT.2** (volume ch. 3), whose bounded convergence theorem is proved from Egorov (`thm-egorov`, §06). index.md says this last payment in one forward sentence (no `@ref`).
- **Second hook (§01, end).** "Could every subset of ℝ have a length?" §01 builds m_* on all sets; §02 restricts to measurable sets without yet saying why; **paid in §04** (`thm-vitali-nonmeasurable`, `cor-outer-not-additive`).
- **Littlewood's three principles** are stated informally at the opening of §06 and each is pointed to its payment: sets (§03, `thm-finite-cube-approximation`), functions (`thm-luzin`), convergence (`thm-egorov`).

## Named moves (index.md, "Named moves")

- **The ε/2ᵏ budget.** To control a countable family, spend ε/2ᵏ on the k-th member; the total spent is ≤ ε. First appearance with Scratch Work: `thm-outer-subadditive`.
- **Cover, compactify, grid.** A countable open cover of a compact set has a finite subcover; a finite union of rectangles can be cut along a common grid into almost disjoint pieces. First appearance with Scratch Work: `thm-outer-measure-cube`.
- **Outside open, inside closed.** A measurable set is squeezed between a closed set and an open set whose difference is ε-small; prove a property for the two and pass to the set. First appearance: `thm-closed-approximation`.
- **Throw away a small set.** Remove a set of measure ≤ ε on which the bad behaviour lives; get uniform (or continuous) behaviour on the rest. First appearance with Scratch Work: `thm-egorov`.

## 01-exterior-measure.md — Exterior measure

Budget ≤ 4,500 words.

- **Opening frame.** Recall HA.9: Jordan content \( c(E) \) is the infimum of total volumes of **finite** covers by rectangles. It is only finitely additive, and ℚ ∩ [0, 1] has outer Jordan content 1 (any finite union of closed intervals covering it covers [0, 1]).
- **Failure-motivated definition.** Two repairs suggest themselves. "Inner content plus outer content" keeps finite covers and fails on ℚ ∩ [0, 1] (inner 0, outer 1). "Allow countably many cubes" makes ℚ ∩ [0, 1] cost nothing: spend ε/2ᵏ on the k-th rational. The second repair is the one that will be countably additive; define it.
- **Preliminaries on cubes** (SS3 §1 technique, our own write-up):
  - `def-closed-rectangle` (with volume \( \abs{R} \)), `def-closed-cube`, `def-almost-disjoint` (interiors disjoint).
  - `lem-rectangle-grid`: if a closed rectangle R is the union of finitely many almost disjoint closed rectangles \( R_1,\dots,R_N \), then \( \abs{R} = \sum \abs{R_k} \); if \( R \subseteq \bigcup_{k\le N} R_k \) (not necessarily almost disjoint), then \( \abs{R} \le \sum \abs{R_k} \). *Route:* extend all edges to a common grid; each grid cell has the product volume; count cells (AN3.4's partition argument, cited in prose; a five-line proof is given so nothing hides).
  - `thm-open-set-cubes`: every open \( O \subseteq \nR \) is a countable union of disjoint open intervals; every open \( O \subseteq \nR^d \) is a countable union of almost disjoint closed cubes. *Route (d ≥ 1):* dyadic cubes of side \( 2^{-n} \); keep those inside O and not inside an already kept larger cube. Countability from F2.3.
- **Definition** `def-exterior-measure`: \( m_*(E) = \inf \sum_{j\ge 1} \abs{Q_j} \) over all countable covers \( E \subseteq \bigcup_j Q_j \) by closed cubes. Values in \( [0, \infty] \); define it on **every** subset of ℝᵈ.
  - Quick check: why does \( m_* \) not change if finite covers are also allowed? (Pad with degenerate cubes, or note a finite cover is a countable one.) Why does it change if *only* finite covers are allowed? (ℚ ∩ [0, 1].)
- **Examples.**
  - `exm-countable-null`: a point has \( m_* = 0 \); every countable set has \( m_* = 0 \); in particular \( m_*(\nQ \cap [0,1]) = 0 \) (pays the first part of the hook). Full ε/2ᵏ argument.
  - `exm-cantor-null`: the Cantor set of AN1.4 has \( m_* = 0 \): \( C \subseteq C_n \), a union of \( 2^n \) intervals of length \( 3^{-n} \), so \( m_*(C) \le (2/3)^n \).
  - Exterior measure of an open cube and of ℝᵈ (= ∞), after `thm-outer-measure-cube`.
- **Theorem** `thm-outer-measure-cube`: for a closed cube Q, \( m_*(Q) = \abs{Q} \). Same for a closed rectangle and its interior (`cor-outer-measure-rectangle`).
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of *cover, compactify, grid*): ≤ is trivial. For ≥, a countable cover by closed cubes \( Q_j \) is useless directly (no grid for infinitely many). Enlarge each \( Q_j \) to an open cube \( S_j \supseteq Q_j \) with \( \abs{\overline{S_j}} \le \abs{Q_j} + \varepsilon/2^j \); Heine–Borel (AN1.4) gives finitely many \( S_j \) covering Q; then `lem-rectangle-grid` on their closures. The ε/2ʲ appears again; say so.
  - **Proof** (typeset register, written in the forward order the Scratch Work found backwards).
  - **Key proof 1** (§11.2): A exercise has comprehension parts (where compactness is used; why the cubes are enlarged *before* the finite subcover is taken; what fails for \( Q = \nR^d \)).
- **Properties** (one theorem each, short proofs):
  - `thm-outer-monotone`: \( E_1 \subseteq E_2 \Rightarrow m_*(E_1) \le m_*(E_2) \).
  - `thm-outer-subadditive`: \( m_*(\bigcup E_j) \le \sum m_*(E_j) \). **Scratch Work** (first appearance of *the ε/2ᵏ budget*): for each j choose a cover of \( E_j \) costing \( \le m_*(E_j) + \varepsilon/2^j \); the union of countably many countable covers is a countable cover (F2.3, countable choice flagged in one clause).
  - `thm-outer-regularity`: \( m_*(E) = \inf\{ m_*(O) : O \supseteq E \text{ open} \} \). Route: enlarge the cubes of a near-optimal cover to open cubes with ε/2ʲ room.
  - `thm-outer-separated-additive`: if \( d(E_1, E_2) > 0 \) then \( m_*(E_1 \cup E_2) = m_*(E_1) + m_*(E_2) \). Route: subdivide cover cubes to diameter < \( d(E_1,E_2) \); each meets at most one of the two sets.
  - `thm-outer-almost-disjoint-cubes`: if \( E = \bigcup_j Q_j \) with the \( Q_j \) almost disjoint closed cubes, then \( m_*(E) = \sum_j \abs{Q_j} \). Route: shrink each \( Q_j \) to a closed cube strictly inside, losing \( \varepsilon/2^j \); finitely many shrunken cubes are pairwise at positive distance; apply `thm-outer-separated-additive` and monotonicity, then let N → ∞.
  - `cor-open-measure-cubes`: for open O written as in `thm-open-set-cubes`, \( m_*(O) = \sum \abs{Q_j} \), independent of the decomposition.
- **Warning.** Disjoint is not enough for additivity: the theorem needs positive distance or cube structure. Whether \( m_*(A \cup B) = m_*(A) + m_*(B) \) for all disjoint A, B is left open; "§04 settles it" (the second hook).
- **Quick checks.** (a) \( m_*([0,1]\times\{0\}) \) in ℝ² (0: cover by d-dimensional thin rectangles, then cubes). (b) Is \( m_*(E) = m_*(\overline{E}) \)? (No: ℚ ∩ [0, 1].)
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1** (key-proof comprehension, `thm-outer-measure-cube`): three parts as above. *Answers:* compactness converts the countable cover into a finite one, needed for the grid lemma; the enlargement must precede compactness because closed cubes do not form an open cover; for ℝᵈ the statement is ∞ = ∞ and the compactness step is unavailable.
  - **A2:** compute \( m_* \) of \( \{1/n : n \ge 1\} \), \( [0,1] \setminus \nQ \) (after `thm-outer-subadditive`: ≥ 1 − 0 = 1, so = 1), the boundary of a square in ℝ².
  - **B1:** \( m_*(E + h) = m_*(E) \) and \( m_*(\delta E) = \delta^d m_*(E) \) for δ > 0 (directly from covers; used in §03).
  - **B2:** a line in ℝ² is null; any countable union of affine hyperplanes is null.
  - **B3 (faded, ε/2ᵏ, last step blank):** "for each k cover \( E_k \) by cubes of total volume \( \le m_*(E_k) + \underline{\quad} \) …" with the prompt *Name the move, then complete it.* Answer: ε/2ᵏ, then sum the geometric series.
  - **C1:** the graph of a continuous \( f: [0,1] \to \nR \) is null in ℝ². *Answer:* uniform continuity (AN1.6 through AN3.4's closure) gives, for each n, a cover by n rectangles of width 1/n and height ε; total area ε.
  - **C2:** show that in the definition of \( m_* \) one may use rectangles instead of cubes, or open cubes, and get the same function. *Answer:* a closed rectangle R is covered by the grid cubes of side s that meet it; their number is at most \( \prod_i (\lceil (b_i - a_i)/s \rceil + 1) \), so their total volume tends to \( \abs{R} \) as s → 0; spend ε/2ʲ on the j-th rectangle. Open cubes: enlarge with ε/2ʲ room, as in `thm-outer-regularity`.

## 02-measurable-sets.md — Measurable sets and countable additivity

Budget ≤ 4,500 words.

- **Opening frame.** We want \( m_*(\bigcup E_j) = \sum m_*(E_j) \) for disjoint \( E_j \). §01 got it only for separated sets and cubes. Restrict to sets that are "almost open".
- **Failure-motivated definition.** First attempt: "E is good if \( m_*(O) - m_*(E) \) can be made small with O ⊇ E open". This is true of **every** set (`thm-outer-regularity`), so it selects nothing; the subtraction hides the problem. The repair measures the **difference set**:
  - `def-lebesgue-measurable`: E ⊆ ℝᵈ is (Lebesgue) measurable if for every ε > 0 there is an open \( O \supseteq E \) with \( m_*(O \setminus E) \le \varepsilon \). For measurable E write \( m(E) = m_*(E) \).
  - Symbols: \( \forall \varepsilon>0\ \exists O \text{ open},\ O \supseteq E,\ m_*(O\setminus E)\le\varepsilon \).
  - Quick check: why is \( m_*(O \setminus E) \le \varepsilon \) stronger than \( m_*(O) - m_*(E) \le \varepsilon \)? (Subadditivity gives one direction only; the other fails for sets §04 will produce.)
- **First stock of measurable sets** (`thm-measurable-first-properties`): (i) open sets; (ii) sets with \( m_*(E) = 0 \), hence every subset of a null set; (iii) countable unions of measurable sets; (iv) closed sets; (v) complements; (vi) countable intersections.
  - (iii): **ε/2ʲ budget again** (one-line Idea only, no Scratch Work: second use).
  - (iv): the hard one. Reduce to compact F (F = ⋃ F ∩ closed ball of radius k, then (iii)). For compact F and open O ⊇ F with \( m(O) \le m(F) + \varepsilon \): O \ F is open, so a countable almost disjoint union of cubes (`thm-open-set-cubes`); any finite subunion K is compact and disjoint from F, so at positive distance (AN2.3, cited in prose); `thm-outer-separated-additive` gives \( m_*(F) + m_*(K) \le m_*(O) \), so every finite partial sum is ≤ ε, and `thm-outer-almost-disjoint-cubes` finishes. Self-explanation prompt: *Before reading on: where is compactness of F used?*
  - (v): from (iv): choose open \( O_n \supseteq E \) with \( m_*(O_n \setminus E) \le 1/n \); \( S = \bigcup O_n^c \subseteq E^c \) is measurable by (iii)–(iv), and \( E^c \setminus S \subseteq O_n \setminus E \) for every n, so it is null.
  - (vi): De Morgan.
- **`thm-measurable-sigma-algebra`.** The measurable sets form a σ-algebra containing the open sets and the null sets. `def-sigma-algebra` introduced here (concrete; MT.3 abstracts it).
- **`thm-countable-additivity`.** If \( E_1, E_2, \dots \) are disjoint measurable sets, then \( m(\bigcup E_j) = \sum m(E_j) \).
  - Route (SS3's, our write-up): first all \( E_j \) bounded. Each \( E_j^c \) is measurable, so there is a closed \( F_j \subseteq E_j \) with \( m(E_j \setminus F_j) \le \varepsilon/2^j \) (this is `thm-closed-approximation`, proved first; see below); the \( F_j \) are compact and disjoint, so pairwise at positive distance, and `thm-outer-separated-additive` with induction gives \( m(\bigcup_{j\le N} F_j) = \sum_{j \le N} m(F_j) \); let N → ∞ and spend the budget. General case: cut ℝᵈ into the bounded measurable shells \( \{k-1 \le \abs{x} < k\} \) and re-index a double sum of non-negative terms (the regrouping fact for non-negative terms is proved in two lines in the text; AN2.12 is not in the s-closure).
  - **Key proof 2** (§11.2).
- **`thm-closed-approximation`** (placed before `thm-countable-additivity`): E is measurable iff for every ε > 0 there is a closed \( F \subseteq E \) with \( m_*(E \setminus F) \le \varepsilon \). First appearance of *outside open, inside closed*; name it here.
- **`thm-measure-continuity`.** (a) If \( E_k \uparrow E \) (measurable, increasing) then \( m(E) = \lim m(E_k) \). (b) If \( E_k \downarrow E \) and \( m(E_1) < \infty \), then \( m(E) = \lim m(E_k) \).
  - Witness that "\( m(E_1) < \infty \)" is needed: `exm-decreasing-infinite`: \( E_k = [k, \infty) \), all of measure ∞, intersection empty.
- **`thm-borel-cantelli`.** If \( \sum m(E_k) < \infty \), then the set of points lying in infinitely many \( E_k \) is null. Route: \( \limsup E_k = \bigcap_n \bigcup_{k \ge n} E_k \), subadditivity. Used in §05 (`thm-step-approximation`) and by PR.1.
- **`thm-caratheodory-criterion`.** E is measurable iff \( m_*(A) = m_*(A \cap E) + m_*(A \setminus E) \) for every A ⊆ ℝᵈ.
  - (⇒) For A, take open O ⊇ A with \( m(O) \le m_*(A) + \varepsilon \); O ∩ E and O \ E are disjoint and measurable, so additivity and monotonicity give ≥; ≤ is subadditivity.
  - (⇐) Fix an open ball \( B_n \). Let \( E_n = E \cap B_n \); pick open O ⊇ \( E_n \) with \( m(O) \le m_*(E_n) + \varepsilon \). Apply the criterion to \( A = O \cap B_n \): \( A \cap E = E_n \), so \( m_*(A \setminus E) = m_*(A) - m_*(E_n) \le \varepsilon \), and A is an open set containing \( E_n \) with \( A \setminus E_n = A \setminus E \). So each \( E_n \) is measurable and \( E = \bigcup E_n \).
  - Remark (one paragraph): this criterion mentions no open sets, so it makes sense for any "exterior measure" on any set. MT.3 takes it as the **definition** there (forward pointer in prose: "in Chapter 4").
- **Warning.** \( m(A \setminus B) = m(A) - m(B) \) needs \( B \subseteq A \) and \( m(B) < \infty \): ∞ − ∞.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1** (key-proof comprehension, `thm-countable-additivity`): why closed subsets rather than open supersets (open supersets of disjoint sets overlap); where boundedness is used (closed + bounded = compact, so positive distance); run the proof on \( E_j = [1/(j+1), 1/j) \).
  - **A2:** which of: \( \nQ \), the Cantor set, \( \{x \in \nR^2 : x_1 \in \nQ\} \), a countable union of lines, is measurable, and why (all are; each by a named clause of `thm-measurable-first-properties`).
  - **B1:** \( m(A \cup B) + m(A \cap B) = m(A) + m(B) \) for measurable A, B.
  - **B2:** if E is measurable and \( m_*(E \triangle F) = 0 \), then F is measurable and \( m(F) = m(E) \).
  - **B3:** the "lim inf" half of Borel–Cantelli: \( m(\liminf E_k) \le \liminf m(E_k) \). Then a sequence with \( \sum m(E_k) = \infty \) and \( m(\limsup E_k) = 0 \): \( E_k = [0, 1/k] \).
  - **C1 (find the error, true conclusion):** a "proof" that a countable union of measurable sets is measurable which picks \( O_j \supseteq E_j \) with \( m_*(O_j \setminus E_j) \le \varepsilon \) for every j and concludes \( m_*(O \setminus E) \le \varepsilon \). *Solution:* the conclusion is true (`thm-measurable-first-properties` (iii)); the illegal step is the last inequality, which needs \( \sum_j \varepsilon \le \varepsilon \). Counterexample to the step: for 0 < ε < 1, \( E_j = (2j, 2j+1) \) and \( O_j = (2j - \varepsilon/2, 2j + 1 + \varepsilon/2) \) satisfy the per-set bound with equality, the \( O_j \) are disjoint, and \( m(O \setminus E) = \infty \). Repair: ε/2ʲ.
  - **C2:** an exterior measure that is additive on the open sets but not countably additive on all sets would contradict nothing proved so far; say what §02 actually used about measurable sets that open sets alone would not give. (Answer: complements; open sets are not closed under complement.)

## 03-borel-sets-and-regularity.md — Borel sets, regularity and invariance

Budget ≤ 4,000 words.

- **Opening frame.** Measurable sets are defined by approximation; this section says how close the approximation is, which sets are measurable "for free", and which motions preserve measure.
- **`def-borel-sigma-algebra`:** the smallest σ-algebra containing the open sets (intersection of all such; one sentence that the family of σ-algebras is non-empty). `def-g-delta-f-sigma`.
- **`thm-borel-measurable`:** every Borel set is measurable (`thm-measurable-sigma-algebra`).
- **`thm-regularity`** (the precise form of *outside open, inside closed*): for E ⊆ ℝᵈ the following are equivalent: (a) E measurable; (b) \( E = G \setminus N \) with G a \( G_\delta \) and N null; (c) \( E = F \cup N \) with F an \( F_\sigma \) and N null. Corollary `cor-inner-regular-compact`: \( m(E) = \sup\{ m(K) : K \subseteq E \text{ compact} \} \) for measurable E.
- **`thm-finite-cube-approximation`** (Littlewood's first principle): if E is measurable and \( m(E) < \infty \), then for every ε > 0 there is a finite union F of closed cubes with \( m(E \triangle F) \le \varepsilon \). Route: open O ⊇ E with small excess, `thm-open-set-cubes`, truncate the cube series.
- **`thm-invariance`:** for measurable E, h ∈ ℝᵈ, δ > 0: E + h, δE, −E are measurable, with \( m(E + h) = m(E) \), \( m(\delta E) = \delta^d m(E) \), \( m(-E) = m(E) \). - **Completeness.** `rem-lebesgue-complete` as a short remark: every subset of a null set is measurable (`thm-measurable-first-properties` (ii)). Whether every measurable set is Borel is answered in §04 (`exm-measurable-not-borel`).
- **Worked examples and weird examples.**

  - `exm-small-dense-open`: enumerate \( \nQ = \{q_k\} \); \( O_\varepsilon = \bigcup_k (q_k - \varepsilon 2^{-k-1}, q_k + \varepsilon 2^{-k-1}) \) is open, dense, and \( m(O_\varepsilon) \le \varepsilon \). So \( [0,1] \setminus O_\varepsilon \) is closed, nowhere dense, of measure ≥ 1 − ε. "Open and dense" says nothing about size.
  - `exm-dense-gdelta-null`: \( G = \bigcap_n O_{1/n} \) is a dense \( G_\delta \) of measure 0. Its complement is a meagre set of full measure (the Baire-category reading is R3 below; no Baire is used here).

## 04-nonmeasurable-sets.md — A set with no length

Budget ≤ 3,500 words.

- **Opening frame.** Pay the second hook: is every set measurable, and is \( m_* \) additive on all disjoint pairs?
- **`def-vitali-set`.** On [0, 1], \( x \sim y \iff x - y \in \nQ \) is an equivalence relation (F2.5 is not in the closure; the three properties are checked in one line). A **Vitali set** \( N \subseteq [0,1] \) contains exactly one element of each class. Its existence uses the axiom of choice (F2.3's statement); **one clause** says so, and that no explicit Vitali set can be written down (prose; ST.6a, a side volume, says more).
- **`thm-vitali-nonmeasurable`:** a Vitali set is not measurable.
  - *Idea:* countably many rational translates of N tile [0, 1] up to a bounded overflow; translation invariance makes them all the same size; no size works.
  - Route: \( \{r_k\} = \nQ \cap [-1, 1] \); \( N_k = N + r_k \) are disjoint, \( [0,1] \subseteq \bigcup N_k \subseteq [-1, 2] \). If N were measurable, `thm-invariance` and `thm-countable-additivity` give \( 1 \le \sum_k m(N) \le 3 \), impossible for both m(N) = 0 and m(N) > 0.
  - **Exit check 1** (with marking scheme): prove it, say exactly where choice is used.
- **`lem-measurable-subset-of-vitali`:** every measurable subset of a Vitali set is null. Same route: \( A + r_k \) disjoint in [−1, 2].
- **`cor-outer-not-additive`** (pays the second hook): there are disjoint \( A, B \subseteq \nR \) with \( m_*(A \cup B) < m_*(A) + m_*(B) \). Route: N fails the Carathéodory criterion (`thm-caratheodory-criterion`), so for some A, \( m_*(A) < m_*(A \cap N) + m_*(A \setminus N) \); take the disjoint pair \( A \cap N \), \( A \setminus N \).
- **`thm-positive-contains-nonmeasurable`:** every \( E \subseteq \nR \) with \( m_*(E) > 0 \) contains a non-measurable subset. Route: the translates \( N + q \) (q ∈ ℚ) cover ℝ; if every \( E \cap (N + q) \) were measurable, each would be null (`lem-measurable-subset-of-vitali` after translating back), and E would be a countable union of null sets. Stated and proved on ℝ only; the ℝᵈ version is C1 below and nothing cites it.
- **`thm-no-invariant-measure-all-sets`:** there is no function μ on all subsets of ℝ that is countably additive, translation invariant, and has \( \mu([0,1]) = 1 \). Same tiling argument. This is "the problem of measure" (SS3 intro, Folland §1.1); one sentence that dropping countable to finite additivity changes the answer on ℝ (Banach) and not in ℝ³ (Banach–Tarski, ST.6b, prose pointer, not proved).
- **`exm-measurable-not-borel`** (worked example; the longest of the section).
  - `lem-cantor-function`: there is a continuous increasing \( c : [0,1] \to [0,1] \), constant on each interval removed in building the Cantor set C, with c(0) = 0, c(1) = 1. Route: \( c_n \) piecewise linear (rising on \( C_n \), flat on the removed intervals of stage ≤ n), \( \abs{c_{n+1} - c_n} \le 2^{-n} \), uniform Cauchy, uniform limit continuous (AN1.9, through HA.9's closure). AN2.15 (optional) shows the same function in its gallery: prose only.
  - \( \varphi(x) = x + c(x) \) is a strictly increasing continuous bijection [0, 1] → [0, 2], hence a homeomorphism (inverse continuous: AN2.3, a continuous bijection from a compact space). φ maps each removed interval onto an interval of the same length (c is constant there), so \( m(\varphi([0,1] \setminus C)) = 1 \) and \( m(\varphi(C)) = 1 \).
  - By `thm-positive-contains-nonmeasurable`, φ(C) contains a non-measurable A. \( B = \varphi^{-1}(A) \subseteq C \) is null, hence measurable. If B were Borel, then \( A = (\varphi^{-1})^{-1}(B) \) would be Borel (preimages of Borel sets under continuous maps are Borel: `lem-continuous-preimage-borel`, the "good sets" σ-algebra argument, stated and proved here because §05 reuses it), hence measurable. So B is measurable and not Borel.
  - Consequence for §05: \( \mathbb{1}_B \circ \varphi^{-1} = \mathbb{1}_A \) is a measurable function composed with a continuous one, and it is not measurable (`exm-composition-not-measurable`, stated in §05).
- **Remark (cardinality, prose):** there are \( 2^{\mathfrak{c}} \) measurable sets (subsets of the Cantor set) and only \( \mathfrak c \) Borel sets; not proved here.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** in the proof of `thm-vitali-nonmeasurable`, why rationals in [−1, 1] and not all rationals (the union must stay bounded for the upper bound 3); why countability of the translates matters (countable additivity).
  - **A2:** can a Vitali set have exterior measure 0? (No: a set of exterior measure 0 is measurable.)
  - **B1:** the complement of a Vitali set in [0, 1] has exterior measure 1. *Answer for the second:* if \( m_*([0,1] \setminus N) < 1 \), an open \( O \supseteq [0,1]\setminus N \) with \( m(O) < 1 \) leaves \( [0,1] \setminus O \subseteq N \), a closed measurable subset of N of positive measure, contradicting `lem-measurable-subset-of-vitali`.
  - **C1:** prove `thm-positive-contains-nonmeasurable` in ℝᵈ. *Answer:* the same argument with \( \nQ^d \) and the equivalence \( x - y \in \nQ^d \).
  - **C2:** show that \( \varphi \) maps some measurable set onto a non-measurable set, and conclude that a homeomorphism of [0, 1] onto [0, 2] need not preserve measurability (answer: B ↦ A above).
  - **Exit check (marking scheme outline, exit check 1):** 2 states the equivalence relation and the choice of N, naming choice; 2 shows the translates are disjoint; 2 shows the containments \( [0,1] \subseteq \bigcup N_k \subseteq [-1,2] \); 3 invariance + countable additivity give \( 1 \le \sum m(N) \le 3 \) and both cases fail; 1 says where choice entered. Fatal: using all of ℚ (no upper bound); using finite additivity only.

## 05-measurable-functions.md — Measurable functions

Budget ≤ 4,500 words.

- **Opening frame.** Q2 of the hook. To integrate f by slicing its **range** (the forward picture of MT.2), each set \( \{f > a\} \) must have a measure.
- **Failure-motivated definition.** "f is measurable if \( f^{-1}(E) \) is measurable for every measurable E" sounds natural and fails: a continuous function would not qualify (`exm-composition-not-measurable` shows a continuous \( \psi \) with \( \psi^{-1}(B) \) non-measurable for a measurable B). Ask only for preimages of half-lines.
  - `def-extended-reals-order` (brief: \( [-\infty, \infty] \), order, the arithmetic conventions \( 0 \cdot \infty = 0 \) and "∞ − ∞ undefined"; AN1.1 flagged these as notation; now they are used).
  - `def-measurable-function`: \( f : E \to [-\infty, \infty] \), E measurable, is measurable if \( \{x \in E : f(x) < a\} \) is measurable for every a ∈ ℝ.
  - `prp-measurable-equivalents`: equivalently with \( \le a \), \( > a \), \( \ge a \); for finite-valued f, equivalently \( f^{-1}(O) \) measurable for every open O ⊆ ℝ, and then for every Borel set.
- **Examples.** Continuous functions on measurable sets (open preimages relative to E); indicators \( \mathbb 1_E \) (measurable iff E is); monotone functions on intervals (preimages of half-lines are intervals).
- **`thm-measurable-closure`:** for measurable f, g (finite-valued where arithmetic needs it) and Φ: ℝ → ℝ continuous: \( \Phi \circ f \), \( f + g \), \( fg \), \( \max(f,g) \), \( \abs{f} \) are measurable. Route for the sum: \( \{f + g < a\} = \bigcup_{r \in \nQ} \{f < r\} \cap \{g < a - r\} \); product via \( fg = \tfrac14((f+g)^2 - (f-g)^2) \).
- **`exm-composition-not-measurable`** (from §04): \( \mathbb 1_B \circ \varphi^{-1} \) with B measurable non-Borel; so "measurable ∘ continuous" fails while "continuous ∘ measurable" holds. Warning with this witness.
- **`thm-measurable-limits`:** for measurable \( f_k \): \( \sup_k f_k \), \( \inf_k f_k \), \( \limsup f_k \), \( \liminf f_k \) are measurable, and so is \( \lim f_k \) where it exists. Route: \( \{\sup f_k > a\} = \bigcup_k \{f_k > a\} \). **This is the point of the chapter:** Riemann integrability was not closed under such limits (HA.9); measurability is.
- **Almost everywhere.** `def-almost-everywhere`; `prp-ae-equal-measurable`: if f is measurable and f = g a.e., g is measurable (completeness, §03). Remark: from now on "f_k → f a.e." is the standing notion; the limit is measurable.
- **Simple and step functions.** `def-simple-function` (finite linear combination of indicators of measurable sets of finite measure, SS3's convention), `def-step-function` (same with rectangles).
- **`thm-simple-approximation`:** (a) if f ≥ 0 is measurable on ℝᵈ (extended-valued), there are simple \( 0 \le \varphi_k \uparrow f \) pointwise; (b) if f is measurable, there are simple \( \varphi_k \) with \( \abs{\varphi_k} \le \abs{\varphi_{k+1}} \) and \( \varphi_k \to f \) pointwise; (c) on every bounded set on which f is bounded, the convergence in (a) is uniform. Route: \( F_k = \min(f, k)\, \mathbb 1_{\{\abs x \le k\}} \), \( \varphi_k = 2^{-k} \lfloor 2^k F_k \rfloor \). **MT.2 defines the integral from (a).**
- **`thm-step-approximation`:** if f is measurable on ℝᵈ and finite a.e., there are step functions \( \psi_k \to f \) a.e. Route (SS3's idea, our write-up): by `thm-simple-approximation` reduce to \( f = \mathbb 1_E \), \( m(E) < \infty \); by `thm-finite-cube-approximation` pick finite unions of cubes \( F_k \) with \( m(E \triangle F_k) \le 2^{-k} \), then rectangles with disjoint interiors (grid), and the boundaries (null) do not matter; `thm-borel-cantelli` shows \( \mathbb 1_{F_k} \to \mathbb 1_E \) off a null set. Used in `thm-luzin` and MT.7 (density of step functions in \( L^1 \)).
- **Quick checks.** (a) Is \( \mathbb 1_\nQ \) measurable? A.e. equal to a continuous function? (Yes; yes, to 0.) Continuous at some point? (No.) (b) If \( f^2 \) is measurable, is f? (No: \( f = \mathbb 1_N - \mathbb 1_{[0,1]\setminus N} \) with N a Vitali set has \( f^2 = 1 \).)
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** decide measurability: \( \mathbb 1_C \), \( x \mapsto \sin(1/x) \) on (0, 1), \( \lfloor x \rfloor \), \( \sup_k \mathbb 1_{[k, k+1/k]} \).
  - **B1:** f is measurable iff \( \{f > r\} \) is measurable for every rational r.
  - **B2:** if \( f : \nR \to \nR \) is differentiable, f′ is measurable. *Answer:* \( f' = \lim n(f(x + 1/n) - f(x)) \), a pointwise limit of continuous functions.
  - **B3:** the set of points where a sequence of measurable functions converges is measurable. *Answer:* \( \{\limsup = \liminf\} \cap \{\text{finite}\} \), or the Cauchy form \( \bigcap_m \bigcup_N \bigcap_{j,k \ge N} \{\abs{f_j - f_k} < 1/m\} \).
  - **C1:** a function \( f : \nR \to \nR \) whose level sets \( \{f = c\} \) are all measurable but which is not measurable. *Answer:* with N a Vitali set, \( f(x) = e^x \) for x ∈ N and \( f(x) = -e^x \) otherwise. Each part is injective and the two parts have disjoint ranges (positive, negative), so every level set has at most one point; but \( \{f > 0\} = N \).
  - **C2:** a measurable \( f : [0,1] \to \nR \) that is not a.e. equal to any continuous function. *Answer:* \( \mathbb 1_K \), K a fat Cantor set. If g is continuous and \( g = \mathbb 1_K \) a.e., then g = 0 a.e. on the open dense set \( [0,1] \setminus K \), hence g = 0 there (a continuous function vanishing a.e. on an open set vanishes on it, because non-empty open sets have positive measure), hence g = 0 on [0, 1] by density; but g = 1 on a subset of K of positive measure.

## 06-littlewood-principles.md — Littlewood's three principles: Egorov and Luzin

Budget ≤ 4,500 words. Last section: carries the R group, the weird-examples table and the List of Results.

- **Opening frame.** Littlewood's three principles, informally: every measurable set is nearly a finite union of intervals (cubes); every measurable function is nearly continuous; every convergent sequence of measurable functions is nearly uniformly convergent. The first is `thm-finite-cube-approximation` (cite). This section proves the third, then the second.
- **`thm-egorov`.** Let E be measurable with \( m(E) < \infty \), and \( f_k \) measurable and finite a.e. on E with \( f_k \to f \) a.e. on E, f finite a.e. Then for every ε > 0 there is a **closed** \( A_\varepsilon \subseteq E \) with \( m(E \setminus A_\varepsilon) \le \varepsilon \) and \( f_k \to f \) uniformly on \( A_\varepsilon \).
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of *throw away a small set*): for each tolerance 1/n, the bad sets \( E_N^n = \{x : \abs{f_k(x) - f(x)} \ge 1/n \text{ for some } k > N\} \) decrease to a null set in N; continuity from above needs \( m(E) < \infty \) (the witness of `exm-decreasing-infinite` is exactly why); choose \( N_n \) with \( m(E^n_{N_n}) \le \varepsilon/2^{n+1} \) (budget again); remove all of them; then a closed subset by `thm-closed-approximation`.
  - **Proof** (forward order). Self-explanation prompt: *Before reading on: which hypothesis makes the bad sets shrink to measure zero?*
  - **Key proof 3** (§11.2): A exercise comprehension parts.
  - **Hypotheses, each with a witness** (STYLE §10): `exm-egorov-infinite-measure`: \( \mathbb 1_{[k, k+1]} \to 0 \) everywhere on ℝ, but uniform convergence on A forces \( A \cap [k, k+1] = \emptyset \) for all large k, so no A with \( m(\nR \setminus A) < \infty \) works. `exm-egorov-epsilon-zero`: \( x^k \to 0 \) a.e. on [0, 1] (except at 1), and uniformly on A ⊆ [0, 1) iff sup A < 1, which no set of full measure in [0, 1] satisfies. Also: "a.e." cannot be weakened to "in measure" (MT.8; prose only).
  - **Exit check 2** (with marking scheme): prove Egorov and show it fails on ℝ with \( \mathbb 1_{[k, k+1]} \). Outline: 2 defines the bad sets with both quantifiers; 2 continuity from above with \( m(E) < \infty \) named; 2 the ε/2ⁿ choice of \( N_n \); 2 uniform convergence on the complement shown with the right quantifier order; 1 closedness via inner approximation; 1 the counterexample. Fatal: \( N \) chosen independent of n (no uniformity in tolerance); continuity from above used without finite measure.
- **Proof ordering** (STYLE §11.4; *throw away a small set* is a genuinely new named move): 8 shuffled lines of Egorov's proof plus two distractors (one fixes N before n; one claims \( E_N^n \downarrow \emptyset \) rather than to a null set without the a.e. clause). Placed as an A item.
- **Continuous extension.** `lem-urysohn-rd`: for disjoint closed \( A, B \subseteq \nR^d \) (non-empty), \( u(x) = d(x, A) / (d(x, A) + d(x, B)) \) is continuous, 0 on A, 1 on B. `thm-tietze-rd`: a continuous \( g : F \to \nR \) on a closed \( F \subseteq \nR^d \) extends to a continuous \( G : \nR^d \to \nR \), with \( \sup \abs{G} = \sup \abs{g} \) when g is bounded. Route: the bounded case by thirds (\( A = \{g \le -M/3\} \), \( B = \{g \ge M/3\} \), correct by \( \tfrac M3(2u - 1) \), iterate with \( (2/3)^n M \), M-test); the unbounded case through arctan and a cut-off by `lem-urysohn-rd` on the closed set where the extension hits ±π/2. Remark: AN2.8 (seg B) proves this in any normal space; prose pointer only, nothing here depends on it.
- **`thm-luzin`.** Let E be measurable with \( m(E) < \infty \) and \( f : E \to \nR \) measurable (finite-valued). For every ε > 0 there is a closed \( F_\varepsilon \subseteq E \) with \( m(E \setminus F_\varepsilon) \le \varepsilon \) such that \( f|_{F_\varepsilon} \) is continuous; moreover there is a continuous \( g : \nR^d \to \nR \) with g = f on \( F_\varepsilon \), and \( \sup \abs g \le \sup \abs f \) when f is bounded.
  - Route: step functions \( \psi_n \to f \) a.e. (`thm-step-approximation`). Each \( \psi_n \) is continuous off the union \( Z_n \) of the boundaries of its finitely many rectangles, a closed null set; by `thm-outer-regularity` pick open \( U_n \supseteq Z_n \) with \( m(U_n) \le \varepsilon/2^{n+2} \). Egorov gives a closed \( A \subseteq E \) with \( m(E \setminus A) \le \varepsilon/2 \) on which \( \psi_n \to f \) uniformly (the a.e. exceptional set is automatically outside A). Put \( F_\varepsilon = A \setminus \bigcup_n U_n \): closed, with \( m(E \setminus F_\varepsilon) \le \varepsilon/2 + \varepsilon/4 \). On \( F_\varepsilon \) each \( \psi_n \) is continuous and the convergence is uniform, so \( f|_{F_\varepsilon} \) is continuous (AN1.9, uniform limit). Extension by `thm-tietze-rd`.
  - `cor-luzin-sigma-finite`: the same with \( m(E) = \infty \) (and \( F_\varepsilon \) closed): apply `thm-luzin` on shells \( E \cap \{k \le \abs x \le k+1\} \) with ε/2ᵏ; a union of closed sets that is locally finite is closed, and continuity is local (pasting lemma on finitely many closed pieces near each point).
  - **Warning** (the most common misreading): "\( f|_{F} \) continuous" is not "f continuous at the points of F". Witness: \( \mathbb 1_\nQ \), restricted to the irrationals in a closed \( F \subseteq [0,1] \setminus \nQ \) of measure ≥ 1 − ε (from `exm-small-dense-open`), is identically 0, yet \( \mathbb 1_\nQ \) is continuous nowhere.
  - Forward pointer (prose): MT.7 uses `thm-luzin` (or `thm-step-approximation`) for the density of \( C_c \) in \( L^p \); FA.2 recasts Luzin for Radon measures.
- **The hook's last payment, forward:** Egorov is the engine of MT.2's bounded convergence theorem, which finally proves HA.9's Arzelà theorem. One sentence, no `@ref`.
