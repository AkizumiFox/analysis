## Chapter hook and where it is paid

**Hook (index page and first paragraph of §01).** The field F(x) = x/|x|ᵈ on ℝᵈ∖{0} has divergence 0 at every point where it is defined. Its flux out of every sphere centred at 0 is the same positive number, and (it will turn out) so is its flux out of the boundary of *any* reasonable bounded region containing 0. Either the divergence theorem is false, or a single point can carry flux. The chapter cannot even ask the question yet: "flux" needs an integral over a surface, and the book has none.

- **Paid in §05**, `prop-gauss-flux`: excise a small ball, apply `thm-divergence` on the remaining C¹ domain; the flux is σ(S^{d−1}) if 0 ∈ Ω and 0 if 0 ∉ Ω̄.
- **Paid again in §06**, `thm-representation-compact-support`: the same excision shows −ΔΦ "is" a unit mass at 0, i.e. u(0) = −∫ΦΔu. Pointer forward (prose only): PDE.1 turns this into ΔΦ = δ.

**Second hook (end of §01).** MT.5 defined σ on the sphere by a cone and a factor d, without ever measuring area. Is that number the area? Paid in §03, `thm-sphere-measure-agrees`.

**Promises received.** AN3.4's blueprint sends Green's theorem here ("Green's theorem has moved to surfaces-divergence-theorem"): paid by `thm-green` (§05). MT.5's note "the next chapter extends σ on S^{d−1} to surface measure on C¹ hypersurfaces": paid by `def-surface-measure` and `thm-sphere-measure-agrees`.

## Named moves (index page)

1. **Gram determinant.** The k-volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the columns of A ∈ ℝ^{d×k} is √det(AᵀA); compute it by QR, never by choosing coordinates in the k-plane.
2. **Localize–flatten–sum.** Cut a global statement into pieces with a partition of unity; on each piece, straighten the boundary by a measure-preserving shear (x′, x_d) ↦ (x′, x_d − g(x′)); prove the flat case by Fubini and the one-variable FTC; add the pieces.
3. **Excise the singularity.** Remove B_ε(a), apply the smooth theorem on what is left, and let ε → 0, using local integrability (MT.5) and dominated convergence for the volume term and the scaling σ(∂B_ε) = ε^{d−1}σ(S^{d−1}) for the boundary term.
4. **Differentiate the spherical average.** For φ(r) = ⨍_{∂B_r(x)} u, φ′(r) is a flux, hence a volume integral of Δu.

## §01 The area of a parametrized patch

Budget: ≤ 3,500 main-line words. Major notions: area element; reparametrization invariance.

**Opening.** The hook (above). Then the try-it-first item.

**Failure first: `ex-schwarz-lantern`.** Inscribe polyhedra in the cylinder of radius 1 and height 1: m + 1 horizontal circles at heights k/m, n equally spaced vertices on each, alternate circles rotated by π/n, 2mn triangles. Total area 2n sin(π/n)·√(1 + m²(1 − cos(π/n))²). With m = n the areas tend to 2π; with m = n² to 2π√(1 + π⁴/4); with m = n³ to ∞. Mesh → 0 in every case. Conclusion stated: area cannot be defined as a limit of inscribed polyhedral areas; what goes wrong is that the triangles' normals do not approach the surface's normals. So area must be defined from the derivative.

**Linear algebra, Scratch Work then proof: `lem-gram-volume`.** For A ∈ ℝ^{d×k} (k ≤ d), the parallelepiped P(A) = {At : t ∈ [0,1]ᵏ} has k-dimensional volume vol_k P(A) = √det(AᵀA). Definition used: vol_k of a subset of a k-dimensional subspace V is m_k(Qᵀ ·) for any Q ∈ ℝ^{d×k} with orthonormal columns spanning V (well defined: two such Q differ by an orthogonal k×k matrix, |det| = 1, MT.5 linear case). *Scratch Work* (set apart): try k = 1 (|a| = √(aᵀa)) and k = 2 in ℝ³ (|a × b|² = |a|²|b|² − (a·b)² = det of the 2×2 Gram matrix), then look for the proof that does not choose coordinates. *Proof:* rank A < k ⇒ both sides 0. Else Gram–Schmidt (LA3.11) gives A = QR, Q orthonormal columns, R upper triangular invertible; P(A) = Q(P(R)), so vol_k = m_k(P(R)) = |det R| (MT.5), and AᵀA = RᵀR gives det(AᵀA) = (det R)² (LA2.7).

**`def-area-element`.** For U ⊂ ℝᵏ open and φ ∈ C¹(U; ℝᵈ), J_φ := √det(Dφᵀ Dφ). φ is an *immersion* if Dφ(u) has rank k for every u. For an injective immersion φ, the k-area of φ(A), A ⊂ U Borel, is ∫_A J_φ dm_k (value in [0, ∞]). Remark: the integrand may be unbounded; the Lebesgue integral needs no improper-integral convention (contrast AN3.4).

**`thm-area-reparametrization`.** Let φ: U → ℝᵈ be a C¹ immersion, τ: Ũ → U a C¹ diffeomorphism of open subsets of ℝᵏ. Then J_{φ∘τ} = (J_φ ∘ τ)·|det Dτ|, and for every Borel A ⊂ U, ∫_{τ^{−1}A} J_{φ∘τ} = ∫_A J_φ. *Proof idea:* chain rule D(φ∘τ) = (Dφ∘τ)Dτ, det multiplicative, then `thm-change-of-variables` (MT.5) with the nonnegative integrand J_φ 1_A.

**`ex-graph-area-element`.** For g ∈ C¹(U), U ⊂ ℝ^{d−1}, φ(x′) = (x′, g(x′)): DφᵀDφ = I + ∇g ∇gᵀ, so J_φ = √(1 + |∇g|²). Proof of det(I + vvᵀ) = 1 + |v|²: v is an eigenvector with eigenvalue 1 + |v|², v^⊥ is the eigenspace for 1 (LA3.11). Curves (k = 1): J_γ = |γ′|, the arc length of AN2.9 (prose revisit).

**Worked examples.** `ex-hemisphere-area`: g(x′) = √(1 − |x′|²) on the open unit disc in ℝ², J = (1 − |x′|²)^{−1/2}, unbounded near the circle; by MT.5 polar coordinates in the plane and MCT, area = 2π∫₀¹ r(1 − r²)^{−1/2} dr = 2π. (Answers the try-it-first.) `ex-torus-area`: φ(θ, ψ) = ((R + r cos ψ) cos θ, (R + r cos ψ) sin θ, r sin ψ), 0 < r < R, on (0, 2π)²: J = r(R + r cos ψ), area 4π²Rr (the missing circles are null, deferred to §02's measure).

**Quick check.** For A = [a b] with a, b ∈ ℝ³, what is √det(AᵀA) when a ⟂ b? *Answer:* |a||b|.

**Warning.** J_φ is not |det Dφ|: Dφ is d×k, not square. For k = d the two agree.

**Second hook** (closing paragraph): MT.5's σ on S^{d−1}; is it area? (→ §03.)

**Exercises.**
- A1. Compute J_φ for φ(u, v) = (u, v, uv) on ℝ². *Answer:* √(1 + u² + v²).
- A2. Area of the paraboloid z = x² + y² over the disc of radius a. *Answer:* (π/6)((1 + 4a²)^{3/2} − 1).
- A3. For A ∈ ℝ^{d×k} and Q ∈ ℝ^{d×d} orthogonal, show √det((QA)ᵀ(QA)) = √det(AᵀA), and say what this means for areas of rotated patches. *Answer:* (QA)ᵀQA = AᵀA; rotation preserves area.
- B1. Prove the Schwarz-lantern area formula from the triangle data and verify the three limits. *Answer:* each triangle has base 2 sin(π/n) and height √(m^{−2} + (1 − cos(π/n))²); multiply by mn; use 1 − cos(π/n) ~ π²/(2n²).
- B2. Show that the area element of the sphere of radius ρ in ℝ³ in the coordinates (θ, z) ↦ (√(ρ² − z²) cos θ, √(ρ² − z²) sin θ, z) is identically ρ, and deduce that the area of a zone a < z < b of the sphere is 2πρ(b − a). *Answer:* direct computation; J = ρ.
- B3. Let φ: U → ℝᵈ be a C¹ immersion with U ⊂ ℝᵏ open and k < d. Show that the image of a compact K ⊂ U is Lebesgue-null in ℝᵈ. *Answer:* φ is locally Lipschitz; cover K by finitely many cubes of side δ, each image lies in a ball of radius Cδ; N ≈ δ^{−k} cubes give measure ≲ δ^{d−k} → 0. (Feeds `prop-hypersurface-null`.)
- C1. (Hint ladder: QR; then the block matrix.) For A ∈ ℝ^{d×(d−1)} of rank d − 1 and n a unit vector orthogonal to the columns of A, prove |det[n | A]| = √det(AᵀA). *Answer:* [n|A]ᵀ[n|A] = diag(1, AᵀA). (This is `lem-normal-column`, used in §03 and §05; the C item states it, §03 restates it with its label.)
- C2. (Hint ladder: parametrize by height; then use B2.) Prove that the orthogonal projection of the unit sphere in ℝ³ onto a diameter pushes σ forward to 2π times Lebesgue measure on [−1, 1]. *Answer:* zone formula from B2.

## §02 Hypersurfaces and surface measure

Budget: ≤ 4,500 main-line words. Major notions: C¹ hypersurface; surface measure.

**Failure first: `ex-figure-eight`.** γ(t) = (sin 2t, sin t), t ∈ (−π, π), is an injective C¹ immersion (γ′ = (2 cos 2t, cos t) never vanishes; γ(s) = γ(t) forces s = t), but near the origin its image is not the graph of a function over either axis: the ends t → ±π return to the origin. Lesson: define surfaces locally as graphs, not as images of one parametrization.

**`def-hypersurface`.** M ⊂ ℝᵈ is a C¹ hypersurface if every p ∈ M has an open W ∋ p, an index i, an open U ⊂ ℝ^{d−1} and g ∈ C¹(U) with M ∩ W = {x ∈ W : x_i = g(x̂_i)}, x̂_i ∈ U, where x̂_i deletes the i-th coordinate. The map φ(x̂_i) = (x̂_i with g inserted in slot i), restricted to the open set {x̂_i ∈ U : φ ∈ W}, is a **graph chart**.

**`prop-level-set-hypersurface`.** If G ∈ C¹(V), V ⊂ ℝᵈ open, and ∇G ≠ 0 on M = {G = 0}, then M is a C¹ hypersurface, and ∇G(p) ⟂ the columns of Dφ for every graph chart φ at p. *Proof:* implicit function theorem (AN3.2) solved for a coordinate i with ∂_iG(p) ≠ 0; differentiate G∘φ = 0.
Examples: spheres ∂B_r(a); graphs; the torus as a level set of (√(x² + y²) − R)² + z² − r². Non-example: the cone x₃ = √(x₁² + x₂²) at 0 (Quick check below).

**`def-chart`** and **`prop-chart-transition`.** A chart of M is an injective C¹ immersion φ: U → M (U ⊂ ℝ^{d−1} open) that is a homeomorphism onto an open subset of M. Graph charts are charts. If φ, φ̃ are charts, then τ = φ̃^{−1}∘φ is a C¹ diffeomorphism between the open sets φ^{−1}(φ̃(Ũ)) and φ̃^{−1}(φ(U)). *Proof:* first for φ̃ a graph chart over x̂_i: φ̃^{−1} is the restriction of the coordinate projection π_i, so τ = π_i ∘ φ is C¹; D(π_i∘φ) is invertible because π_i is injective on the tangent plane (tangent planes of graphs over x̂_i contain no multiple of e_i); the inverse function theorem (AN3.2) plus injectivity give a C¹ inverse. General φ̃: factor through a graph chart.

**Failure again: `prop-hypersurface-null`.** Every C¹ hypersurface is Lebesgue-null in ℝᵈ. *Proof:* each graph piece {x_i = g(x̂_i)} is null by Tonelli (MT.4): each vertical line meets it once; countably many pieces cover M (`lem-countable-charts`). So m cannot measure surfaces; a new measure is needed.

**`lem-countable-charts`.** Every C¹ hypersurface is covered by countably many graph charts; a compact one by finitely many. *Proof:* rational balls form a countable base of ℝᵈ; for each p pick a rational ball B ∋ p inside a graph neighbourhood; there are countably many such balls.

**`def-surface-measure`.** Let M be covered by charts φ_j: U_j → M (j ∈ ℕ), with images V_j. Put E_1 = V_1, E_j = V_j ∖ (V_1 ∪ … ∪ V_{j−1}) (Borel subsets of M). For a Borel set E ⊂ M,
σ_M(E) := Σ_j ∫_{φ_j^{−1}(E ∩ E_j)} J_{φ_j} dm_{d−1}.
For f: M → [0, ∞] Borel, ∫_M f dσ_M as usual (MT.3's integral against a measure). Written σ when M is clear.

**`thm-surface-measure-well-defined`** (key proof). (a) σ_M is a Borel measure on M. (b) It does not depend on the chart cover or its order. (c) For every chart φ: U → M and Borel A ⊂ U, σ_M(φ(A)) = ∫_A J_φ dm_{d−1}; consequently ∫_{φ(U)} f dσ = ∫_U (f∘φ) J_φ for Borel f ≥ 0 or σ-integrable f. (d) σ_M is finite on compact subsets of M.
*Proof route:* (c) first, for a single chart pair: on φ(U) ∩ V_j, `prop-chart-transition` + `thm-area-reparametrization`; sum over j using the disjointness of the E_j and countable additivity of each ∫_{(·)} J_{φ_j}. (b) follows from (c) applied to each chart of the second cover. (a): each summand is a measure in E (MCT), countable sums of measures are measures. (d): compact K ⊂ M is covered by finitely many graph charts whose parameter sets can be shrunk to have compact closure in U, on which J is bounded.
*Comprehension parts (A3):* why are the E_j disjoint and why does that matter for (a); which earlier theorem converts one chart's integral into another's.

**Remarks (prose).** σ is invariant under rigid motions (A3 of §01 + translation). For a graph over x̂_i: dσ = √(1 + |∇g|²) dx̂_i. The thickening limit σ(E) = lim_{ε→0} m({x : dist(x, E) < ε})/(2ε) for compact E inside one graph chart is stated without proof (B-level evidence: the sphere, B2). Closing remark: σ_M equals the normalized (d−1)-dimensional Hausdorff measure on M (stated, not proved; reference line only, no attribution in the text body per STYLE).

**Quick checks.** (1) Is the cone {x₃ = |x′|} a C¹ hypersurface? *Answer:* not near 0 (no C¹ graph over any coordinate plane contains the vertex), but the cone minus the vertex is. (2) Compute σ of the graph of g(x′) = a·x′ + b over a Borel set A. *Answer:* √(1 + |a|²)·m(A).

**Exercises.**
- A1. Show that ∂B_r(a) is a C¹ hypersurface via `prop-level-set-hypersurface` and list 2d graph charts covering it. *Answer:* G(x) = |x − a|² − r², hemispheres ±(x_i − a_i) > 0.
- A2. Area of the torus via `thm-surface-measure-well-defined` (c), using §01's parametrization on (0, 2π)² and showing the omitted set is σ-null. *Answer:* 4π²Rr; the two omitted circles are images of null sets under charts.
- A3. (Comprehension, key proof.) The two questions above, plus: where would the proof fail if the E_j were replaced by the V_j? *Answer:* overlaps would be counted twice; σ(V_1 ∪ V_2) would exceed the true value whenever V_1 ∩ V_2 ≠ ∅.
- B1. Prove that a graph chart is a chart (homeomorphism onto an open subset of M). *Answer:* inverse is π_i, continuous; image is M ∩ W.
- B2. For the unit sphere in ℝ³ and E a polar cap {x₃ > 1 − h}, show m({x : dist(x, E) < ε})/(2ε) → 2πh = σ(E). *Answer:* the shell volume (4π/3)((1+ε)³ − (1−ε)³)·(cap fraction) plus O(ε²) edge terms; divide and let ε → 0.
- B3. (Find the error; true conclusion.) "Let φ and φ̃ = φ∘τ be charts of the same patch. Then J_φ̃ = (J_φ∘τ)·det Dτ by the chain rule, so ∫J_φ̃ = ∫J_φ by change of variables." *Answer:* the factor is |det Dτ|; with det Dτ < 0 the displayed identity is false (J_φ̃ ≥ 0), and change of variables uses |det|. The conclusion is still true.
- C1. (Hint ladder: one chart; then a σ-null set; then countable additivity.) Prove that if M = ∪_j M_j with each M_j a hypersurface and M_j ∩ M_k σ-null, then σ_M = Σ σ_{M_j} on Borel sets of M. *Answer:* by (c) on charts of each M_j and the disjointification of §02.
- C2. (Hint ladder: a graph chart at p; its Lipschitz constant; J ≥ 1.) Let M be a C¹ hypersurface and p ∈ M. Prove there are c, ε₀ > 0 with σ(M ∩ B_ε(p)) ≥ cε^{d−1} for 0 < ε < ε₀. *Answer:* in a graph chart φ with φ(p′) = p, φ is L-Lipschitz on a ball around p′, so φ(B′_{ε/L}(p′)) ⊂ B_ε(p); J_φ = √(1 + |∇g|²) ≥ 1 gives σ ≥ m_{d−1}(B′_{ε/L}) = |B′₁|(ε/L)^{d−1}. (So σ charges every relatively open subset of M.)

## §03 Surface measure on spheres and polar coordinates revisited

Budget: ≤ 3,000 main-line words. Major notion: none new (one comparison theorem); the try-it-first opens the section.

**`lem-normal-column`.** (Restates §01 C1 with its label.) For A ∈ ℝ^{d×(d−1)} and a unit vector n ⟂ col(A): |det[n | A]| = √det(AᵀA). *Proof:* [n|A]ᵀ[n|A] = diag(1, AᵀA); LA2.7.

**`thm-sphere-measure-agrees`.** The surface measure σ_{S^{d−1}} of `def-surface-measure` equals the measure σ defined in MT.5 by σ(E) = d·m(C(E)), C(E) = {rω : 0 < r ≤ 1, ω ∈ E}, on every Borel E ⊂ S^{d−1}.
*Proof route:* cover S^{d−1} by the 2d hemisphere graph charts and disjointify; it suffices to prove d·m(C(φ(A))) = ∫_A J_φ for the upper hemisphere chart φ(x′) = (x′, √(1 − |x′|²)) on the open unit ball B′ ⊂ ℝ^{d−1} (others by a coordinate permutation, which preserves m and σ). Ψ(r, x′) = rφ(x′) is a C¹ diffeomorphism (0, 1) × B′ → C(upper hemisphere) ∖ {r = 1}; DΨ = [φ | rDφ]; φ(x′) is a unit vector orthogonal to the columns of Dφ(x′) (differentiate |φ|² = 1), so |det DΨ| = r^{d−1}J_φ by `lem-normal-column`. Change of variables (MT.5) and Tonelli: m(C(φ(A))) = ∫₀¹ r^{d−1} dr ∫_A J_φ = (1/d)∫_A J_φ (the slice r = 1 is null).

**`cor-sphere-area`.** σ(S^{d−1}) = d·|B₁| = 2π^{d/2}/Γ(d/2). Table d = 1, 2, 3, 4: 2, 2π, 4π, 2π².

**`cor-polar-spheres`.** For f ≥ 0 Borel or f ∈ L¹(ℝᵈ): ∫_{ℝᵈ} f = ∫₀^∞ (∫_{∂B_r(a)} f dσ) dr, and ∫_{B_R(a)} f = ∫₀^R ∫_{∂B_r(a)} f dσ dr. *Proof idea:* MT.5's polar formula, translated, plus `prop-sphere-scaling`.

**`prop-sigma-rotation-invariant`.** σ(QE) = σ(E) for Q orthogonal. Two proofs offered in prose: from MT.5's definition (m is rotation invariant) and from charts (§01 A3); their agreement is a sanity check of the theorem.

**Worked example `ex-gaussian-sphere`.** Recover σ(S^{d−1}) from ∫e^{−|x|²} = π^{d/2} (MT.5) and `cor-polar-spheres`: π^{d/2} = σ(S^{d−1})∫₀^∞ e^{−r²}r^{d−1} dr = σ(S^{d−1})Γ(d/2)/2.

**Quick check.** Differentiate R ↦ m(B_R) and compare with σ(∂B_R). *Answer:* d|B₁|R^{d−1} = σ(∂B_R); the derivative of volume is area.

## §04 C¹ domains, the outward normal, and partitions of unity

Budget: ≤ 3,500 main-line words. Major notions: C¹ domain with outward normal; smooth partition of unity.

**Failure first: `ex-slit-disc`.** Ω = {|x| < 1} ∖ ([0, 1) × {0}) in ℝ². Here ∂Ω = circle ∪ slit, and Ω lies on both sides of the slit, so the slit has no outward direction. Take F = (0, 1): ∫_Ω div F = 0 and the flux out of the circle is ∫ν₂ dσ = 0, but counting the slit once with either normal (0, ∓1) adds ∓1 (its length is 1). Whatever "∫_{∂Ω} F·ν dσ" means, it cannot include the slit once. Lesson: near each boundary point, Ω must lie on exactly one side of ∂Ω.

**`def-c1-domain`.** A bounded open Ω ⊂ ℝᵈ is a C¹ domain if for every p ∈ ∂Ω there are a rigid motion R (x ↦ Q(x − p), Q orthogonal) taking p to 0, numbers r, h > 0 and g ∈ C¹(B′_r) (B′_r the open ball of radius r in ℝ^{d−1}) with g(0) = 0, |g| < h/2 on B′_r, and
R(Ω) ∩ (B′_r × (−h, h)) = {(x′, x_d) : x′ ∈ B′_r, −h < x_d < g(x′)}.
Such a cylinder is a **boundary cylinder**.

**`prop-outward-normal`.** If Ω is a C¹ domain then ∂Ω is a compact C¹ hypersurface; in a boundary cylinder, ν = R^{−1}(−∇g, 1)/√(1 + |∇g|²) is independent of the cylinder chosen, continuous, of unit length, orthogonal to the tangent plane, and p + tν ∉ Ω̄, p − tν ∈ Ω for 0 < t small (hence "outward"). *Proof idea:* uniqueness: the two characterizations (unit, normal, points out) determine ν.
Examples: balls, annuli B_R ∖ B̄_ε (normal −x/ε on the inner sphere; used in §05), {G < 0} with ∇G ≠ 0 on {G = 0} and the set bounded (ν = ∇G/|∇G|, `prop-sublevel-domain`). Non-examples: the slit disc; two tangent open discs (boundary not a graph at the contact point); a square (corners) — the divergence theorem still holds for the square (§05 `ex-divergence-box`), so C¹ is sufficient, not necessary; a domain that near 0 equals {(x, y) : 0 < x < δ, |y| < x²} (an outward cusp at 0; prose only).

**`lem-smooth-bump`.** h(t) = e^{−1/t} (t > 0), 0 (t ≤ 0) is C^∞; hence for every ball B_r(a) there is ψ ∈ C_c^∞(ℝᵈ), 0 ≤ ψ ≤ 1, ψ > 0 exactly on B_r(a). *Proof:* induction: h^{(n)}(t) = p_n(1/t)e^{−1/t} for t > 0, → 0 as t ↓ 0; ψ(x) = h(r² − |x − a|²).
*Quick check:* the divergence theorem's proof needs only C¹ cut-offs; give one (*Answer:* (r² − |x − a|²)₊²). C^∞ is used because MT.7 (mollifiers) and PDE.1 reuse this lemma.

**`thm-smooth-partition-compact`.** Let K ⊂ ℝᵈ be compact and W₁, …, W_N open with K ⊂ ∪W_i. There are ψ_i ∈ C_c^∞(W_i), ψ_i ≥ 0, with Σψ_i = 1 on a neighbourhood of K (the bound ψ_i ≤ 1 is not claimed and not needed). *Proof:* cover K by finitely many balls B_k with B̄_k ⊂ W_{i(k)} (compactness); bumps φ_k from `lem-smooth-bump`, positive exactly on B_k; χ_i = Σ_{i(k)=i} φ_k ∈ C_c^∞(W_i); s = Σ_iχ_i is positive on K, so c = min_K s > 0. With a smooth step θ (0 on t ≤ c/4, 1 on t ≥ c/2, built from h as θ(t) = h(t − c/4)/(h(t − c/4) + h(c/2 − t))), put ρ(t) = θ(t)t + (1 − θ(t))c/4 ≥ c/4 on t ≥ 0 and ψ_i = χ_i/ρ(s). Then ψ_i ∈ C_c^∞(W_i) and Σψ_i = s/ρ(s) = 1 on the open set {s > c/2} ⊃ K. Label distinct from AM.1's partition-of-unity theorem (see coordinator notes).

**Exercises.**
- A1. Verify that the annulus 1 < |x| < 2 is a C¹ domain and give ν on both spheres. *Answer:* x/|x| on |x| = 2, −x/|x| on |x| = 1.
- A2. Show {x : x₁⁴ + x₂⁴ < 1} is a C¹ domain and compute ν. *Answer:* ∇G/|∇G| with G = x₁⁴ + x₂⁴ − 1, ∇G ≠ 0 on {G = 0}.

- B2. Show that Ω = B₁(−e₁) ∪ B₁(e₁) ⊂ ℝ² is not a C¹ domain. *Answer:* both circles have tangent line x₁ = 0 at the contact point 0, so a boundary cylinder at 0 would have axis ±e₁ and ∂Ω would be a single graph over the x₂-axis near 0; but each line x₂ = c (c ≠ 0 small) meets ∂Ω near 0 in the two points x₁ = ±(1 − √(1 − c²)).

## §05 The divergence theorem

Budget: ≤ 4,800 main-line words. Major notion: the divergence theorem (one theorem; Green as its d = 2 case).

**Convention (NOTATION addition).** For Ω open, C¹(Ω̄; ℝᵈ) is the set of restrictions to Ω̄ of C¹ fields on some open set containing Ω̄. (Weaker hypotheses: remark and C2.) div F = Σ ∂_iF_i.

**Warm-up with Scratch Work: `ex-divergence-box`.** For a box Q = Π[a_i, b_i] and F ∈ C¹(Q̄), ∫_Q ∂_iF_i = ∫ over the face x_i = b_i of F_i − ∫ over the face x_i = a_i of F_i: Fubini (MT.4) and the FTC in x_i. Summing over i gives the divergence theorem for a box (faces have σ = m_{d−1}, outward normals ±e_i). Scratch Work, set apart: "the only tools are Fubini and the FTC; the rest of the section is about making a curved boundary flat and a global field local."

**`lem-compact-support-divergence`.** For F ∈ C_c¹(ℝᵈ; ℝᵈ), ∫ div F = 0. If G is C¹ on an open set containing the closed half space H = {x_d ≤ 0} and G vanishes on H outside a compact set, then ∫_{x_d<0} div G = ∫_{ℝ^{d−1}} G_d(x′, 0) dx′. *Proof:* Fubini and FTC in each variable; the half-space boundary term appears only for i = d.

**`thm-divergence`** (key proof; registry label `thm-divergence`). Let Ω ⊂ ℝᵈ be a C¹ domain with outward unit normal ν and surface measure σ on ∂Ω, and F ∈ C¹(Ω̄; ℝᵈ). Then ∫_Ω div F dm = ∫_{∂Ω} F·ν dσ.

*Scratch Work* (set apart, first appearance of localize–flatten–sum): (1) both sides are linear in F; (2) if F is supported in a boundary cylinder, rotate so the boundary is a graph x_d = g(x′); (3) shear the graph flat; (4) apply the half-space lemma; (5) check that the boundary term is exactly F·ν dσ in the graph chart; (6) glue.

*Proof route (written in full in the text):*
1. **Rigid motions.** For x ↦ Rx + c with R orthogonal: div(R^{−1}F(R· + c)) = (div F)(R· + c); m, σ, ν transform correspondingly (`lem-divergence-rigid`, short).
2. **Local piece.** Let F be supported in a boundary cylinder Z = B′_r × (−h, h) in which Ω = {x_d < g(x′)}. Shear Θ(x′, x_d) = (x′, x_d − g(x′)): C¹ diffeomorphism of B′_r × ℝ, det DΘ = 1, Θ(Ω ∩ Z) = {y_d < 0} ∩ Θ(Z). F (already multiplied by its cut-off) is C¹ on a neighbourhood of Ω̄ ∩ Z = {x_d ≤ g(x′)} ∩ Z and vanishes outside a compact subset of Z; so G = F∘Θ^{−1}, extended by 0, satisfies the half-space hypothesis of `lem-compact-support-divergence`, and the chain rule gives (∂_jF_j)∘Θ^{−1} = ∂_jG_j − (∂_jg)∂_dG_j for j < d, and (∂_dF_d)∘Θ^{−1} = ∂_dG_d. Change variables (det 1; or Tonelli on vertical lines with one-dimensional translation invariance — the text uses the latter, avoiding MT.5), apply `lem-compact-support-divergence` to (G_1, …, G_{d−1}, 0) on ℝ^{d−1} slices and FTC in y_d:
∫_Ω div F = ∫_{ℝ^{d−1}} [G_d(y′, 0) − Σ_{j<d} ∂_jg(y′) G_j(y′, 0)] dy′ = ∫ F(x′, g(x′))·(−∇g(x′), 1) dx′.
By `ex-graph-area-element` and `prop-outward-normal`, (−∇g, 1) = ν·J_φ in the graph chart φ(x′) = (x′, g(x′)), so the right side is ∫_{∂Ω} F·ν dσ by `thm-surface-measure-well-defined` (c).
3. **Interior piece.** If supp F ⊂ Ω compactly, both sides are 0 (`lem-compact-support-divergence`).
4. **Glue.** Cover the compact set Ω̄ by finitely many boundary cylinders (rotated) and one open set W₀ with W̄₀ ⊂ Ω; `thm-smooth-partition-compact` gives ψ₀, …, ψ_N with Σψ_i = 1 near Ω̄; F = Σψ_iF, each ψ_iF ∈ C¹ with support in one piece; add.

*Comprehension parts (A3):* why the shear has Jacobian 1 and why that matters; where compactness of Ω̄ is used; what fails in step 2 if Ω lay on both sides of the graph (`ex-slit-disc`).

**`cor-divergence-consequences`.** (a) ∫_{∂Ω} ν dσ = 0. (b) m(Ω) = (1/d)∫_{∂Ω} x·ν dσ. (c) For B_R: σ(∂B_R) = (d/R)m(B_R), a third derivation of `cor-sphere-area`.

**`thm-green`** (keeps the label moved from AN3.4). Let Ω ⊂ ℝ² be a C¹ domain, τ = (−ν₂, ν₁) the unit tangent with Ω on its left, and P, Q ∈ C¹(Ω̄). Define ∮_{∂Ω}(P dx + Q dy) := ∫_{∂Ω}(P, Q)·τ dσ. Then ∫_Ω (∂_xQ − ∂_yP) = ∮_{∂Ω}(P dx + Q dy). *Proof:* `thm-divergence` with F = (Q, −P). A remark identifies ∮ with the line integral along a positively oriented C¹ parametrization of each boundary circle (σ = |γ′| dt from §01).

**Worked example `ex-area-by-boundary`.** m(Ω) = ∮x dy = ½∮(x dy − y dx) for planar C¹ domains; the ellipse area πab.

**Hook paid: `prop-gauss-flux`.** Let F(x) = x/|x|ᵈ on ℝᵈ∖{0} and Ω a C¹ domain with 0 ∉ ∂Ω. Then ∫_{∂Ω} F·ν dσ = σ(S^{d−1}) if 0 ∈ Ω and 0 if 0 ∉ Ω̄. *Proof (Scratch Work set apart: first appearance of "excise the singularity"):* div F = 0 off 0 (compute). If 0 ∉ Ω̄, F ∈ C¹(Ω̄): `thm-divergence`. If 0 ∈ Ω, choose ε with B̄_ε ⊂ Ω; Ω_ε = Ω ∖ B̄_ε is a C¹ domain with boundary ∂Ω ∪ ∂B_ε and normal −x/ε on the inner sphere; F ∈ C¹(Ω̄_ε); flux through ∂B_ε outward is ε^{1−d}·σ(∂B_ε) = σ(S^{d−1}) (`prop-sphere-scaling`). So divergence-free fields can have flux: the point 0 carries it.

**Remark (revisits AN3.7, prose only).** For an oriented compact hypersurface, F·ν dσ is the pull-back of the (d−1)-form ι_F(dx₁ ∧ … ∧ dx_d), and `thm-divergence` is Stokes' theorem ∫_∂Ω ι_F vol = ∫_Ω d(ι_F vol). The measure version needs no orientation of ∂Ω beyond ν and integrates non-smooth functions against σ; the forms version generalizes to manifolds that do not sit in ℝᵈ.

**Remark (weaker hypotheses).** True for F ∈ C¹(Ω) ∩ C(Ω̄) with div F ∈ L¹(Ω); proved here only for balls/star-shaped domains (C2). Also true for Lipschitz domains (boxes, polygons); statement only.

**Quick check.** Flux of F(x) = x out of the unit cube [0, 1]³? *Answer:* 3 (div F = 3, volume 1).

## §06 Integration by parts, Green's identities, and harmonic functions

Budget: ≤ 4,800 main-line words. Major notions: harmonic function (mean value property); fundamental solution.

**`cor-integration-by-parts`.** For a C¹ domain Ω and u, v ∈ C¹(Ω̄): ∫_Ω u ∂_jv = ∫_{∂Ω} uvν_j dσ − ∫_Ω v ∂_ju. *Proof idea:* §05 B3 with f = uv.

**`thm-green-identities`.** For u ∈ C¹(Ω̄), v ∈ C²(Ω̄): (first) ∫_Ω (uΔv + ∇u·∇v) = ∫_{∂Ω} u ∂_νv dσ; for u, v ∈ C²(Ω̄): (second) ∫_Ω (uΔv − vΔu) = ∫_{∂Ω} (u∂_νv − v∂_νu) dσ, where ∂_νv = ∇v·ν. *Proof idea:* `thm-divergence` with F = u∇v; subtract.

**`cor-dirichlet-uniqueness-energy`.** If u₁, u₂ ∈ C²(Ω̄) satisfy Δu₁ = Δu₂ in Ω and u₁ = u₂ on ∂Ω, then u₁ = u₂. *Proof:* w = u₁ − u₂; first identity with u = v = w: ∫|∇w|² = 0; w is constant on each component of Ω; each component is bounded, so its closure meets ∂Ω, where w = 0. (Hypothesis check for S2a: "each component of Ω has boundary in ∂Ω" holds for bounded open sets.)

**`def-harmonic`.** u ∈ C²(U), U ⊂ ℝᵈ open, with Δu = 0. Averages: ⨍_{∂B_r(x)} u := σ(∂B_r)^{−1}∫_{∂B_r(x)} u dσ, ⨍_{B_r(x)} u := m(B_r)^{−1}∫_{B_r(x)} u.

**`lem-differentiate-sphere-average`.** For u ∈ C¹(U) and B̄_R(x) ⊂ U, φ(r) = ⨍_{S^{d−1}} u(x + rω) dσ(ω) is differentiable on (0, R) with φ′(r) = ⨍_{S^{d−1}} ∇u(x + rω)·ω dσ(ω). *Proof:* difference quotients, mean value theorem, uniform continuity of ∇u on B̄_R(x), DCT (σ finite).

**`thm-mean-value-rd`** (key proof). If u is harmonic on U and B̄_r(x) ⊂ U, then u(x) = ⨍_{∂B_r(x)} u = ⨍_{B_r(x)} u.
*Proof (support faded: the reader names the move "differentiate the spherical average"):* φ′(r) = σ(S^{d−1})^{−1} r^{1−d}∫_{∂B_r(x)} ∂_νu dσ (`prop-sphere-scaling`) = σ(S^{d−1})^{−1} r^{1−d}∫_{B_r(x)} Δu = 0 (`thm-divergence` with F = ∇u); φ(r) → u(x) as r → 0 (§03 C2); ball average from `cor-polar-spheres`.
*Comprehension parts (A3):* which hypothesis on u makes F = ∇u admissible in `thm-divergence`; why φ(0⁺) = u(x); how the ball average follows from the sphere averages.

**`thm-max-principle-rd`.** (Strong) If U is connected, u harmonic on U and u attains its supremum over U at a point of U, then u is constant. (Weak) If Ω is bounded, u ∈ C²(Ω) ∩ C(Ω̄) harmonic in Ω, then max_{Ω̄} u = max_{∂Ω} u. *Proof:* {u = M} is closed in U and open by the ball average (a continuous function ≤ M with average M on a ball equals M there); connectedness. Weak from strong on each component. Corollary: uniqueness for the Dirichlet problem with u ∈ C²(Ω) ∩ C(Ω̄) (a second route to `cor-dirichlet-uniqueness-energy` with weaker regularity).

**`def-fundamental-solution`.** Φ(x) = −(1/2π) log|x| (d = 2), Φ(x) = |x|^{2−d}/((d − 2)σ(S^{d−1})) (d ≥ 3), x ≠ 0. Facts (`prop-fundamental-solution`): Φ ∈ C^∞(ℝᵈ∖{0}), ΔΦ = 0 there, ∇Φ(x) = −x/(σ(S^{d−1})|x|ᵈ) (so −∇Φ is the Gauss field of the hook divided by σ(S^{d−1})), Φ ∈ L¹_loc(ℝᵈ) (MT.5 integrability with α = d − 2 < d, or the logarithm).

**Hook paid again: `thm-representation-compact-support`.** For u ∈ C_c²(ℝᵈ): u(0) = −∫_{ℝᵈ} Φ Δu dm. *Proof:* excise B_ε (named move; the text writes the volume step and leaves the reader to write the two boundary estimates from the hint "size of Φ and of ∂_νΦ on ∂B_ε"): ∫ΦΔu = lim_{ε→0} ∫_{|x|>ε} ΦΔu by DCT (Φ ∈ L¹_loc, Δu bounded with compact support); on Ω_ε = B_R ∖ B̄_ε (supp u ⊂ B_R) the second identity with v = Φ gives ∫_{Ω_ε} ΦΔu = ∫_{∂B_ε}(Φ∂_νu − u∂_νΦ) dσ with ν = −x/ε; the first term is O(ε|log ε|) (d = 2) or O(ε) (d ≥ 3); ∂_νΦ = σ(S^{d−1})^{−1}ε^{1−d}, so the second term is −⨍_{∂B_ε} u → −u(0).
