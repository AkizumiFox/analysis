# MT.11: Signed Measures, Radon–Nikodym and Lebesgue Decomposition

Directory: `src/ch53-signed-measures-radon-nikodym/`. State: not started.

**Part IV, Measure and Integration.** Starting from Part III's list of what the Riemann integral lacks, build Lebesgue measure and the Lebesgue integral on ℝᵈ, then abstract measures, products (Fubini–Tonelli and change of variables in full), surfaces and the divergence theorem, Lᵖ, modes of convergence, differentiation, and signed measures up to Radon–Nikodym, ending with probability on measure spaces (the laws of large numbers and the central limit theorem). Lusin and the density of C_c use only the metric Urysohn and Tietze theorems of Part II.

*decisions.md 9: L² is used only for von Neumann's proof, and the L² facts it needs (nearest point, Riesz representation) are proved here for L²(μ) concretely; the abstract Hilbert-space chapter in Part VI will recall this as its first instance (spiral). No Lᵖ duality here even though RN makes it one step away — flag it as the Part VI forward hook. The Part IV qualifying-exam check has moved to Gate IV at the end of probability-measure-spaces, the Part's last chapter.*

## Planned sections

1. Signed measures; positive, negative and null sets; the Hahn decomposition; the Jordan decomposition; total variation |ν|
2. Complex measures and their total variation (brief)
3. Absolute continuity and mutual singularity; the ε-δ characterization for finite ν
4. An L² toolkit proved for L²(μ) only: parallelogram law, nearest point in a closed subspace, every bounded linear functional on L²(μ) is integration against some g
5. Radon–Nikodym and Lebesgue decomposition by von Neumann's proof: finite measures, then σ-finite; the chain rule for dν/dμ
6. Where it fails: Lebesgue measure ≪ counting measure on [0,1] with no density
7. Measures on ℝᵈ and ℝ: differentiation of measures dν/dm = lim ν(B)/m(B) a.e.; NBV functions ↔ finite Borel measures, F AC ⇔ μ_F ≪ m, and the BV decomposition as a Lebesgue decomposition

## Must prove

- Hahn decomposition theorem
- Jordan decomposition theorem
- ν ≪ μ ⇔ ε-δ condition (ν finite)
- L²(μ) projection onto a closed subspace and representation of bounded functionals on L²(μ)
- Radon–Nikodym theorem (σ-finite) via von Neumann
- Lebesgue decomposition theorem, with uniqueness
- dν/dm(x) = lim ν(B(x,r))/m(B(x,r)) a.e. for regular ν on ℝᵈ
- F ∈ NBV is AC ⇔ μ_F ≪ m

## Key examples and counterexamples

- ν(E) = ∫_E f dμ with f of both signs; its Hahn sets
- Dirac δ₀ ⊥ m
- Cantor measure ⊥ m
- counting vs Lebesgue on [0,1]: RN fails without σ-finiteness

- Prove the Hahn decomposition
