## Chapter hook and where it is paid

**Hook (opening of the chapter, half a page).** MT.10 split every increasing F on ℝ by hand into F_ac + F_j + F_s (`thm-decomposition-increasing`), and the tool was the derivative: F_ac(x) = ∫ F′, the jumps are read off F, the rest has F′ = 0 a.e. Two questions are put to the reader and left open:

1. *When is a measure an integral?* On an arbitrary measure space there are no derivatives. Which measures ν are of the form ν(E) = ∫_E f dμ? An obvious necessary condition: μ(E) = 0 ⇒ ν(E) = 0. Is it enough — and how would one even produce f?
2. *Does MT.10's split survive without derivatives?* Given two measures μ, ν on any space, is ν = (a part with a density) + (a part living where μ does not)? And when the space is ℝᵈ, is the density still a derivative?

**Paid:** question 1 at `thm-radon-nikodym` (§5): yes when μ and ν are σ-finite; f comes from FA.6's Riesz representation; W6/W7 show σ-finiteness cannot be dropped. Question 2 at `thm-lebesgue-decomposition` (§5) for the split, and at `thm-differentiation-of-measures` and `thm-nbv-lebesgue-decomposition` (§6) for "the density is still a derivative" and "on ℝ this is MT.10's split". The chapter's last paragraph returns to MT.10's decomposition and to the Cantor function explicitly.

**Hooks this chapter pays from earlier chapters.** FA.6 `rem-radon-nikodym-preview` (φ ↦ ∫φ dν on L²(μ + ν); Riesz hands back a density) — paid in §5 by `lem-von-neumann-finite`. **Hooks this chapter opens (forward).** (a) §5 closing remark: for 1 ≤ p < ∞ and σ-finite μ, a bounded linear functional on Lᵖ(μ) restricted to indicator functions is a measure ≪ μ; Radon–Nikodym gives the candidate g. ### §1 Signed measures (budget 3,200)

**Opening (failure-motivated definition).** Start from ν_f(E) = ∫_E f dμ for f ∈ L¹(μ): countably additive, possibly negative. Try to allow any measurable f: on ℝ with f = 1 on [0, ∞), f = −1 on (−∞, 0), ν_f(ℝ) would be ∞ − ∞. The definition must forbid one of the two infinities; that is the only change from a measure. Second failure: try defining "ν restricted to A is positive" by ν(A) ≥ 0 — W1 kills it, which motivates hereditary positivity.

- `def-signed-measure`. On a measurable space (X, 𝓜): ν: 𝓜 → [−∞, ∞] with ν(∅) = 0, taking at most one of the values +∞, −∞, and countably additive: for disjoint E_1, E_2, … ∈ 𝓜, ν(⋃E_j) = Σ ν(E_j), the series converging absolutely when the left side is finite (and diverging to the right infinity otherwise). Note: absolute convergence is forced — the left side does not depend on the order, so AN1.3 (Riemann) gives it.
- `exm-signed-measure-from-density`. f measurable with ∫ f⁻ dμ < ∞ (or ∫ f⁺ < ∞): ν_f(E) = ∫_E f⁺ − ∫_E f⁻ is a signed measure. Also μ_1 − μ_2 for positive measures, one of them finite.
- `prp-signed-measure-continuity`. (a) E_1 ⊆ E_2 ⊆ … ⇒ ν(⋃E_j) = lim ν(E_j). (b) E_1 ⊇ E_2 ⊇ … with ν(E_1) finite ⇒ ν(⋂E_j) = lim ν(E_j). (c) If ν omits +∞ and ν(E) is finite, then every measurable subset of E has finite measure. *Proof sketch:* (a), (b) as for measures, by disjointification and countable additivity. (c): if B ⊆ E had ν(B) = −∞ then ν(E) = ν(B) + ν(E ∖ B) = −∞ since ν(E ∖ B) < ∞.
- `def-positive-negative-null-set`. E ∈ 𝓜 is positive for ν if ν(F) ≥ 0 for every measurable F ⊆ E; negative if ν(F) ≤ 0 for all such F; null if both. Warning: a null set is not the same as a set of measure 0. In W1, E = [1/2, 2] has ν(E) = 2·(1/2) − 1 = 0, but its subset [1/2, 1] has measure 1.
- `prp-positive-sets-closed`. Measurable subsets of a positive set are positive; a countable union of positive sets is positive. *Sketch:* disjointify P_n' = P_n ∖ ⋃_{k<n} P_k ⊆ P_n; F ⊆ ⋃P_n gives ν(F) = Σ ν(F ∩ P_n') ≥ 0.
- `lem-positive-subset`. If ν omits +∞, E ∈ 𝓜 and 0 < ν(E) < ∞, then E has a positive subset A with ν(A) ≥ ν(E) > 0. (The symmetric version for ν omitting −∞ follows by applying it to −ν; state it as a remark.)

  **Scratch Work (first appearance of the greedy "smallest integer" construction; set apart from the proof).** Idea: E fails to be positive because it has subsets of negative measure; throw them away. Throwing away an arbitrary negative subset may take forever and remove nothing substantial, so each time remove one that is nearly as negative as possible — measured coarsely by the smallest n with a subset below −1/n. Because ν(E) is finite, the removed amounts form a convergent series, so the thresholds 1/n_k → 0; whatever is left can have no subset below −1/(n_k − 1) for any k, hence none below 0. Removing negative pieces only raises the measure, which gives ν(A) ≥ ν(E).

  *Proof sketch.* If E is positive take A = E. Otherwise let n_1 be the smallest positive integer such that some measurable E_1 ⊆ E has ν(E_1) < −1/n_1. Recursively, if E ∖ (E_1 ∪ … ∪ E_{k−1}) is not positive, let n_k be the smallest positive integer with a measurable E_k ⊆ E ∖ (E_1 ∪ … ∪ E_{k−1}) and ν(E_k) < −1/n_k; if it is positive, stop and take A to be that set. In the infinite case put A = E ∖ ⋃E_k. By `prp-signed-measure-continuity` (c) all these sets have finite measure, and ν(E) = ν(A) + Σ ν(E_k), so Σ 1/n_k < Σ |ν(E_k)| < ∞ and n_k → ∞; also ν(A) = ν(E) − Σν(E_k) ≥ ν(E) > 0. If F ⊆ A had ν(F) < 0, pick k with n_k ≥ 2 and ν(F) < −1/(n_k − 1); F ⊆ E ∖ (E_1 ∪ … ∪ E_{k−1}), contradicting minimality of n_k.

**Worked example.** `exm-hahn-sets-by-hand`: ν_f on ℝ with f(x) = x e^{−x²}; find a positive set, a negative set, and a set of measure 0 that is not null.

**Quick checks.** (Q1.1) Why does a signed measure with ν(E) = +∞ for some E satisfy ν(F) > −∞ for every F? *Answer:* it omits one infinity, and it takes +∞. (Q1.2) Is the empty set positive? Negative? *Answer:* both; it is null.

**Exercises.**
- A1. Show ν(E ∖ F) = ν(E) − ν(F) when F ⊆ E and ν(F) is finite. *Answer:* additivity on E = F ⊔ (E ∖ F).
- A2. For ν = δ_0 − δ_1 on the Borel sets of ℝ, list all positive sets. *Answer:* the Borel sets not containing 1.
- A3. Give a signed measure ν and sets E ⊆ F with ν(E) > ν(F). *Answer:* W1 with E = [0, 1], F = [0, 2].
- B1. Show that the sum of two signed measures that both omit +∞ is a signed measure, and give two signed measures whose sum is not defined. *Answer:* termwise sums of series in (−∞, ∞] are defined and countably additive; ν_1 = ∞·δ_0 and ν_2 = −∞·δ_0 have no sum at {0}.
- B2. Let ν = ∞·δ_0 − δ_1 on the subsets of {0, 1}, E = {0, 1}. Show that the greedy construction of `lem-positive-subset` still runs but its estimate Σ 1/n_k < ∞ is no longer justified by the proof, and find a positive A ⊆ E with ν(A) > 0 directly. *Answer:* E_1 = {1}, n_1 = 1, then {0} is positive; the proof's bound used ν(E) < ∞. A = {0}. (The statement survives for ν(E) = ∞ by §2; the proof does not.)
- B3. Let ν be a signed measure and E_n ↓ with ν(E_1) = −∞ and ⋂E_n = ∅. Show that ν(E_n) = −∞ for all n is possible. *Answer:* ν = −counting on ℕ, E_n = {n, n+1, …}.
- C1. (Write-up.) Prove `lem-positive-subset` for ν omitting −∞ directly, without passing to −ν, adapting the greedy construction; identify exactly where finiteness of ν(E) enters. *Answer:* same construction; finiteness bounds Σν(E_k) below. Marking scheme: construction (3), convergence of Σ1/n_k (3), positivity of A (3), ν(A) ≥ ν(E) (1).
- C2. Show that every signed measure is bounded on one side: if ν omits +∞ then sup{ν(E): E ∈ 𝓜} < ∞. *Answer:* otherwise E_n with ν(E_n) ≥ n; by `lem-positive-subset` take positive A_n ⊆ E_n with ν(A_n) ≥ n; ⋃A_n is positive with ν(⋃A_n) ≥ ν(A_n) ≥ n for all n, so = +∞, contradiction. (Fading: this is the "union of positive sets" step of §2 met early.)

### §2 The Hahn and Jordan decompositions (budget 3,800)

**Opening.** For ν_f the obvious split is X = {f ≥ 0} ⊔ {f < 0}. With no f in sight, find the split anyway.

- `thm-hahn-decomposition`. For every signed measure ν on (X, 𝓜) there are disjoint P, N ∈ 𝓜 with X = P ∪ N, P positive, N negative. If (P′, N′) is another such pair, P △ P′ (= N △ N′) is ν-null.

  **Scratch Work (first appearance of "attain a supremum by a countable union"; this is the chapter's faded move).** We want P as large as possible among positive sets. "Largest" by inclusion need not exist, but the largest *value* of ν(P) does: take s = sup{ν(P): P positive}, a sequence P_j with ν(P_j) → s, and the union P = ⋃P_j. Unions of positive sets are positive, and ν(P) ≥ ν(P_j) for every j, so the sup is attained. Then whatever is left can contain no set of positive measure — otherwise `lem-positive-subset` hands us a positive piece to add, beating s.

  *Proof sketch.* WLOG ν omits +∞ (else use −ν and swap P, N). s as above; s < ∞ by §1 C2 or directly since ν(P) = s is a value of ν. P = ⋃P_j positive (`prp-positive-sets-closed`), ν(P) = s. N = X ∖ P. If E ⊆ N, ν(E) > 0, then ν(E) < ∞, so `lem-positive-subset` gives positive A ⊆ E with ν(A) > 0; P ∪ A is positive and ν(P ∪ A) = s + ν(A) > s. Contradiction. Uniqueness: P ∖ P′ ⊆ P ∩ N′ is both positive and negative, so null; same for P′ ∖ P.
- `def-mutually-singular` (for positive measures here; extended in §4). Positive measures λ, ρ on (X, 𝓜) are mutually singular, λ ⊥ ρ, if X = E ⊔ F with λ(E) = 0 and ρ(F) = 0. *Failure motivation:* "λ and ρ never both give a set positive measure" is too weak to be useful and does not even mean anything for overlapping sets; the right notion is "they live on disjoint sets."
- `thm-jordan-decomposition-measure`. Every signed measure ν is ν = ν⁺ − ν⁻ for a unique pair of mutually singular positive measures ν⁺, ν⁻, at least one finite. Moreover, if ν = λ_1 − λ_2 with λ_i positive measures (one finite), then ν⁺ ≤ λ_1 and ν⁻ ≤ λ_2. *Sketch:* existence: ν⁺(E) = ν(E ∩ P), ν⁻(E) = −ν(E ∩ N). Uniqueness: given ν = μ⁺ − μ⁻ with μ⁺(F) = 0 = μ⁻(E), X = E ⊔ F, then E is positive and F negative for ν, so (E, F) is a Hahn pair; by `thm-hahn-decomposition`, E △ P is null; compute μ⁺(A) = ν(A ∩ E) = ν(A ∩ P) = ν⁺(A). Minimality: ν⁺(A) = ν(A ∩ P) = λ_1(A ∩ P) − λ_2(A ∩ P) ≤ λ_1(A).
- `def-total-variation-signed`. |ν| = ν⁺ + ν⁻; ν is finite if |ν|(X) < ∞.
- `prp-total-variation-signed-properties`. (a) |ν(E)| ≤ |ν|(E). (b) E is ν-null ⇔ |ν|(E) = 0. (c) |ν|(E) = sup{Σ_{j=1}^n |ν(E_j)| : n ≥ 1, E_1, …, E_n ∈ 𝓜 disjoint with union E}. *Sketch:* (c) ≥: Σ|ν(E_j)| ≤ Σ|ν|(E_j) = |ν|(E); ≤: the partition E ∩ P, E ∩ N attains it. (c) is the bridge to §3.
- `def-integral-signed-measure`. L¹(ν) := L¹(|ν|); ∫ g dν := ∫ g dν⁺ − ∫ g dν⁻. `prp-integral-signed-bound`: |∫ g dν| ≤ ∫ |g| d|ν|.

**Worked examples.** `exm-hahn-for-density`: for ν_f, the pair ({f ≥ 0}, {f < 0}) is a Hahn decomposition and |ν_f| = ν_{|f|}. `exm-jordan-not-minimal`: δ_0 = 2δ_0 − δ_0 is a non-minimal decomposition; Jordan picks (δ_0, 0).

**Quick checks.** (Q2.1) For ν = δ_0 − δ_1 on ℝ, give two different Hahn decompositions. *Answer:* P = {0}, N = ℝ ∖ {0}; P = ℝ ∖ {1}, N = {1}. (Q2.2) Is |ν(E)| = |ν|(E) for all E possible with ν ≠ ν⁺, ν ≠ −ν⁻? *Answer:* no. If ν⁺ ≠ 0 and ν⁻ ≠ 0, pick A ⊆ P, B ⊆ N with 0 < ν(A) < ∞ and 0 < −ν(B) < ∞ (possible by continuity from below); E = A ∪ B has |ν(E)| < ν(A) − ν(B) = |ν|(E).

**Exercises.**
- A1. Compute ν⁺, ν⁻, |ν| for ν = ν_f on [0, 2π], f = sin. *Answer:* ν⁺ = ν_{sin⁺}, |ν|([0, 2π]) = 4.
- A2. Show (ν⁺ ⊥ ν⁻ and) |ν|(E) = 0 ⇔ every measurable subset of E has ν-measure 0. *Answer:* `prp-total-variation-signed-properties` (b).
- A3. True or false: if ν(E) ≥ 0 for every E in a Hahn negative set N, then ν⁻ = 0. *Answer:* true: on N, ν ≤ 0 and ≥ 0, so ν⁻(E) = −ν(E ∩ N) = 0.
- B1. Prove ν⁺(E) = sup{ν(F): F ⊆ E measurable} and ν⁻(E) = −inf{ν(F): F ⊆ E}. *Answer:* ≥ via F = E ∩ P; ≤ via ν(F) = ν⁺(F) − ν⁻(F) ≤ ν⁺(E).
- B2. Let ν_1, ν_2 be finite signed measures. Show |ν_1 + ν_2| ≤ |ν_1| + |ν_2|. *Answer:* partition formula (c).
- B3. (Faded move, last step blank: "Name the move, then complete it.") Let λ be a finite positive measure and μ a positive measure on (X, 𝓜). Put s = sup{λ(E): μ(E) = 0}. Choose E_j with μ(E_j) = 0 and λ(E_j) → s, and let E_0 = ⋃E_j. Then μ(E_0) = 0 and λ(E_0) = s. [Blank: show that every F ⊆ X ∖ E_0 with μ(F) = 0 has λ(F) = 0.] *Answer:* else E_0 ∪ F is μ-null with λ-measure > s. (This set E_0 reappears in §4 B2 and §5.)
- C1. (Write-up, exit check 2.) Prove the Hahn decomposition theorem from `lem-positive-subset`. Marking scheme: reduction to ν omitting +∞ (1); sup attained by a countable union (3); complement negative via the lemma (4); uniqueness up to null sets (2).
- C2. Let ν be a signed measure and g ∈ L¹(|ν|). Show that E ↦ ∫_E g dν is a signed measure whose total variation is E ↦ ∫_E |g| d|ν|. *Answer:* Hahn set for the new measure is ({g ≥ 0} ∩ P) ∪ ({g < 0} ∩ N); compute.

### §3 Complex measures and the space M(X) (budget 3,200)

**Opening (failure-motivated definition).** For complex f ∈ L¹(μ), ν_f(E) = ∫_E f dμ is complex-valued and countably additive; there is no positive/negative split, so "size" must be defined differently. First attempt |ν|(E) := |ν(E)| fails: it is not additive (δ_0 − δ_1 has |ν(ℝ)| = 0). Second attempt: break E into pieces and add the sizes; the finer the breaking, the less cancellation; take the sup. That is the definition.

- `def-complex-measure`. ν: 𝓜 → ℂ, ν(∅) = 0, countably additive (the series then converge absolutely, by AN1.3 applied to real and imaginary parts). Real and imaginary parts ν_r, ν_i are finite signed measures. A complex measure is never infinite.
- `def-total-variation-complex`. |ν|(E) = sup{Σ_{j=1}^n |ν(E_j)| : E_1, …, E_n ∈ 𝓜 disjoint, union E}. Agrees with §2 for finite signed measures by `prp-total-variation-signed-properties` (c).
- `thm-total-variation-measure`. |ν| is a finite positive measure, and |ν(E)| ≤ |ν|(E) for all E.

  **Scratch Work (first appearance of "refine and regroup").** Countable additivity of a sup of finite sums needs two inequalities. For ≤: a partition of E = ⊔E_k is cut by the E_k into a finer family; the triangle inequality only grows the sum when we refine, and the refined pieces regroup as partitions of each E_k. For ≥: partitions of finitely many E_1, …, E_K together form a partition of a subset of E; monotonicity of |ν| (adding the leftover piece only adds a term) finishes. Finiteness is the cheap part here because we already have Jordan: |ν(E)| ≤ |ν_r(E)| + |ν_i(E)|.

  *Proof sketch.* Monotone: add E ∖ F to any partition of F. ≤: for a partition (F_j) of E, Σ_j |ν(F_j)| = Σ_j |Σ_k ν(F_j ∩ E_k)| ≤ Σ_k Σ_j |ν(F_j ∩ E_k)| ≤ Σ_k |ν|(E_k) (absolute convergence justifies the swap). ≥: given partitions of E_1, …, E_K with sums within ε/K of |ν|(E_k), their union partitions ⋃_{k ≤ K} E_k, so Σ_{k ≤ K} |ν|(E_k) − ε ≤ |ν|(⋃_{k≤K}E_k) ≤ |ν|(E). Finite: |ν|(X) ≤ |ν_r|(X) + |ν_i|(X) < ∞ from the partition sums.
- `def-measure-space-M`. M(X, 𝓜) (written M(X)): the complex measures on (X, 𝓜), a complex vector space under setwise operations, with ‖ν‖ = |ν|(X).
- `thm-measures-banach`. ‖·‖ is a norm and M(X) is a Banach space. *Sketch:* triangle inequality from partition sums. Completeness: (ν_n) Cauchy ⇒ |ν_n(E) − ν_k(E)| ≤ ‖ν_n − ν_k‖ uniformly in E, so ν(E) = lim ν_n(E) exists uniformly; finite additivity passes to the limit; countable additivity: |ν(⋃_{j>J} E_j)| ≤ ε + |ν_n|(⋃_{j>J}E_j) → ε by continuity from above of the finite measure |ν_n|; ‖ν − ν_n‖ ≤ sup_{k ≥ n} ‖ν_k − ν_n‖ by taking limits in each partition sum.

**Worked example.** `exm-total-variation-dirac-combination`: ν = Σ c_k δ_{x_k} with Σ|c_k| < ∞ and distinct x_k has ‖ν‖ = Σ|c_k|; so ℓ¹ sits isometrically inside M(ℝ). Closing remark: the forward hook to FA.2 (M(X) is the dual of C₀(X) for locally compact Hausdorff X).

**Quick checks.** (Q3.1) Compute |ν|(ℝ) for ν = δ_0 + iδ_1. *Answer:* 2, not √2. (Q3.2) Why is "sup over countable partitions" the same number? *Answer:* for a countable partition (E_j), Σ_{j ≤ n} |ν(E_j)| + |ν(⋃_{j>n} E_j)| is a finite-partition sum, and its last term → 0; conversely finite partitions are countable ones.

**Exercises.**

### §4 Absolute continuity and mutual singularity (budget 3,400)

**Opening.** The necessary condition of the hook's question 1 gets a name; the opposite extreme — measures living on disjoint sets — got one in §2. Both are extended to signed and complex ν.

- `def-absolutely-continuous-measure`. For ν signed or complex and μ positive on (X, 𝓜): ν ≪ μ if μ(E) = 0 ⇒ ν(E) = 0.

- `prp-ac-iff-total-variation-ac`. ν ≪ μ ⇔ |ν| ≪ μ ⇔ (signed case) ν⁺ ≪ μ and ν⁻ ≪ μ. *Sketch:* if μ(E) = 0, every subset of E and every piece of a partition of E is μ-null; for signed, ν⁺(E) = ν(E ∩ P) = 0.
- `prp-ac-and-singular-zero`. (a) ν ≪ μ and ν ⊥ μ ⇒ ν = 0. (b) Sums: ν_1, ν_2 ⊥ μ ⇒ ν_1 + ν_2 ⊥ μ (when defined); same for ≪. (c) λ ≪ μ and ν ⊥ μ ⇒ λ ⊥ ν. *Sketch:* (a) ν is concentrated on a μ-null set E, and |ν|(E) = 0 by `prp-ac-iff-total-variation-ac`. (b) union of two null sets.
- `thm-ac-epsilon-delta`. Let ν be a complex measure (or a finite signed measure) and μ positive. Then ν ≪ μ ⇔ for every ε > 0 there is δ > 0 with μ(E) < δ ⇒ |ν(E)| < ε.

  **Scratch Work (first appearance in this volume of "Borel–Cantelli contradiction").** ⇐ is immediate (μ(E) = 0 < δ for every δ). For ⇒, suppose some ε defeats every δ: for δ = 2^{−n} get E_n with μ(E_n) < 2^{−n} but |ν|(E_n) ≥ ε (work with |ν|, a finite positive measure with |ν| ≪ μ). The E_n are μ-small but |ν|-large; their lim sup F = ⋂_k ⋃_{n≥k} E_n is μ-null by Borel–Cantelli and |ν|-large by continuity from above — which needs |ν| finite. That is why W3 escapes.

(MT.2 or MT.8 may already have this as "absolute continuity of the integral"; if so, present this as a second proof and cite it — S2a to check.)

### §5 The Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodym theorem (budget 5,000)

**Opening.** Answer the hook's question 1. Paying it requires producing a function f out of nothing but set function data; the Riesz representation theorem of FA.6 produces vectors out of functionals, and integration against ν is a functional. That is the whole idea.

- `lem-von-neumann-finite`. Let μ, ν be finite positive measures on (X, 𝓜). There are S ∈ 𝓜 with μ(S) = 0 and a measurable f: X → [0, ∞) with ∫ f dμ < ∞ such that ν(E) = ν(E ∩ S) + ∫_E f dμ for all E ∈ 𝓜.

  **Scratch Work (first appearance of "dominate both measures by their sum" and "Riesz representation in L²(ρ)").** Neither μ nor ν dominates the other, but ρ = μ + ν dominates both: ρ(E) = 0 ⇒ μ(E) = ν(E) = 0. On the real Hilbert space L²(ρ), g ↦ ∫ g dν is linear and bounded (Cauchy–Schwarz against the constant 1, which is in L²(ρ) because ρ is finite). FA.6 gives h with ∫ g dν = ∫ g h dρ = ∫ g h dμ + ∫ g h dν. Testing on indicators shows 0 ≤ h ≤ 1, and moving terms gives ∫ g(1 − h) dν = ∫ g h dμ. Where h = 1 the left side vanishes, so μ there is 0: that is the singular set S. Where h < 1, choose g = (1 + h + … + h^{n−1})χ_E to cancel the factor 1 − h; the geometric series sums to h/(1 − h). Every step is a choice of test function g.

  *Proof sketch.* φ(g) = ∫ g dν on L²(ρ) (real-valued): well-defined on classes since ρ-null ⇒ ν-null; |φ(g)| ≤ ∫|g| dρ ≤ ρ(X)^{1/2} ‖g‖_{L²(ρ)}. By FA.6 there is h ∈ L²(ρ) with (∗) ∫ g dν = ∫ g h dρ for all g ∈ L²(ρ). With g = χ_E: 0 ≤ ν(E) = ∫_E h dρ ≤ ρ(E). If ρ({h < 0}) > 0 then ∫_{h<0} h dρ < 0, contradiction; if ρ({h > 1}) > 0 then ∫_{h>1} h dρ > ρ({h > 1}) ≥ ν({h > 1}), contradiction. Choose a representative with 0 ≤ h ≤ 1 everywhere. Rewrite (∗) as (∗∗) ∫ g(1 − h) dν = ∫ g h dμ for bounded measurable g, and by MCT for all measurable g ≥ 0. S = {h = 1}; g = χ_S gives μ(S) = 0. For E ∈ 𝓜, n ≥ 1, g = (1 + h + … + h^{n−1}) χ_{E∖S}: ∫_{E∖S} (1 − hⁿ) dν = ∫_{E∖S} (h + … + hⁿ) dμ. On X ∖ S, hⁿ ↓ 0, so the left side → ν(E ∖ S) (MCT on 1 − hⁿ ↑ 1); the right side → ∫_{E∖S} f dμ with f = h/(1 − h) on X ∖ S, f = 0 on S (MCT). Hence ν(E ∖ S) = ∫_E f dμ (μ(S) = 0), and ∫ f dμ = ν(X ∖ S) < ∞.
- `thm-lebesgue-decomposition`. Let μ be a σ-finite positive measure and ν a σ-finite signed measure (or a complex measure) on (X, 𝓜). There are unique signed (complex) measures ν_a, ν_s with ν = ν_a + ν_s, ν_a ≪ μ, ν_s ⊥ μ. If ν is positive, so are ν_a and ν_s.
- `thm-radon-nikodym`. (Registry label; registry statement A2/R3.) Let μ be a σ-finite positive measure and ν a σ-finite signed measure (or complex measure) with ν ≪ μ. There is a measurable f (extended real-valued with ∫ f⁻ dμ < ∞ or ∫ f⁺ dμ < ∞ in the signed case; f ∈ L¹(μ) in the complex case; f ≥ 0 if ν is positive) with ν(E) = ∫_E f dμ for all E ∈ 𝓜; f is unique μ-a.e.

  *Proof sketch for both.* Positive finite case: `lem-von-neumann-finite`, ν_s = ν(· ∩ S), ν_a = ν_f. σ-finite positive: X = ⊔X_n with μ(X_n), ν(X_n) < ∞ (intersect the two sequences); apply on each X_n and add (f = Σ f χ_{X_n}, S = ⋃S_n). Signed: apply to ν⁺ and ν⁻ (σ-finite since ν is: |ν| σ-finite, define σ-finite signed as |ν| σ-finite); complex: to ν_r^±, ν_i^±. Uniqueness of the decomposition: on each X_n where everything is finite, ν_a − ν_a′ = ν_s′ − ν_s is both ≪ μ and ⊥ μ (`prp-ac-and-singular-zero` (b)), hence 0. Radon–Nikodym: if ν ≪ μ then ν_s ≪ μ and ν_s ⊥ μ, so ν_s = 0. Uniqueness of f from MT.2 on each X_n.
- `def-radon-nikodym-derivative`. dν/dμ := f, defined μ-a.e.; notation dν = f dμ.
- `prp-integrate-against-density`. If ν ≥ 0, dν = f dμ, then ∫ g dν = ∫ g f dμ for measurable g ≥ 0, and g ∈ L¹(ν) ⇔ gf ∈ L¹(μ). *Sketch:* indicators, simple functions, MCT.
- `prp-chain-rule-rn`. (a) If ν ≪ μ ≪ λ (σ-finite, ν signed or complex), dν/dλ = (dν/dμ)(dμ/dλ) λ-a.e. (b) If μ ≪ ν ≪ μ (positive, σ-finite), (dν/dμ)(dμ/dν) = 1 a.e. *Sketch:* `prp-integrate-against-density` with g = (dν/dμ)χ_E, then uniqueness.
- `thm-total-variation-of-density`. If μ is positive, f ∈ L¹(μ) (complex) and ν = ν_f, then |ν| = ν_{|f|}, i.e. d|ν| = |f| dμ. *Sketch:* ≤: Σ|ν(E_j)| ≤ Σ ∫_{E_j} |f| dμ. ≥: let σ = f̄/|f| on {f ≠ 0}, 0 elsewhere; simple s_n with |s_n| ≤ 1, s_n → σ pointwise; |∫_E s_n f dμ| ≤ Σ_k |c_k||ν(E ∩ A_k)| ≤ |ν|(E); DCT gives ∫_E |f| dμ ≤ |ν|(E).
- `cor-polar-decomposition`. For a complex measure ν, ν ≪ |ν| and h = dν/d|ν| satisfies |h| = 1 |ν|-a.e.; dν = h d|ν|. *Sketch:* `thm-radon-nikodym` with μ = |ν| (finite), then `thm-total-variation-of-density` gives |ν|(E) = ∫_E |h| d|ν| for all E, so |h| = 1 a.e. For finite signed ν, h = χ_P − χ_N.
- `def-integral-complex-measure`. For g ∈ L¹(|ν|), ∫ g dν := ∫ g h d|ν|; then |∫ g dν| ≤ ∫ |g| d|ν|, and this agrees with §2 for signed ν.

**Weird examples in this section:** `exm-rn-counting-fails` (W6, both halves), `exm-rn-infinite-weight-fails` (W7), `exm-equivalent-unbounded-density` (W8). Each W is introduced as the answer to "which hypothesis did the proof use?": W6 breaks σ-finiteness of the reference measure; W7 shows that even with ν finite the reference measure must be σ-finite.

**Worked examples.** `exm-rn-gaussian`: for the standard Gaussian measure γ on ℝ, dγ/dm = (2π)^{−1/2}e^{−x²/2} and dm/dγ is its reciprocal (`prp-chain-rule-rn` (b)). `exm-rn-discrete`: X countable, μ counting, ν any measure: dν/dμ(x) = ν({x}). `exm-rn-pushforward-linear`: for T(x) = 2x on ℝ, the image measure m∘T^{−1} = (1/2) m, so its density is the constant 1/2 (ties to MT.5's linear change of variables).

**Forward remarks (hooks opened).** Lᵖ duality (FA.1b), conditional expectation (PR.2), as in "Chapter hook" above. Two short paragraphs, no proofs.

**Quick checks.** (Q5.1) If ν ≪ μ and dν/dμ = 0 a.e., what is ν? *Answer:* 0. (Q5.2) In the proof of `lem-von-neumann-finite`, why is χ_E ∈ L²(ρ)? *Answer:* ρ(E) ≤ ρ(X) < ∞. (Q5.3) What is d|ν|/dμ for ν = ν_f? *Answer:* |f|.

**Exercises.**

### §6 Measures on ℝᵈ and ℝ: derivatives of measures and NBV functions (budget 4,200)

**Opening.** MT.9 computed f(x) as the limit of averages A_r f(x) = ν(B(x,r))/m(B(x,r)) for ν = f dm, and planted the question of what this ratio does for δ₀ and for the Cantor measure. With the Lebesgue decomposition in hand the answer is uniform: the ratio converges a.e. to the density of the absolutely continuous part, and the singular part is invisible to it. On ℝ, complex measures and NBV functions are the same objects, and MT.10's by-hand split of increasing functions is the Lebesgue decomposition read through this dictionary. MT.10 already proved the increasing case of everything on ℝ; this section adds what needs the new tools: general d, signed and complex measures, and the singular part's ratios.

- `lem-singular-density-zero`. Let λ be a positive Borel measure on ℝᵈ, finite on compact sets and outer regular, with λ ⊥ m. Then lim_{r→0} λ(B(x, r))/m(B(x, r)) = 0 for m-a.e. x.

  **Scratch Work (first appearance of "outer regularity on the complement of the carrier").** λ lives on an m-null Borel set A. The danger is at points x ∉ A where λ still has much mass near x relative to volume. Outer regularity approximates the λ-null set ℝᵈ ∖ A from outside by an open U with λ(U) < ε — we approximate the complement of the carrier, not the carrier. Every bad point lies in U, so its witnessing small ball can be taken inside U; `lem-vitali-finite` picks disjoint balls carrying a 3^{−d} share of the volume; their λ-mass is at most λ(U) < ε. So the bad set has outer measure ≤ 3^d k ε. (Compare MT.10's `lem-maximal-measure-1d`, the same covering estimate for a whole finite measure; here the smallness comes from U, not from λ(ℝ).)

  *Proof sketch.* A Borel with m(A) = 0, λ(ℝᵈ ∖ A) = 0. F_k = {x ∈ ℝᵈ ∖ A : lim sup_{r→0} λ(B(x, r))/m(B(x, r)) > 1/k}. Given ε, open U ⊇ ℝᵈ ∖ A with λ(U) < ε. Each x ∈ F_k has r_x with B(x, r_x) ⊆ U and λ(B(x, r_x)) > m(B(x, r_x))/k. Let V = ⋃_x B(x, r_x) ⊇ F_k, open. For compact K ⊆ V, finitely many of the balls cover K; `lem-vitali-finite` gives disjoint B_1, …, B_n among them with m(K) ≤ 3^d Σ m(B_i) < 3^d k Σ λ(B_i) ≤ 3^d k λ(U) < 3^d k ε. Inner regularity of m (MT.1) gives m(V) ≤ 3^d k ε, so m*(F_k) ≤ 3^d k ε for every ε. F_k is null; the exceptional set A ∪ ⋃_k F_k is null. No measurability of F_k is needed.
- `thm-differentiation-of-measures`. Let ν be a signed Borel measure on ℝᵈ with |ν| finite on compact sets, or a complex Borel measure, and suppose |ν| is outer regular. Let dν = f dm + dν_s be its Lebesgue decomposition (`thm-lebesgue-decomposition`; m is σ-finite, |ν| is σ-finite). Then f ∈ L¹_loc, and for m-a.e. x, lim_{r→0} ν(E_r)/m(E_r) = f(x) for every family {E_r} shrinking regularly to x in MT.9's sense (in particular for balls B(x, r)). *Sketch:* the f dm part is `thm-differentiation-regular-families`. For the singular part, |ν_s(E_r)| ≤ |ν_s|(E_r) ≤ |ν_s|(B(x, r)) and m(E_r) ≥ c·m(B(x, r)); apply `lem-singular-density-zero` to |ν_s|, which is ⊥ m, finite on compacts, and outer regular because |ν_s| ≤ |ν| and |ν_s| is |ν| restricted to a Borel set (S2a: confirm the restriction of an outer regular locally finite measure to a Borel set is outer regular on ℝᵈ; true for locally finite Borel measures on ℝᵈ, all of which are regular — cite MT.3 if it proves this, else add the hypothesis "|ν_s| outer regular", as Folland's "regular" does).
- `def-nbv`. NBV: F: ℝ → ℂ of bounded variation on ℝ (sup over all partitions of all bounded intervals finite), right-continuous, with lim_{x→−∞} F(x) = 0.
- `thm-nbv-measures`. If ν is a complex Borel measure on ℝ then F_ν(x) = ν((−∞, x]) is in NBV. Conversely every F ∈ NBV is F_ν for a unique complex Borel measure ν, written μ_F. *Sketch:* ν ↦ F_ν: Σ|F(x_j) − F(x_{j−1})| = Σ|ν((x_{j−1}, x_j])| ≤ |ν|(ℝ); right-continuity and the limit at −∞ by continuity from above. Converse: real F ∈ NBV is G − H with G, H increasing (`thm-jordan-decomposition-recalled`, with G(x) = T_F(x) := total variation of F on (−∞, x]); G and H bounded, right-continuous (S2a item: right-continuity of T_F when F is right-continuous; a two-line lemma `lem-variation-right-continuous` here if MT.10 lacks it), with limit 0 at −∞; MT.3 gives finite μ_G, μ_H; complex F by real and imaginary parts. Uniqueness: MT.3's uniqueness from values on (a, b].
- `thm-nbv-ac-iff-measure-ac`. For F ∈ NBV the following are equivalent: (a) F is absolutely continuous on ℝ (global form: ∀ε ∃δ, finitely many disjoint (a_j, b_j) of total length < δ ⇒ Σ|F(b_j) − F(a_j)| < ε); (b) μ_F ≪ m. Then F′ ∈ L¹(ℝ), dμ_F/dm = F′ a.e., and F(x) = ∫_{−∞}^x F′ dm. *Sketch:* (b) ⇒ (a): μ_F = f dm with f ∈ L¹ (`thm-radon-nikodym`); |μ_F| = |f| dm (`thm-total-variation-of-density`); Σ|F(b_j) − F(a_j)| ≤ ∫_{⋃(a_j,b_j]} |f| < ε by `cor-ac-integral`. (a) ⇒ (b): F AC on ℝ ⇒ its real and imaginary parts' Jordan components G, H are AC (S2a: true, since T_F is AC when F is; cite MT.10 if present, else prove here in a paragraph), so MT.10's `thm-ac-iff-measure-ac` gives μ_G = G′ dm, μ_H = H′ dm; subtract. The identification dμ_F/dm = F′ uses uniqueness in `thm-radon-nikodym`.
- `thm-nbv-lebesgue-decomposition`. (Pays MT.10's closing remark.) Every F ∈ NBV splits uniquely as F = F_ac + F_sc + F_pp with each term in NBV: F_ac absolutely continuous; F_sc continuous with μ_{F_sc} ⊥ m; F_pp(x) = Σ_{t ≤ x} (F(t) − F(t−)), summed over the countable set of discontinuities. Moreover F′ exists m-a.e. and equals dμ_F/dm = F_ac′, and F_sc′ = F_pp′ = 0 a.e. For increasing F this is MT.10's `thm-decomposition-increasing` with F_j = F_pp, F_s = F_sc (up to the normalization). *Sketch:* `thm-lebesgue-decomposition` of μ_F with respect to m; split (μ_F)_s into its atomic part (atoms are countable: at most n|μ_F|(ℝ) atoms of |μ_F|-mass > 1/n) and the rest; translate back by `thm-nbv-measures`; uniqueness from uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition and of the atomic/non-atomic split. Derivatives: `thm-differentiation-of-measures` with d = 1 and the regular families (x, x + h], (x − h, x] gives F′(x) = dμ_F/dm(x) a.e. (|μ_F| is a finite Borel measure on ℝ, hence outer regular by MT.3's Lebesgue–Stieltjes regularity).

**Worked examples.** `exm-delta-and-cantor-ratios` (pays MT.9's planted question): for δ₀ the ratio → ∞ at 0 and → 0 elsewhere; for μ_C it → 0 off K (`lem-singular-density-zero`; K is null) and → ∞ on K (C3). `exm-nbv-three-parts`: F(x) = (x + C(x))/2 + (1/2)χ_{[1/2,∞)}(x), clamped to be constant on (−∞, 0] and [1, ∞) and normalized; find F_ac, F_sc, F_pp and the lost mass. *Answer:* on [0, 1], F_ac = x/2, F_sc = C/2, F_pp = (1/2)χ_{[1/2,∞)}; μ_F((0, 1]) = 3/2, ∫_0^1 F′ = 1/2, lost mass 1 (1/2 from the jump, 1/2 from C/2). `exm-strictly-increasing-singular-revisited` (W9): MT.10's F = Σ 2^{−n} c_n has μ_F = Σ 2^{−n} μ_{c_n}, a countable sum of measures each carried by an m-null affine copy of K, hence ⊥ m; so F′ = 0 a.e. by `thm-nbv-lebesgue-decomposition`, without MT.10's `thm-fubini-series-derivative`.

**Closing paragraph.** Returns to the hook: question 1 is `thm-radon-nikodym` with W6/W7 marking its edge; question 2 is `thm-lebesgue-decomposition`, and on ℝ it is exactly MT.10's split; the Cantor function is the pure singular continuous case.
