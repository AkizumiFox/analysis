## Planned sections

1. Product σ-algebras; measurability of sections of sets and functions (monotone class / π-λ)
2. Product measure of two σ-finite measures: existence and uniqueness
3. Tonelli's theorem and Fubini's theorem, full proofs
4. Lebesgue measure on ℝ^{d₁+d₂} as the completion of the product; the Fubini theorem for complete measures
5. Where the hypotheses bite: counting × Lebesgue on the diagonal, a double series with unequal iterated sums, a non-integrable f with unequal iterated integrals
6. Applications: measure of a set as the integral of its sections, area under a graph, convolution is defined a.e., integrability of |x|^{−α}, exchanging sums and integrals
7. Payoff for Part III: Fourier inversion for f ∈ L¹(ℝᵈ) with f̂ ∈ L¹, via Gaussians and Fubini

## Must prove

- sections of product-measurable sets and functions are measurable
- existence and uniqueness of μ × ν for σ-finite μ, ν
- Tonelli's theorem
- Fubini's theorem

- m(E) = ∫ m(E_x) dx

## Key examples and counterexamples

- counting measure × Lebesgue on [0,1]², diagonal set
- a_{mn} = 1 if m=n, −1 if m=n+1: unequal iterated sums
- (x²−y²)/(x²+y²)² on (0,1]²
- Sierpiński's set under CH (optional remark)
