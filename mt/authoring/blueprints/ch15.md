## Chapter hook and where it is paid

**Opening problem (chapter introduction, about 400 words).** Let f = 1_{[1/2,1]} on [0,1]. Minimize the energy

  E(u) = ∫₀¹ (½u′² + ½u² − f u) dx  over u ∈ C²[0,1] with u(0) = u(1) = 0.

E is bounded below on this class (by Cauchy–Schwarz and ‖u‖_∞ ≤ ‖u′‖₂), so the infimum is a real number. Yet no C² function attains it: a minimizer would have to satisfy −u″ + u = f, and then u″ = u − f jumps at 1/2. The reader is told the fault is not with the problem but with the space: C² with this energy is not complete. The chapter's job is to say which spaces are complete *and* have enough geometry for "minimize" to produce a point.

- **Paid in §2** (first payment): in a complete inner-product space, a closed convex set has a nearest point (thm-nearest-point). The chapter opener's "minimize" becomes "project".
- **Paid in full in §7** (ex-dirichlet-1d): the same E on the completed space H = {g ∈ L²(0,1) : ∫g = 0}, with u = Vg, has a unique minimizer by Lax–Milgram/Riesz; it is C¹ with u″ jumping at 1/2, which is exactly why C² failed.

**Second, smaller hook (opening of §2).** In the plane with the sup norm, the x-axis has infinitely many points nearest to (0,1); in C[0,1] with the sup norm, a closed convex set can have no nearest point to 0 at all. Paid at once in §2: the parallelogram law is what both examples lack.

## Sections

### §1 Inner products and the parallelogram law (`inner-products`; target 3,600 words)

*Failure-motivated opening.* FA.1a gave norms, and with them distance, but not angle. The chapter-opening energy and the sup-norm plane example (previewed) show that "minimize" needs more than a norm. Definition comes after the example C[0,1] with ∫ f ḡ: an inner product whose space is not complete (fn → sign-like function in L² norm, no continuous limit), so "inner product" and "complete" are two separate requirements.

- `def-inner-product`: inner product on a vector space V over 𝔽 ∈ {ℝ, ℂ}: linear in the first slot, conjugate-symmetric, positive definite (slot convention per NOTATION.md; S2a item). `def-inner-product-space`, induced norm ‖x‖ = ⟨x,x⟩^{1/2}.
- `def-orthogonal`: x ⊥ y iff ⟨x,y⟩ = 0. `prop-pythagoras`: x ⊥ y ⇒ ‖x+y‖² = ‖x‖² + ‖y‖²; for finitely many pairwise orthogonal vectors likewise.
- `thm-cauchy-schwarz`: |⟨x,y⟩| ≤ ‖x‖‖y‖, equality iff x, y linearly dependent. *Proof sketch:* y = 0 trivial; else write x = cy + w with c = ⟨x,y⟩/‖y‖², w ⊥ y; Pythagoras gives ‖x‖² = |c|²‖y‖² + ‖w‖² ≥ |⟨x,y⟩|²/‖y‖²; equality iff w = 0.
  **Scratch Work (first appearance: optimize a one-parameter quadratic).** Where does c come from? Minimize t ↦ ‖x − t y‖² over t ∈ 𝔽: expanding, the minimum is at t = ⟨x,y⟩/‖y‖², and the minimum value ‖x‖² − |⟨x,y⟩|²/‖y‖² is ≥ 0. The proof then hides the search and keeps the decomposition.
- `cor-inner-product-norm`: ‖·‖ is a norm (triangle inequality from Cauchy–Schwarz). `prop-inner-product-continuous`: (x,y) ↦ ⟨x,y⟩ is continuous (|⟨x,y⟩ − ⟨x′,y′⟩| ≤ ‖x−x′‖‖y‖ + ‖x′‖‖y−y′‖).
- `prop-parallelogram`: ‖x+y‖² + ‖x−y‖² = 2‖x‖² + 2‖y‖². `prop-polarization`: real case ⟨x,y⟩ = ¼(‖x+y‖² − ‖x−y‖²); complex case ⟨x,y⟩ = ¼ Σ_{k=0}^{3} iᵏ‖x + iᵏy‖².
- `def-hilbert-space`: an inner product space complete in its induced norm. `def-pre-hilbert` (the term for an incomplete inner product space).
- **Examples.** `ex-ell-two` (main example, series constraint): ℓ² = ℓ²(ℕ) with ⟨x,y⟩ = Σ xₙȳₙ; convergence by Cauchy–Schwarz in 𝔽ᴺ and a limit; completeness is FA.1a's ℓᵖ completeness at p = 2 (cite, do not reprove). ℓ²(Γ) for any index set Γ is introduced in §4. `ex-L-two-revisited`: L²(μ) is a Hilbert space — MT.7's Riesz–Fischer, restated (revisits MT.7). `ex-c00-pre-hilbert`: c₀₀ (finitely supported sequences) with the ℓ² inner product is pre-Hilbert and dense in ℓ². `ex-continuous-L2-incomplete`: C[0,1] with ∫ f ḡ is pre-Hilbert; fₙ(x) = clamp(n(x − ½), 0, 1) is Cauchy with no continuous limit.
- **Non-examples (parallelogram test).** `ex-ellp-not-hilbert`: in ℓᵖ, e₁, e₂ give 2·2^{2/p} on the left and 4 on the right; equal iff p = 2. `ex-sup-norm-not-hilbert`: in C[0,1] with ‖·‖_∞, f = 1, g = x give 4 + 1 = 5 ≠ 4.
- **Worked example** `ex-angle-in-L2`: the angle between 1 and x on [0,1] in L²: ⟨1,x⟩ = ½, ‖1‖ = 1, ‖x‖ = 1/√3, cos θ = √3/2, θ = π/6.
- **Quick check** `chk-inner-products-1`: In ℓ², let x = (1, ½, ¼, …) (xₙ = 2^{1−n}) and y = e₁ + e₂. Compute ⟨x,y⟩ and ‖x‖². *Answer:* 3/2; Σ 4^{1−n} = 4/3.
- **Quick check** `chk-inner-products-2`: Does ‖(a,b)‖ = |a| + |b| on ℝ² come from an inner product? *Answer:* no; (1,0),(0,1) give 4 + 4 = 8 vs 2 + 2 = 4.
- **Warning:** "complete" is a separate hypothesis; every theorem from §2 on names it, and the weird-examples rows show each one failing without it.

**Exercises (§1).**
- `exr-inner-products-a1` [A1] (a) Verify that ⟨f,g⟩ = ∫₀¹ f(x)g(x) x dx is an inner product on C[0,1] (real). (b) Is ⟨f,g⟩ = ∫₀^{1/2} f g an inner product on C[0,1]? *Answer:* (a) positivity: ∫ f² x dx = 0 forces f² x = 0 on [0,1], so f = 0 on (0,1], and f(0) = 0 by continuity. (b) No: f supported in (½,1) has ⟨f,f⟩ = 0.

### §2 Nearest points and orthogonal decomposition (`nearest-points`; target 4,800 words; key proof 1)

*Opening* (the second hook): `ex-sup-norm-many-nearest` and `ex-no-nearest-point` (below), stated before any theorem; the reader is asked which identity of §1 each space lacks.

- `thm-nearest-point`: H Hilbert, C ⊆ H nonempty, closed, convex, x ∈ H. Then there is a unique y ∈ C with ‖x − y‖ = d(x,C). Write y = P_C x. *All hypotheses named; S2a checks each is needed (see weird rows).* *Proof sketch:* d = inf; choose yₙ ∈ C with ‖x − yₙ‖² < d² + 1/n; midpoint m = (yₙ+yₘ)/2 ∈ C by convexity; parallelogram on x − yₙ, x − yₘ gives ‖yₙ − yₘ‖² = 2‖x−yₙ‖² + 2‖x−yₘ‖² − 4‖x − m‖² < 2/n + 2/m; Cauchy, converges by completeness, limit in C by closedness, attains d by continuity of the norm. Uniqueness: same identity with two minimizers gives distance 0.
  **Scratch Work (first appearance: the midpoint squeeze).** Show the reader the search: we want ‖yₙ − yₘ‖ small but only know ‖x − yₙ‖, ‖x − yₘ‖ near d. The parallelogram law converts "both near d" plus "midpoint at least d" into "close together". Draw the picture: two points on a sphere of radius slightly above d whose midpoint is outside the ball of radius d must be close.
  This is **key proof 1** (§11.2): A-exercise proof-comprehension item exr-nearest-points-a2; faded version exr-nearest-points-b3.
- `thm-projection-variational`: in the setting above, y = P_C x iff y ∈ C and Re⟨x − y, z − y⟩ ≤ 0 for all z ∈ C. *Proof sketch:* (⇒) for z ∈ C, t ∈ (0,1], y + t(z − y) ∈ C; expand ‖x − y − t(z−y)‖² ≥ ‖x−y‖², divide by t, let t → 0⁺. (⇐) ‖x − z‖² = ‖x − y‖² − 2Re⟨x−y, z−y⟩ + ‖z−y‖² ≥ ‖x−y‖².
  **Scratch Work (first appearance: perturb the minimizer).** "A minimizer cannot be improved by moving toward any competitor"; the derivative at t = 0⁺ of the one-variable function is the inequality.
- `cor-projection-lipschitz`: ‖P_C x − P_C x′‖ ≤ ‖x − x′‖. *Proof sketch:* add the two variational inequalities (z = P_C x′ in the first, z = P_C x in the second), then Cauchy–Schwarz.
- `def-orthogonal-complement`: S⊥ = {x : ⟨x,s⟩ = 0 ∀s ∈ S}; `prop-complement-closed-subspace`: S⊥ is a closed subspace, S⊥ = (closure of span S)⊥.
- `thm-projection-subspace`: M closed subspace of Hilbert H. Then y = P_M x iff y ∈ M and x − y ∈ M⊥; P_M is linear, P_M² = P_M, ‖P_M x‖ ≤ ‖x‖, ker P_M = M⊥, ran P_M = M. *Proof sketch:* apply thm-projection-variational with z = y ± w, y ± i w for w ∈ M to get ⟨x − y, w⟩ = 0; linearity from uniqueness of the characterization; norm bound from Pythagoras.
- `thm-orthogonal-decomposition`: H = M ⊕ M⊥ for M closed (every x is uniquely m + m′), and P_{M⊥} = I − P_M.
- `cor-double-complement`: for any subspace M, M⊥⊥ = closure of M. `cor-density-criterion`: a subspace M is dense iff M⊥ = {0}. (This is the test §4 and §5 use for completeness of orthonormal systems.)
- **Worked examples.** `ex-project-positive-cone`: real L²(μ), C = {g ≥ 0 a.e.}: P_C f = f⁺; verify with the variational inequality (⟨f − f⁺, g − f⁺⟩ = −∫ f⁻ g ≤ 0). `ex-project-ball`: P onto the closed unit ball is x ↦ x / max(1, ‖x‖). `ex-project-hyperplane-ell2`: M = {x ∈ ℓ² : Σ xₙ/n = 0} = a⊥ with a = (1/n); P_M x = x − (⟨x,a⟩/‖a‖²) a, ‖a‖² = π²/6; for x = e₁: P_M e₁ = e₁ − (6/π²)a, distance from e₁ to M = 1/‖a‖ = √6/π.
- **Weird examples (each kills one hypothesis of thm-nearest-point).**
  - `ex-sup-norm-many-nearest` (inner product dropped): (ℝ², ‖·‖_∞), M = x-axis, x = (0,1): every (t,0) with |t| ≤ 1 is at distance 1.
  - `ex-no-nearest-point` (inner product dropped, Banach): in (C[0,1], ‖·‖_∞), φ(f) = ∫₀^{1/2} f − ∫_{1/2}^1 f, C = {f : φ(f) = 1} closed convex; d(0, C) = 1 but no f ∈ C has ‖f‖_∞ = 1 (equality would force f = 1 on [0,½] and −1 on [½,1]).
  - `ex-nonclosed-no-nearest` (closedness dropped): c₀₀ ⊂ ℓ², x = (1/n): d(x, c₀₀) = 0, not attained.
  - `ex-nonconvex-two-nearest` (convexity dropped): unit sphere in ℝ², x = 0: every point is nearest.
  - `ex-incomplete-no-decomposition` (completeness dropped): in the pre-Hilbert space c₀₀, M = {y ∈ c₀₀ : Σ yₙ/n = 0} is closed (kernel of a continuous functional), M⊥ ∩ c₀₀ = {0}, but M ≠ c₀₀; so c₀₀ ≠ M ⊕ M⊥ and cor-double-complement fails. *Route:* in ℓ², the closure of M is a⊥ (density of c₀₀), so a z ∈ c₀₀ orthogonal to M lies in a⊥⊥ = span a, forcing z = 0.
- **Quick check** `chk-nearest-points-1`: In ℓ², M = span{e₁, e₂}. What is P_M x and d(x,M)? *Answer:* x₁e₁ + x₂e₂; (Σ_{n≥3}|xₙ|²)^{1/2}.
- **Quick check** `chk-nearest-points-2`: For M = {0}, what are M⊥ and P_M? For M = H? *Answer:* H and 0; {0} and I.
- **Named moves:** *midpoint squeeze*; *perturb the minimizer*.

**Exercises (§2).**

### §3 The Riesz representation theorem (`riesz-representation`; target 3,400 words; key proof 2)

*Failure-motivated opening.* FA.1a identified (ℓᵖ)* with ℓ^q only through a computation with coordinates. In a Hilbert space every vector y gives a functional x ↦ ⟨x,y⟩ of norm ‖y‖ (Cauchy–Schwarz). Do all functionals arise so? On c₀₀ they do not (weird rows below), so completeness must enter.

- `thm-riesz-representation`: H Hilbert, φ ∈ H*. There is a unique y ∈ H with φ(x) = ⟨x,y⟩ for all x, and ‖φ‖ = ‖y‖. *Proof sketch:* φ = 0: y = 0. Else N = ker φ is a closed proper subspace (FA.1a: bounded ⇒ continuous); by thm-orthogonal-decomposition pick z ∈ N⊥ with ‖z‖ = 1; for any x, φ(x)z − φ(z)x ∈ N, so 0 = ⟨φ(x)z − φ(z)x, z⟩ gives φ(x) = ⟨x, \overline{φ(z)} z⟩. Uniqueness: ⟨x, y − y′⟩ = 0 for x = y − y′. Norm: Cauchy–Schwarz and x = y.
  **Scratch Work (first appearance: read a functional off its kernel).** A nonzero functional is fixed by its kernel up to a scalar; the kernel has codimension one, so N⊥ is a line; the representing vector must lie on that line, and the scalar is fixed by evaluating at one point. Write the reader's guess y = c z, then solve for c.
  This is **key proof 2**: proof-comprehension item exr-riesz-representation-a2; exit check exr-riesz-representation-c1.
- `cor-hilbert-self-dual`: J : H → H*, y ↦ ⟨·, y⟩ is a conjugate-linear isometric bijection; H* is a Hilbert space with ⟨φ_y, φ_z⟩ := ⟨z, y⟩.
- `cor-hilbert-reflexive`: the canonical embedding H → H** of FA.1a is onto. *Proof sketch:* apply Riesz twice. (Forward pointer: FA.10.)
- `cor-kernel-codim`: for φ ≠ 0, (ker φ)⊥ is one-dimensional.
- `rem-adjoint-preview` (remark, no label citation required later): for bounded T on H, y ↦ ⟨Tx, y⟩ … Riesz gives T* with ⟨Tx, y⟩ = ⟨x, T*y⟩; FA.9 starts here.
- `rem-radon-nikodym-preview` (remark): for finite measures ν ≤ μ on the same σ-algebra, φ(f) = ∫ f dν is bounded on L²(μ) (|φ(f)| ≤ ν(X)^{1/2}‖f‖_{L²(ν)} ≤ ν(X)^{1/2}‖f‖_{L²(μ)}), so φ(f) = ∫ f ḡ dμ for some g. MT.11 makes this the Radon–Nikodym theorem.

### §4 Orthonormal families and bases (`orthonormal-bases`; target 4,800 words)

*Failure-motivated opening.* In 𝔽ⁿ (LA3.11) an orthonormal basis gives x = Σ⟨x,eₖ⟩eₖ, a finite sum. In ℓ²(ℝ) (functions on ℝ with countable support and Σ|x_t|² < ∞) the natural orthonormal family {e_t : t ∈ ℝ} has no order to sum in. Definition of unordered sums follows.

- `def-orthonormal-family` (indexed by any set Γ). `def-unordered-sum`: Σ_{γ∈Γ} x_γ = x iff for each ε > 0 there is a finite F₀ ⊆ Γ with ‖Σ_{γ∈F} x_γ − x‖ < ε for all finite F ⊇ F₀. `ex-ell-two-gamma`: ℓ²(Γ). `prop-countable-support`: if Σ_{γ∈Γ} |c_γ|² < ∞ (sup over finite partial sums), then {γ : c_γ ≠ 0} is countable. (Each set {|c_γ| > 1/k} is finite.)
- `thm-bessel`: {e_γ} orthonormal ⇒ Σ_γ |⟨x,e_γ⟩|² ≤ ‖x‖². *Proof sketch:* finite F: x − Σ_F ⟨x,e_γ⟩e_γ ⊥ each e_γ (γ ∈ F), Pythagoras; then sup over F.
  **Scratch Work.** Not a new technique (Pythagoras, as in thm-cauchy-schwarz): one-line Proof Idea only (fading).
- `thm-riesz-fischer-abstract`: {e_γ} orthonormal in a Hilbert space; Σ c_γ e_γ converges (unordered) iff (c_γ) ∈ ℓ²(Γ), and then ‖Σ c_γ e_γ‖² = Σ |c_γ|². *Proof sketch:* countable support; the partial sums over an enumeration are Cauchy by Pythagoras; unordered convergence from the tail estimate; completeness used here.
- `thm-projection-orthonormal`: M = closure of span{e_γ}; P_M x = Σ ⟨x,e_γ⟩e_γ. *Proof sketch:* the sum converges by Bessel and thm-riesz-fischer-abstract; x minus it is orthogonal to every e_γ, hence to M; apply thm-projection-subspace.
- `thm-orthonormal-basis-tfae`: for an orthonormal family {e_γ} in Hilbert H, TFAE: (a) span is dense; (b) {e_γ}⊥ = {0}; (c) x = Σ ⟨x,e_γ⟩e_γ for all x; (d) ‖x‖² = Σ|⟨x,e_γ⟩|² for all x (Parseval); (e) ⟨x,y⟩ = Σ ⟨x,e_γ⟩\overline{⟨y,e_γ⟩}. `def-orthonormal-basis`: a family with these properties. *Proof sketch:* (a)⇔(b) cor-density-criterion; (a)⇒(c) thm-projection-orthonormal with M = H; (c)⇒(e) continuity of the inner product; (e)⇒(d) y = x; (d)⇒(b) clear.
- **Warning** `warn-onb-not-hamel`: an orthonormal basis of ℓ² is not a Hamel basis; x = (1/n) is not a finite combination of the eₙ. (Exercise C3 shows an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space has no countable Hamel basis, citing FA.4's Baire category theorem as a soft see.)
- `thm-gram-schmidt`: a linearly independent sequence (xₙ) yields an orthonormal sequence (eₙ) with span{e₁..eₙ} = span{x₁..xₙ} for all n; explicit recursion. Recall LA3.11; proof by induction with thm-projection-subspace for finite-dimensional M.
- `thm-onb-exists`: every Hilbert space has an orthonormal basis; every orthonormal family extends to one. *Proof sketch:* Zorn (F2.4) on orthonormal families containing the given one, ordered by inclusion; unions of chains are orthonormal; a maximal family satisfies (b), since a unit vector in its complement could be added. Separable case without Zorn: Gram–Schmidt on a linearly independent subsequence of a dense sequence with the same span.
- `thm-separable-iso-ell2`: an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space is unitarily isomorphic to ℓ² (a linear bijection preserving inner products); a finite-dimensional one to 𝔽ⁿ; any Hilbert space with ONB {e_γ}_{γ∈Γ} to ℓ²(Γ) via x ↦ (⟨x,e_γ⟩). - **Worked examples.** `ex-gram-schmidt-ell2`: Gram–Schmidt on x₁ = e₁ + e₂, x₂ = e₂ + e₃ in ℓ²: e₁′ = (e₁+e₂)/√2; x₂ − ⟨x₂,e₁′⟩e₁′ = e₂ + e₃ − ½(e₁+e₂) = −½e₁ + ½e₂ + e₃, norm √(3/2), so e₂′ = (−e₁ + e₂ + 2e₃)/√6. `ex-weighted-sum`: Σ_{n} (1/n) eₙ converges in ℓ² to (1/n) with norm² π²/6, while Σ (1/√n) eₙ diverges (ℓ² test).

### §6 Weak convergence in Hilbert space (`weak-convergence`; target 3,200 words)

*Failure-motivated opening.* In ℓ², ‖eₙ − eₘ‖ = √2: the closed unit ball is not compact, so the Bolzano–Weierstrass step behind every "a minimizing sequence has a convergent subsequence" fails. Yet (eₙ) tends to 0 against every fixed y (Bessel). This weaker convergence is what survives.

- `def-weak-convergence`: xₙ ⇀ x iff ⟨xₙ, y⟩ → ⟨x, y⟩ for all y ∈ H (by Riesz, iff φ(xₙ) → φ(x) for all φ ∈ H*; FA.5 makes this a topology).
- `prop-weak-basic`: weak limits are unique; xₙ → x ⇒ xₙ ⇀ x; orthonormal sequences ⇀ 0 (Bessel); ‖x‖ ≤ lim inf ‖xₙ‖ if xₙ ⇀ x; xₙ ⇀ x and ‖xₙ‖ → ‖x‖ ⇒ xₙ → x (expand ‖xₙ − x‖²).
- `prop-weak-bounded`: a weakly convergent sequence is bounded. *Proof:* by uniform boundedness (FA.4) applied to the functionals ⟨·, xₙ⟩ (b FA.4, scope this proposition; coordinator note).
- `thm-weak-sequential-compactness`: every bounded sequence in a Hilbert space has a weakly convergent subsequence. *Proof sketch:* M = closed span of {xₙ} is separable; pick dense (yₖ) in M; diagonal subsequence with ⟨x_{n_j}, yₖ⟩ convergent for every k; extends to all y ∈ M by density and boundedness (ε/3); ℓ(y) = lim ⟨y, x_{n_j}⟩ is then a bounded linear functional on M; Riesz in M gives x ∈ M with ⟨x_{n_j}, y⟩ → ⟨x, y⟩ for y ∈ M; for y ∈ M⊥ both sides are 0; thm-orthogonal-decomposition.
  **Scratch Work (first appearance: diagonal, then Riesz).** The diagonal argument (recalled from Arzelà–Ascoli, AN2.6-level, as a soft see) gives convergence on a countable set; density spreads it; Riesz turns the limiting functional into a vector. Separate the three jobs in the scratch work.
- `prop-closed-convex-weakly-closed`: a closed convex C is weakly sequentially closed: xₙ ∈ C, xₙ ⇀ x ⇒ x ∈ C. *Proof sketch:* with y = P_C x, Re⟨x − y, xₙ − y⟩ ≤ 0 (thm-projection-variational); let n → ∞: ‖x − y‖² ≤ 0.
- `cor-minimizers-exist`: if C is closed, convex, nonempty and F : C → ℝ is continuous, convex, and F(x) → ∞ as ‖x‖ → ∞ in C, then F attains its minimum. *Proof sketch:* bounded minimizing sequence, weakly convergent subsequence, its limit in C; the sublevel sets {F ≤ t} are closed and convex, hence weakly sequentially closed. (This is the second proof route for §7's symmetric case.)
- **Weird examples.** `ex-sphere-not-weakly-closed`: eₙ ⇀ 0, ‖eₙ‖ = 1: the unit sphere is not weakly sequentially closed, and the norm is not weakly continuous. `ex-weak-not-strong-L2`: sin(nx) ⇀ 0 in L²(0, π) (Bessel for the orthogonal family, or Riemann–Lebesgue) with norm √(π/2). `ex-moving-bump-weak`: √n 1_{[0,1/n]} ⇀ 0 in L²[0,1]: ⟨√n 1_{[0,1/n]}, g⟩ ≤ √n ∫₀^{1/n}|g| ≤ (∫₀^{1/n}|g|²)^{1/2} → 0, while norms are 1.
- **Quick check** `chk-weak-convergence-1`: Does xₙ = e₁ + eₙ converge weakly in ℓ²? Strongly? *Answer:* weakly to e₁; not strongly (‖xₙ − e₁‖ = 1).

### §7 Coercive forms: Lax–Milgram and the Dirichlet principle (`lax-milgram`; target 4,400 words; key proof 3)

*Opening, hook payment.* Return to the chapter-opening energy and to `ex-weierstrass-degenerate`: minimize ∫_{−1}^{1} x² u′² over u ∈ C¹[−1,1], u(±1) = ±1. Clamped ramps u_ε = clamp(x/ε, −1, 1) (piecewise C¹; smoothed corners, S2a item) give energy 2ε/3 → 0, but energy 0 forces u′ = 0 a.e. on each side of 0, hence a jump: the infimum is never attained in *any* space of continuous functions. Two diseases: the space (opening problem) and the form (here, no coercivity near 0). Definitions follow.

- `def-bounded-form`, `def-coercive-form`: a sesquilinear a : H × H → 𝔽 is bounded if |a(u,v)| ≤ M‖u‖‖v‖, coercive if Re a(u,u) ≥ α‖u‖² for some α > 0.
- `thm-lax-milgram`: H Hilbert, a bounded and coercive, φ ∈ H*. For every f ∈ H there is a unique u ∈ H with a(u, v) = ⟨f, v⟩ for all v ∈ H, and ‖u‖ ≤ ‖f‖/α. (Equivalently, by Riesz, a(u, ·) can be made equal to any prescribed bounded conjugate-linear functional; the drafter phrases it to match NOTATION.md's slot convention, S2a item 1.) *Proof sketch (key proof 3):* exr-riesz-representation-c2 gives bounded A with a(u,v) = ⟨Au, v⟩, ‖A‖ ≤ M; Riesz gives f with the right-hand side ⟨f, v⟩; solve Au = f. For ρ > 0, Tu = u − ρ(Au − f) satisfies ‖Tu − Tw‖² = ‖z‖² − 2ρ Re⟨Az, z⟩ + ρ²‖Az‖² ≤ (1 − 2ρα + ρ²M²)‖z‖², z = u − w; for 0 < ρ < 2α/M² this is a contraction; Banach's fixed point theorem (AN2.2, recalled) gives the unique u. Bound: α‖u‖² ≤ Re a(u,u) = Re⟨f,u⟩ ≤ ‖f‖‖u‖.
  **Scratch Work (first appearance in this volume: turn the equation into a contraction).** We want Au = f, A "almost the identity in the direction of u" (coercive). Try u ↦ u − ρ(Au − f); compute the Lipschitz constant as a quadratic in ρ; choose ρ to make it < 1. Contrast with the second route (exr-lax-milgram-b2): coercive ⇒ injective with closed range; range⊥ = {0} ⇒ onto.
  Proof-comprehension: exr-lax-milgram-a2.
- `thm-dirichlet-principle-abstract`: if moreover a is symmetric (a(u,v) = \overline{a(v,u)}), the u of thm-lax-milgram is the unique minimizer of E(v) = ½a(v,v) − Re ⟨f, v⟩. *Proof sketch:* a is an inner product equivalent to ⟨·,·⟩ (α‖v‖² ≤ a(v,v) ≤ M‖v‖²), so (H, a) is a Hilbert space and Riesz in (H, a) is the theorem; E(u + v) = E(u) + ½a(v,v) by the equation.
- `ex-dirichlet-1d` (hook paid): H = {g ∈ L²(0,1) real : ∫₀¹ g = 0} = 1⊥ (closed, so Hilbert, exr-inner-products-c2), (Vg)(x) = ∫₀ˣ g. (Vg)(0) = (Vg)(1) = 0, ‖Vg‖_∞ ≤ ‖g‖₂ (Cauchy–Schwarz). a(g,h) = ∫ g h + ∫ (Vg)(Vh): symmetric, bounded (M = 2), coercive (α = 1). For f ∈ L²(0,1), φ(h) = ∫ f · Vh is bounded (|φ(h)| ≤ ‖f‖₂‖h‖₂). The minimizer g gives u = Vg with ∫ (u′v′ + uv) = ∫ f v for all v = Vh — the weak form of −u″ + u = f, u(0) = u(1) = 0. For f = 1_{[1/2,1]}: solve classically on each half and match u, u′ at ½ to exhibit u; u′ is continuous, u″ = u − f jumps by −1 at ½ (C¹, not C²). The infimum over C² functions equals min over H (density: V maps the dense subspace of continuous mean-zero g onto C¹ functions vanishing at 0 and 1; C² by mollifying, S2a item), so the opening problem has no C² minimizer. Remark: u is absolutely continuous (see MT.10). Closing paragraph: PDE.1 does the same with −Δ in ℝᵈ.
- `ex-nonsymmetric-coercive` (weird): on ℝ², a(u,v) = ⟨u,v⟩ + ⟨Ju,v⟩ with J the rotation by π/2: ⟨Ju,u⟩ = 0, so coercive with α = 1, not symmetric; Lax–Milgram solves a(u,·) = ⟨f,·⟩ (u = (I + J)^{−1}f… careful with the slot: (I + J)ᵀ = I − J, S2a item), but there is no energy whose minimizer is u. Lax–Milgram is genuinely more than Riesz.
- `ex-weierstrass-degenerate` (weird, opening): as above; a(u,v) = ∫ x² u′v′ is not coercive on any reasonable H.
- `thm-stampacchia` (optional, `{.optional}`): K ⊆ H nonempty closed convex, a bounded coercive (real H): there is a unique u ∈ K with a(u, v − u) ≥ ⟨f, v − u⟩ for all v ∈ K; symmetric case = minimizing E over K. Proof by T u = P_K(u − ρ(Au − f)) and cor-projection-lipschitz — faded in exr-lax-milgram-c2.
- **Quick check** `chk-lax-milgram-1`: On ℝ, which a(u,v) = cuv are coercive? *Answer:* c > 0.
