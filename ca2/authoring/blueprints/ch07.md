# Blueprint: CA.15 — Elliptic Functions (optional)

**Purpose.**
- Meromorphic functions with **two** independent periods are the next thing after exp, sin and tan. They are so rigid that a handful of contour integrals (the Liouville theorems) decides almost everything about them, and one function, Weierstrass ℘, generates all of them.
- The chapter builds ℘ by repairing a divergent sum, derives its differential equation, and shows the field of elliptic functions is ℂ(℘, ℘′). It constructs σ as a Weierstrass product (the s-edge to CA.7) and uses it to build every elliptic function with admissible zeros and poles.
- The payoff is a dictionary: a lattice Λ, the torus ℂ/Λ, and the plane cubic y² = 4x³ − g₂x − g₃ are one object, and ℘ is the inverse of the elliptic integral ∫ dx/√(4x³ − g₂x − g₃). The last section shows every such cubic comes from a lattice (via the modular function λ) and, as an optional corollary, re-proves the little Picard theorem along Ahlfors's classical route.
- **Optional chapter.** Nothing in the main line depends on it. CA.8b (optional) may point at §07 in prose; it must not be proved from it.

**Named moves** (in `index.md`, "Named moves").
- **Translate and cancel.** Integrate an elliptic (or quasi-periodic) integrand over ∂P_a; opposite sides are translates by ω₁ or ω₂, so they cancel exactly (or leave a computable defect for quasi-periodic integrands). First appearance §02, with Scratch Work.
- **Shift the parallelogram.** Replace P by P_a with a chosen so no zero or pole lies on ∂P_a; possible because there are finitely many in any bounded set.
- **Kill the principal parts.** Subtract from f a combination of ℘, ℘′, ℘″, … (or of ζ_Λ-differences) matching all principal parts; the remainder is an entire elliptic function, hence constant. First appearance §03, with Scratch Work.
- **Parity-and-period test.** To show g ≡ 0 for g built from f(z) − f(−z) or f(z + ω) − f(z): show g has no poles (so is constant) and evaluate at a half period or at 0.
- **Count by order.** An elliptic function of order m takes every value m times in P; compare orders of both sides to pin a rational function's degree.

## index.md

- **Hook (opened here, paid in §06 and §07).** On [−1, 1] the integral u(x) = ∫₀ˣ dt/√(1 − t²) is increasing; its inverse is sin. In ℂ, the integrand is two-valued and the value of the integral depends on the path; going once around both branch points ±1 adds 2π. That ambiguity *is* the period of sin. Ask: replace 1 − t² by a cubic 4t³ − g₂t − g₃ with distinct roots. There are now three finite branch points and ∞, and there are two essentially different loops. If the inverse function exists, it should have **two** independent periods. Does it exist, and what is it? The answer (℘) is §06; that *every* such cubic is reached is §07.

## 01-periods-and-lattices.md — Periodic functions and period lattices

Budget ≤ 3,000 main-line words.

- **Hook for the section.** Why do periodic functions look like functions of e^{2πiz/ω}? And can a function have periods 1 and √2?
- **`def-period`.** ω ≠ 0 is a period of f meromorphic on ℂ if f(z + ω) = f(z) for all z. The set M_f of periods together with 0 is an additive subgroup of ℂ.
- **`thm-simply-periodic-exponential`.** Let Ω be a region invariant under z ↦ z ± ω, f meromorphic on Ω with period ω. Then f = F(e^{2πiz/ω}) for a unique meromorphic F on the image region Ω′. If Ω′ contains an annulus r₁ < |ζ| < r₂ free of poles, f has the Fourier development f(z) = Σ_{n∈ℤ} c_n e^{2πinz/ω} on the corresponding strip, with c_n = (1/ω)∫_a^{a+ω} f(z) e^{−2πinz/ω} dz. *Proof sketch:* F is well defined because z is determined by ζ up to ℤω; F is meromorphic by local inverses of the exponential (a local log, CA.1); the series is the Laurent series of F (CA.3) after the substitution.
- **`exm-tan-order-one`.** tan z = −i(ζ − 1)/(ζ + 1) with ζ = e^{2iz}. F is a Möbius map, so tan takes every value except ±i exactly once in each period strip and omits ±i. Contrast for §02: a simply periodic function of "order one" exists; a doubly periodic one does not (`cor-no-order-one`).
- **Failure-motivated definition of a lattice.** `exm-dense-periods`: if f has periods 1 and √2, then every m + n√2 is a period; this set is dense in ℝ, so f(z + t) = f(z) for a dense set of real t, hence for all real t by continuity away from poles, and f′ ≡ 0 on any line segment free of poles: f is constant. Lesson: for a nonconstant f the period group has **no accumulation point**. That is the property to name.
- **`thm-discrete-subgroups-c`.** A discrete subgroup M of ℂ is {0}, ℤω for some ω ≠ 0, or ℤω₁ + ℤω₂ with ω₁, ω₂ linearly independent over ℝ. *Proof sketch:* take ω₁ ∈ M ∖ {0} of minimal modulus (exists by discreteness: finitely many points of M in any disc). If M ⊄ ℝω₁, take ω₂ ∈ M ∖ ℝω₁ of minimal modulus. Any ω ∈ M is sω₁ + tω₂ with real s, t; subtracting the nearest integer combination leaves an element of M in the closed parallelogram spanned by ω₁, ω₂ of modulus smaller than |ω₂| unless it is 0, hence it lies on ℝω₁, hence it is an integer multiple of ω₁ by minimality. Scratch Work is **not** given (this is a T2 volume and the argument is a minimal-element argument the reader knows from AN1); give the Proof Idea with the picture.
- **`def-lattice`.** A lattice is ℤω₁ + ℤω₂ with Im(ω₂/ω₁) > 0 (orientation fixed now, used for Legendre in §05). **`def-period-parallelogram`** P_a. Every z ∈ ℂ is congruent mod Λ to exactly one point of P_a.
- **`thm-lattice-basis-change`.** (ω₁′, ω₂′) with ω₂′ = aω₂ + bω₁, ω₁′ = cω₂ + dω₁, a, b, c, d ∈ ℤ, is a basis of the same lattice iff ad − bc = ±1; with the orientation convention, iff ad − bc = 1, and then τ′ = (aτ + b)/(cτ + d). *Proof sketch:* the inverse matrix must be integral, so the determinant is a unit of ℤ; orientation: Im τ′ = (ad − bc) Im τ / |cτ + d|². Ties to CA.10: SL₂(ℤ) acting on ℍ.
- Remark (no proof): the area |Im(ω̄₁ω₂)| of P does not depend on the basis (F4.10 for the area formula).
- **Warning.** Two bases of one lattice can have very differently shaped parallelograms: (1, i) and (1, 1 + i) give the same ℤ[i], one a square, one a rhombus-like parallelogram of the same area.
- **Quick check.** Is ℤ + ℤ√2 (inside ℝ) the period group of a nonconstant meromorphic function? *Answer:* no; it is not discrete (`exm-dense-periods`).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Which of (1, i), (1 + i, 1 − i), (2 + i, 1 + i) is a basis of ℤ[i]? *Answer:* the first and third (determinants 1 and 2·1 − 1·1 = 1); the second has |det| = 2 and spans an index-2 sublattice.
  - **A2.** Write cot z as F(e^{2iz}) and say which values cot omits. *Answer:* cot z = i(ζ + 1)/(ζ − 1); omits ±i.
  - **B1.** Prove the area of P_a is independent of a and of the basis. *Answer:* translation invariance; |det| = 1 for the change of basis.
  - **B2.** Show that if f is entire with period ω, then its Fourier series converges on all of ℂ, and identify c_n for f = cos²z with ω = π. *Answer:* F holomorphic on ℂ ∖ {0}, Laurent on the whole punctured plane; cos²z = ½ + ¼e^{2iz} + ¼e^{−2iz}, so c₀ = ½, c_{±1} = ¼, others 0.
  - **C1.** (Fill a gap.) In the proof of `thm-discrete-subgroups-c`, prove that a discrete subgroup meets every bounded set in finitely many points. *Answer:* if infinitely many, Bolzano–Weierstrass gives a convergent sequence of distinct points, and their differences are nonzero elements of M tending to 0, contradicting discreteness at 0.
  - **C2.** Show that the group of periods of a nonconstant meromorphic function on ℂ is discrete, in full (repair `exm-dense-periods` for a general accumulating sequence of periods). *Answer:* if ω_n → 0 with ω_n ≠ 0 periods, pick z₀ not a pole; f(z₀ + ω_n) = f(z₀), so f − f(z₀) has zeros accumulating at z₀, hence f ≡ f(z₀) by the identity theorem (CA.2).

## 02-liouville-theorems.md — What every elliptic function must satisfy

Budget ≤ 3,500 main-line words. Full Scratch Work for translate-and-cancel (first appearance).

- **`def-elliptic-function`.** Meromorphic on ℂ with every ω ∈ Λ as a period. **`def-order-elliptic`.** The number of poles in P_a counted with multiplicity (independent of a, by periodicity, when no pole is on ∂P_a; with the convention that P_a is half-open, independent of a always).
- **`thm-liouville-elliptic-entire`.** An entire elliptic function is constant. *Proof:* continuous on the compact closure of P, so bounded there, so bounded on ℂ by periodicity; Liouville (CA.2).
- **Scratch Work (translate and cancel), set apart.** Draw ∂P_a as four directed sides. Write the top side as the bottom side shifted by ω₂ with reversed direction, the left as the right shifted by −ω₁ with reversed direction. For periodic g the pairs cancel. Note what is needed: g continuous on ∂P_a, i.e. no poles on the boundary, which is why the shift is chosen first (**shift the parallelogram**). Then write the proof cleanly.
- **`thm-liouville-elliptic-residues`.** The sum of the residues of an elliptic f at its poles in P_a is 0. *Proof:* translate and cancel applied to (1/2πi)∮_{∂P_a} f.
- **`cor-no-order-one`.** There is no elliptic function of order 1. Proof from the residue sum: a single simple pole has nonzero residue.
- **`thm-liouville-elliptic-count`.** A nonconstant elliptic f of order m takes every value c ∈ ℂ exactly m times in P_a, with multiplicity. *Proof Idea:* apply `thm-liouville-elliptic-residues` to f′/(f − c), which is elliptic; residues are multiplicities (CA.4).
- **`thm-abel-relation`.** If a₁, …, a_m and b₁, …, b_m are the zeros and poles of a nonconstant elliptic f in P_a (with multiplicity), then Σa_j − Σb_j ∈ Λ. *Proof:* (1/2πi)∮_{∂P_a} z f′(z)/f(z) dz = Σa_j − Σb_j (CA.4, weighted argument principle). Translate and cancel no longer cancels exactly, because z is not periodic: the bottom/top pair gives −(ω₂/2πi)∮ f′/f along the bottom side, which is ω₂ times the winding number of the closed curve f(bottom side) about 0, an integer. Similarly for the other pair. So the sum is n₁ω₁ + n₂ω₂.
- **Worked example** (no ℘ yet; `exm-wp-over-wp-minus-e1` comes in §03). Given only the four theorems, decide whether an elliptic function can have a double pole at 0 and no other poles in P (yes, order 2, residue 0 is consistent; existence is §03), or simple poles at 0 and ω₁/2 with residues 1 and 1 (no: residue sum 2 ≠ 0).
- **Warning.** "Order" counts poles with multiplicity in **one** parallelogram, not in ℂ (where there are infinitely many).
- **Quick check.** Can an elliptic function have exactly one pole in P, a double pole with nonzero residue? *Answer:* no; the residue sum would be nonzero.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** f is elliptic of order 3. How many solutions does f(z) = 5 have in P, with multiplicity? How many does f(z) = ∞ have? *Answer:* 3 and 3.
  - **A2.** Show that if f is elliptic and nonconstant then so is f′, and that order(f′) = order(f) + (number of distinct poles of f in P). *Answer:* a pole of order k becomes a pole of order k + 1.
  - **B1 (faded, last step blank).** Prove `thm-liouville-elliptic-count` from `thm-liouville-elliptic-residues`, with the setup given and the residue computation left blank. *Name the move, then complete it.* *Answer:* translate and cancel; the residue of f′/(f − c) at a zero of order k of f − c is k, at a pole of order k of f is −k.
  - **B2.** Show that the zeros of an elliptic function in P_a form a finite set. *Answer:* the zeros of a nonconstant meromorphic function are isolated and P_a has compact closure.
  - **C1 (faded, last half blank).** Prove `thm-abel-relation` given only the first displayed line. *Name the move, then complete it.* *Answer:* translate and cancel with a defect; each defect is a period times an integer winding number.
  - **C2.** Let f be elliptic with Λ and let ω/2 be a half period not in Λ. Show that if f is odd, then f(ω/2) ∈ {0, ∞}. *Answer:* f(ω/2) = f(−ω/2 + ω) = f(−ω/2) = −f(ω/2).

## 03-weierstrass-p.md — The Weierstrass ℘-function

Budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words. Full Scratch Work for kill the principal parts (first appearance); Proof Idea elsewhere.

- **Failure first.** The simplest candidate allowed by §02 has a double pole at each lattice point and nothing else. Try Σ_{ω∈Λ} (z − ω)⁻². It diverges for every z: **`lem-lattice-sum-convergence`** shows why.
- **`lem-lattice-sum-convergence`.** Σ_{ω∈Λ*} |ω|^{−s} converges iff s > 2. *Proof:* the lattice points ω = mω₁ + nω₂ with max(|m|, |n|) = k number 8k, and c k ≤ |ω| ≤ C k on that ring for constants 0 < c ≤ C depending on Λ (compactness of the unit square's boundary and linear independence). Compare with Σ 8k · k^{−s}. Uses AN2.12 for double series in any order.
- **`def-weierstrass-p`.** ℘(z) ≔ z⁻² + Σ_{ω∈Λ*} [(z − ω)⁻² − ω⁻²]. The correction makes the terms O(|ω|⁻³) uniformly for z in a compact set, so the series converges absolutely and locally uniformly on ℂ ∖ Λ; ℘ is meromorphic with double poles exactly at Λ (CA.2 Weierstrass). Prose: this is the Mittag-Leffler recipe of CA.12 with an explicit correction term.
- **`thm-wp-elliptic`.** ℘ is even and elliptic of order 2. *Proof:* evenness by Λ = −Λ. ℘′(z) = −2Σ_{ω∈Λ}(z − ω)⁻³ needs no correction, so it is visibly periodic. Then ℘(z + ω_j) − ℘(z) is constant; evaluate at z = −ω_j/2 using evenness (**parity-and-period test**): the constant is 0.
- **`def-eisenstein-lattice`.** G_k ≔ Σ_{ω∈Λ*} ω^{−k}, k ≥ 3; G_k = 0 for k odd. Prose pointer: these are the lattice analogues of ζ(k) of CA.13 (see-edge).
- **`thm-wp-laurent`.** ℘(z) = z⁻² + Σ_{n≥1} (2n + 1) G_{2n+2} z^{2n} for 0 < |z| < min_{ω∈Λ*}|ω|. *Proof Idea:* expand (z − ω)⁻² − ω⁻² geometrically and swap sums (absolute convergence).
- **Scratch Work (kill the principal parts), set apart.** Write ℘ = z⁻² + 3G₄z² + 5G₆z⁴ + …, then ℘′, ℘′², ℘³ to the z⁰ term. Find the combination ℘′² − 4℘³ whose principal part is a multiple of z⁻²: it is −60G₄ z⁻² − 140G₆ + O(z²). Adding 60G₄℘ kills the z⁻² term. What is left is an entire elliptic function that vanishes at 0 after subtracting the constant. Then write the proof.
- **`thm-wp-differential-equation`.** ℘′² = 4℘³ − g₂℘ − g₃ with g₂ = 60G₄, g₃ = 140G₆.
- **`thm-wp-roots-distinct`.** ℘′ has simple zeros exactly at the three half periods ω₁/2, ω₂/2, ω₃/2 mod Λ; e_j = ℘(ω_j/2) are pairwise distinct; hence 4x³ − g₂x − g₃ = 4(x − e₁)(x − e₂)(x − e₃) and Δ = g₂³ − 27g₃² ≠ 0. *Proof Idea:* ℘′ odd ⇒ vanishes at half periods (§02 C2); order 3 ⇒ no other zeros and they are simple; ℘ − e_j has a double zero at ω_j/2 and order 2, so ℘ takes e_j only there, so e_j ≠ e_k.
- **`thm-wp-values-twice`.** For c ∈ ℂ, ℘(z) = c has exactly two solutions ±z₀ in P mod Λ, coinciding iff c ∈ {e₁, e₂, e₃}.
- **`exm-wp-over-wp-minus-e1`.** f = ℘′/(℘ − e₁) is elliptic of order 2 with simple poles at 0 (residue −2) and ω₁/2 (residue +2), zeros at ω₂/2 and ω₃/2. Check Abel: (ω₂ + ω₃)/2 − (0 + ω₁/2) = ω₂ ∈ Λ.
- **`exm-square-lattice`.** Λ = ℤ[i]: iΛ = Λ gives G₆ = i⁻⁶G₆ = −G₆, so g₃ = 0; ℘(iz) = −℘(z); e₃ = ℘((1 + i)/2) = 0 and e₂ = −e₁ (sidecar: verified numerically by truncated lattice sum, 2026-10-25).
- **`exm-hexagonal-lattice`.** Λ = ℤ[ρ], ρ = e^{2πi/3}: ρΛ = Λ gives G₄ = ρ⁻⁴G₄ = ρ²G₄, so g₂ = 0.
- **Warning.** g₂ and g₃ depend on Λ, not on a chosen basis; e₁, e₂, e₃ depend on the labelling of the half periods (relabelling permutes them).
- **Quick check.** Why does Σ(z − ω)⁻³ need no correction term while Σ(z − ω)⁻² does? *Answer:* Σ|ω|⁻³ < ∞ but Σ|ω|⁻² = ∞ (`lem-lattice-sum-convergence`).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Differentiate the differential equation to show ℘″ = 6℘² − g₂/2. *Answer:* 2℘′℘″ = (12℘² − g₂)℘′, divide by ℘′ (not identically 0).

## 04-the-field-of-elliptic-functions.md — Every elliptic function is a rational function of ℘ and ℘′

Budget ≤ 3,500 main-line words. Proof Ideas only; one bare proof (addition theorem) with the derivation in a worked example.

- **`thm-even-elliptic-rational-wp`.** Every even elliptic function f for Λ is a rational function of ℘. *Proof:* zeros and poles of an even f off the half periods come in pairs ±a; at a half period or at 0 they have even multiplicity (f even). Form R(℘) = Π(℘ − ℘(a_j))^{±1} over half of them (excluding 0); then f/R(℘) is elliptic without zeros or poles off Λ, and by evenness and parity of orders, also none at 0: constant (Liouville). Use **count by order**.
- **`thm-elliptic-field`.** Every elliptic f is R₁(℘) + ℘′R₂(℘) with R₁, R₂ rational; the field of elliptic functions for Λ is ℂ(℘, ℘′), and ℘′ satisfies the quadratic equation of `thm-wp-differential-equation` over ℂ(℘). *Proof:* f = ½(f(z) + f(−z)) + ½(f(z) − f(−z)); the odd part divided by ℘′ is even.
- **`thm-wp-addition`.** For u, v with u ± v ∉ Λ, ℘(u + v) = ¼((℘′(u) − ℘′(v))/(℘(u) − ℘(v)))² − ℘(u) − ℘(v). *Proof route:* fix v; choose A, B with ℘′ − A℘ − B vanishing at u and v (the line through (℘(u), ℘′(u)) and (℘(v), ℘′(v))). This elliptic function has a triple pole at 0 and order 3, so its third zero w satisfies u + v + w ∈ Λ (`thm-abel-relation`), i.e. w ≡ −(u + v). The three values ℘(u), ℘(v), ℘(w) are roots of 4x³ − g₂x − g₃ − (Ax + B)², whose x² coefficient gives ℘(u) + ℘(v) + ℘(u + v) = A²/4. (Sidecar: verified numerically, 2026-10-25.) Degenerate case u = v by limit (B1).
- **Worked example.** Express ℘(z + ω₁/2) as a rational function of ℘(z): by the addition theorem with v = ω₁/2 (℘′(v) = 0) and the cubic, ℘(z + ω₁/2) = e₁ + (e₁ − e₂)(e₁ − e₃)/(℘(z) − e₁). (Sidecar: verified numerically, 2026-10-25.)
- **Warning.** The addition formula is false at u = ±v (the denominator vanishes); it is not a defect of ℘ but of the chosen line.
- **Quick check.** Is ℘(2z) a rational function of ℘(z)? Of what degree? *Answer:* yes (even); ℘(2z) has double poles at the four classes of ½Λ, so order 8 for Λ, so degree 4 in ℘.
- **Exercises.**

  - **B2.** Show that any two elliptic functions f, g for Λ satisfy a nontrivial polynomial equation P(f, g) = 0. *Answer:* both lie in ℂ(℘, ℘′), which has transcendence degree 1 over ℂ; or directly: f, g of orders m, n; count poles of Σ c_{jk} f^j g^k with j < n + 1, k < m + 1 and choose coefficients to kill all principal parts and the constant (more unknowns than conditions).

## 05-sigma-and-zeta.md — σ, ζ_Λ, and functions with prescribed zeros and poles

Budget ≤ 4,000 main-line words. Scratch Work for quasi-periodicity bookkeeping (first appearance). This section pays the s-edge to CA.7.

- **Failure first.** Abel (§02) forbids an elliptic function with a single simple zero in P. But the building block "zero exactly at the lattice, simple" is too useful to give up. Give up periodicity instead, and ask for the next best thing.
- **`def-weierstrass-sigma`.** σ(z) ≔ z Π_{ω∈Λ*} (1 − z/ω) exp(z/ω + z²/(2ω²)), the Weierstrass canonical product of genus 2 for Λ.
- **`thm-sigma-entire`.** The product converges absolutely and locally uniformly; σ is entire, odd, with simple zeros exactly at Λ. *Proof:* the factors are elementary factors E₂(z/ω) (`def-elementary-factor`) and |E₂(z/ω) − 1| ≤ |z/ω|³ for |z/ω| ≤ 1 (CA.7 `lem-elementary-factor-bound`), so `thm-holomorphic-product` applies since Σ|ω|⁻³ < ∞ (`lem-lattice-sum-convergence`); this is `def-canonical-product` of genus 2. Oddness from Λ = −Λ.
- **`def-weierstrass-zeta`.** ζ_Λ ≔ σ′/σ = 1/z + Σ_{ω∈Λ*}[1/(z − ω) + 1/ω + z/ω²]. Then ζ_Λ′ = −℘ (termwise, CA.7 `thm-product-log-derivative`). **Notation warning in the text:** ζ_Λ is not CA.13's Riemann ζ.
- **Scratch Work (quasi-periodicity bookkeeping), set apart.** ζ_Λ(z + ω_j) − ζ_Λ(z) has derivative −℘(z + ω_j) + ℘(z) = 0, so it is a constant η_j. Integrate σ′/σ: σ(z + ω_j) = C_j e^{η_j z} σ(z). Find C_j by evaluating at z = −ω_j/2, where σ is odd and nonzero (ω_j/2 ∉ Λ): C_j = −e^{η_jω_j/2}. Then state cleanly.
- **`def-quasi-period`** η₁, η₂.
- **`thm-quasi-periodicity`.** ζ_Λ(z + ω_j) = ζ_Λ(z) + η_j and σ(z + ω_j) = −e^{η_j(z + ω_j/2)} σ(z), j = 1, 2. Also η_j = 2ζ_Λ(ω_j/2) (ζ_Λ odd).
- **`thm-legendre-relation`.** η₁ω₂ − η₂ω₁ = 2πi (with Im(ω₂/ω₁) > 0). *Proof:* translate and cancel applied to ζ_Λ over ∂P_a with 0 the only pole inside, residue 1: the two defects are η₁ω₂ and −η₂ω₁. The orientation convention of §01 fixes the sign. (Sidecar: verified numerically, 2026-10-25.)
- **`exm-zeta-difference`.** ζ_Λ(z − a) − ζ_Λ(z − b) is elliptic (the η's cancel), of order 2 for a ≢ b, with simple poles at a, b and residues +1, −1. This is the "missing" order-2 building block with simple poles.
- **`thm-elliptic-prescribed-divisor`.** Let a₁, …, a_n, b₁, …, b_n ∈ ℂ with no a_j ≡ b_k mod Λ, and Σa_j = Σb_j (exactly, not just mod Λ). Then f(z) = Π σ(z − a_j)/σ(z − b_j) is elliptic, with zeros exactly at the a_j + Λ and poles exactly at the b_j + Λ (with multiplicity). Conversely every elliptic function is a constant times such a product (choose representatives so that Abel's congruence becomes an equality, by moving one b_j by a period). *Proof:* multiplying factors from `thm-quasi-periodicity`: the signs (−1)ⁿ cancel and the exponentials give exp(η_j Σ(b_k − a_k)) = 1. Converse: f divided by the product is elliptic without zeros or poles.
- **`thm-elliptic-partial-fractions`.** Every elliptic f with poles b_k (k = 1, …, r) in P and principal parts Σ_{m} c_{k,m}(z − b_k)^{−m} equals C + Σ_k c_{k,1}ζ_Λ(z − b_k) + Σ_k Σ_{m≥2} c_{k,m} ((−1)^m/(m − 1)!) ℘^{(m−2)}(z − b_k), with Σ_k c_{k,1} = 0. *Proof:* kill the principal parts; the residue condition is exactly what makes the ζ_Λ-combination elliptic. (Check the sign constant: ζ_Λ^{(m−1)}(z) has principal part (−1)^{m−1}(m − 1)! z^{−m}, and ℘^{(m−2)} = −ζ_Λ^{(m−1)}; so the coefficient of ℘^{(m−2)}(z − b_k) is c_{k,m}(−1)^m/(m − 1)!. S2a: recheck.)
- **Warning.** σ is entire and nonconstant, so it cannot be periodic (Liouville); ζ_Λ has an elliptic derivative but is not elliptic. Both appear in the Strange examples table.
- **Quick check.** Is ζ_Λ(z) − ζ_Λ(z − a) elliptic? *Answer:* yes; increments η_j − η_j = 0.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Show ζ_Λ is odd and η_j = 2ζ_Λ(ω_j/2). *Answer:* term pairing ω ↔ −ω; ζ_Λ(ω_j/2) = ζ_Λ(−ω_j/2) + η_j = −ζ_Λ(ω_j/2) + η_j.

## 06-the-cubic-curve.md — The torus, the cubic, and the elliptic integral

Budget ≤ 4,000 main-line words. Bare proofs; the hook is paid here.

- **`thm-torus-cubic`.** Let E be the projective curve y²z = 4x³ − g₂xz² − g₃z³ in ℙ²(ℂ), with g₂, g₃ from Λ. The map Φ: ℂ/Λ → E, z ↦ [℘(z) : ℘′(z) : 1] for z ∉ Λ and 0 ↦ [0 : 1 : 0], is a bijection. *Proof:* well defined (periodicity) and lands on E (`thm-wp-differential-equation`); surjective: for (x, y) on the affine curve, ℘(z) = x has solutions ±z₀ (`thm-wp-values-twice`) with ℘′(±z₀) = ±y; injective: ℘ takes x only at ±z₀, and ℘′(z₀) ≠ ℘′(−z₀) unless ℘′(z₀) = 0, i.e. z₀ a half period, where ±z₀ coincide mod Λ. The point at infinity is hit only by 0. Prose: E is nonsingular because Δ ≠ 0 (`thm-wp-roots-distinct`); the holomorphic structure of ℂ/Λ and E as Riemann surfaces is mentioned, not developed.
- **`thm-collinear-sum`.** Three points Φ(z₁), Φ(z₂), Φ(z₃) of E (with multiplicity) lie on a line iff z₁ + z₂ + z₃ ∈ Λ. *Proof:* for a non-vertical line y = Ax + B, ℘′ − A℘ − B has a triple pole at 0 and order 3; Abel (`thm-abel-relation`). Vertical lines: ℘(z) = c has zeros ±z₀ and the third point is ∞ = Φ(0). Prose: the group law on the torus is the chord-and-tangent law on the cubic; the addition theorem of §04 is this statement in coordinates.
- **`thm-elliptic-integral-inversion`** (pays the hook, part 1). Let w₀ ∈ ℂ ∖ {e₁, e₂, e₃} and let γ be a path in ℂ ∖ {e₁, e₂, e₃} from w₀ to w, along which a branch of √(4x³ − g₂x − g₃) is continued from a chosen value y₀ with Φ(z₀) = (w₀, y₀). Then z₀ + ∫_γ dx/√(4x³ − g₂x − g₃) ≡ z (mod Λ) where ℘(z) = w and ℘′(z) equals the continued branch at w. In particular the set of values of the integral over all such paths is a union of cosets of Λ, and every period of Λ arises from a closed loop. *Proof:* lift γ through the local inverses of ℘ (℘′ ≠ 0 away from the half periods): z(t) with ℘(z(t)) = γ(t); then dz = dx/℘′(z) = dx/y; continuation along γ is analytic continuation along a path (CA.8a), and uniqueness of continuation identifies the branch. For the last claim: a straight segment in ℂ from z₀ to z₀ + ω (avoiding ½Λ) maps under ℘ to a closed loop.
- **Example (real rectangular lattice).** For Λ = ℤω₁ + ℤω₂ with ω₁ > 0, ω₂ ∈ iℝ₊, the e_j are real, e₁ > e₃ > e₂, ℘ is real on ℝ and decreases from +∞ to e₁ on (0, ω₁/2]; so ω₁ = 2∫_{e₁}^∞ dx/√(4x³ − g₂x − g₃). Compare: 2π = 2∫_{−1}^1 dt/√(1 − t²) for sin. This closes the hook's analogy. (S2a: check the ordering e₁ > e₃ > e₂ with Ahlfors's labelling e₁ = ℘(ω₁/2), e₂ = ℘(ω₂/2), e₃ = ℘(ω₃/2); for the rectangular lattice ℘ on the segment from ω₁/2 to ω₃/2 is real and runs between e₁ and e₃, and on ω₂/2 to ω₃/2 between e₂ and e₃.)
- **The converse question**, stated and deferred: given g₂, g₃ with g₂³ − 27g₃² ≠ 0, is there a lattice with these invariants? Answered in §07 (`thm-cubic-uniformization`).
- **Warning.** "The" elliptic integral has no single value: it is defined up to sign and Λ, exactly as arcsin is defined up to sign and 2πℤ (plus π-shifts).

## 07-modular-lambda.md — The modular function λ, and every cubic comes from a lattice

Budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words. Bare proofs; one long proof (the mapping theorem) given in steps.

- **Lead-in.** The e_j depend on the lattice; scaling Λ scales them (§03 B1). The scale-free quantity is a cross-ratio of e₁, e₂, e₃, ∞.
- **`def-modular-lambda`.** With ω₁ = 1, ω₂ = τ ∈ ℍ, λ(τ) ≔ (e₃ − e₂)/(e₁ − e₂). It is holomorphic on ℍ (the e_j are holomorphic in τ by locally uniform convergence of the defining series), and λ(τ) ∉ {0, 1, ∞} (`thm-wp-roots-distinct`).
- **`thm-lambda-invariance`.** λ(τ) depends only on Λ and the labelling of half periods; it is invariant under Γ(2) = {(a b; c d) ∈ SL₂(ℤ) : a ≡ d ≡ 1, b ≡ c ≡ 0 mod 2}, and λ(τ + 1) = λ(τ)/(λ(τ) − 1), λ(−1/τ) = 1 − λ(τ). *Proof:* Γ(2) fixes each half period class mod Λ; the two generators of SL₂(ℤ) permute them as stated, and the formula follows by homogeneity (§03 B1).
- **`thm-lambda-mapping`.** Let Ω = {τ ∈ ℍ : 0 < Re τ < 1, |τ − ½| > ½}. λ maps Ω conformally onto ℍ, extends continuously to ∂Ω with λ real there, and maps the boundary pieces (Re τ = 0), (Re τ = 1), (|τ − ½| = ½) onto (0, 1), (−∞, 0), (1, ∞) respectively: on Re τ = 0 the lattice is rectangular, so e₁ > e₃ > e₂ (§06) and λ ∈ (0, 1); the other two follow from `thm-lambda-invariance` via τ ↦ τ + 1 and τ ↦ 1 − 1/τ (sidecar: λ(0.8i) ≈ 0.73, λ(1 + 0.8i) ≈ −2.70, λ on the circle ≈ 20.2, 2026-10-25). By Schwarz reflection (CA.5) across the sides, λ: ℍ → ℂ ∖ {0, 1} is surjective, and it is a covering map; Ω ∪ (its reflection across Re τ = 0) is a fundamental domain for Γ(2). *Proof route (Ahlfors 7.3.5 technique):* (i) λ is real on Re τ = 0 and Re τ = 1 and on the circle (the series for e_j are conjugation-symmetric there); (ii) q-expansion: λ(τ)e^{−πiτ} → 16 as Im τ → ∞ (sidecar: at τ = 2i the truncated sum gives 15.76, consistent with the limit; S2a to verify the limit analytically from the e_j series); (iii) by `thm-lambda-invariance`, the behaviour at the cusps 0 and 1 follows from that at ∞; (iv) argument principle (CA.4) on a truncated Ω: for w₀ ∉ ℝ, λ − w₀ has exactly one zero in Ω if Im w₀ > 0, none if Im w₀ < 0; (v) reflection and the fundamental-domain statement give the covering property. Steps (iv) and (v) are the long part; give full detail for (iv), a careful sketch for (v).
- **`thm-cubic-uniformization`** (pays the hook, part 2). For every (g₂, g₃) ∈ ℂ² with g₂³ − 27g₃² ≠ 0 there is a lattice Λ with g₂(Λ) = g₂ and g₃(Λ) = g₃. *Proof:* let ε₁, ε₂, ε₃ be the distinct roots of 4x³ − g₂x − g₃ (sum 0). Choose τ with λ(τ) = (ε₃ − ε₂)/(ε₁ − ε₂) (surjectivity onto ℂ ∖ {0, 1}). For Λ_τ = ℤ + ℤτ, the e_j(τ) satisfy the same cross-ratio and also sum to 0, so (ε₁, ε₂, ε₃) = μ(e₁, e₂, e₃) for one μ ≠ 0 (an affine map of ℂ fixing the cross-ratio and the centroid 0 is linear). Take Λ = μ^{−1/2}Λ_τ, by §03 B1 (e_j scale by c⁻²). Then the roots agree, hence g₂, g₃ agree (`thm-wp-roots-distinct`, A2 of §03).
- **`cor-picard-via-lambda`** (`.optional`; nothing depends on it). An entire function omitting two values is constant. *Proof route:* normalize the omitted values to 0, 1. ℂ is simply connected and λ: ℍ → ℂ ∖ {0, 1} is a covering, so f lifts to an entire F: ℂ → ℍ with λ ∘ F = f (path lifting and monodromy, CA.8a). The Cayley transform (CA.10) composed with F is bounded entire, hence constant (CA.2). Prose: compare CA.14's proof via Bloch and Schottky, which needs no elliptic functions; this one needs the covering, which is the hard half of `thm-lambda-mapping`.
- Prose (one paragraph, no proof): the Klein invariant J = (4/27)(1 − λ + λ²)³/(λ²(1 − λ)²) is invariant under the whole SL₂(ℤ), and J = g₂³/Δ; two lattices are homothetic iff they have the same J. Point to CA.8b (optional, if read) for the theta-function construction of λ.
- **Warning.** λ is not invariant under all of SL₂(ℤ): the six values λ, 1 − λ, 1/λ, 1/(1 − λ), λ/(λ − 1), (λ − 1)/λ are permuted (this is where the cross-ratio's six values reappear, CA.10).
- **Quick check.** Why can λ never take the values 0 and 1? *Answer:* λ = 0 means e₃ = e₂ and λ = 1 means e₃ = e₁, both excluded by `thm-wp-roots-distinct`.

  - **C2.** Find a lattice Λ with g₂(Λ) = 4 and g₃(Λ) = 0. *Answer:* the roots of 4x³ − 4x are 1, −1, 0, which have the shape (e₁, −e₁, 0) of the square lattice (§03 C1), and cross-ratio λ = ½ = λ(i) (A2). So Λ = cℤ[i] with e₁(cℤ[i]) = c⁻²e₁(ℤ[i]) = 1, i.e. c = √(e₁(ℤ[i])), with e₁(ℤ[i]) > 0 (℘ is real and decreasing on (0, ½]); either sign of c gives the same lattice.
- **R group** (last section of the chapter; each ≤ 20 min; sources named only in solutions).
  - **R1** (one chapter back, CA.14): λ restricted to the disc |τ − 2i| < 1 omits 0 and 1. What does Schottky's theorem say about |λ| on |τ − 2i| ≤ ½? *Answer:* a bound depending only on |λ(2i)| and the radius ratio ½ (CA.14's Schottky theorem applied to w ↦ λ(2i + w)).
  - **R2** (3–4 chapters back, CA.7): show σ is an entire function of order 2. *Answer:* the number of ω ∈ Λ with |ω| ≤ r is ~ πr²/area(P), so the exponent of convergence of the zeros is 2; a genus-2 canonical product with that exponent has order 2 (CA.7).
  - **R3** (spiral, CA.4): compute (1/2πi)∮_{|z|=2} z f′(z)/f(z) dz for f(z) = (z − 1)²/(z + ½). *Answer:* 2·1 − (−½) = 5/2.
  - **R4** (no method hint): show that a nonconstant elliptic function cannot be injective on any period parallelogram P. *Answer:* the order is at least 2 (`cor-no-order-one`), so every value is taken at least twice in P (`thm-liouville-elliptic-count`); look-alike tools (open mapping, Picard) give nothing here.
