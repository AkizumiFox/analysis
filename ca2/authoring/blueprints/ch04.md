**Purpose.**
- Two functions that start life as real objects (the factorial and Σ n^{−s}) turn out to be shadows of meromorphic functions on ℂ. The chapter shows how to continue each one, how to pin it down uniquely once continued, and what the continuation knows that the original formula did not.
- Γ: Euler's integral → continuation by the functional equation → Weierstrass product and reflection → characterisations (Bohr–Mollerup on ℝ, Wielandt on ℂ) → complex Stirling.
- ζ: Dirichlet series and Euler product → continuation by subtracting the divergent part → Riemann's loop integral and the functional equation → zeros (trivial zeros, no zeros on Re s = 1, the critical strip, infinitely many nontrivial zeros).
- The prime number theorem is not proved. The chapter ends at its analytic input (ζ(1+it) ≠ 0) and says so in prose.

**Named moves** (in `index.md`, "Named moves"):
1. **Continue by the functional equation** (from CA.8a, reused). If f(z+1) = z f(z) on a half-plane, define f on the next strip to the left by f(z) = f(z+1)/z, and repeat. (§01, §02 for Gauss's formula, §03 for Wielandt; for ζ the functional equation in §05 plays the same role across Re s = ½.)
2. **Subtract the divergent part.** Isolate the piece of a series or integral that diverges (1/(s − 1) for ζ, ∫₀¹ for Γ), compute it in closed form, and show what is left converges on a larger region. (§01 Prym, §05 Euler summation.)
3. **Uniqueness by boundedness.** To show two solutions of the same functional equation agree, form a combination that is periodic (or antiperiodic) and bounded on a period strip, and apply Liouville. (§03, Wielandt.)
4. **Pay for decay by integrating by parts against a periodic function.** Replace ∫ P₁ f by ∫ Q f′: one more derivative on f, one more power of decay. (§04, §05.)
5. **Open the cut and push the contour out.** Write a real integral as a loop around a slit, then deform the loop outward and collect residues. (§05.)
6. **The positivity trick.** 3 + 4 cos θ + cos 2θ = 2(1 + cos θ)² ≥ 0 combined with non-negative Dirichlet coefficients of log ζ. (§06.)

## 01-gamma-integral.md — Interpolating the factorial (≤ 3,500 words)

- **Hook (section).** Which number should ½! be? Infinitely many smooth functions pass through the points (n, (n−1)!). Show three graphs through the same integer points. The section produces one candidate, continues it to ℂ, and ends by showing the functional equation alone does **not** single it out (the impostor). The fix is §03.
- **`def-gamma`.** Γ(z) = ∫₀^∞ t^{z−1} e^{−t} dt for Re z > 0, with t^{z−1} = e^{(z−1) log t}. Recall (prose, revisits AN2.11) the real case and Γ(n) = (n−1)!.
- **`lem-holomorphic-parameter-integral`** (only if CA.2 has no such label). Let φ: [a, b] × U → ℂ be continuous, with φ(t, ·) holomorphic on the open set U for each t. Then F(z) = ∫ₐᵇ φ(t, z) dt is holomorphic on U and F′(z) = ∫ₐᵇ ∂_zφ(t, z) dt.
  - *Proof route:* on a closed disc D̄(z₀, 2r) ⊂ U, Cauchy's formula for φ(t, ·) on |w − z₀| = 2r writes the difference quotient (φ(t, z+h) − φ(t, z))/h − ∂_zφ(t, z) as an integral bounded by C|h| sup|φ| for |z − z₀| ≤ r, |h| ≤ r/2, uniformly in t (the sup is finite by compactness). Uniform convergence under ∫ (AN1.9) finishes. No Fubini.
- **`thm-gamma-holomorphic`.** Γ is holomorphic on Re z > 0.
  - *Proof route:* Fₙ(z) = ∫_{1/n}^{n} t^{z−1}e^{−t} dt is holomorphic (lemma). On a compact K ⊂ {a ≤ Re z ≤ b}, 0 < a, |t^{z−1}e^{−t}| = t^{x−1}e^{−t} ≤ t^{a−1}e^{−t} + t^{b−1}e^{−t}, so the tails are uniformly small; Weierstrass convergence theorem (CA.2).
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance: locally uniform convergence of an improper integral).** Show the reader how to find the dominating function: the only thing that depends on y is a unimodular factor t^{iy}, so |t^{z−1}| = t^{x−1}; then bound t^{x−1} on (0, 1] by t^{a−1} and on [1, ∞) by t^{b−1}. Then the proof, written forwards.
- **`prp-gamma-modulus-bound`.** |Γ(z)| ≤ Γ(Re z) for Re z > 0. One line from |t^{z−1}| = t^{x−1}. Used in §03 (boundedness on strips).
- **`thm-gamma-functional-equation`.** Γ(z + 1) = z Γ(z) for Re z > 0; Γ(1) = 1. Integration by parts on [ε, R], boundary terms vanish because Re z > 0.
- **`thm-gamma-continuation`.** Γ extends uniquely to a meromorphic function on ℂ, holomorphic except for simple poles at 0, −1, −2, …, and the extension still satisfies Γ(z+1) = zΓ(z).
  - *Proof route (named move 1, introduced in CA.8a):* apply `prp-functional-equation-continuation` (CA.8a) to Γ with `thm-gamma-holomorphic` and `thm-gamma-functional-equation`. The poles are actually present because the residues (−1)ⁿΓ(1)/n! ≠ 0. This pays CA.8a's hook "Γ extends by the shift lemma". No Scratch Work here (the technique's first appearance is in CA.8a); a Proof Idea recalls the strip picture in two sentences.
- **`cor-gamma-residues`.** Res_{z=−n} Γ = (−1)ⁿ/n!. From the residue formula of `prp-functional-equation-continuation` with Γ(1) = 1; one line re-derives it from Γ(z) = Γ(z + n + 1)/(z(z+1)⋯(z+n)) for the reader who skipped CA.8a's exercise.
- **`prp-gamma-prym`** (named move 2, first appearance). For z ∉ {0, −1, −2, …}:
  Γ(z) = Σ_{n≥0} (−1)ⁿ/(n!(z + n)) + ∫₁^∞ t^{z−1} e^{−t} dt.
  The series converges locally uniformly on ℂ∖(−ℕ) (M-test with 1/n! away from the poles), the integral is entire (lemma + tails). Proof: split Γ = ∫₀¹ + ∫₁^∞ on Re z > 0, expand e^{−t} in ∫₀¹ and integrate termwise (uniform convergence on [0,1] after multiplying out t^{z−1}; Re z > 0 needed for each ∫₀¹ t^{z+n−1}). Then the right side is meromorphic on ℂ and agrees with Γ on Re z > 0. Remark (prose): this is a partial-fraction expansion of exactly the shape CA.12's Mittag-Leffler theorem guarantees in general — no `@ref`, CA.12 is not an ancestor.
- **Worked example (full).** Γ(−3/2) from Γ(½) = √π (promised for §02): Γ(−½) = Γ(½)/(−½) = −2√π, Γ(−3/2) = Γ(−½)/(−3/2) = (4/3)√π.
- **`exm-gamma-impostor`** (weird example, first row of the table). f(z) = Γ(z)(1 + sin² πz). Then f(z + 1) = z f(z), f(1) = 1, f(n) = (n − 1)!, and f > 0 on (0, ∞). So "interpolates the factorial and satisfies the functional equation" does not determine Γ. Generally f = pΓ with p meromorphic, 1-periodic, p(1) = 1. **This is the failure that motivates §03** (log-convexity on ℝ; boundedness on a strip in ℂ).
- **Warning.** "Γ(z) = ∫₀^∞ t^{z−1}e^{−t} dt for every z ≠ 0, −1, …" is false: the integral diverges at 0 for Re z ≤ 0. The continuation is *defined* by the functional equation (or Prym), not by the integral.
- **Quick check.** Is Γ(z) = ∫₀^∞ t^{z−1}e^{−t} dt at z = −½ + 7i? *Answer:* no, the integral diverges (|t^{z−1}| = t^{−3/2} near 0); Γ(−½ + 7i) = Γ(½ + 7i)/(−½ + 7i).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Compute Γ(5/2), Γ(−½), Γ(−5/2) given Γ(½) = √π. *Answer:* (3/4)√π; −2√π; −(8/15)√π [Γ(−5/2) = Γ(−3/2)/(−5/2) = (4/3)√π · (−2/5)].
  - **A2.** Res_{z=−3} Γ and Res_{z=−4} Γ. *Answer:* −1/6; 1/24.
  - **B1.** For a > 0 show ∫₀^∞ t^{z−1}e^{−at} dt = a^{−z}Γ(z) (Re z > 0); then extend to complex a with Re a > 0, a^{−z} = e^{−z Log a}. *Answer:* substitution for real a; both sides are holomorphic in a on Re a > 0 (lemma + locally uniform tails in a) and agree on (0, ∞); identity theorem. (Used in §05.)
  - **B2.** Show Γ(z̄) = conj Γ(z) on ℂ∖(−ℕ). *Answer:* true on Re z > 0 from the integral; z ↦ conj Γ(z̄) is meromorphic and agrees with Γ on (0, ∞); identity theorem.
  - **B3.** Prove `prp-gamma-prym` in full, including the locally uniform convergence of the series on ℂ∖(−ℕ).
  - **C1 (exit check).** For −1 < Re z < 0 show Γ(z) = ∫₀^∞ t^{z−1}(e^{−t} − 1) dt. *Answer:* the integral converges (near 0, |e^{−t} − 1| ≤ t; near ∞, |t^{z−1}| = t^{x−1} with x < 0); integrate by parts with u = e^{−t} − 1, dv = t^{z−1}dt: boundary terms t^z(e^{−t} − 1)/z vanish at 0 (Re z > −1) and at ∞ (Re z < 0), leaving (1/z)∫₀^∞ t^z e^{−t} dt = Γ(z+1)/z = Γ(z). Marking outline: convergence at both ends (2), integration by parts with both boundary terms justified (3), identification with the continuation (1).

## 02-gamma-product.md — The product formula and the reflection formula (≤ 4,000 words)

- **Hook.** §01 has Γ as an integral. CA.7 built entire functions with prescribed zeros; Γ has no zeros (to be proved) but prescribed poles, so 1/Γ should be such a product. Find which.
- **`lem-exp-approximation`.** For n ≥ 1 and 0 ≤ t ≤ n: 0 ≤ e^{−t} − (1 − t/n)ⁿ ≤ t² e^{−t}/n.
  - *Proof route:* the lower bound from 1 − u ≤ e^{−u}. Upper bound: e^t(1 − t/n)ⁿ ≥ (1 + t/n)ⁿ(1 − t/n)ⁿ = (1 − t²/n²)ⁿ ≥ 1 − t²/n by Bernoulli (valid as t²/n² ≤ 1).
- **`thm-gauss-formula`.** For z ∉ {0, −1, −2, …}: Γ(z) = lim_{n→∞} n! nᶻ / (z(z+1)⋯(z+n)), locally uniformly on ℂ∖(−ℕ).
  - *Proof route:* (i) Re z > 0: ∫₀ⁿ t^{z−1}(1 − t/n)ⁿ dt = nᶻ ∫₀¹ u^{z−1}(1−u)ⁿ du = n! nᶻ/(z(z+1)⋯(z+n)) by n integrations by parts. (ii) By the lemma, |Γ(z) − that| ≤ Γ(x + 2)/n + ∫ₙ^∞ t^{x−1}e^{−t} dt → 0, uniformly on compacts of Re z > 0. (iii) Write Gₙ(z) for the quotient; Gₙ(z + 1) = z Gₙ(z) · n/(z + n + 1), so the convergence transfers strip by strip to the left (named move 1 again, now for a sequence).
- **`def-euler-mascheroni`.** γ = lim (Hₙ − log n), Hₙ = 1 + ½ + ⋯ + 1/n. Existence: Hₙ − log n is decreasing and bounded below by 0 (comparison with ∫ dt/t). One paragraph; revisit pointer AN2.11 if it is there.
- **`thm-gamma-weierstrass-product`.** 1/Γ(z) = z e^{γz} Π_{k≥1} (1 + z/k) e^{−z/k}, the product converging locally uniformly on ℂ.
  - *Proof route:* 1/Gₙ(z) = z e^{z(Hₙ − log n)} Π_{k=1}^{n} (1 + z/k)e^{−z/k} (algebra: nᶻ = e^{z log n}, multiply and divide by e^{zHₙ}). The product converges locally uniformly by `thm-holomorphic-product` (CA.7), since |(1 + w)e^{−w} − 1| ≤ C|w|² for |w| ≤ 1 (`def-elementary-factor`, E₁). Let n → ∞ using `thm-gauss-formula` and `def-euler-mascheroni`.

## 03-characterizations.md — What singles out Γ (≤ 3,500 words)

- **Hook.** Return to the impostor `exm-gamma-impostor`. Two extra conditions each kill it: on ℝ, log-convexity (Bohr–Mollerup); on ℂ, boundedness on a vertical strip (Wielandt). The section proves both and uses Wielandt as a machine for identities (duplication, Beta).
- **Failure-motivated condition.** Show the impostor failing log-convexity: with g(x) = log(1 + sin² πx), g″(n + ½) = −π², while ψ′(½) = π²/2 < π² and ψ′ decreases; so (log f)″ < 0 at every x = n + ½ (n ≥ 0). *Check of the numbers (S2a):* ψ′(½) = Σ 1/(k + ½)² = 4 Σ 1/(2k+1)² = 4 · π²/8 = π²/2.
- **`thm-bohr-mollerup`** (revisits AN2.11). If f: (0, ∞) → (0, ∞) is log-convex, f(x + 1) = x f(x) and f(1) = 1, then f = Γ on (0, ∞).

- **`thm-wielandt`.** Let F be holomorphic on Re z > 0 with F(z + 1) = zF(z), F(1) = 1, and F bounded on the strip {1 ≤ Re z ≤ 2}. Then F = Γ on Re z > 0.

Put H(z) = G(z)G(1 − z): entire, H(z + 1) = G(z+1)G(−z) = zG(z) · G(1 − z)/(−z) = −H(z), bounded on the strip 0 ≤ Re z ≤ 1 (both z and 1 − z lie in it), hence bounded on ℂ by antiperiodicity; Liouville: H is constant, = H(1) = G(1)G(0) = 0. So G(z)G(1−z) ≡ 0, and since the ring of entire functions has no zero divisors (identity theorem), G ≡ 0.

## 04-stirling.md — Stirling's formula in the complex plane (≤ 4,500 words)

- **Hook.** Γ(n + 1) = n! grows like √(2πn)(n/e)ⁿ (AN2.11, revisited). In ℂ there is more to know: how fast does Γ decay up a vertical line? §03's impostor and §05's ζ estimates both need the answer. One formula does it, valid in every sector avoiding the negative axis — and only there (`exm-stirling-fails-near-poles`).
- **`def-periodic-bernoulli`.** P₁(t) = {t} − ½, Q(t) = ({t}² − {t})/2. Facts: Q is continuous and 1-periodic, Q(n) = 0, Q′ = P₁ off ℤ, −1/8 ≤ Q ≤ 0.
- **`lem-euler-summation`** (re-derived; revisits AN2.11). For integers a < b and f ∈ C¹[a, b]: Σ_{n=a}^{b} f(n) = ∫ₐᵇ f + (f(a) + f(b))/2 + ∫ₐᵇ P₁(t) f′(t) dt. Proof: integrate by parts on each [n, n+1], where P₁(t) = t − n − ½. *Sanity check for S2a:* f(t) = t² on [0, 1] gives 1 = 1/3 + 1/2 + 1/6.
  - Named move 4: ∫ P₁ f′ = −∫ Q f″ when Q f′ → 0 at the ends (Q(a) = Q(b) = 0).
- **`lem-sector-bound`.** For 0 < δ ≤ π put κ_δ = min(1, 1 − cos δ). For z = re^{iθ} with |θ| ≤ π − δ and t ≥ 0: |z + t|² ≥ κ_δ(r² + t²). Hence ∫₀^∞ dt/|z + t|² ≤ π/(2κ_δ|z|).
  - *Proof:* |z + t|² = r² + t² + 2rt cos θ. If cos θ ≥ 0 this is ≥ r² + t². If cos θ < 0 then π/2 < |θ| ≤ π − δ, so |cos θ| ≤ cos δ, and 2rt|cos θ| ≤ (r² + t²) cos δ, giving ≥ (1 − cos δ)(r² + t²). Then ∫₀^∞ dt/(r² + t²) = π/(2r).
  - *Why the min:* without it the bound is false for δ > π/2 (at θ = 0, (r + t)² < 2(r² + t²) when t ≠ r). S2a item.
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance: estimates uniform in a sector).** Draw the ray −t and the sector; explain why the bound must degenerate as δ → 0 (z near the negative axis can be close to −t).
- **`def-log-gamma`.** On S = ℂ∖(−∞, 0], log Γ is the holomorphic logarithm of Γ that is real on (0, ∞). Existence: S is simply connected and Γ is holomorphic and zero-free on S (`cor-reciprocal-gamma-entire`), so `cor-roots-and-logs-by-monodromy` (CA.8a) gives a holomorphic logarithm; normalise it to be real on (0, ∞). The proof of the next theorem constructs it explicitly anyway, and the two agree (they differ by a constant in 2πiℤ that vanishes on (0, ∞)).
- **`thm-log-gamma-binet`.** For z ∈ S:
  log Γ(z) = (z − ½) Log z − z + ½ log 2π + μ(z),  μ(z) = −∫₀^∞ Q(t)/(z + t)² dt.
  - *Proof route (a key proof):* (i) Eₙ(z) := log n! + z log n − Σ_{k=0}^{n} Log(z + k) is holomorphic on S and e^{Eₙ} = Gₙ (Gauss quotient). (ii) Euler summation with f(t) = Log(z + t) on [0, n]: Σ Log(z+k) = (z+n)Log(z+n) − (z+n) − z Log z + z + ½(Log z + Log(z+n)) + ∫₀ⁿ P₁(t)/(z + t) dt. (iii) Write log n! = (n + ½)log n − n + cₙ, where cₙ = 1 + ∫₁ⁿ P₁(t)/t dt (Euler summation for log on [1, n]) converges to some C₀. (iv) (z + n + ½)Log(1 + z/n) → z. Collect: Eₙ(z) → C₀ + (z − ½)Log z − z − ∫₀^∞ P₁(t)/(z + t) dt locally uniformly on S. (v) Integrate by parts once (named move 4) to get −∫₀^∞ Q(t)/(z+t)² dt, absolutely convergent by `lem-sector-bound`. (vi) So the limit L(z) is holomorphic with e^L = Γ (Gauss) and real on (0, ∞): L = log Γ. (vii) C₀ = ½ log 2π: insert the asymptotic (with μ → 0 on (0, ∞)) into `thm-legendre-duplication` at real z = x → ∞; the x log x, x and log x terms cancel and 2C₀ − C₀ = ½ log π + ½ log 2.
  - *S2a items:* the limit in (iv) is locally uniform on S; the branch identity in (ii) uses ∫₀ⁿ Log(z + t) dt computed with an antiderivative valid on S (z + t ∈ S for t ≥ 0).
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance: matching asymptotic expansions term by term).** The bookkeeping of (iv)–(vii) is done once in scratch, showing which terms cancel and why the unknown C₀ must be pinned by an independent identity.
- **`thm-stirling-complex`.** For 0 < δ ≤ π and z ∈ S with |arg z| ≤ π − δ: |μ(z)| ≤ π / (16 κ_δ |z|). In particular log Γ(z) = (z − ½)Log z − z + ½ log 2π + O(1/|z|) uniformly in the sector. One line from |Q| ≤ 1/8 and `lem-sector-bound`.
- **`cor-stirling-factorial`.** n! = √(2πn)(n/e)ⁿ e^{μ(n)} with 0 < μ(n) ≤ 1/(8n). (n! = nΓ(n). For real x > 0, μ(x) > 0 because −Q ≥ 0 and Q ≢ 0, and μ(x) ≤ ⅛∫₀^∞ dt/(x + t)² = 1/(8x) by direct integration — sharper than the sector bound here.) - **`cor-gamma-vertical`.** For x in a compact interval [a, b] and |y| → ∞: |Γ(x + iy)| = √(2π)|y|^{x−½} e^{−π|y|/2}(1 + o(1)), uniformly in x.
  - *Proof route:* Re[(z − ½)Log z] = (x − ½)log|z| − y arg z, with y arg z = π|y|/2 − x + o(1) and log|z| = log|y| + o(1/|y|)·(bounded); the −x cancels the −Re z. Points with x ≤ 0 are eventually in the sector |arg z| ≤ 3π/4.

- **`exm-stirling-fails-near-poles`** (weird example). μ is unbounded on S near each −n (n ≥ 1): Γ(z) ~ (−1)ⁿ/(n!(z + n)), so Re log Γ(−n + iε) = −log ε + O(1) → +∞ as ε → 0⁺, while (z − ½)Log z − z stays bounded near −n + i0. ## 05-zeta.md — The zeta function: continuation and functional equation (≤ 5,000 words)

- **Hook.** The chapter hook: "1 + 2 + 3 + ⋯ = −1/12". Σ n^{−s} converges only for Re s > 1. Is there a meromorphic function on a larger region that agrees with it there — and if so, is it unique (CA.8a)? Three methods, one answer.
- **`def-zeta`, `thm-zeta-holomorphic`.** ζ(s) = Σ_{n≥1} n^{−s}, n^{−s} = e^{−s log n}, holomorphic on Re s > 1 (M-test on Re s ≥ 1 + ε; Weierstrass convergence theorem). |n^{−s}| = n^{−σ}.
- **`thm-euler-product`.** For Re s > 1, ζ(s) = Π_p (1 − p^{−s})^{−1}, the product over primes converging absolutely.
  - *Proof route:* for N ≥ 2, Π_{p≤N}(1 − p^{−s})^{−1} = Σ' n^{−s} over n whose prime factors are all ≤ N (finite product of absolutely convergent geometric series; unique factorisation, prose pointer F3.5). The difference from ζ(s) is bounded by Σ_{n>N} n^{−σ} → 0. Absolute convergence of the product: Σ_p |p^{−s}| ≤ Σ n^{−σ} < ∞ (CA.7 `thm-product-abs-criterion`). Revisits AN2.12 (real s) in prose.
- **`thm-zeta-continuation-strip`** (named move 2, the chapter's second use). For Re s > 1:
  ζ(s) = 1/(s − 1) + ½ − s ∫₁^∞ P₁(t) t^{−s−1} dt = 1/(s − 1) + ½ − s(s + 1) ∫₁^∞ Q(t) t^{−s−2} dt.
  The first integral converges absolutely for Re s > 0, the second for Re s > −1; so ζ continues meromorphically to Re s > −1 with a single simple pole at s = 1.
  - *Proof route:* `lem-euler-summation` with f(t) = t^{−s} on [1, N], N → ∞; then named move 4. Holomorphy of the integrals in s: `lem-holomorphic-parameter-integral` on [1, R] plus locally uniform tails.
  - Remark (prose): repeating with higher periodic Bernoulli functions reaches every half-plane (Euler–Maclaurin); not needed, because the loop integral below reaches ℂ in one step.
- **`cor-zeta-pole`.** Res_{s=1} ζ = 1, and ζ(0) = −½ (put s = 0 in the second formula).
- **`thm-gamma-zeta-mellin`.** For Re s > 1: Γ(s)ζ(s) = ∫₀^∞ t^{s−1}/(eᵗ − 1) dt.
  - *Proof route:* §01 B1 gives Γ(s)n^{−s} = ∫₀^∞ t^{s−1}e^{−nt} dt. For real s > 1 sum over n: integrands non-negative, Σ_{n≤N} e^{−nt} ↑ 1/(eᵗ − 1), and on [ε, R] the convergence is uniform; the improper integrals then pass to the limit by monotonicity (spell out the two inequalities). Both sides are holomorphic on Re s > 1 (right side: lemma + tails); identity theorem.
- **`def-hankel-loop`.** For 0 < ε < 2π, C_ε is the path that comes in from +∞ to ε along the upper edge of the cut [0, ∞), goes once counterclockwise around |w| = ε, and returns to +∞ along the lower edge. (−w)^{s−1} = e^{(s−1)Log(−w)} on ℂ∖[0, ∞).
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance: arguments on the two edges of a cut).** On the upper edge w = t + i0, −w = −t − i0 has argument −π, so (−w)^{s−1} = t^{s−1}e^{−iπ(s−1)}; on the lower edge argument +π, factor e^{+iπ(s−1)}. A picture with both edges labelled. This is the step drafts most often get wrong; the Scratch Work does it slowly, then the proof uses it in one line.
- **`thm-zeta-hankel`.** I(s) = ∫_{C_ε} (−w)^{s−1}/(e^w − 1) dw is an entire function of s, independent of ε ∈ (0, 2π), and for every s ∉ {1, 2, 3, …}:
  ζ(s) = −(Γ(1 − s)/(2πi)) I(s).
  - *Proof route:* (i) entire: on the two rays |(−w)^{s−1}| ≤ e^{π|Im s|}t^{σ−1}, so the integrand is bounded by e^{π|Im s|}t^{σ−1}/(eᵗ − 1) on t ≥ ε, integrable at ∞ uniformly for s in compacts; the circle part is entire by the lemma. (ii) Independence of ε: Cauchy's theorem in the slit region, which contains no pole of 1/(e^w − 1) when ε < 2π. (iii) For Re s > 1 let ε → 0: the circle contributes O(ε^{σ−1}) → 0; the edges give (e^{iπ(s−1)} − e^{−iπ(s−1)})Γ(s)ζ(s) = −2i sin(πs) Γ(s)ζ(s) by `thm-gamma-zeta-mellin`. (iv) Reflection: −I(s)/(2i sin(πs)Γ(s)) = −Γ(1 − s)I(s)/(2πi). (v) Identity theorem: the right side is meromorphic on ℂ.
- **`thm-zeta-meromorphic`.** ζ extends to a meromorphic function on ℂ whose only pole is a simple pole at s = 1 with residue 1. Proof: poles of the right side can only be at poles of Γ(1 − s), i.e. s = 1, 2, …; for s = n ≥ 2, ζ is already holomorphic (so I(n) = 0 — and directly: the integrand is single-valued and holomorphic at 0, so the edges cancel and the circle integral is 0). At s = 1: I(1) = ∮ dw/(e^w − 1) = 2πi, Γ(1 − s) ~ −1/(s − 1).
  - **Remark (CA.8a).** The three formulas (series on Re s > 1, Euler summation on Re s > −1, loop on ℂ) are three function elements of one global analytic function; uniqueness of continuation (`thm-continuation-along-path-unique`, CA.8a) says the agreement on Re s > 1 already forces agreement wherever two are defined. Since ζ is single-valued meromorphic on ℂ, no monodromy appears; contrast with log Γ, which needed the slit.
- **`def-bernoulli-numbers`.** w/(e^w − 1) = Σ_{k≥0} Bₖ wᵏ/k! for |w| < 2π. B₀ = 1, B₁ = −½, B₂ = 1/6, B₃ = 0, B₄ = −1/30; B_{2j+1} = 0 for j ≥ 1 (w/(e^w − 1) + w/2 is even).
- **`thm-zeta-negative-integers`.** For m ≥ 0: ζ(−m) = (−1)^m B_{m+1}/(m + 1). Hence ζ(0) = −½, ζ(−1) = −1/12, ζ(−2k) = 0 for k ≥ 1, ζ(1 − 2k) = −B_{2k}/(2k).
  - *Proof route:* at s = −m the integrand is single-valued, so I(−m) = 2πi Res_{w=0} (−w)^{−m−1}/(e^w − 1) = 2πi(−1)^{m+1}B_{m+1}/(m+1)!; multiply by −Γ(1 + m)/(2πi) = −m!/(2πi).
- **`exm-minus-one-twelfth`** (pays the hook). ζ(−1) = −1/12 is a value of the continuation; the partial sums Σ_{n≤N} n → +∞. Warning text: "the series does not converge to −1/12; the function the series defines on Re s > 1 continues to a function whose value at −1 is −1/12." Consistency with the Euler-summation formula is not available at s = −1 (it only reaches Re s > −1); the loop integral is needed.
- **`thm-zeta-functional-equation`.** For all s (as meromorphic functions): ζ(s) = 2^s π^{s−1} sin(πs/2) Γ(1 − s) ζ(1 − s).
  - *Proof route (named move 5; a key proof):* for Re s < 0, enlarge the loop: let R_N = (2N + 1)π. The positively oriented boundary of {|w| < R_N}∖[0, R_N] is the big circle minus the truncated loop, so ∮_{|w|=R_N} − ∫_{C_ε ∩ {|w|≤R_N}} = 2πi Σ_{0<|k|≤N} Res_{w=2πik}, each residue = (−2πik)^{s−1}. On |w| = R_N, |1/(e^w − 1)| is bounded independently of N (S2a: confirm — the circles pass midway between the poles; give the bound via the two regimes |Re w| ≥ π/2 and |Re w| < π/2 where |Im w − 2πk| ≥ π/2... or use squares instead of circles if cleaner) and |(−w)^{s−1}| ≤ R_N^{σ−1}e^{π|Im s|}; so the big circle → 0 since σ < 0. Hence I(s) = −2πi Σ_{k≠0}(−2πik)^{s−1}. With (−2πik)^{s−1} = (2π|k|)^{s−1}e^{∓iπ(s−1)/2} for k ≷ 0, the sum is 2(2π)^{s−1} sin(πs/2) ζ(1 − s) (Re(1 − s) > 1). Then `thm-zeta-hankel`. Extend from Re s < 0 by the identity theorem.
  - **Ordering exercise** placed here (STYLE §11): the six steps of this proof, shuffled; the reader orders them and names the hypothesis each uses.
  - Remark: the theta-function route (Poisson summation) is `see HA.4` and `see CA.8b`; no statement from there is used.
- **`cor-completed-zeta`.** ξ(s) = ½ s(s − 1) π^{−s/2} Γ(s/2) ζ(s) is entire and ξ(s) = ξ(1 − s).
  - *Proof route:* duplication at z = (1 − s)/2 gives Γ(1 − s) = 2^{−s}π^{−½}Γ((1 − s)/2)Γ(1 − s/2); reflection gives Γ(s/2)Γ(1 − s/2) = π/sin(πs/2). Substitute into the functional equation: π^{−s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s) = π^{−(1−s)/2}Γ((1 − s)/2)ζ(1 − s). Entire: the pole of ζ at 1 and of Γ(s/2) at 0 are cancelled by s(s − 1); the poles of Γ(s/2) at −2k are cancelled by the trivial zeros.
- **Warning.** "ζ(s) = Σ n^{−s} for all s ≠ 1" — false for Re s ≤ 1 (divergence); and the functional equation does not relate the series at s and 1 − s, since at most one of them converges.
- **Quick check.** ζ(−2) from `thm-zeta-negative-integers`, and from the functional equation. *Answer:* B₃ = 0 gives 0; sin(−π) = 0 gives 0 (ζ(3), Γ(3) finite).

## 06-zeros.md — Where ζ vanishes (≤ 4,000 words)

- **Hook.** Euler's product shows ζ has no zeros where the series converges. The functional equation reflects that region to Re s < 0, where the only zeros are forced by sin(πs/2). Everything else lives in the strip 0 ≤ Re s ≤ 1, and the edge Re s = 1 is the hardest — it is the analytic input of the prime number theorem (prose; not proved in this series).
- **`prp-eta-relation`.** For Re s > 0, s ≠ 1: (1 − 2^{1−s}) ζ(s) = η(s). Proof: on Re s > 1 subtract 2·Σ(2n)^{−s} from ζ; both sides holomorphic on Re s > 0 (§05 C3), identity theorem. **Pays the hook's consistency:** η(−1) = (1 − 4)ζ(−1) = ¼ (η is entire: it equals (1 − 2^{1−s})ζ(s), whose only possible pole at s = 1 is cancelled), and ¼ is the Abel sum of 1 − 2 + 3 − ⋯ (prose AN2.13). The agreement is observed, not claimed as a general theorem.
- **`exm-eta-zeros-on-line`** (weird example). η(1 + 2πik/log 2) = 0 for k ∈ ℤ∖{0}: there 1 − 2^{1−s} = 0 and ζ is holomorphic. So η has zeros on Re s = 1 that ζ does not have (`thm-zeta-nonvanishing-line`).
- **`thm-zeta-zero-free-right`.** ζ(s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 1: an absolutely convergent product of non-zero factors (CA.7 `thm-product-abs-criterion`).
- **`thm-zeta-zeros-left`.** For Re s < 0 the zeros of ζ are exactly s = −2, −4, −6, …, all simple. Proof: in the functional equation, ζ(1 − s) ≠ 0, Γ(1 − s) is finite and non-zero, 2^sπ^{s−1} ≠ 0; sin(πs/2) has simple zeros at even integers.
- **`prp-zeta-negative-on-unit-interval`.** ζ(σ) < 0 for 0 < σ < 1 (and ζ(0) = −½). Proof: η(σ) > 0 (alternating series with decreasing terms, first term 1, η(σ) ≥ 1 − 2^{−σ} > 0) and 1 − 2^{1−σ} < 0. Weird example row: "sum of positive terms" negative.
- **`lem-three-four-one`.** 3 + 4 cos θ + cos 2θ = 2(1 + cos θ)² ≥ 0.
- **`thm-zeta-nonvanishing-line`.** ζ(1 + it) ≠ 0 for real t ≠ 0.
  - *Proof route (named move 6; a key proof):* for σ > 1, log|ζ(s)| = Re Σ_p Σ_{m≥1} p^{−ms}/m = Σ_n cₙ n^{−σ} cos(t log n) with cₙ ≥ 0 (cₙ = 1/m if n = p^m, else 0). No branch issue: exp of the double series' partial sums equals the Euler partial products, so |ζ(s)| = exp(Re ⋯). Hence |ζ(σ)³ ζ(σ + it)⁴ ζ(σ + 2it)| ≥ 1 by the lemma. If ζ(1 + it) = 0: as σ → 1⁺, ζ(σ)³ = O((σ − 1)^{−3}) (`cor-zeta-pole`), ζ(σ + it)⁴ = O((σ − 1)⁴), ζ(σ + 2it) = O(1) (holomorphic at 1 + 2it since 2t ≠ 0); the product → 0. Contradiction.
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance: positivity of Dirichlet coefficients).** Why exponents (3, 4, 1): a zero of order ≥ 1 at 1 + it must beat the pole of order 1 at 1; show the reader that the naive ζ(σ)|ζ(σ + it)| ≥ 1 (from 1 + cos θ ≥ 0) gives (σ − 1)^{−1}·O(σ − 1) = O(1), no contradiction, and how weighting fixes the count.
- **`def-critical-strip`, `cor-nontrivial-zeros-strip`.** The critical strip is 0 < Re s < 1. Every zero of ζ other than −2, −4, … lies in it. Proof: Re s > 1 (`thm-zeta-zero-free-right`), Re s = 1 (s = 1 is a pole; `thm-zeta-nonvanishing-line`), Re s < 0 (`thm-zeta-zeros-left`), Re s = 0 (functional equation reflects Re s = 1; ζ(0) = −½ ≠ 0).
- **`prp-zero-symmetry`.** If ρ is a zero in the critical strip, so are ρ̄, 1 − ρ, 1 − ρ̄. Proof: ζ(s̄) = conj ζ(s) (true on Re s > 1, identity theorem) and ξ(s) = ξ(1 − s), with the zeros of ζ in the strip exactly the zeros of ξ.
- **`thm-xi-order-one`.** ξ is entire of order exactly 1, and not of exponential type.
  - *Proof route:* for Re s ≥ ½, |s| ≥ 2: |s(s − 1)ζ(s)| ≤ C|s|³ (§05 B3), |π^{−s/2}| ≤ e^{C|s|}, |Γ(s/2)| ≤ e^{C|s| log|s|} (`thm-stirling-complex` with δ = π/2: |arg(s/2)| ≤ π/2 for Re s ≥ 0). So |ξ(s)| ≤ e^{C|s|log|s|}; for Re s ≤ ½ use ξ(s) = ξ(1 − s). Order ≤ 1 (CA.7 `def-order-entire`). On the real axis ξ(σ) ≥ c Γ(σ/2) π^{−σ/2} for σ ≥ 2 (ζ(σ) ≥ 1), superexponential by `cor-stirling-factorial`: order ≥ 1 and not of exponential type.
- **`cor-infinitely-many-zeros`.** ζ has infinitely many zeros in the critical strip. Proof: if ξ had finitely many zeros, Hadamard (CA.7 `thm-hadamard`) with order 1 gives ξ(s) = P(s)e^{as + b}, so |ξ(σ)| ≤ Cσ^d e^{|a|σ}, contradicting `thm-xi-order-one`.
- **Remark (prose, no claim proved):** the Riemann hypothesis states every zero in the critical strip has real part ½; it is open. The prime number theorem follows from `thm-zeta-nonvanishing-line` with a Tauberian argument not given in this series.
- **Warning.** "No zeros on Re s = 1" does not follow from the Euler product: the product does not converge absolutely on Re s = 1 (Σ 1/p diverges, prose AN2.12), so `thm-zeta-zero-free-right`'s argument stops at Re s > 1.
- **Quick check.** Is ζ(½) zero? *Answer:* no: ½ ∈ (0, 1) and `prp-zeta-negative-on-unit-interval` gives ζ(½) < 0.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** List the zeros of ζ in [−10, 0). *Answer:* −2, −4, −6, −8, −10.
  - **A2.** Find all zeros of 1 − 2^{1−s}. *Answer:* s = 1 + 2πik/log 2, k ∈ ℤ.
  - **B1.** Show the real zeros of ζ are exactly −2, −4, …. *Answer:* (1, ∞) positive series; s = 1 pole; (0, 1) and 0 by `prp-zeta-negative-on-unit-interval`; (−∞, 0) by `thm-zeta-zeros-left`.
  - **B2.** Prove `lem-three-four-one` and find its equality cases. *Answer:* θ ≡ π (mod 2π).
  - **B3.** Show ζ(s̄) = conj ζ(s) on ℂ∖{1}.
  - **C1.** Explain why 1 + cos θ ≥ 0 does not suffice in `thm-zeta-nonvanishing-line`. *Answer:* it gives |ζ(σ)ζ(σ + it)| ≥ 1, i.e. O((σ−1)^{−1})·O(σ − 1) ≥ 1, which is consistent.
  - **C2 (exit check).** Prove ζ(1 + it) ≠ 0 for t ≠ 0. Marking outline: log|ζ| as a Dirichlet series with non-negative coefficients (2); the inequality (2); orders of growth at σ → 1⁺ (2); contradiction including the use of t ≠ 0 for ζ(σ + 2it) (1).
  - **C3.** Show that the number of zeros of ξ in |s| < r is O(r^{1+ε}) for every ε > 0. *Answer:* order 1 and CA.7 `cor-zero-count-growth` / `thm-finite-order-zero-sum`.
  - **R. Review** (last section): **R1.** Prove Γ has no zeros in two ways (product; reflection + Γ finite off ℤ). **R2.** Compute ζ(0) by the Euler-summation formula and by the loop integral, and explain why the answers must agree. **R3.** Starting from the impostor of §01, list every theorem in the chapter that rules it out, and say which hypothesis it fails.
