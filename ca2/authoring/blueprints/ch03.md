# CA.12: Runge's Theorem, Mittag-Leffler, and Simply Connected Regions

Directory: `ca2/src/ch03-runge-mittag-leffler/` (CA2 chapter 3 in the order of math-atlas spec/08 §D2). State: S1 blueprint, 2026-10-25; not yet refereed (S2a list at the end).

**Volume CA2, *Complex Analysis II: Global Theory*.** Chapter 2 (CA.7) built entire functions with prescribed zeros, the convergence-producing factors being exponentials of Taylor polynomials, and expanded π cot πz in partial fractions. The same idea prescribes poles on ℂ (Taylor corrections). This chapter asks what replaces Taylor polynomials on a region with holes. The answer is Runge's theorem: a function holomorphic near a compact set K is a uniform limit on K of rational functions whose poles may be pushed to one point in each hole of K. Three global results follow, each a qualifying-exam staple: Mittag-Leffler and Weierstrass on an arbitrary open set, and the ten equivalent descriptions of a simply connected region, which close the account of simple connectivity that CA.4 began.

## Chapter hook and where it is paid

**Hook (index.md and §1 opening).** Weierstrass (AN2.6) says every continuous function on [0,1] is a uniform limit of polynomials. On the unit circle the polynomials in z cannot approach 1/z, because each of them integrates to 0 around the circle and 1/z integrates to 2πi. On the upper half of the same circle, where the integral argument has nothing to say, can they? The question "which compact sets let polynomials approximate every holomorphic function?" is posed on two pictures: the full circle (no) and the half circle (unknown).

- Paid in §1 (the obstruction: a bounded hole always blocks polynomials, `thm-hole-obstructs`) and in §2 (`cor-runge-polynomial`: no bounded hole, polynomials suffice). The half circle is answered in the §2 Quick check and recalled in R4.
- **Second hook (§5 opening):** on ℂ, prescribed poles are reached by subtracting Taylor polynomials (thm-mittag-leffler-plane, proved first in §5, the additive twin of CA.7's Weierstrass products). On ℂ∖{0} with poles at 1/k, every polynomial correction is at uniform distance ≥ 1 from 1/(z−1/k) on the unit circle (the §1 obstruction again). Paid in §5 by corrections whose poles sit at 0, supplied by Runge.
- **Hook opened for later volumes:** the space H(G) with locally uniform convergence (CA.5) has polynomials dense exactly when G is simply connected (§4, condition (d)). Paid as a see-pointer in FA.11 (Fréchet spaces); CA.8a uses the equivalences for the monodromy theorem.
- **Hook paid from earlier:** CA.7 stated Weierstrass on a region without proof; §6 proves it. CA.4 proved "simply connected ⇒ logarithms exist"; §4 proves the converse.

## Sections

### §1 Approximation and the Holes of a Compact Set (budget 3,500 words)

Opening frame: the hook above. One major notion (holes of K, counted in ℂ∞), one theorem.

- **def-hole** (failure-motivated definition). First attempt, suggested by the circle: "a point a is surrounded by K if some closed path in K winds around a". It fails: a closed-up topologist's sine curve (a "Warsaw circle", drawn) bounds a region, polynomials still cannot approximate 1/(z−a) there (by the theorem below), yet K contains no closed path winding around a. So the definition must not mention paths. Definition: a *hole* of a compact K ⊂ ℂ is a bounded component of ℂ∖K; equivalently a component of ℂ∞∖K not containing ∞. Fact (proved): ℂ∞∖K is open in ℂ∞, has exactly one component containing ∞, and every other component is a bounded open subset W of ℂ with ∂W ⊂ K.
- **def-rational-poles-in-E**: for E ⊂ ℂ∞∖K, R(E) is the set of rational functions all of whose poles lie in E (a pole at ∞ means polynomial part). R({∞}) = polynomials.
- **thm-hole-obstructs.** *Let K ⊂ ℂ be compact, W a hole of K and a ∈ W. If R is rational with no pole in W ∪ K, then sup_K \|(z−a)R(z) − 1\| ≥ 1. In particular 1/(z−a) is not a uniform limit on K of rational functions with no poles in W; in particular not of polynomials.* Proof sketch: g(z) = (z−a)R(z) − 1 is holomorphic on a neighborhood of W̄ = W ∪ ∂W ⊂ W ∪ K; maximum modulus (CA.2) on the bounded open W gives \|g(a)\| ≤ sup_{∂W}\|g\| ≤ sup_K\|g\|, and g(a) = −1. If \|R − 1/(z−a)\| < ε on K then sup_K\|g\| ≤ ε·max_K\|z−a\|, so ε ≥ 1/max_K\|z−a\|.
- **Worked example exm-pole-to-quarter** (first pole pushing, by hand): on K = {\|z\| = 1}, 1/z is not a polynomial limit, but it is a uniform limit of polynomials in 1/(z − 1/4): 1/z = Σ_{n≥0} (−1/4)ⁿ (z − 1/4)^{−n−1}, uniformly on K because \|z − 1/4\| ≥ 3/4 > 1/4 there. The pole moved inside the same hole. Contrast b = 1/2: the series still converges at each point of K except the ratio reaches 1 at z = 1, so uniformity fails; a two-step chain 0 → 1/4 → 1/2 works (prepares the §2 lemma and the faded B item).
- Warning: the obstruction is about holes, not about K being "thin": K = [0,1] ∪ {\|z − 3\| = 1} has one hole, the open disc \|z−3\| < 1.
- Non-example: the closed annulus 1 ≤ \|z\| ≤ 2 is "fat" but has a hole; the arc {e^{it}: 0 ≤ t ≤ π} is thin and has none.
- Remark (contrast with AN2.6): polynomials in z separate points of the circle and contain constants, yet are not dense in C(T); Stone–Weierstrass needs closure under conjugation, and z̄ = 1/z on T is exactly the function the obstruction excludes.
- Quick checks: (1) list the components of ℂ∞∖K for K = closed annulus 1 ≤ \|z\| ≤ 2 (answer: {\|z\| < 1}, and {\|z\| > 2} ∪ {∞}); (2) does thm-hole-obstructs forbid approximating 1/(z−5) on the unit circle by polynomials? (no: 5 is in the unbounded component; the Taylor series about 0 already works).
- Exercises.
  - A1. Show 1/(z−1) is not a uniform limit of polynomials on \|z\| = 2, by the integral. *Answer:* ∮ p = 0 for polynomials, ∮ dz/(z−1) = 2πi, and uniform convergence passes through the integral over a path of length 4π.
  - A2. K = [0,1] ∪ {\|z−3\| = 1}. For which a ∉ K is 1/(z−a) a uniform limit of polynomials on K? *Answer:* exactly the a with \|a−3\| > 1 (and a ∉ [0,1]); "no" for \|a − 3\| < 1 by thm-hole-obstructs; "yes" otherwise is deferred to cor-runge-polynomial, and the solution says so.
  - B1. In exm-pole-to-quarter, find N with \|1/z − Σ_{n<N}(−1/4)ⁿ(z−1/4)^{−n−1}\| < 10⁻³ on \|z\| = 1. *Answer:* the n-th term is at most (4/3)(1/3)ⁿ, tail from N at most 2·3^{−N}; N = 7 works (2/2187 < 10⁻³), N = 6 does not by this bound (2/729).
  - C1. Prove the general form: if W is a hole of K, a ∈ W, then 1/(z−a) is not a uniform limit on K of rational functions with poles in ℂ∞∖(W ∪ K). (Same maximum-modulus argument; the solution states where "no pole in W" is used.) Hint ladder: (1) multiply by z − a; (2) apply the maximum principle on W.

### §2 Runge's Theorem (budget 5,000 words)

Opening frame: §1 showed holes block polynomials; now the converse, with rational functions for holed sets. Major notions: the grid cycle; pole pushing. Key proof 1 of the chapter.

- **lem-grid-cycle.** *Let U ⊂ ℂ be open and K ⊂ U compact, K ≠ ∅. There are finitely many oriented segments σ₁, …, σ_m in U∖K whose sum Γ is a cycle with n(Γ, a) = 0 for every a ∉ U and n(Γ, z) = 1 for every z ∈ K. Consequently f(z) = (1/2πi)∫_Γ f(w)/(w−z) dw for every f holomorphic on U and z ∈ K.* Proof sketch: choose δ with 0 < δ√2 < dist(K, ℂ∖U) (δ arbitrary if U = ℂ); grid of closed squares of side δ; Q₁, …, Q_r the squares meeting K, each contained in U; Γ = Σ∂Q_j (positively oriented), and an edge shared by two of the Q_j cancels. An edge that meets K lies in both squares it borders, both meet K, so the edge cancels: Γ* ⊂ U∖K. For a ∉ U, a ∉ Q_j, so n(∂Q_j, a) = 0 and n(Γ,a) = 0. For z ∈ K in the interior of some Q_k: n(∂Q_j, z) = δ_{jk}, so n(Γ, z) = 1; z ∈ K on an edge: n(Γ, ·) is constant on the component of ℂ∖Γ* containing z, which meets the interior of an adjacent Q_k. The formula: global Cauchy integral formula (CA.4) for Γ in U, since n(Γ,a) = 0 off U.
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of the technique): draw K as an L-shaped blob, shade the squares that meet it, erase the interior edges; say why "erase edges shared by two shaded squares" is the same as "keep edges not meeting K"; then the forward proof.
  - Quick check: run the construction for K = {0}, U = D, grid lines through 0, δ = 1/2 (four squares; Γ is the boundary of [−1/2,1/2]², inside D since √2/2 < 1; n(Γ, 0) = 1).
- **lem-riemann-sum-rational.** *With Γ as above and f holomorphic on U, the function F(z) = (1/2πi)∫_Γ f(w)/(w−z) dw is a uniform limit on K of functions Σ_i c_i/(z − w_i) with w_i ∈ Γ*.* Proof sketch: parametrise each σ_j on [0,1]; the integrand (t, z) ↦ f(σ_j(t))σ_j′/(σ_j(t) − z) is uniformly continuous on [0,1] × K since dist(Γ*, K) > 0; Riemann sums converge uniformly in z.
- **lem-pole-pushing.** *Let K be compact and B a closed subalgebra of C(K) (uniform norm) containing the constants. Let V be a component of ℂ∞∖K. If 1/(z−b) ∈ B for one b ∈ V∩ℂ, or B contains all polynomials and ∞ ∈ V, then 1/(z−a) ∈ B for every a ∈ V∩ℂ.* Proof sketch: let S = {a ∈ V∩ℂ : 1/(z−a) ∈ B}. Step 1, S is open and closed in V∩ℂ: if b ∈ S and \|a − b\| < dist(b, K), then 1/(z−a) = Σ_{n≥0}(a−b)ⁿ(z−b)^{−n−1}, uniformly on K (ratio ≤ \|a−b\|/dist(b,K) < 1); each power of 1/(z−b) lies in B (algebra) and B is closed. Closedness in V∩ℂ: if a_k ∈ S, a_k → a ∈ V∩ℂ, then \|a − a_k\| < dist(a_k, K) for large k, so a ∈ S by the same step. Step 2, S ≠ ∅: given b, or, when ∞ ∈ V, for \|a\| > 2 max_K\|z\| the expansion 1/(z−a) = −Σ zⁿ/a^{n+1} converges uniformly on K and its partial sums are polynomials. Step 3, V∩ℂ is connected (V open connected in ℂ∞; removing the single point ∞ keeps it connected), so S = V∩ℂ.
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance): the chain-of-discs picture inside one hole: each disc centred at a pole reachable, radius its distance to K; why the radius depends on the centre (so the step is not uniform and connectedness must replace "walk along a path"). Then the forward proof.
  - **Try it first (ten minutes):** "You may approximate 1/(z − b) on K. Find an approximation of 1/(z − a) for a close to b. How close must a be?" The Idea starts from the obstacle such an attempt meets: the geometric series needs \|a − b\| < dist(b, K), so only nearby poles are reached, and repeated steps need a connectedness argument.
- **thm-runge.** *Let K ⊂ ℂ be compact and E ⊂ ℂ∞∖K a set meeting every component of ℂ∞∖K. If f is holomorphic on an open set containing K, then for every ε > 0 there is R ∈ R(E) with sup_K \|f − R\| < ε.* Proof: B = uniform closure of R(E) in C(K), a closed subalgebra with constants. lem-pole-pushing (applied in each component, starting from the point of E in it; if ∞ ∈ E, B contains the polynomials) gives 1/(z−a) ∈ B for every a ∉ K. lem-grid-cycle and lem-riemann-sum-rational make f a uniform limit on K of finite sums Σ c_i/(z−w_i) with w_i ∉ K; each summand is in B; B is closed. **Key proof 1** (STYLE §11.2).
- **cor-runge-polynomial.** *If K is compact and ℂ∖K is connected, every function holomorphic near K is a uniform limit of polynomials on K.* (E = {∞}.) Together with thm-hole-obstructs: polynomials approximate every function holomorphic near K iff K has no hole.
- Warning: holomorphic on a *neighborhood* of K, not merely on the interior: the theorem says nothing about functions continuous on the closed disc and holomorphic inside (Mergelyan's theorem, stated as a remark, not proved, not used).
- Quick check: answer the hook. The upper semicircle arc has connected complement, so 1/z is a uniform limit of polynomials there; the integral argument fails because the arc is not closed.
- Exercises.
  - A1 (comprehension on thm-runge, §11.2). (a) Where is "E meets every component" used? (In pole pushing, to start S in each component containing a point of Γ*.) (b) Which step fails if f is only continuous on K? (lem-grid-cycle's Cauchy formula.) (c) Run the proof on K = {\|z\|=1}, f = 1/z, E = {1/4}: name the cycle (any grid cycle in an annulus 1/2 < \|z\| < 3/2 around K, chosen with δ√2 < 1/2) and the poles produced (points of Γ*, pushed to 1/4).
  - A2. Why is Γ* disjoint from K? (Shared-edge argument.)
  - B1 (proof ordering, new move): seven shuffled lines of the proof of lem-grid-cycle plus one distractor ("choose δ < dist(K, ℂ∖U)", which lets a square meeting K stick out of U: the diagonal is δ√2). Answer gives an admissible order and the distractor's failure.
  - B2 (faded, last step blank; "Name the move, then complete it."). K = {\|z\| = 1}, E = {1/2}. Step 1 written: 1/z as a uniform limit of polynomials in 1/(z − 1/4). Step 2 blank: from 1/4 to 1/2 (\|1/2 − 1/4\| = 1/4 < dist(1/2, K) = 1/2). *Answer:* 1/(z − 1/4) = Σ_{n≥0} (1/4 − 1/2)ⁿ(z − 1/2)^{−n−1} with ratio ≤ (1/4)/(1/2) = 1/2, then powers and closure.
  - C1 (faded, last half blank). On the closed annulus A = {1 ≤ \|z\| ≤ 2}, show every function holomorphic near A is a uniform limit of Laurent polynomials Σ_{−N}^{N} c_n zⁿ. First half given (Runge with E = {0, ∞}); second half blank: identify R({0, ∞}) with Laurent polynomials (partial fractions: a rational function with poles only at 0 and ∞ is p(z) + q(1/z)). Hint ladder: (1) which components does ℂ∞∖A have? (2) what does a rational function with poles only at 0 look like?

### §3 Exhaustions and Strange Limits (budget 3,500 words)

Opening frame: Runge on an open set, by covering it with compact sets whose holes are controlled. One major notion (exhaustion with the component property); the chapter's showpiece weird example.

- **lem-exhaustion.** *Let G ⊊ ℂ be open (for G = ℂ take K_n = {\|z\| ≤ n}). Put K_n = {z : \|z\| ≤ n, dist(z, ℂ∖G) ≥ 1/n}. Then each K_n is compact, K_n ⊂ int K_{n+1}, ⋃ K_n = G, every compact L ⊂ G lies in some K_n, and every component of ℂ∞∖K_n contains a component of ℂ∞∖G.* Proof sketch: ℂ∞∖K_n = ({\|z\| > n} ∪ {∞}) ∪ ⋃_{a ∈ ℂ∖G} B(a, 1/n); each piece is connected and meets ℂ∞∖G; a component of ℂ∞∖K_n is a union of such pieces, so meets ℂ∞∖G; a component D of ℂ∞∖G meeting it is a connected subset of ℂ∞∖K_n, hence inside it. Compact L: the open sets int K_n increase and cover G.
  - Failure-motivated: an arbitrary compact exhaustion does not serve. Runge on a compact L ⊂ G needs a pole in every hole of L, and a hole lying inside G admits no pole outside G: in the slit plane G = ℂ∖(−∞, 0], L = {\|z − 3\| = 1} has the hole \|z − 3\| < 1 ⊂ G. The component property (every hole of K_n reaches ℂ∞∖G) is the repair.
- **thm-runge-open.** *Let G ⊂ ℂ be open and E ⊂ ℂ∞∖G meet every component of ℂ∞∖G. Every f ∈ H(G) is the locally uniform limit on G of a sequence R_n ∈ R(E).* Proof: thm-runge on K_n with ε = 1/n: every component of ℂ∞∖K_n contains a component of ℂ∞∖G, which meets E.
- **exm-half-plane-limit** (weird example; first row of the chapter's weird-examples table). *There are polynomials P_n with P_n(z) → 1 for Im z > 0 and P_n(z) → 0 for Im z ≤ 0.* Construction: A_n = {\|z\| ≤ n, Im z ≥ 1/n}, B_n = [−n, n], C_n = {\|z\| ≤ n, Im z ≤ −1/n}; K_n = A_n ∪ B_n ∪ C_n has connected complement (the two thin strips 0 < ±Im z < 1/n open out near \|z\| = n); f_n = 1 near A_n, 0 near B_n ∪ C_n is holomorphic near K_n; cor-runge-polynomial gives \|P_n − f_n\| < 1/n on K_n; each z lies in the right piece for all large n. Lesson: pointwise limits of polynomials on ℂ can be discontinuous; locally uniform convergence (CA.2) is what preserves holomorphy, and the limit here is not locally uniform near ℝ.
- Remark (stated only): Mergelyan's theorem; not used anywhere.
- Quick checks: (1) compute K_n for G = D (the closed disc \|z\| ≤ 1 − 1/n for n ≥ 2, empty for n = 1); (2) for G = ℂ∖{0}, K_n = {1/n ≤ \|z\| ≤ n}; which E work? (any E containing 0 and ∞; E = {0} alone does not, since the unbounded component contains no point of E).
- Exercises.
  - A1. For G = ℂ∖{0}, deduce from thm-runge-open that every f ∈ H(G) is a locally uniform limit of Laurent polynomials. (E = {0, ∞}; R({0, ∞}) as in §2 C1.)
  - B1. In exm-half-plane-limit, suppose the strip S between A_n and B_n were closed off (a hole of K_n bounded by an edge of A_n and a piece of B_n). Show that no polynomials converge uniformly on K_n to f_n. *Answer:* uniform convergence on ∂S ⊂ K_n forces uniform convergence on S̄ (maximum principle, CA.2); the limit is holomorphic in S, continuous on S̄, equal to 1 on the top edge and 0 on the bottom; Schwarz reflection across the top edge (CA.5) and the identity theorem make it ≡ 1, a contradiction.
  - B2 (find the error, **true conclusion**, STYLE §11.3). Claim: every f holomorphic on the slit plane G = ℂ∖(−∞, 0] is a locally uniform limit of polynomials. "Proof": every compact K ⊂ G has connected complement, so cor-runge-polynomial applies on each K. *Answer:* the conclusion is true (G is simply connected; §4 (d)), the step is false: K = {\|z − 2\| = 1} ⊂ G has a hole. Repair: the sets K_n of lem-exhaustion have no holes because ℂ∞∖G is connected.
  - C1. Construct polynomials Q_n with Q_n(0) = 1 for all n and Q_n(z) → 0 for every z ≠ 0. Hint ladder: (1) keep a small neighborhood of 0 and a slit annulus in K_n, together with a piece of the positive axis; (2) K_n = {0} ∪ [2/n, n] ∪ {1/n ≤ \|z\| ≤ n, dist(z, [0,∞)) ≥ 1/n}. *Answer:* complement connected through the slit around the positive axis; f_n = 1 near 0, 0 elsewhere on K_n; P_n from cor-runge-polynomial, Q_n = P_n − P_n(0) + 1; every z ≠ 0 lies in the second or third piece for large n. Marking scheme (10): 3 for a K_n with connected complement (stated and justified), 2 for f_n holomorphic near K_n, 2 for applying Runge with explicit tolerance, 3 for "every z ≠ 0 eventually in K_n∖{0}" including z on the positive axis. Fatal: a K_n with a hole; ignoring the positive axis.

### §4 Simply Connected Regions: Ten Equivalent Conditions (budget 5,000 words)

Opening frame: CA.4 defined simple connectivity by homotopy and proved one consequence (logarithms). Which of the many faces of "no holes" is the right one? All of them; the proof is a cycle of implications, and Runge closes it. Key proof 2.

- **thm-simply-connected-equivalences.** *For a region G ⊂ ℂ the following are equivalent:*
  - (a) *G is simply connected (every closed path in G is homotopic in G to a constant, CA.4);*
  - (b) *n(γ, a) = 0 for every closed rectifiable path γ in G and every a ∈ ℂ∖G;*
  - (c) *ℂ∞∖G is connected;*
  - (d) *every f ∈ H(G) is a locally uniform limit on G of polynomials;*
  - (e) *∫_γ f = 0 for every f ∈ H(G) and every closed rectifiable path γ in G;*

### §5 Mittag-Leffler's Theorem on an Open Set (budget 4,000 words)

Opening frame: the second hook. Major notion: the telescoping correction, first with Taylor polynomials on ℂ, then with poles outside G. Key proof 3.

- **thm-mittag-leffler-plane.** *Let {a_k} be distinct complex numbers with \|a_k\| → ∞ (or finitely many) and S_k a principal part at a_k. There is f meromorphic on ℂ with poles exactly at the a_k and principal parts S_k.* Proof sketch: for a_k ≠ 0, S_k is holomorphic on \|z\| < \|a_k\|; choose a Taylor polynomial P_k of S_k at 0 with \|S_k − P_k\| < 2^{−k} on \|z\| ≤ \|a_k\|/2; f = Σ (S_k − P_k) converges uniformly on each disc minus finitely many terms. **Scratch Work** (first appearance of the telescoping correction in the book: CA.7 used its multiplicative twin): why the corrections must be chosen term by term with summable errors, and why adding a holomorphic correction does not change a principal part. Worked check: the recipe recovers thm-cot-partial-fractions' series 1/z + Σ 2z/(z² − n²) up to an entire function (identified by CA.7's Liouville argument).

- Hook made precise: G = ℂ∖{0}, poles at a_k = 1/k (k ≥ 2), principal parts 1/(z − a_k). Any polynomial P satisfies ∮_{\|z\|=1}(1/(z − a_k) − P) = 2πi, so sup_{\|z\|=1}\|1/(z − a_k) − P\| ≥ 1: Taylor corrections cannot make the series converge on the unit circle. The fix: subtract the first terms of the expansion of 1/(z − a) in powers of 1/z.
- **def-discrete-in-G**: a set A ⊂ G with no limit point in G (equivalently, A ∩ L finite for every compact L ⊂ G). Recall principal part S_a(z) = Σ_{j=1}^{m} c_j (z − a)^{−j} (CA.3).
- **thm-mittag-leffler-open.** *Let G ⊂ ℂ be open, {a_k} a sequence of distinct points of G without limit point in G, and S_k a principal part at a_k for each k. There is a meromorphic f on G, holomorphic on G∖{a_k}, whose principal part at each a_k is S_k.* Proof sketch: K_n from lem-exhaustion (K_0 = ∅); I_n = {k : a_k ∈ K_n∖K_{n−1}} is finite; f_n = Σ_{k ∈ I_n} S_k is rational with poles in K_n∖K_{n−1}, so holomorphic near K_{n−1}. Choose E ⊂ ℂ∞∖G meeting every component of ℂ∞∖G; by the component property E meets every component of ℂ∞∖K_{n−1}, so thm-runge gives R_n ∈ R(E) with \|f_n − R_n\| < 2^{−n} on K_{n−1} (R_1 = 0). f = Σ_n (f_n − R_n). On int K_m, Σ_{n > m} converges uniformly to a holomorphic function (CA.2), and the finitely many terms n ≤ m carry exactly the principal parts S_k with a_k ∈ K_m; the R_n add no poles in G. **Key proof 3.**
  - Idea block (no Scratch Work: the telescoping correction appeared in thm-mittag-leffler-plane): "the same series as on ℂ; only the source of the corrections changed".
- **exm-ml-punctured** (worked example, explicit): on G = ℂ∖{0}, f(z) = Σ_{k≥1} 1/(k² z²(z − 1/k)) has simple poles at each 1/k with residue 1 (k ≥ 2 inside the series' tail; check: 1/(z−a) − 1/z − a/z² = a²/(z²(z − a))) and no other poles in G; the series converges locally uniformly on G∖{1/k} since \|z − 1/k\| ≥ \|z\|/2 once k ≥ 2/\|z\|. ### §6 Weierstrass Products on a Region and Interpolation (budget 4,500 words)

Opening frame: CA.7 stated, without proof, that zeros may be prescribed on any region. Now prove it, and combine it with §5 to prescribe values. Closes the chapter: weird-examples table, List of Results, R group, exit checks.

- **Proof of `thm-weierstrass-region`** (CA.7 §02 states it for every open set and promises the proof here; this chapter owns the proof and adds no new label; the case G = ℂ is CA.7 `thm-weierstrass-existence`). Restatement: *Let G ⊊ ℂ be open and {a_n} a sequence in G (repetitions allowed, giving multiplicity) without limit point in G. There is f ∈ H(G) whose zeros are exactly the a_n, with multiplicity.* Proof route (the splitting trick; **Scratch Work**, first appearance): let d_n = dist(a_n, ℂ∖G) and pick w_n ∈ ℂ∖G with \|a_n − w_n\| = d_n. Split the indices: P = {n : d_n ≤ 1/(1 + \|a_n\|)}, Q its complement.
  - Q-part: for each R > 0, the points a_n (n ∈ Q) with \|a_n\| ≤ R satisfy d_n > 1/(1+R), so lie in the compact {\|z\| ≤ R, dist(z, ℂ∖G) ≥ 1/(1+R)} ⊂ G and are finitely many. So {a_n : n ∈ Q} is finite or tends to ∞; CA.7 `thm-weierstrass-existence` gives an entire h_Q with exactly these zeros.
  - P-part: \|a_n − w_n\| → 0 along P (bounded subsequences: d_n → 0 by discreteness in G; unbounded: 1/(1+\|a_n\|) → 0). Put g_P(z) = Π_{n∈P} E_n((a_n − w_n)/(z − w_n)). On compact L ⊂ G, \|(a_n − w_n)/(z − w_n)\| ≤ \|a_n − w_n\|/dist(L, ℂ∖G) ≤ 1/2 for all but finitely many n, so \|1 − E_n(·)\| ≤ 2^{−n−1} by CA.7 `lem-elementary-factor-bound`, and the product converges locally uniformly on G (CA.7 `thm-holomorphic-product`); E_n(u) = 0 only at u = 1, i.e. z = a_n.
  - f = h_Q · g_P.
  - Scratch Work content: why one cannot simply use w_n for all n (if a_n → ∞ with d_n bounded below, (a_n − w_n)/(z − w_n) does not tend to 0); why the far points are exactly the ones ℂ can handle.
- **cor-meromorphic-quotient-region.** *Every meromorphic function on a region G is a quotient g/h of functions holomorphic on G.* (h from thm-weierstrass-region with the poles as zeros, multiplicity included; g = fh has removable singularities.)
- **thm-interpolation.** *Let G be open, {a_k} distinct points of G without limit point in G, and for each k a finite list w_{k,0}, …, w_{k,m_k} of complex numbers. There is f ∈ H(G) with f^{(j)}(a_k) = w_{k,j} for all k and 0 ≤ j ≤ m_k.* Proof sketch: g ∈ H(G) with a zero of order exactly m_k + 1 at each a_k (thm-weierstrass-region); for each k choose the principal part S_k so that g·S_k matches the prescribed jet to order m_k at a_k (solve a triangular system using the Laurent expansion of 1/g at a_k, which has a pole of order m_k + 1); h meromorphic with principal parts S_k (thm-mittag-leffler-open); f = g·h, and g·(h − S_k) vanishes to order m_k + 1 at a_k.
- **thm-no-extension** (every region is a domain of holomorphy). *For every region G ⊊ ℂ there is f ∈ H(G) that is not the restriction of a holomorphic function on any region G′ ⊋ G.* Proof sketch: choose {a_n} ⊂ G without limit point in G but with every point of ∂G a limit point (C1 below builds one); f from thm-weierstrass-region, f ≢ 0. A region G′ ⊋ G meets ∂G (connectedness of G′); at a boundary point b ∈ G′ the zeros of an extension accumulate, so the extension vanishes identically on G′ (identity theorem, CA.2), contradicting f ≢ 0.

## Named moves (index.md list)

1. **The grid cycle**: cover a compact set by grid squares inside the open set and keep the edges that avoid it (§2; reused in §4 (b)⇒(c)).
2. **Discretise a Cauchy integral**: Riemann sums turn ∫_Γ f(w)/(w−z)dw into rational functions, uniformly in z away from Γ* (§2).
3. **Pole pushing**: move a pole within one component of the complement by geometric series plus an open–closed argument (§2; faded triple: §1 worked example and §2 proof; §2 B2 last step blank; §2 C1 last half blank).
4. **Exhaustion with controlled holes**: K_n = {\|z\| ≤ n, dist(z, ℂ∖G) ≥ 1/n} (§3; used in §§4, 5).
5. **Telescoping corrections**: subtract approximants with summable errors, first Taylor polynomials (ℂ), then rational functions with poles outside G (§5; additive twin of CA.7's convergence factors).
6. **Split near and far**: points close to the boundary are handled by factors with zeros pushed to the boundary, points running to ∞ by the plane (§6).
7. **TFAE cycle**: prove a ring of implications, choosing the order so that each step uses one tool (§4).
