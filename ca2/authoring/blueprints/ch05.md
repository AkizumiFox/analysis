**Purpose.**
- A holomorphic function known on a small disk is already determined everywhere it can reach (identity theorem). This chapter asks the other half: *how far can it reach, and does the answer depend on the route?*
- Two answers carry the chapter. The **monodromy theorem**: on a simply connected region, a germ that continues along every path gives one global function. The **natural boundary**: some power series cannot be continued across any point of their circle of convergence.
- The lifting theorem of §4 packages monodromy into the form later chapters use (logs, roots, inverse functions of a local homeomorphism, the modular route of CA.8b).

## index.md

- **Story.** The hook above, then: the identity theorem says a holomorphic function has no freedom once a germ is fixed; this chapter measures how far that germ travels.
- **What you need:** CA.4 (homotopy, simply connected regions, homotopy Cauchy, log of a nonvanishing function), CA.2 (identity theorem, Taylor radius), CA.1 (re-centering power series); CA.7 for one remark in §5.
- **Roadmap.** §1 elements and singular points; §2 continuation along a path; §3 monodromy; §4 branches and lifts; §5 natural boundaries.
- **Named moves** (listed in index.md):
  - **Open–closed on [0, 1].** To show a property holds along a whole path, show the set of good times is nonempty, open and closed. (Used in §2, §4.)
  - **Slide the path.** Continuations along nearby paths agree; push one path to another through a homotopy, one small square at a time. (§3.)
  - **Re-expand and look farther.** Re-center a power series at an interior point; the new disk may leave the old one. (§1, §5.)
  - **Lift through a local inverse.** To solve φ(g) = f, continue the germ ψ ∘ f, where ψ is a local inverse of φ. (§4.)
  - **Shift by a functional equation.** f(z) = f(z + n)/(z(z + 1)⋯(z + n − 1)) pushes a half-plane of holomorphy leftward. (§4.)
  - **Blow up on a dense set.** A function unbounded along radii ending at a dense set of boundary points cannot be continued across any boundary point. (§5.)

## 01-elements-and-singular-points.md — Function elements, direct continuation, singular points

Budget ≤ 3,500 main-line words.

- **Failure first.** "log z on ℂ ∖ {0}" is not a function: any choice on the plane is discontinuous somewhere (cite CA.4's non-existence of a continuous log on a circle). What *is* well defined is a holomorphic function on a disk together with the disk. Hence:
- `def-function-element`: a pair (f, D), D an open disk, f holomorphic on D. `def-direct-continuation`: (g, E) is a direct continuation of (f, D) if D ∩ E ≠ ∅ and f = g on D ∩ E. Remark: D ∩ E is convex, hence connected, so by the identity theorem (cite CA.2) agreement on any nonempty open subset of D ∩ E suffices. Non-transitivity: show by log on three disks around 0 that direct continuation is not transitive (Quick check 1.2).
- `def-germ`: [f]ₐ, the equivalence class of elements whose functions agree near a. Two elements with the same germ at a point of D ∩ E are direct continuations of each other.
- `prp-reexpansion-radius`: if f(z) = Σ aₙ zⁿ has radius R ∈ (0, ∞] and |b| < R, the Taylor series of f at b has radius ≥ R − |b|; it may be larger. Proof: CA.2's Taylor radius (≥ distance to the boundary of D(0, R)), cite CA.1 re-centering for the coefficients.
- `exm-geometric-reexpansion`: Σ zⁿ re-expanded at b, |b| < 1, has radius exactly |1 − b|, which exceeds 1 − |b| unless b ∈ [0, 1). Picture: the re-expanded disks leave the unit disk everywhere except near 1. This is the hook's first function.
- **Failure-motivated definition.** The radius says *some* obstruction sits on |z| = R, not where. `def-regular-boundary-point`: ζ with |ζ| = R is regular for f if there are r > 0 and g holomorphic on D(ζ, r) with g = f on D(ζ, r) ∩ D(0, R); otherwise singular. Quick check: the singular points of 1/(1 + z²) at 0 are ±i only.
- `thm-singular-point-on-circle`: a power series with radius R ∈ (0, ∞) has at least one singular point on |z| = R.
  - Hypotheses: R finite and positive (R = ∞: no circle; R = 0: no disk).
  - Proof sketch: suppose every ζ regular, with (g_ζ, D(ζ, r_ζ)), r_ζ < R. By compactness and a Lebesgue number (AN2.3 via CA.4), choose finitely many ζ_j in cyclic order with consecutive disks overlapping in a lens that meets D(0, R); consecutive g's agree there (identity theorem on the connected lens). Glue with f to a holomorphic function on D(0, R + ε) for some ε > 0; its Taylor series at 0 is Σ aₙ zⁿ and converges on D(0, R + ε) (cite CA.2), contradicting the radius.
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance of compactness-and-gluing on the circle):** draw the circle, the finitely many disks, the annulus R − δ < |z| < R + ε covered by them; explain why gluing needs overlaps meeting the original disk. Kept apart from the proof.
- `thm-pringsheim`: if aₙ ≥ 0 for all n and the radius is R ∈ (0, ∞), then z = R is singular.
  - Proof sketch (R = 1): if 1 were regular, f extends to D(0,1) ∪ D(1, r), an open set containing the compact disk D̄(1/2, 1/2); so the Taylor series at 1/2 converges at x = 1/2 + δ for some δ > 1/2. Its coefficients are f^{(k)}(1/2)/k! = Σₙ C(n, k) aₙ 2^{−(n−k)} ≥ 0; the double series Σ_k Σₙ C(n,k) aₙ 2^{−(n−k)} (x − 1/2)^k has nonnegative terms, so it may be summed in the other order (cite CA.1, re-centering) and equals Σ aₙ xⁿ, which therefore converges at x > 1. Contradiction.
  - All hypotheses needed: Σ (−1)ⁿ zⁿ has real coefficients of both signs and is regular at 1.
- **Worked example.** Σ zⁿ/n² converges absolutely on the closed disk, yet 1 is singular (Pringsheim). Convergence on the circle does not mean continuation across it.
- **Quick checks.**
  - 1.1 Radius of the re-expansion of Σ zⁿ at b = i/2? *Answer:* |1 − i/2| = √5/2 > 1/2.
  - 1.2 Give three disks D₁, D₂, D₃ with branches of log such that (f₂, D₂) continues (f₁, D₁) directly, (f₃, D₃) continues (f₂, D₂) directly, D₁ ∩ D₃ ≠ ∅, but f₁ ≠ f₃ on D₁ ∩ D₃. *Answer:* disks of radius 0.9 centred at 1, ω, ω² (ω = e^{2πi/3}), branches with arguments chosen continuously going counterclockwise; on D₃ ∩ D₁ they differ by 2πi.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Find the singular points on |z| = 1 of Σ z^{2n} and of Σ (n + 1) zⁿ. *Answer:* ±1 (it is 1/(1 − z²)); 1 only (it is 1/(1 − z)²).
  - A2. True or false: a power series of radius 1 converging absolutely on all of |z| ≤ 1 has no singular point on |z| = 1. *Answer:* false; Σ zⁿ/n² (Pringsheim / `thm-singular-point-on-circle`).
  - B1. Show the set of singular points on |z| = R is closed. *Answer:* the regular set is open in the circle: a disk witnessing ζ regular witnesses every nearby circle point.
  - B2. Suppose f = Σ aₙ zⁿ has radius 1 and f − c/(1 − z) extends holomorphically to D(0, 1 + δ). Show aₙ → c. *Answer:* the difference has coefficients aₙ − c and radius > 1, so aₙ − c → 0.
  - C1. Let aₙ ≥ 0 and suppose Σ aₙ zⁿ has radius 1 and Σ aₙ = ∞. Show f(r) → ∞ as r → 1⁻, and deduce again that 1 is singular without Pringsheim. *Answer:* monotone convergence of partial sums, Σ_{n≤N} aₙ rⁿ → Σ_{n≤N} aₙ; a regular point would make f bounded near 1 inside the disk.

## 02-continuation-along-paths.md — Continuation along a path

Budget ≤ 4,000 main-line words.

- **Failure first.** Chains of disks (Quick check 1.2) depend on the choice of disks; even with the same start and end disk the end function changes. Make the route the primary object.
- `def-continuation-along-path`: γ: [0, 1] → ℂ a path. A continuation along γ is a family (f_t, D_t), t ∈ [0, 1], of function elements with γ(t) ∈ D_t, such that for each t there is δ > 0 with: |s − t| < δ ⇒ γ(s) ∈ D_t and [f_s]_{γ(s)} = [f_t]_{γ(s)}. Quantifiers shown in symbols after the prose form. Quick check 2.1 swaps "for each t there is δ" into "there is δ for all t" and asks whether the definition changes (answer: by compactness of [0, 1], no; that is a Lebesgue-number argument).
- `thm-continuation-along-path-unique`: if (f_t, D_t) and (g_t, E_t) are continuations along γ with [f_0]_{γ(0)} = [g_0]_{γ(0)}, then [f_t]_{γ(t)} = [g_t]_{γ(t)} for all t; in particular the end germs agree.
  - Proof (full): T = {t : [f_t]_{γ(t)} = [g_t]_{γ(t)}}. 0 ∈ T. Open: if t ∈ T, f_t = g_t on the connected D_t ∩ E_t near γ(t), hence on all of D_t ∩ E_t (identity theorem); for s near t, [f_s] = [f_t] = [g_t] = [g_s] at γ(s). Closed: if t is a limit of points of T, pick s ∈ T close enough that γ(s) ∈ D_t ∩ E_t and both local conditions hold; then f_t and g_t agree near γ(s), so on D_t ∩ E_t, so t ∈ T. Connectedness of [0, 1].
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance of open–closed on [0, 1]):** set up T, what each of "open" and "closed" requires, and where the identity theorem enters; then the clean proof.
- `prp-continuation-concatenation`: (a) a continuation along γ followed by one along σ (σ(0) = γ(1), matching germs) is a continuation along γ·σ; (b) running a continuation along γ backwards is a continuation along γ⁻; hence continuing along γ then γ⁻ returns the original germ. (c) Orientation-preserving reparametrization does not change the end germ. Proof: direct from the definition plus `thm-continuation-along-path-unique`.
- `exm-log-around-origin`: start with the principal Log near 1; along γ_k(t) = e^{2πikt} the end germ is Log + 2πik. Written out with D_t = D(γ(t), 1/2).
- `exm-sqrt-around-origin`: √z around the unit circle once ends at −√z, twice returns. Note: continuation along every path in ℂ ∖ {0} exists for both examples.
- `exm-integral-loop-creates-pole` (worked example, a weird example): F(z) = ∫₀¹ dt/(1 − zt) is holomorphic on ℂ ∖ [1, ∞) (holomorphy of parameter integrals, cite CA.2) and equals Σ zⁿ/(n + 1) = −Log(1 − z)/z near 0, with a removable singularity at 0. Continue F across (1, ∞) from the upper half-plane into the lower: the continuation is F(z) + 2πi/z there. Continue along a loop from near 0 around 1 once counterclockwise: back near 0 the germ is F + 2πi/z, which has a **pole** at 0. A loop around one point changed the nature of a different point. (Check of signs: F(x + i0) − F(x − i0) = 2πi/x for x > 1.)
- **Quick checks.**
  - 2.1 above.
  - 2.2 Continue Log along γ(t) = e^{−4πit} (clockwise twice). *Answer:* Log − 4πi.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Continue z^{1/3} (principal near 1) once around the unit circle. *Answer:* multiplied by e^{2πi/3}.
  - A2. Show that the continuation of a germ along a path, if it exists, does not depend on the disks chosen (only on the germs). *Answer:* `thm-continuation-along-path-unique`.
  - B1. Let h be a germ of √(z(z − 1)) at z = 2. Continue it along |z| = 2 once, and along a small circle about 0 starting from 1/2 after a radial path from 2. *Answer:* the big circle winds once around both 0 and 1: two sign changes, h returns. The small circle around 0 only: sign changes.
  - B2. Show `exm-integral-loop-creates-pole`'s sign claim from the formula with Log. *Answer:* for x > 1, Log(1 − (x ± i0)) = ln(x − 1) ∓ iπ.
  - C1. For a continuation (f_t, D_t) along γ, let r(t) be the radius of convergence of the Taylor series of f_t at γ(t). Show either r ≡ ∞ or r(t) < ∞ for all t and |r(t) − r(s)| ≤ |γ(t) − γ(s)|. *Answer:* for s near t, the Taylor series of f_t at γ(t) gives a holomorphic function on D(γ(t), r(t)) agreeing with f_s near γ(s); so r(s) ≥ r(t) − |γ(s) − γ(t)|; symmetric; then propagate along [0, 1] (open–closed) for the dichotomy and the inequality.

## 03-monodromy.md — The monodromy theorem

Budget ≤ 4,500 main-line words.

- **Failure first.** On ℂ ∖ {0}, log continues along every path but there is no global log (`exm-punctured-plane-no-log`, citing CA.4 for non-existence). What breaks is that two paths from 1 to −1 (upper and lower semicircle) give different end germs. Wanted: a condition under which the end germ depends only on the endpoints.
- `def-fep-homotopy`: paths γ₀, γ₁ in G from a to b are homotopic with fixed endpoints if there is a continuous H: [0, 1]² → G with H(·, 0) = γ₀, H(·, 1) = γ₁, H(0, s) = a, H(1, s) = b.
- `lem-nearby-paths`: let (f_t, D_t) be a continuation along γ. There is ε > 0 such that: if σ is a path with σ(0) = γ(0), |σ(t) − γ(t)| < ε for all t, and (g_t, E_t) is a continuation along σ with [g_0]_{σ(0)} = [f_0]_{γ(0)}, then g_1 = f_1 near σ(1) (so [g_1]_{σ(1)} = [f_1]_{σ(1)}).
  - Proof (full): by compactness choose a partition 0 = t₀ < ⋯ < t_n = 1 and ε > 0 with γ([t_{j}, t_{j+1}]) and its ε-neighbourhood inside D_{t_j}, and f_{t_j} = f_{t_{j+1}} on D_{t_j} ∩ D_{t_{j+1}} (Lebesgue number, AN2.3). Then σ([t_j, t_{j+1}]) ⊂ D_{t_j}. Induct on j: g_{t_j} = f_{t_j} near σ(t_j); along σ restricted to [t_j, t_{j+1}], the constant family f_{t_j} on D_{t_j} is a continuation; uniqueness (`thm-continuation-along-path-unique`) gives g_{t_{j+1}} = f_{t_j} = f_{t_{j+1}} near σ(t_{j+1}).
- `lem-continuation-square`: let H: [0, 1]² → G be continuous, a = H(0, 0), and let (f, D) be an element at a that can be continued along every path in G starting at a. Then continuing along the bottom edge H(·, 0) and then the right edge H(1, ·) gives the same end germ at H(1, 1) as continuing along the left edge H(0, ·) and then the top edge H(·, 1).
  - Proof (full): σ_s(t) = H(0, 2ts) for t ≤ 1/2, H(2t − 1, s) for t ≥ 1/2: left edge up to height s, then across. s ↦ σ_s is uniformly continuous into paths with the sup distance (uniform continuity of H). Let φ(s) be the end germ at H(1, s) of the continuation along σ_s. By `lem-nearby-paths` applied at each s₀, for s near s₀ the germ φ(s) is the germ at H(1, s) of the end element at s₀; so s ↦ φ(s) is a continuation along the right edge starting from φ(0). By uniqueness it equals the continuation of φ(0) along the right edge; at s = 1 this is the claim (σ₀ is the bottom edge after a constant stretch, σ₁ is left·top, `prp-continuation-concatenation` (c)).
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance of sliding the path):** the square picture; why one uses σ_s rather than H(·, s) (the paths must all start at a); where uniform continuity enters.
- `thm-monodromy-homotopy`: if γ₀ and γ₁ are homotopic with fixed endpoints in G and (f, D) at a continues along every path in G starting at a, the continuations along γ₀ and γ₁ have the same end germ. Proof: `lem-continuation-square` with constant left and right edges. Given as a short sketch the reader completes (fading support): Quick check 3.1 asks for the two lines.
- `thm-monodromy-simply-connected`: let G be a simply connected region (CA.4's definition), a ∈ G, and (f, D) an element at a that continues along every path in G starting at a. Then there is a unique holomorphic F on G with F = f near a.
  - Hypotheses: G open and connected; simply connected; continuation along **every** path (needed: Σ z^{2ⁿ} on G = D(0, 2) does not continue, and F cannot exist).
  - Proof sketch: (i) Loops return the germ: for a closed path σ at a, CA.4 gives a homotopy H through closed paths from σ to a constant c; its sides H(0, ·) = H(1, ·) = β. `lem-continuation-square`: continuing f along σ then β equals continuing along β then the constant path, i.e. along β. Reverse along β⁻ (`prp-continuation-concatenation` (b)): the end germ along σ is [f]ₐ. (ii) Path independence: for paths γ₀, γ₁ from a to z, continue along γ₀·γ₁⁻·γ₁; by (i) applied to the loop γ₀·γ₁⁻, this equals continuing along γ₁. (iii) Define F(z) = the value at z of the end element along any path from a to z; near z, F equals that end element (extend paths by segments inside its disk), so F is holomorphic. Uniqueness: identity theorem.
  - No FEP-homotopy equivalence lemma is needed: step (i) uses only CA.4's closed-path homotopy, through the square lemma.
- `exm-punctured-plane-no-log`: hypotheses all hold except simple connectivity; conclusion fails.
- `exm-sqrt-z2-minus-1` (worked example: monodromy is sufficient, not necessary): on G = ℂ ∖ [−1, 1], not simply connected, √(z² − 1) nevertheless has a holomorphic branch. Argument: continuation of √(z − 1)·√(z + 1) along a closed path γ in G multiplies the germ by (−1)^{n(γ, 1) + n(γ, −1)} (winding numbers, cite CA.4), and n(γ, 1) = n(γ, −1) because [−1, 1] is connected and misses γ. Then define the branch by continuation from z = 2 as in step (iii). Contrast: ℂ ∖ ([−1, 0] ∪ [1, 2]) admits loops around 1 alone, and no branch exists.
- **Quick checks.**
  - 3.1 Derive `thm-monodromy-homotopy` from `lem-continuation-square` in two lines. *Answer:* left and right edges are constant paths; continuing along a constant path does nothing.
  - 3.2 Which hypothesis of `thm-monodromy-simply-connected` fails for √z on ℂ ∖ {0}? For √z on ℂ ∖ (−∞, 0]? *Answer:* simple connectivity; none fail (the region is star-shaped about 1, so simply connected by CA.4), so a branch exists.
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Use `thm-monodromy-simply-connected` to produce a branch of log(1 − z²) on the unit disk. *Answer:* 1 − z² ≠ 0 on D; germs of log(1 − z²) continue along every path in D (locally take Log of 1 − z² near any point after a rotation); D is convex.
  - A2. Show the F of `thm-monodromy-simply-connected` is unique. *Answer:* identity theorem on the region G.
  - B1. Let p be a polynomial with n distinct roots inside D(0, R). Show √p has a holomorphic branch on |z| > R if n is even, and none if n is odd. *Answer:* along a loop in |z| > R the germ changes by (−1)^{Σ n(γ, zⱼ)} = (−1)^{n·n(γ, 0)}; even n: always +1, branch by step (iii); odd n: the circle |z| = 2R flips the sign, so no continuous branch.
  - B2. Show the hypothesis "every path" in `thm-monodromy-simply-connected` cannot be weakened to "every path in a dense open subset of G". *Answer:* Σ z^{2ⁿ}, G = D(0, 2), dense open subset D(0, 1) ∪ {|z| > 1}: continuation possible along paths staying in each piece, but no F.
  - C1. Let G be a region, a ∈ G, and (f, D) an element at a continuing along every path in G. Show the end germs at a along loops at a depend only on the class of the loop in the fixed-endpoint homotopy relation, and that the set of such germs is finite if and only if some finite collection of loops determines all end germs. Then compute it for f = √(z(z − 1)(z + 1)) on G = ℂ ∖ {0, 1, −1}. *Answer:* first part `thm-monodromy-homotopy`; each loop multiplies by (−1)^{n(γ,0)+n(γ,1)+n(γ,−1)}, so exactly two end germs, ± f.

## 04-branches-and-lifts.md — Branches, lifts and continuation by a functional equation

Budget ≤ 3,500 main-line words. Fading support: proofs as Proof Idea plus the key lines; the reader completes `prp-exp-path-lifting`.

- **Failure first.** CA.4 obtains log f on a simply connected region by integrating f′/f. That trick does not solve φ(g) = f for a general φ (cos g = f, g + e^g = f): there is nothing to integrate. Continuation gives a uniform method.
- `def-path-lifting-property`: φ: U → V holomorphic between open sets has the path-lifting property if for every u₀ ∈ U and every path γ in V with γ(0) = φ(u₀) there is a path γ̃ in U with γ̃(0) = u₀ and φ ∘ γ̃ = γ.
- `thm-lift-local-homeomorphism`: let φ: U → V be holomorphic with φ′ ≠ 0 on U and the path-lifting property. Let W be a simply connected region, f: W → V holomorphic, w₀ ∈ W, u₀ ∈ U with φ(u₀) = f(w₀). Then there is a unique holomorphic g: W → U with φ ∘ g = f and g(w₀) = u₀.
  - Hypotheses, each needed: φ′ ≠ 0 (φ(w) = w² on U = ℂ, f(z) = z on D: no g, Quick check 4.2); path lifting (φ = exp on the strip U = {|Im w| < π}, V = ℂ ∖ {0}, f(z) = z on W = D(−2, 1): every log of z on W has imaginary parts filling an interval around π + 2πk, so none maps W into U; the path from 1 to −2 along the upper unit semicircle and then the segment has no lift); W simply connected (φ = exp on ℂ, f(z) = z on ℂ ∖ {0}).
  - Proof Idea: let ψ be a local inverse of φ at u₀ (cite CA.4). The germ of ψ ∘ f at w₀ continues along every path δ in W: lift f ∘ δ to γ̃ from u₀, take local inverses ψ_t of φ at γ̃(t), and use the elements (ψ_t ∘ f, D_t); local injectivity of φ makes ψ_s = ψ_t near γ̃(s) for s near t. `thm-monodromy-simply-connected` gives g; φ ∘ g = f holds near w₀, hence on W. Uniqueness: two lifts agree on an open–closed set.
- `prp-exp-path-lifting`: exp: ℂ → ℂ ∖ {0} has the path-lifting property. Proof Idea: Lebesgue number for γ with disks avoiding 0 on which branches of log exist; glue. The reader writes the gluing (fading support).
- `cor-roots-and-logs-by-monodromy`: on a simply connected region W, a nonvanishing holomorphic f has a holomorphic log and an n-th root for every n ≥ 1. Proof: `thm-lift-local-homeomorphism` with φ = exp, then g ↦ exp(g/n). This reproves CA.4's result without integrating; say so and compare in two sentences.
- **Motivating instance (hook to CA.8b).** If φ: D → ℂ ∖ {0, 1} were holomorphic, with φ′ ≠ 0 and the path-lifting property, every entire f omitting 0 and 1 would lift to g: ℂ → D, bounded, hence constant (Liouville, CA.2). Such a φ exists (the modular function); constructing it is CA.8b. CA.14 proves the same conclusion by the Bloch route.
- `prp-functional-equation-continuation`: let f be holomorphic on {Re z > 0} with f(z + 1) = z f(z). Then f extends uniquely to a meromorphic function on ℂ whose poles lie in {0, −1, −2, …}, each at most simple, with residue (−1)^k f(1)/k! at −k.
  - Proof: on {Re z > −n} ∖ {0, …, −(n − 1)} set F_n(z) = f(z + n)/(z(z + 1)⋯(z + n − 1)); the functional equation makes F_{n+1} = F_n where both are defined, and F_n = f on Re z > 0. Residue computation at −k from F_{k+1}. Uniqueness: identity theorem on ℂ minus a discrete set.
  - All hypotheses used: holomorphy on a half-plane (not merely on (0, ∞)); the exact form of the equation determines the pole set.
  - **Hook paid in CA.13:** CA.13 proves Γ is holomorphic on Re z > 0 and applies this proposition; it also continues ζ. State the ζ question here (is ζ(−1) meaningful?) without answering it.
- **Worked example (real shadow, b AN2.11).** If such an extension of Γ exists, its value at −1/2 is forced: Γ(−1/2) = Γ(1/2)/(−1/2) = −2√π.
- **Quick checks.**
  - 4.1 Γ(−3/2) under the same extension? *Answer:* Γ(−1/2)/(−3/2) = 4√π/3.
  - 4.2 Why does f(z) = z on D admit no holomorphic g with g² = f, and which hypothesis of `thm-lift-local-homeomorphism` fails? *Answer:* g(0) = 0 would force 2g(0)g′(0) = 1; φ(w) = w² has φ′(0) = 0 (restricting U to ℂ ∖ {0} fails instead because f takes the value 0 ∉ V).
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Give a holomorphic cube root of 1 + z on D explicitly and check it against `cor-roots-and-logs-by-monodromy`. *Answer:* exp(Log(1 + z)/3).
  - A2. Verify the residue formula of `prp-functional-equation-continuation` at −2. *Answer:* F₃(z) = f(z + 3)/(z(z + 1)(z + 2)); at −2 the residue is f(1)/((−2)(−1)) = f(1)/2 = (−1)² f(1)/2!.
  - B1. Show that φ(w) = wⁿ: ℂ ∖ {0} → ℂ ∖ {0} has the path-lifting property, using `prp-exp-path-lifting`. *Answer:* lift γ through exp to Γ̃, then exp(Γ̃/n) lifts through wⁿ (adjust the start by an n-th root of unity).
  - B2. Let f be holomorphic on {Re z > 0} with f(z + 1) = z² f(z)? Find where the extension can have poles and of which order. *Answer:* f(z) = f(z + n)/∏ (z + j)²: poles at most double, at 0, −1, −2, ….
  - C1. Let W be simply connected and f: W → ℂ ∖ {1, −1} holomorphic. Show there is a holomorphic g with cos g = f. *Answer:* f² − 1 has no zeros, so take h with h² = f² − 1 (`cor-roots-and-logs-by-monodromy`); w = f + h is nonvanishing since (f + h)(f − h) = 1; take G with e^G = w and g = −iG; then cos g = (w + 1/w)/2 = f.

## 05-natural-boundaries.md — Natural boundaries

Budget ≤ 3,500 main-line words. Last section: carries the R group, the weird-examples table, and the List of Results.

- **Failure first.** `thm-singular-point-on-circle` guarantees one singular point. Could every point be singular? `def-natural-boundary`: |z| = R is a natural boundary of f if every point of it is singular (more generally, ∂G is a natural boundary of f ∈ H(G) if f continues across no boundary point).
- `thm-lacunary-dyadic-natural-boundary`: f(z) = Σ_{n≥0} z^{2ⁿ} has |z| = 1 as natural boundary.
  - Proof: for ζ with ζ^{2^k} = 1 and 0 < r < 1, f(rζ) = Σ_{n<k} (rζ)^{2ⁿ} + Σ_{n≥k} r^{2ⁿ} → ∞ as r → 1⁻. These ζ are dense on the circle. If ζ₀ were regular with g on D(ζ₀, ε), g is bounded on D̄(ζ₀, ε/2), yet some dyadic ζ lies in D(ζ₀, ε/4) and f(rζ) = g(rζ) is unbounded as r → 1⁻. Contradiction.
  - **Scratch Work (first appearance of blow up on a dense set):** why "unbounded along one radius" proves nothing alone (1/(1 − z)) and density is the point.
- `thm-hadamard-gap`: let f(z) = Σ_k a_k z^{n_k} have radius 1, with n_{k+1} ≥ λ n_k for all k and some λ > 1. Then |z| = 1 is a natural boundary.
  - Proof sketch (Mordell's substitution; full lines in the draft): discard finitely many terms so that m n_{k+1} > (m + 1) n_k for a fixed m ≥ 1 with (m + 1)/m < λ. By rotation it suffices to show 1 is singular. Suppose not, so f extends to Ω = D ∪ D(1, r). p(w) = (w^m + w^{m+1})/2 maps D̄ into D ∪ {1} (|p(w)| ≤ |w|^m |1 + w|/2 ≤ 1 with equality only at w = 1), so p(D(0, 1 + δ)) ⊂ Ω for some δ > 0 and F = f ∘ p is holomorphic on D(0, 1 + δ). For |w| < 1, F(w) = Σ_k a_k 2^{−n_k} Σ_{j=0}^{n_k} C(n_k, j) w^{m n_k + j}, and the blocks of exponents [m n_k, (m + 1) n_k] are disjoint, so this is the Taylor series of F, which converges absolutely at w = ρ ∈ (1, 1 + δ). Summing each block: Σ_k |a_k| x^{n_k} < ∞ with x = ρ^m(1 + ρ)/2 > 1, contradicting radius 1.
  - Proof Idea only for the dyadic case's reuse: Σ z^{n!} (Exercise A1).
- `exm-smooth-natural-boundary` (weird): f(z) = Σ_n 2^{−n²} z^{2ⁿ}. Radius 1 (2^{−n²/2ⁿ} → 1); every derivative series Σ (2ⁿ)^k 2^{−n²} converges, so f and all its derivatives extend continuously to |z| ≤ 1; yet |z| = 1 is a natural boundary by `thm-hadamard-gap`. Smoothness up to the boundary does not give continuation.
- `exm-every-region-natural-domain` (remark): for any region G ≠ ℂ there is f holomorphic on G continuing across no boundary point. Sketch: choose points of G accumulating at every point of ∂G and no point of G; take f ∈ H(G) with exactly these zeros (cite CA.7 `thm-weierstrass-region`, stated there, proof promised to CA.12). A continuation g on a disk about ζ ∈ ∂G has zeros accumulating at ζ, so g ≡ 0, so f ≡ 0 on a component of the disk ∩ G, so f ≡ 0. Mark as resting on CA.7's region theorem (proved there or stated; coordinator note 3).

- **Quick checks.**
  - 5.1 Is |z| = 1 a natural boundary of Σ z^{2ⁿ} + 1/(1 − z)? *Answer:* yes; adding a function regular at ζ ≠ 1 keeps every ζ ≠ 1 singular, and the singular set is closed (Exercise B1 of §1).
  - 5.2 Does `thm-hadamard-gap` apply to Σ z^{n²}? *Answer:* no, (n + 1)²/n² → 1; the gap theorem is silent (the circle is in fact a natural boundary, by other means; not proved here).
- **Exercises.**
  - A1. Show |z| = 1 is a natural boundary of Σ z^{n!}. *Answer:* at ζ = e^{2πip/q}, terms with n ≥ q are r^{n!}; blow up on the dense set of roots of unity.
  - A2. Verify that Σ 2^{−n²} z^{2ⁿ} and all its derivatives extend continuously to the closed disk. *Answer:* Weierstrass M-test with M_n = 2^{nk − n²}.
  - B1. Show f(z) = Σ z^{2ⁿ} satisfies f(z²) = f(z) − z, and use it to re-prove unboundedness along dyadic radii. *Answer:* f(z) = Σ_{j<k} z^{2^j} + f(z^{2^k}); with z = rζ, ζ^{2^k} = 1, f(z^{2^k}) = f(r^{2^k}), and f(x) → ∞ as x → 1⁻ along the reals (positive coefficients, divergent sum).
  - B2. Show that if g has radius > 1 then f + g has the same natural boundary as f. *Answer:* g is regular at every point of the circle.
  - C1. For f(z) = Σ z^{2ⁿ} and r ∈ [1/2, 1), show f(r) > (1/4) log₂(1/(1 − r)). *Answer:* for r ∈ [1 − 2^{−m}, 1 − 2^{−m−1}), m ≥ 1, each term with n ≤ m is ≥ (1 − 2^{−m})^{2^m} ≥ 1/4, so f(r) ≥ (m + 1)/4 > (1/4) log₂(1/(1 − r)).
- **R group (reader alone; no hints in the text, solutions folded).**
  - R1. Decide whether log(z² + 1) has a holomorphic branch on ℂ ∖ [−i, i]. *Answer:* loops around both ±i change it by 2πi(n(γ, i) + n(γ, −i)) = 4πi n(γ, 0) ≠ 0 for a loop winding once: no branch. (Contrast with √.)
  - R2. Prove: if f ∈ H(D) continues along every path in D(0, 2) starting in D, then f extends to H(D(0, 2)). *Answer:* `thm-monodromy-simply-connected` with G = D(0, 2) (convex).
  - R3. Give a power series with radius 1 whose only singular points are ±1 and ±i, and one whose singular set is the whole circle and which is bounded on D. *Answer:* 1/(1 − z⁴); Σ 2^{−n} z^{2ⁿ} (|f| ≤ Σ_{n≥0} 2^{−n} = 2; natural boundary by `thm-hadamard-gap`).
