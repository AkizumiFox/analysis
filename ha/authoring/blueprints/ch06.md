# HA.6: The Fourier Transform on ℝᵈ and the Wave Equation

Directory: `src/ch39-fourier-transform-rd/`. State: not started.

**Part III, Fourier Analysis in the Riemann Setting.** Fourier series and the Fourier transform on 𝒮(ℝᵈ), built entirely on the Riemann integral: summability, mean-square and pointwise convergence, applications, and (optionally) finite abelian groups and Dirichlet's theorem. The Part ends by listing what the Riemann framework cannot do, and that list motivates Part IV.

*Needs polar coordinates in the Riemann setting. If Part II's ℝⁿ module does not include them, this chapter proves them for continuous functions of moderate decrease (SS1 App. §2.4, 3.3). The general C¹ change of variables is not used; Part IV proves it in full. Route R7 (roadmap A7): the Kirchhoff/descent/Huygens section and the Radon section are optional; the main line is integration on ℝᵈ, 𝒮(ℝᵈ) with inversion and Plancherel, the wave equation by Fourier transform with energy, and radial functions (about 35 pp.).*

**Page budget:** about 50 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** HA.1 (Fourier Series: Origins and Basic Properties), HA.5 (The Fourier Transform on ℝ).

**Needs from other Parts:**

- Riemann integral on ℝⁿ, Jordan content, Fubini for continuous functions, linear change of variables (Part II)

## Planned sections

1. Riemann integration on ℝᵈ for functions of moderate decrease; polar coordinates for continuous functions (proved from Part II's Jordan content and linear change of variables)
2. 𝒮(ℝᵈ) and the Fourier transform; behaviour under translations, dilations and rotations; inversion and Plancherel
3. The wave equation in ℝᵈ × ℝ: solution by Fourier transform, conservation of energy, uniqueness
4. Radial functions: the Fourier transform of a radial function is radial; Bessel functions
5. (Optional) ℝ³: spherical means, Kirchhoff's formula, Huygens' principle; ℝ²: the method of descent and the failure of Huygens
6. (Optional) The X-ray transform in ℝ² and the Radon transform in ℝ³

## Must prove

- Fourier transform commutes with rotations
- inversion and Plancherel on 𝒮(ℝᵈ)
- existence and energy-uniqueness for the wave equation with 𝒮 data
- (optional section) Kirchhoff's formula in ℝ³
- polar-coordinate formula for continuous functions of moderate decrease

## Key examples and counterexamples

- e^{−π|x|²} on ℝᵈ
- transform of surface measure on S² (sin(2π|ξ|)/(π|ξ|))
- (optional section) Huygens fails in ℝ²

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Solve the wave equation in ℝᵈ with 𝒮 data by the Fourier transform and prove conservation of energy
- (Optional sections) Derive the solution of the wave equation in ℝ³ and explain Huygens' principle
