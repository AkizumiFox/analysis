# Chapter 76: H¹₀, the Poincaré Inequality and Rellich Compactness

Directory: `src/ch76-poincare-rellich/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*The capstone needs only H¹₀(Ω), for which Rellich follows from extension by zero and Kolmogorov–Riesz with no boundary regularity; the W^{1,p}(Ω) version with extension operators is stated. Spiral table: compactness row, Lᵖ column (Alaoglu; Kolmogorov–Riesz; Rellich). Traces use surface measure on ∂Ω from surfaces-divergence-theorem (Part IV), which is what makes the trace statement precise.*

## Must prove

- Kolmogorov–Riesz compactness criterion in Lᵖ(ℝⁿ)
- Rellich: H¹(𝕋ⁿ) ↪ L²(𝕋ⁿ) and H¹(I) ↪ L²(I) are compact
- Rellich–Kondrachov: H¹₀(Ω) ↪ L²(Ω) is compact for bounded Ω

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Poincaré fails on H¹(Ω) (constants) and on unbounded Ω (ℝⁿ)
- sin(nx)/n: bounded in H¹(0,π), no H¹-convergent subsequence, L²-convergent
- translates of a bump: bounded in H¹(ℝ) with no L²-convergent subsequence (why boundedness of Ω matters)
