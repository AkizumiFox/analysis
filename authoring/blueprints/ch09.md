# Chapter 9: Sequences, Subsequences and Cauchy Sequences

Directory: `src/ch09-sequences-and-limits/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

*Proves MCT, BW and Cauchy completeness FROM the LUB axiom only; their converses belong to forms-of-completeness. Skills named here: ε allocation (ε/2), extracting subsequences (trigger: bounded sequence → extract), sup approximation. Rudin's chapter 3 works in metric spaces; specialize to ℝ (see exemption for PMA.2 → PMA.3).*

**Page budget:** about 65 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 8 (Ordered Fields and the Least Upper Bound Axiom).

## Planned sections

1. Convergence of a sequence: the ε-N definition as a quantifier string; uniqueness; convergent ⇒ bounded
2. Algebraic and order limit theorems; the squeeze theorem
3. Monotone convergence theorem; recursively defined sequences (√2 iteration); e as lim (1+1/n)ⁿ
4. Divergence to ±∞ and the extended real line
5. Subsequences; every sequence has a monotone subsequence; Bolzano–Weierstrass
6. limsup and liminf: as limits of tail suprema and as extreme subsequential limits; the two definitions agree; convergence iff limsup = liminf
7. Cauchy sequences and the Cauchy criterion
8. Standard limits: aⁿ, n^{1/n}, a^{1/n}, n^k/aⁿ

## Must prove

- uniqueness of limits
- convergent ⇒ bounded
- algebraic limit theorem
- order limit theorem and squeeze
- monotone convergence theorem
- subsequences of a convergent sequence converge to the same limit
- monotone subsequence lemma
- Bolzano–Weierstrass
- the two definitions of limsup agree
- aₙ → a iff limsup = liminf = a
- Cauchy criterion (both directions)

## Key examples and counterexamples

- (−1)ⁿ: bounded, divergent, two subsequential limits
- √2 iteration x_{n+1} = (x_n + 2/x_n)/2
- (1+1/n)ⁿ increasing and bounded
- a sequence whose subsequential limits are all of [0,1] (enumeration of ℚ∩[0,1])
- Cauchy in ℚ with no rational limit

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- show that the two definitions of limsup agree
- negate 'aₙ → a' and 'aₙ is Cauchy' and prove a given sequence is not Cauchy
- prove BW by the monotone-subsequence route and by nested intervals
