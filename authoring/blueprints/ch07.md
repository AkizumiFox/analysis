# Chapter 7: Cardinality: Countable and Uncountable Sets

Directory: `src/ch07-countable-and-uncountable/`. State: not started.

**Part 0, Foundations: Logic, Sets, Numbers.** Teaches the grammar of proof (natural deduction for connectives, its truth-table semantics with soundness and completeness, the quantifier and equality rules, and the passage from derivations to mathematical prose), then sets, functions, relations, induction and cardinality as the main-line bridge, and, beside the main line, builds ℕ, ℤ, ℚ and ℝ and proves the complete ordered field unique; forced by a reader who has never written a proof and by Part I's decision to start from the least upper bound axiom.

*The diagonal proof that ℝ is uncountable uses decimal (or binary) expansions taken from school as a stated fact, with the rigour issue flagged (non-uniqueness 0.4999… = 0.5 handled by avoiding 9s/0s); Part I can give the nested-interval proof and should point back. Do not cite the optional constructions. The axiom of choice is STATED here as a main-line axiom (`ax-choice`) because main-line proofs (Vitali, Hahn–Banach, Tychonoff) must cite it; its equivalents (Zorn, well-ordering) and ZF context are in the optional zf-and-natural-numbers. Zorn's lemma is proved just in time in Part VI's hahn-banach chapter. Last main-line chapter of Part 0: it ends with Gate 0 (reader B2).*

## Planned sections

1. Equinumerosity; finite sets and the pigeonhole principle; a finite set is not equinumerous with a proper subset
2. Countable sets: ℕ×ℕ, ℤ, ℚ; subsets and images of countable sets
3. A countable union of countable sets is countable; the axiom of choice stated here as ax-choice (main line), with this proof as its first flagged use
4. Diagonalization: {0,1}^ℕ and ℝ are uncountable; Cantor's theorem, no surjection X → P(X)
5. Comparing cardinalities: injections, the Schröder–Bernstein theorem, examples ℝ ~ (0,1) ~ [0,1]
6. Infinite sets: every infinite set has a countably infinite subset (choice again), hence is equinumerous with a proper subset
7. ## Must prove

- ℕ×ℕ and ℚ are countable
- a countable union of countable sets is countable (flag choice)
- ℝ is uncountable (diagonal argument)
- Cantor: there is no surjection X → P(X)
- Schröder–Bernstein theorem
- an infinite set is equinumerous with a proper subset of itself
- statement of the axiom of choice (ax-choice) and its use in the countable-union theorem

## Key examples and counterexamples

- ℕ ~ 2ℕ (Hilbert's hotel)
- [0,1] ~ (0,1) via Schröder–Bernstein and by an explicit shift map
- algebraic numbers are countable, so transcendental numbers exist

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Prove that a countable union of countable sets is countable and point to the step that uses choice
- Prove Cantor's theorem and deduce that P(ℕ) is uncountable
