# Chapter 7: Building ℤ and ℚ from Equivalence Classes

Directory: `src/ch07-integers-and-rationals/`. State: not started.

**Part 0, Foundations: Logic, Sets, Numbers.** Teaches the grammar of proof (natural deduction for connectives, quantifiers and equality), then sets, functions, relations, induction and cardinality as the main-line bridge, and, beside the main line, builds ℕ, ℤ, ℚ and ℝ and proves the complete ordered field unique; forced by a reader who has never written a proof and by Part I's decision to start from the least upper bound axiom.

**Optional chapter.** It sits beside the main line: mark its results `.optional`; no main-line proof may cite them (`tools/check_optional.py`).

*Optional (decisions 4). Most field and order axioms are left as exercises. The gaps section is the motivation for the next two chapters and mirrors Part I ch 1's example, which Part I proves independently.*

**Page budget:** about 55 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 3 (Sets, Functions, Images and Preimages), Chapter 6 (The ZF Axioms, Choice, and the Natural Numbers).

## Planned sections

1. ℤ as classes of pairs (a,b) with (a,b) ~ (c,d) iff a + d = b + c; ℕ embedded
2. Well-definedness: the central lemma of every quotient construction
3. ℤ as an ordered integral domain: representative verifications, the rest as exercises
4. ℚ as classes of pairs (p,q), q ≠ 0; field operations and order
5. Ordered fields in general; absolute value and the triangle inequality; the Archimedean property of ℚ
6. The gaps in ℚ: no rational square root of 2, and {x ∈ ℚ : x² < 2} has no supremum in ℚ

## Must prove

- ~ on ℕ×ℕ is an equivalence relation, and addition and multiplication of ℤ are well defined
- multiplication on ℚ is well defined and ℚ is a field (representative axioms)
- ℚ is an ordered field and is Archimedean
- {x ∈ ℚ : x² < 2} is bounded above but has no least upper bound in ℚ

## Key examples and counterexamples

- an ill-defined 'operation' [p/q] ↦ p + q
- the order on ℤ/n cannot make it an ordered ring

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Show that (a,b) ~ (c,d) ⟺ a + d = b + c is an equivalence relation and that addition respects it
- Prove that no rational number squares to 2 and that the set {x ∈ ℚ : x² < 2} has no sup in ℚ
