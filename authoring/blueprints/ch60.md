# Chapter 60: Topological Spaces, Products and Compactness

Directory: `src/ch60-topological-spaces-compactness/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

4. Continuous maps and homeomorphisms; initial topologies: subspaces and arbitrary products with their universal properties (the product topology on ℝ^X as pointwise convergence)
5. Hausdorff spaces: uniqueness of limits; metrizable ⇒ Hausdorff; non-metrizable examples

## Must prove

- a family of sets satisfying the basis axioms generates a topology
- closure = intersection of closed supersets = A ∪ (limit points of A)
- in a first-countable space closure is given by sequences; the counterexample without first countability
- continuity via open sets, closed sets, closure and neighbourhoods are equivalent
- universal properties of the subspace and product topologies
- limits are unique in a Hausdorff space; metric spaces are Hausdorff
- closed subset of compact is compact; compact subset of Hausdorff is closed
- tube lemma; a finite product of compact spaces is compact
- a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism
- (optional section) universal property of the quotient topology; [0,1]/(0∼1) ≅ S¹

## Key examples and counterexamples

- cofinite topology on an infinite set: compact, not Hausdorff, every sequence of distinct points converges to every point
- cocountable topology on ℝ: convergent sequences are eventually constant, yet closure is not sequential
- Sorgenfrey line
- indiscrete topology: not metrizable
- [0,1) → S¹, t ↦ e^{2πit}: continuous bijection, not a homeomorphism
- ℝ^[0,1] with the product topology: pointwise convergence, not first countable
- (optional section) the line with two origins; the torus as [0,1]²/∼ and as S¹×S¹

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Show that the cofinite topology on ℝ is not metrizable
- Prove that a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, and show both hypotheses are needed
- Find the error in a 'proof' that sequential closure equals closure in every topological space
