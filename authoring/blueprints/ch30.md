# Chapter 30: Topological Spaces

Directory: `src/ch30-topological-spaces/`. State: not started.

**Part II, Metric Spaces and Several Variables.** Lifts convergence, completeness, compactness and continuity from ℝ to metric spaces and C(K), forced by concrete problems (ODE existence and uniqueness, polynomial approximation, typical continuous functions), then does calculus in ℝⁿ (inverse and implicit functions, the Riemann integral on ℝⁿ) and ends by abstracting open sets into topological spaces.

*First week of the 2-week topology module (decisions.md 8, end of Part II). Only finite products; infinite products and Tychonoff are Part VI. Countability and separation beyond T₂ (regular, normal, Urysohn) are Part IV. Recall each metric version explicitly (spiral).*

**Page budget:** about 55 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 21 (Metric Spaces, Open Sets and Continuity).

## Planned sections

1. Why forget the metric: equivalent metrics share every topological notion; the axioms for open sets
2. Examples: metric topologies, discrete, indiscrete, cofinite, cocountable, the Sorgenfrey line; comparing topologies
3. Bases and neighbourhoods; generating a topology; the metric topology as the one generated by balls
4. Closed sets, closure, interior, boundary and limit points; sequences do not determine the topology (cocountable topology), first countability and when sequences suffice
5. Continuous maps and homeomorphisms; topological properties
6. Subspaces and finite products: the subspace and product topologies, their universal properties; the product topology on ℝⁿ equals the metric one
7. Hausdorff spaces: uniqueness of limits, metrizable ⇒ Hausdorff, non-metrizable examples

## Must prove

- a family of sets satisfying the basis axioms generates a topology
- closure = intersection of closed supersets = A ∪ (limit points of A); interior and closure are dual under complements
- in a first-countable space, closure is given by sequences; the counterexample without first countability
- continuity via open sets, closed sets, closure and neighbourhoods are equivalent
- universal property of subspace and finite product topologies
- limits are unique in a Hausdorff space; metric spaces are Hausdorff

## Key examples and counterexamples

- cofinite topology on an infinite set: not Hausdorff, every sequence of distinct points converges to every point
- cocountable topology on ℝ: convergent sequences are eventually constant, yet closure is not sequential
- Sorgenfrey line
- indiscrete topology: not metrizable
- identity from discrete to usual ℝ: continuous bijection, not a homeomorphism

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- show the cofinite topology on ℝ is not metrizable
- prove the product and metric topologies on ℝ² coincide
- find the error in a 'proof' that sequential closure equals closure in every topological space
