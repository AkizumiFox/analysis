# Chapter 54: Laurent Series, Singularities and Residues

Directory: `src/ch54-singularities-residues/`. State: not started.

**Part V, Complex Analysis.** Complex differentiability turns out to be so rigid that one integral formula controls everything; the Part runs from Goursat to the Riemann mapping theorem and then through the qualifying-exam second layer (harmonic and subharmonic functions, normal families, products, continuation, Picard), with normal families as the complex spiral node of Arzelà–Ascoli.

*Only the local (disc and toy-contour) residue theorem is proved here; the homology version waits for global-cauchy-theory.*

**Page budget:** about 48 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 52 (Holomorphic Functions and Power Series), Chapter 53 (Cauchy's Theorem and Its First Consequences).

## Planned sections

1. Laurent series on annuli: existence, uniqueness, and the Cauchy formula on an annulus
2. Isolated singularities: Riemann's removable-singularity theorem, poles and orders, essential singularities
3. Casorati–Weierstrass, and the behaviour of e^{1/z}
4. Meromorphic functions on ℂ and on the Riemann sphere; meromorphic on the sphere ⇔ rational
5. The residue theorem on a disc with finitely many poles, and computing residues
6. Real integrals: rational functions, trigonometric integrals, Jordan's lemma, keyhole contours with log and x^α
7. Summing series with π cot πz

## Must prove

- existence and uniqueness of the Laurent expansion
- Riemann's removable-singularity theorem
- Casorati–Weierstrass
- the residue theorem (disc/toy-contour version)
- Jordan's lemma
- a function meromorphic on the extended plane is rational

## Key examples and counterexamples

- e^{1/z}: essential singularity, every value except 0 attained infinitely often
- ∫₀^∞ x^{α−1}/(1+x) dx = π/sin(πα)
- ∫₀^{2π} dθ/(a+cos θ)
- Σ 1/n² = π²/6 from π cot πz
- 1/sin(1/z): a non-isolated singularity at 0
