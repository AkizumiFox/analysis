# Blueprint: Chapter 4 — From Derivations to Mathematical Prose

**Directory:** `src/ch04-from-derivations-to-prose/` (slug `from-derivations-to-prose`). **Part 0, the last of four logic chapters**, main line. It is the bridge from the formal calculus of Chapters 1–3 to the prose of the rest of the book. Its final section is the dispute over excluded middle (decision 3, spec below). Level reference: none directly (Huth & Ryan has no counterpart); the rhetoric is ours, and the education research behind §04 and §06 is `authoring/design/research-reader.md` A1, B4 and `curriculum.md` "Evidence".

**Purpose.**
- Mathematicians do not write Fitch boxes, yet every proof in this book **is** a derivation underneath. This chapter makes the correspondence exact and usable in both directions:
  - **reading:** recover the derivation (at least its skeleton) from a prose proof, and so check it;
  - **writing:** start from the derivation's skeleton and produce the prose a mathematician would write, knowing which steps may go silent and which never may.
- Tools: a **rhetoric dictionary** (every primitive and derived rule, the phrases that carry it, an analysis example), **four levels of abbreviation** of one proof (full derivation → skeleton → annotated prose → plain prose), the book's single **self-explanation training passage** (STYLE §11.1), and the **"what is not allowed" catalogue** with proof verification.
- It hands over to the rest of the book: from Chapter 5 on, proofs are plain prose (level 3), with Part 0's rule tags (level 2) allowed in Part 0 only (STYLE §10), and the catalogue is cited whenever a warning concerns one of its errors.
- It ends with excluded middle: the one rule (RAA) that separates classical from constructive mathematics, its history, and what it means for analysis. The main line stays classical.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`):
- **Hear the rule in the phrase.** "Let" is \( \forall \)I or an assumption; "choose" is \( \exists \)E; "take" is \( \exists \)I; "suppose, for contradiction" is RAA or \( \neg \)I.
- **Expand to check.** When a step is doubtful, rewrite that stretch one level lower.
- **Skeleton first.** Write the first and last sentence of every box before filling any.
- **Self-explain each line** by earlier lines, definitions and named results — never by paraphrase.
- **Two columns when checking:** *is the line true?* and *does it follow, and by which rule?*
- **Say what depends on what.** Every chosen object carries its dependencies, which are the boxes open when it was chosen.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 1–3 (all main-line labels). The analysis examples take their definitions as quantifier patterns (Chapter 3 §01, §07) and school facts about ℝ; say that Part I (Chapter 10 on) develops them (prose pointers). Chapter 2's optional `thm-raa-independent` may be **pointed at** in §07 prose but never cited in a proof.

**Notation.** No new symbols except in §07: BHK is prose only; Lean identifiers in `code`. Rule tags in annotated prose are written `[\( \rulename{\forall I} \)]` (small caps are not available in math; use `\rulename` in brackets, matching STYLE §10's `[∀I]`).

## The rhetoric dictionary (specification for §01–§02)

Each row: **rule** → **phrases** (in the order a writer should prefer them) → **analysis example** (a sentence from a proof the reader has seen or will see in Part I, stated here in full, with the rule's premises visible) → **may it be silent in plain prose?** (feeds `def-silent-steps`) → **Lean** (one column, optional). Every row gets one worked `exm-rhetoric-*` in its section where the phrase is shown doing its job; rows grouped as in the labels list. The prose phrases must be our own choices; common phrases ("let", "suppose", "hence") are common property.

| Rule | Phrases | Analysis example (to be written out in full in the section) | Silent at level 3? |
|---|---|---|---|
| \( \wedge \)I | "…, and …"; "we show (a) … and (b) …"; "both … hold" | "\( s \) is an upper bound of \( A \), and no smaller number is" (sup, two clauses) | often, when both conjuncts are displayed |
| \( \wedge \)E | "in particular"; "so \( a > 0 \)"; silently using one clause of a hypothesis | "since \( x \in (0, 1) \), in particular \( x > 0 \)" | yes |
| \( \to \)I | "assume …; then …; hence if … then …"; "suppose …"; "if …, then …" as the frame of the whole proof | "Suppose \( \abs{x - 1} < 1 \). Then … so \( x > 0 \)." | never the assumption; the closing "hence" may be silent |
| \( \to \)E | "since …, …"; "by (hypothesis/lemma), …"; "hence" | "Since \( f \) is continuous at \( a \) and \( a_n \to a \), …" | the citation, never |
| \( \vee \)I | "…, so certainly \( P \) or \( Q \)"; "in particular \( x \ne 0 \) or …" | "\( x > 0 \), so \( x \ge 0 \)" (weakening a strict inequality is \( \vee \)I) | yes |
| \( \vee \)E | "we consider two cases"; "Case 1 … Case 2 …"; "in either case"; "otherwise" | the triangle inequality by signs | never the split; the "in either case" may be silent |
| \( \neg \)I | "suppose \( P \); … a contradiction; hence \( \neg P \)"; "\( P \) is impossible, since …" | "suppose some rational \( r \) has \( r^2 = 2 \) …" | never |
| \( \neg \)E | "this contradicts …"; "which is impossible"; "but …" | "then \( n \ge 1/\varepsilon \ge n \), a contradiction" | never |
| \( \bot \)E | "this case cannot occur"; "vacuously"; "there is nothing to prove" | "if \( A = \emptyset \) there is nothing to prove" | yes |
| RAA | "suppose, for contradiction, that \( \neg P \)"; "suppose not"; "assume the contrary" | "Suppose \( \sup A \) is not an upper bound …" | never |
| \( \leftrightarrow \)I | "\( (\Rightarrow) \) … \( (\Leftarrow) \) …"; "conversely"; "we prove both directions" | `thm-squares-order` | never |
| \( \leftrightarrow \)E | "by definition"; "that is"; "i.e."; "equivalently" | "\( f \) is increasing, that is, \( x < y \) implies \( f(x) < f(y) \)" | yes for definitional iff |
| R | "recall that"; "as noted above" | — | yes |
| MT / CP | "…; so \( P \) cannot hold"; "we prove the contrapositive"; "equivalently, if not \( Q \) then not \( P \)" | `thm-square-even-implies-even` | the announcement "we prove the contrapositive" never |
| \( \neg\neg \)E, LEM | "either \( P \) or not"; "\( P \) must hold, since …"; "otherwise" | "either the sequence has infinitely many terms in the left half, or it does not" (the Bolzano–Weierstrass split, previewed) | never in this book: §07's flag |
| DS | "since \( x \ne 0 \), the first case is impossible, so …" | "\( x^2 = x \) so \( x = 0 \) or \( x = 1 \); since \( x \ne 0 \), \( x = 1 \)" | yes |
| De Morgan, quantifier negation | "not all … so some … fails"; "it is not the case that …, so …" | negating "\( s \) is an upper bound" | the negated form must be written |
| \( \forall \)I | "let \( x \in A \)"; "fix \( \varepsilon > 0 \)"; "let \( \varepsilon > 0 \) be given"; "since \( x \) was arbitrary" | "Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \)." as the first line of every limit proof | never the introduction; "since \( x \) was arbitrary" may be silent |
| \( \forall \)E | "in particular, for \( x = \)…"; "applying … with \( \varepsilon/2 \) in place of \( \varepsilon \)" | "apply the definition of \( a_n \to L \) with \( \varepsilon/2 \)" | the instance must be named when it is not the obvious one |
| \( \exists \)I | "take \( N = \)…"; "let \( \delta \coloneqq \)…; then …"; "… will do" | "Let \( \delta \coloneqq \min\{1, \varepsilon/10\} \)" (STYLE §4 G1–G2) | never; dependencies stated |
| \( \exists \)E | "choose \( N \) such that"; "by hypothesis there is \( k \) with \( n = 2k \)"; "let \( x_0 \) be such a point" | "choose \( N \) (depending on \( \varepsilon \)) such that …" | never; the name must be fresh |
| =I, =E | "substituting"; "\( = \) … \( = \)" chains; "by (1)" | a calc chain for \( \abs{x^2 - 4} \) | yes, inside chains |
| \( \exists! \) | "there is exactly one"; "existence … uniqueness …"; "suppose \( y \) also …" | uniqueness of the supremum | never both halves |
| induction (`ax-induction`, `thm-strong-induction`) | "we argue by induction on \( n \)"; "base case"; "suppose the claim holds for \( n \)" | \( 2^n \ge n + 1 \) | never the base case |
| cut / lemma (`lem-weakening-cut`) | "we first show (Claim)"; "it suffices to show"; "we claim" | "it suffices to show \( \abs{a_n - L} < \varepsilon \) for \( n \ge N \)" | — |
| symmetry (not a rule: a derived pattern) | "without loss of generality"; "similarly"; "by symmetry" | "WLOG \( x \le y \)" as \( \vee \)E whose second box is the first with letters swapped | allowed only when the swap is literally exact |

Also in the dictionary text (not table rows): the **hidden universal closure** convention (a theorem "if \( n \) is even then \( n^2 \) is even" means \( \forall n \)); "for some" vs "for all" placement ambiguity in English ("\( x^2 > y \) for some \( x \) for all \( y \)"); "any".

The hidden universal closure. "Any".

## 03-levels-of-abbreviation.md — Four Levels of One Proof

- **Hook.** A derivation, a skeleton and a paragraph are the same proof written for different readers.
- **`def-levels-of-proof`** (minor definition; the chapter's organizing idea):
  - **Level 0 — full derivation.** Every line one rule; arithmetic facts entered as premises.
  - **Level 1 — skeleton.** The boxes and the logical lines only; each mathematical stretch compressed into one line justified "arith", "by def", or a cited result.
  - **Level 2 — annotated prose.** Sentences, each tagged with its rule in brackets (the Part 0 rule tags of STYLE §10).
  - **Level 3 — plain prose.** What Part I onward writes.
- **`def-silent-steps`:** the convention, from the dictionary's last column: at level 3, \( \wedge \)I/E, R, \( \vee \)I, definitional \( \leftrightarrow \)E, the obvious \( \forall \)E, =E in chains, and the closing "hence \( P \to Q \)" may be silent; the **openings** of \( \to \)I, \( \neg \)I, RAA, \( \forall \)I, \( \exists \)E, the case split of \( \vee \)E, the witness of \( \exists \)I with its dependencies, and every citation may **never** be silent.
- **Worked examples** (key proofs; each shown at all four levels, side by side where the page allows, otherwise in sequence with line cross-references):
  - `exm-sqrt2-four-levels`: `thm-sqrt2-irrational` (Chapter 1) — \( \neg \)I, \( \exists \)E on "\( p/q \) in lowest terms", \( \forall \)E of the even-square lemma. **Faded triple, full version.** *Before reading on* prompt.
  - `exm-uniform-four-levels`: Chapter 3's `exm-uniform-implies-continuous`.
- **Expand to check** (subsection): when a level-3 step is doubtful, drop that stretch to level 1. Show one: a plausible sentence ("hence, choosing \( \delta \) as above for every \( x \) …") which, expanded, needs a \( \forall \)I over a name already bound by an \( \exists \)E.
- **Warnings.** Level 3 is not "level 0 with steps missing at random": what is silent is fixed by `def-silent-steps`. A proof that is only a skeleton (level 1) is not finished prose.
- **Quick checks.** Which lines of the level-0 derivation of `exm-sqrt2-four-levels` became silent at level 3?
- **Exercises.** A1: comprehension parts on `exm-uniform-four-levels`: which level-0 lines correspond to "Let \( x \in A \) and \( \varepsilon > 0 \)"; where the dependence of \( \delta \) is recorded at each level; the idea in two sentences. B1: write levels 1–3 for Chapter 3's `prp-exists-forall-swap` read as a statement about numbers (\( R(x, y) \) as "\( x \le y \)" on ℕ). B2: from a level-3 proof ("the product of two odd integers is odd"), reconstruct level 1. C1: levels 1 and 3 for `prp-arbitrarily-close-equal`. C2 [C1: Exit check: levels]: given a level-0 derivation of "if \( a_n \to L \) and \( c \in \nR \), then \( c\, a_n \to cL \)" skeleton (case \( c \ne 0 \); school arithmetic as premises), write levels 2 and 3. **Marking scheme** (10): every non-silent step present at level 3 (4: \( \forall \)I for \( \varepsilon \), \( \exists \)E for \( N \) with \( \varepsilon/\abs{c} \), \( \exists \)I with the dependence stated, the \( \forall \)I for \( n \ge N \)); level-2 tags correct (3); no silent step beyond `def-silent-steps` (2); the case \( c = 0 \) handled or excluded explicitly (1). Fatal: \( N \) chosen before \( \varepsilon \); the case \( c = 0 \) divided by.

## 04-reading-proofs.md — Reading a Proof: Self-Explanation

- **The training passage** (STYLE §11.1; `exm-self-explanation`). One short proof of our own that the reader has not seen explained before: "for real \( x > 0 \), \( x + 1/x \ge 2 \)" (four lines: \( (x - 1)^2 \ge 0 \); expand; divide by \( x \), legitimate because \( x > 0 \); rearrange). It is short, every line has a checkable warrant, and one line (the division) is where the hypothesis is used. Show it explained twice:
  - **well:** each line justified by earlier lines, definitions and named results, and the rule named ("line 3 follows from line 2 by dividing by \( x \); the inequality keeps its direction because \( x > 0 \), and this is the only place the hypothesis is used");
  - **badly:** each line paraphrased ("so now we divide and it still works").
  Then say why the first helps (it checks the warrant of each step and finds the gap if there is one) and the second does not (it restates, and would read a wrong proof just as happily). Method from the research (`research-reader.md` B4.1), our own example and wording; nobody named.
- **Strategies of good readers** (short subsection, our phrasing of research-reader A1's list): try the theorem first; identify the framework (the skeleton — the level 1 of §03); break it into sub-proofs (claims = cuts); illustrate a hard line with an example; compare with your own attempt.
- **Warning.** Self-explanation is not re-reading, and not summarizing.
- **Quick check.** Self-explain lines 2–4 of a given six-line proof; compare with the folded model answer.
- **Exercises.** A1: classify six explanations as "justifies" or "paraphrases". B1: self-explain a proof from Chapter 3 (the negation example of §05) line by line, citing labels. B2: a proof with one unjustified line; self-explanation finds it — which line, and what would justify it? ## 05-writing-proofs.md — Writing a Proof

*Migrates:* `exm-annotate-abs` ("label every sentence"), `exm-write-from-derivation` (pilot §07 "From proof to rules, and back"), pilot §07 C2 ("label the lines").

- **Hook.** A proof is written for a reader who will check it line by line (§04). Write so that each line's warrant is visible.
- **The procedure** (an `::: {.algorithm}` block): (1) write the goal in symbols; (2) unwind definitions (\( \leftrightarrow \)E); (3) read the skeleton off the goal (Chapter 1 §07) and write the level-1 skeleton; (4) fill boxes, working backwards in scratch (the Idea) and forwards on the page (the Proof) — STYLE §4 "backwards Idea, forward Proof", said here as a logical fact: the search is goal-directed, the derivation is written top-down; (5) choose constants last in the search, first on the page, with dependencies; (6) level 3 by `def-silent-steps`; (7) reread as a checker (§06's two columns).
- **Conventions of written mathematics** (short list): announce the skeleton ("we prove the contrapositive"; "by induction on \( n \)"); name every object when introduced; one claim per sentence; end with what was shown; displayed claims labelled for citation.
- **Worked examples.** `exm-annotate-abs`, `exm-write-from-derivation` (migrated). One new: from goal to prose for "if \( A \subseteq B \) are sets of reals and \( B \) is bounded above, then every upper bound of \( B \) is an upper bound of \( A \)" (membership as a predicate; Chapter 5 later).
- **Warnings.** Writing the search instead of the proof (the scratch work in the proof). Introducing a constant without its dependency.
- **Quick check.** Which step of the procedure produces the sentence "Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \)"?
- **Exercises.** A1: label the lines of a proof (pilot §07 C2). B1: three short proofs written from given skeletons. **B2 (proof ordering):** see Scaffolding. **B3 (faded, last step blank):** see Scaffolding. C1: write "if \( \abs{x - a} < \delta \) implies \( \abs{f(x) - f(a)} < \varepsilon \), for \( f(x) = 2x + 3 \), find \( \delta \)" as Idea and Proof (backwards, then forwards). C2: a proof that is correct but badly written (search mixed into proof, undeclared dependencies); rewrite it at level 3.

## 06-checking-proofs.md — Checking Proofs, and What Is Not Allowed

*Migrates:* pilot §07 "Checking a proof" (`exm-verify-cases`, `exm-verify-converse`, `exm-verify-negation`, `exm-verify-scope`) and exercise C3 ("four proofs to check").

- **Hook.** The reader working alone is the person least able to judge their own proof (research-reader A1). The remedy is procedural: two columns, and a catalogue of what fails.
- **The two-column check** (kept from the pilot, sharpened): for each line, *is it true?* and *does it follow, by which rule, from which available lines?* Why they are different questions: a line can be true and not follow (the dangerous case), or follow and be false (then an earlier line is false).
- **`exm-not-allowed-catalogue`** (decision 2's catalogue; every entry: the error, the rule broken, a concrete failing instance with a **true** or **false** conclusion as marked, and the chapter where the rule lives):
  - (N1) verifying instances and concluding \( \forall \) — \( \forall \)I (Chapter 3 §03);
  - (N2) a witness that depends on a variable introduced later, while claiming uniformity (\( \delta \) depending on \( x \)) — \( \exists \)I after \( \forall \)I, eigenvariable condition (Chapter 3 §07);
  - (N3) treating the name from \( \exists \)E as arbitrary — \( \exists \)E / \( \forall \)I (Chapter 3 §04);
  - (N4) reusing one witness name for two \( \exists \)E's — fresh-name condition (Chapter 3 §04);
  - (N5) proving the converse — \( \to \)I with the box opened on the conclusion (Chapter 1 §02);
  - (N6) affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent — not rules; refuted by a row (Chapter 2 §05);
  - (N7) a missing case — \( \vee \)E (Chapter 1 §03);
  - (N8) cases that do not exhaust — \( \vee \)E citing an underived disjunction (Chapter 1 §03);
  - (N9) circular reasoning — citing a line that is not available (the goal itself, or a result proved later) (Chapter 1 §02);
  - (N10) using an assumption after its box has closed — scope (Chapter 1 §02);
  - (N11) a wrong negation — RAA opened with a statement that is not the negation of the goal; a bound negated instead of a quantifier (Chapter 1 §06, Chapter 3 §05).
  Say that the catalogue will be cited by number from later Parts (STYLE §10).
- **Worked examples.** The four migrated Proofs A–D, each checked in two columns, each error keyed to its catalogue entry.
- **Warnings.** A proof with a true conclusion can still be wrong; "I know the result is true" is not a check.
- **Quick check.** Which catalogue entry does "for every \( n \), choose \( N \) larger than all the \( N_\varepsilon \)" commit, and when is it *not* an error (finitely many)?

## 07-excluded-middle.md — The Dispute over Excluded Middle

Specification: `decisions.md` item 3, binding. All results here are `.optional` (nothing later depends on them); later chapters may point here in prose when they flag a non-constructive step (STYLE §10).

- **Hook.** Chapter 1 §06 found that one rule, RAA, is the only way to remove a negation, and that it is interderivable with \( \neg\neg \)E and LEM (`thm-classical-rules-interderivable`). Chapter 2 §05 (optional) showed that it cannot be derived from the others. Some mathematicians refused it. Why, and what is lost without it?
- **One extra rule** (recall, no re-proof): LEM, \( \neg\neg \)E and RAA are one principle over the other rules; the **intuitionistic** system is Chapter 1's primitive rules minus RAA (this is why \( \bot \)E was kept primitive — pay Chapter 1's remark).
- **`prp-not-not-excluded-middle`** (**Try it first**; key proof): \( \vdash \neg\neg(P \vee \neg P) \) without RAA — a \( \neg \)I box on \( \neg(P \vee \neg P) \), inside it a \( \neg \)I box on \( P \). Aftermath: nobody can *refute* excluded middle; the intuitionist declines to *assert* it. Remark (no proof): every classical propositional tautology becomes intuitionistically derivable after \( \neg\neg \) is put in front (Glivenko, 1929).
- **BHK reading.** A proof of \( P \wedge Q \) is a pair of proofs; of \( P \vee Q \), a proof of one **with a label saying which**; of \( P \to Q \), a method turning proofs of \( P \) into proofs of \( Q \); of \( \neg P \), a method turning proofs of \( P \) into proofs of \( \bot \) (Chapter 1 §04's remark that \( \neg P \) behaves like \( P \to \bot \)); of \( \exists x\, \varphi \), a witness with a proof; of \( \forall x\, \varphi \), a method for every \( x \). Read LEM under BHK: a uniform method deciding every statement — which nobody has.
- **`thm-irrational-power-rational`** (`.optional`): there are irrational \( a, b \) with \( a^b \) rational.
  - *Non-constructive proof:* LEM on "\( \sqrt 2^{\sqrt 2} \) is rational". Either it is (take \( a = b = \sqrt 2 \)) or not (take \( a = \sqrt 2^{\sqrt 2} \), \( b = \sqrt 2 \); \( a^b = 2 \)). Say exactly which line uses LEM, and that the proof does not tell us which case holds. (It is the second, by the Gelfond–Schneider theorem, 1934: prose remark, no proof.)
  - *Constructive proof* (**Try it first**): \( a = \sqrt 2 \), \( b = \log_2 9 \), \( a^b = 3 \). Needs `prp-log2-9-irrational` (`.optional`): if \( \log_2 9 = p/q \) then \( 2^p = 9^q \), even = odd (uses \( p, q \ge 1 \), since \( \log_2 9 > 0 \)). Exponent laws for real exponents are school facts here; pointer to the Part I chapter that defines \( a^x \) (prose only; take its number from `book.yaml`).
- **History** (prose, dated, checked; every quotation either verified against a primary source or omitted — `TODO(verify)` until checked): Brouwer 1908 (the unreliability of the logical principles); Kolmogorov 1925; Hilbert's 1927 Hamburg lecture and the Grundlagenstreit (including the 1928 *Mathematische Annalen* editorial board affair — one sentence); Heyting 1930 formalizes intuitionistic logic; the Gödel (1933) – Gentzen (1936) double-negation translation: classical arithmetic is consistent if intuitionistic arithmetic is; Bishop 1967, *Foundations of Constructive Analysis*: constructive analysis without Brouwer's philosophy.
