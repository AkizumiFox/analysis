# Chapter 64: The Hahn–Banach Theorems

Directory: `src/ch64-hahn-banach/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Duality arguments (decisions.md 14) are named here: test against functionals. Zorn's lemma is proved here from ax-choice (Part 0 main line) and is then cited by Tychonoff in weak-topologies-alaoglu.*

## Planned sections

1. Zorn's lemma, proved from the axiom of choice (ax-choice, Part 0) just in time; sublinear functionals and the analytic Hahn–Banach theorem
2. Complex and normed versions; norming functionals; X* separates points
3. The bidual: the canonical embedding J: X → X** is an isometry; annihilators and duals of subspaces and quotients
4. The Minkowski gauge and the geometric forms: separating an open convex set from a point, strict separation
5. Density criterion: a subspace is dense iff every functional vanishing on it is zero
6. Adjoint operators between Banach spaces; ‖T*‖ = ‖T‖
7. Banach limits; (ℓ^∞)* ≠ ℓ¹ and (L^∞)* ≠ L¹; Riesz's original theorem on C[a,b] via Hahn–Banach

## Must prove

- Zorn's lemma from the axiom of choice
- analytic Hahn–Banach (sublinear domination)
- complex Hahn–Banach
- existence of norming functionals
- J: X → X** is an isometry
- first and second geometric forms (separation)
- density criterion via annihilators
- existence of Banach limits

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Banach limit on ℓ^∞: a functional not given by any ℓ¹ sequence
- separation fails without openness/compactness (two closed convex sets at distance 0)
- finitely additive translation-invariant 'measure' on all subsets of ℝ (problem of measure)

- Show (ℓ^∞)* is strictly larger than ℓ¹ using a Banach limit
