**Part IV, Measure and Integration.** Starting from Part III's list of what the Riemann integral lacks, build Lebesgue measure and the Lebesgue integral on ℝᵈ, then abstract measures, products (Fubini–Tonelli and change of variables in full), Lᵖ, modes of convergence, differentiation, and signed measures up to Radon–Nikodym, preceded by the Urysohn and Tietze theorems that Lusin and the density of C_c need.

## Planned sections

1. The question: is d/dx ∫ₐˣ f = f a.e.? Averages over balls and the obstruction
2. The Vitali covering lemma (finite, 3ᵈ version) and the Vitali covering theorem for fine covers
3. The Hardy–Littlewood maximal function; the weak (1,1) inequality; the Lᵖ bound for p > 1 via the layer-cake formula
4. The Lebesgue differentiation theorem: the template 'prove it on a dense class, extend with the maximal inequality'

6. Approximations to the identity converge at every Lebesgue point; Fejér and Poisson means of L¹(𝕋) functions converge a.e. (closing Part III)

## Must prove

- Vitali covering lemma
- Vitali covering theorem (fine covers)
- weak (1,1) maximal inequality
- ‖Mf‖_p ≤ C_p‖f‖_p for 1 < p ≤ ∞
- Lebesgue differentiation theorem
- a.e. point is a Lebesgue point
- Lebesgue density theorem
- f*K_ε → f at Lebesgue points for approximate identities with radially decreasing integrable majorant

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Mf ∉ L¹ for f = 1_[0,1] (Mf ~ 1/|x|)
- density of a fat Cantor set at its points
- a set with density ½ everywhere cannot exist (exercise)
