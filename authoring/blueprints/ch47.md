## Planned sections

1. Lᵖ norms; Young's inequality for products, Hölder, Minkowski; L^∞ and the essential supremum
2. Completeness (Riesz–Fischer); Lᵖ convergence gives an a.e.-convergent subsequence; inclusions on finite measure spaces and for ℓᵖ

4. Continuity of translation in Lᵖ, p < ∞
5. Convolution: Minkowski's integral inequality, Young's inequality ‖f*g‖_p ≤ ‖f‖_p‖g‖₁, smoothing properties
6. Approximate identities in Lᵖ norm; mollifiers; C_c^∞(ℝᵈ) is dense in Lᵖ; smooth Urysohn functions
7. L²(𝕋) completes ℛ(𝕋): Riesz–Fischer for Fourier series, Parseval for all f ∈ L²(𝕋), Fejér in L¹ norm and uniqueness for L¹(𝕋) — the Part III list closed

## Must prove

- Hölder's inequality
- Minkowski's inequality
- Lᵖ(μ) is complete for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞
- C_c(ℝᵈ) dense in Lᵖ for 1 ≤ p < ∞
- ‖τ_h f − f‖_p → 0 for p < ∞
- Young's inequality ‖f*g‖_p ≤ ‖f‖_p‖g‖₁
- mollification f*φ_ε ∈ C^∞ and → f in Lᵖ
- L²(𝕋) ↔ ℓ²(ℤ) via Fourier coefficients is an isometric isomorphism

## Key examples and counterexamples

- x^{−a} on (0,1) and (1,∞): which Lᵖ
- L^∞ is not separable and C_c is not dense in L^∞
- the standard bump e^{−1/(1−|x|²)}
- Lᵖ(0,1) ⊃ L^q(0,1) for p<q but no inclusion on ℝ
