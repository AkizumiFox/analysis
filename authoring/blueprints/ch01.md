# Chapter 1: The Grammar of Proof I: Connectives and Natural Deduction

Directory: `src/ch01-logic-connectives/`. State: not started.

**Part 0, Foundations: Logic, Sets, Numbers.** Teaches the grammar of proof (natural deduction for connectives, quantifiers and equality), then sets, functions, relations, induction and cardinality as the main-line bridge, and, beside the main line, builds ℕ, ℤ, ℚ and ℝ and proves the complete ordered field unique; forced by a reader who has never written a proof and by Part I's decision to start from the least upper bound axiom.

*Follow curriculum Stage 0 ND chapter order items 1–3 (connectives by rules, Fitch boxes, truth tables as summary). Truth tables come AFTER the rules (decisions 2). Every rule gets at least one analysis-flavoured example (decisions 1). The full rule / phrase / Lean table is started here (propositional rows: constructor, .1/.2, intro, apply/exact, left/right, rcases, intro…False, exfalso, by_contra) and completed in the next chapter. Lean is shown, never required.*

**Page budget:** about 52 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** none.

## Planned sections

1. Why proofs have a grammar: statements, connectives, and a proof as a chain of licensed steps (mathematical English beside symbols)
2. ∧ and →: introduction and elimination; assumptions opened and discharged; Fitch boxes and the scope of an assumption
3. ∨, ¬ and ⊥: proof by cases, ¬I (assume and reach ⊥), ⊥-elimination, and vacuous truth derived from ⊥E
4. ↔ and definitions: the 'if' of a definition means 'iff' (a convention of language, not a rule of logic)
5. RAA as a separate rule: why ¬I is not proof by contradiction, and where RAA is needed (¬¬P ⊢ P, contrapositive in reverse)
6. Truth tables as a summary of the rules; tautologies, standard equivalences, soundness explained informally
7. Proof skeletons read off the rules: direct, contrapositive, contradiction, cases; the propositional half of the rule / phrase / Lean table

## Must prove

- P→Q ⊢ ¬Q→¬P by →I and ¬I; the converse ¬Q→¬P ⊢ P→Q needs RAA
- ⊥ ⊢ P and ¬P ⊢ P→Q (vacuous truth from ⊥E)
- De Morgan laws by natural deduction, flagging that ¬(P∧Q) ⊢ ¬P∨¬Q uses RAA
- P∨Q, ¬P ⊢ Q (disjunctive syllogism) and distributivity of ∧ over ∨
- for integers n: n² even ⇒ n even (via contrapositive), with each step labelled by its rule

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Wason selection task: the conditional is not the biconditional
- 'x² < 0 ⇒ x = 7' is true for every real x (vacuous truth)
- definition 'f is increasing if …' used in both directions
- a truth table row read as a failed attempt to build a derivation

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Write a Fitch derivation of (P→Q)∧(Q→R) ⊢ P→R and then the same argument as fluent prose
- Label each line of a short proof (n² odd ⇒ n odd) with the rule it uses
