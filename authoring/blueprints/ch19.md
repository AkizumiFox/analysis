# Chapter 19: Power Series, Taylor Series and the Elementary Functions

Directory: `src/ch19-power-series/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

## Planned sections

1. The complex numbers: ℂ as ℝ² with its multiplication, modulus, and complex series (absolute convergence carries over)
2. Radius of convergence: Cauchy–Hadamard via limsup; the ratio formula; behaviour at the endpoints
3. Uniform convergence on [−r, r] for r < R; continuity; Abel's theorem and ln 2 = 1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − ⋯
4. Term-by-term differentiation and integration; power series are C^∞ and their coefficients are Taylor coefficients
5. Taylor series versus Taylor polynomials: Lagrange and integral remainders; e^{−1/x²} is smooth but not analytic; the binomial series
6. exp from its power series; exp(x+y) = exp x · exp y; log as its inverse and as ∫₁ˣ dt/t; real powers aˣ
7. sin and cos from power series; the definition of π; periodicity; addition formulas (retiring the 'provisional' flags); e^{iθ} = cos θ + i sin θ and the polar form of a complex number

## Must prove

- Cauchy–Hadamard formula for the radius of convergence
- a power series converges uniformly on [−r, r] for every r < R
- term-by-term differentiation of power series (radius unchanged)
- Abel's theorem
- uniqueness of power-series coefficients
- e^{−1/x²} (extended by 0) has all derivatives 0 at 0
- exp(x+y) = exp(x)exp(y)
- existence of a smallest positive zero of cos (definition of π)

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Σ xⁿ/n: converges at −1, diverges at 1
- Σ xⁿ/n²: converges on [−1,1]
- e^{−1/x²}: Taylor series converges but not to f
- 1/(1+x²) on ℝ: radius 1 though smooth everywhere (complex-analysis hook)
- ln(1+x) and arctan x series at the endpoint via Abel
