## Planned sections

1. From Part III's list: Jordan content is not countably additive; what a length/volume on all 'reasonable' sets must satisfy
2. Exterior (outer) measure via countable covers by cubes; monotonicity, countable subadditivity, approximation by open sets; m*(interval) = volume
3. Measurable sets (open-set approximation definition), the σ-algebra they form, countable additivity, continuity from above and below; equivalence with Carathéodory's criterion
4. Borel sets, G_δ and F_σ regularity; invariance under translations, dilations and reflections
5. Non-measurable sets: the Vitali set, with the use of choice flagged; no translation-invariant measure on all subsets
6. Cantor sets: the ternary set (uncountable, null) and fat Cantor sets (nowhere dense, positive measure)
7. Measurable functions: closure under limits, simple and step-function approximation, a.e. conventions
8. Littlewood's three principles: Egorov's theorem and Lusin's theorem (via Tietze)

## Must prove

- m* is countably subadditive and m*(Q) = |Q| for closed cubes
- measurable sets form a σ-algebra; m is countably additive
- continuity from above and below
- E measurable ⇔ E = G_δ minus null ⇔ F_σ plus null
- translation and dilation invariance
- Vitali set is not measurable
- every measurable f ≥ 0 is an increasing limit of simple functions; every measurable f is an a.e. limit of step functions
- Egorov's theorem
- Lusin's theorem

## Key examples and counterexamples

- Vitali set
- Cantor set
- fat Cantor set
- a Lebesgue-measurable non-Borel set (Cantor function preimage; proved after bv-and-absolute-continuity or given as a cardinality argument)
- a compact plane set without Jordan area
