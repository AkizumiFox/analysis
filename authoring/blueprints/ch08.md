# Chapter 8: Ordered Fields and the Least Upper Bound Axiom

Directory: `src/ch08-least-upper-bound/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

*Opens Part I from the axiom (environment ax, label ax-least-upper-bound) and cites no Part 0 construction. The axiom is a theorem on the Dedekind route of Part 0; say so in a remark without an @ref into the optional chapter. Do NOT prove any equivalence of completeness forms here (that is the last chapter, to avoid a cycle). Misconceptions to address explicitly: sup = max; every bounded set has a maximum; forgetting 'nonempty'; forgetting that the a in the ε-characterization depends on ε; proving one inequality only. Introduces the skills 'sup approximation', 'equality by two inequalities', 'constructing a point as a sup' (reused in IVT and Heine–Borel). Rational exponents only here; real exponents a^x wait for the power-series chapter.*

## Must prove

- uniqueness of sup
- ε-characterization of sup (both directions)
- inf A = −sup(−A) and the GLB property
- Archimedean property (by contradiction via sup ℕ) and 1/n < ε
- density of ℚ in ℝ (integer step by well-ordering)
- existence of √2 (ruling out s²<2 and s²>2)
- existence of positive n-th roots
- nested interval property
- sup(A+B) = sup A + sup B
- sup(cA) = c·sup A for c > 0

## Key examples and counterexamples

- (0,1): sup not attained
- {1 − 1/n}: sup = 1 proved with ε
- finite sets: sup = max
- {x∈ℚ : x²<2} has no sup in ℚ
- ℝ(x) with x infinite: ℕ bounded without sup, Archimedes fails
