**Part III, Fourier Analysis in the Riemann Setting.** Fourier series and the Fourier transform on 𝒮(ℝᵈ), built entirely on the Riemann integral: summability, mean-square and pointwise convergence, applications, and (optionally) finite abelian groups and Dirichlet's theorem. The Part ends by listing what the Riemann framework cannot do, and that list motivates Part IV.

## Planned sections

1. Good kernels (approximate identities): the three properties and the theorem f*K_n → f at continuity points, uniformly when f is continuous
2. The Dirichlet kernel is not a good kernel: closed form, ‖D_N‖₁ ≍ log N, and what this predicts about convergence
3. Cesàro means, the Fejér kernel and Fejér's theorem
4. Consequences: trigonometric polynomials are dense in C(𝕋); Weierstrass's theorem again; uniqueness again
5. Abel means, the Poisson kernel, Abel summability of Fourier series
6. The Dirichlet problem in the unit disc: the Poisson integral solves it for continuous boundary data; uniqueness by the maximum principle
7. Approximate identities as a pattern: the skill named, and a checklist (positivity, mass one, concentration) reused in Parts IV and VI

## Must prove

- good-kernel convergence theorem
- ‖D_N‖₁ ≥ c log N (and ≤ C log N)
- Fejér's theorem
- trigonometric polynomials are dense in C(𝕋)
- Weierstrass approximation theorem via Fejér
- Poisson kernel is a good kernel; Abel summability of Fourier series
- Poisson integral solves the Dirichlet problem in the disc

## Key examples and counterexamples

- D_N(x) = sin((N+½)x)/sin(x/2)
- F_N ≥ 0 closed form
- 1−1+1−… is Cesàro and Abel summable to ½
- Σ n(−1)ⁿ is Abel but not Cesàro summable
