# Chapter 74: Tempered Distributions, the Fourier Transform and Plancherel

Directory: `src/ch74-tempered-distributions-fourier/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Spiral from Part III: Fourier on 𝒮 is recalled, not redone; the new content is L¹/L² in the Lebesgue setting and the extension to 𝒮′. The optional last section (route R3) is the characteristic-function half of probability: it uses probability-measure-spaces (Part IV), riesz-markov and the weak* topology of weak-topologies-alaoglu.*

## Must prove

- 𝔉 is a continuous isomorphism of 𝒮 and of 𝒮′
- Plancherel's theorem on L²(ℝⁿ)
- Riemann–Lebesgue for L¹
- 𝔉(p.v. 1/x) = −iπ sgn ξ
- 𝔉(u*φ) = û φ̂ for u ∈ 𝒮′, φ ∈ 𝒮
- (optional section) Lévy's continuity theorem, and the CLT via characteristic functions

## Key examples and counterexamples

- 𝔉δ = 1 and 𝔉1 = δ
- e^{x} is a distribution but not tempered
- Fourier transform of the Heaviside function
- the Dirac comb Σδ_n and Poisson summation
- the Hilbert transform as convolution with p.v. 1/(πx)
