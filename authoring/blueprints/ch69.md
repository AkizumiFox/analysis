# Chapter 69: Hilbert Spaces

Directory: `src/ch69-hilbert-spaces/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Abstract Hilbert space theory lives here (decisions.md 9); Part IV used only the L² projection. Lax–Milgram is proved here and applied in the capstone. Placed after the Banach-space chapters so Hilbert spaces are seen as the reflexive, self-dual special case (spiral: Hilbert column).*

## Planned sections

1. Inner products, Cauchy–Schwarz, the parallelogram law; L², ℓ², and H¹ as a preview
2. Projection onto closed convex sets; orthogonal complements and H = M ⊕ M^⊥
3. The Riesz representation theorem; Hilbert adjoints; self-adjoint, unitary, normal operators; direct weak compactness of the ball
4. Orthonormal systems: Bessel, Parseval, Riesz–Fischer; orthonormal bases via Zorn; separable Hilbert spaces ≅ ℓ²
5. Examples of bases: the trigonometric system in L²(𝕋) (Part III revisited), Legendre and Hermite polynomials
6. Stampacchia's theorem and the Lax–Milgram theorem
7. Hilbert-space proofs revisited: von Neumann's Radon–Nikodym, and the Dirichlet principle as a minimization problem

## Must prove

- projection theorem onto a closed convex set
- Riesz representation theorem
- Bessel's inequality, Parseval's identity, Riesz–Fischer for orthonormal systems
- every Hilbert space has an orthonormal basis; separable ones are isometric to ℓ² or ℂⁿ
- Lax–Milgram theorem

## Key examples and counterexamples

- projection fails in a non-closed subspace and in non-Hilbert norms (non-uniqueness in ℓ^∞)
- an uncountable orthonormal basis: almost-periodic functions (exercise)
- Legendre polynomials as Gram–Schmidt of 1, x, x², …
- a coercive non-symmetric form: Lax–Milgram without a minimization principle

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Prove the projection theorem and the Riesz representation theorem unaided
- Prove Lax–Milgram and explain why symmetry reduces it to Riesz
