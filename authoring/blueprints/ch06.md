# Chapter 6: The ZF Axioms, Choice, and the Natural Numbers

Directory: `src/ch06-zf-and-natural-numbers/`. State: not started.

**Part 0, Foundations: Logic, Sets, Numbers.** Teaches the grammar of proof (natural deduction for connectives, quantifiers and equality), then sets, functions, relations, induction and cardinality as the main-line bridge, and, beside the main line, builds ℕ, ℤ, ℚ and ℝ and proves the complete ordered field unique; forced by a reader who has never written a proof and by Part I's decision to start from the least upper bound axiom.

The axiom of choice itself is stated on the main line (countable-and-uncountable, ax-choice); here it is placed among the ZF axioms, and Zorn and well-ordering are stated as equivalents with a pointer to Part VI, where Zorn is proved from choice.*

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 3 (Sets, Functions, Images and Preimages).

## Planned sections

1. Why axioms: Russell's paradox and restricted comprehension; the ZF axioms listed with what each one licenses (no metamathematics)
2. Sets that do the work: Kuratowski pairs, relations and functions as sets
3. Inductive sets and ω: von Neumann naturals 0 = ∅, n⁺ = n ∪ {n}
4. The Peano axioms, as theorems about ω or (Tao's route) as axioms assumed outright
5. The recursion theorem, then addition, multiplication and order: representative proofs, the rest as exercises
6. Categoricity: any two Peano systems are isomorphic
7. The axiom of choice: statement, the equivalent forms (Zorn, well-ordering) stated, and where the book uses it: right inverses, countable unions, the Vitali set, Hahn–Banach, Tychonoff

## Must prove

- (a,b) = (c,d) ⇒ a = c and b = d for Kuratowski pairs
- ω satisfies the Peano axioms
- the recursion theorem on ω
- commutativity and associativity of addition (representative cases)
- any two Peano systems are isomorphic

## Key examples and counterexamples

- 0 = ∅, 1 = {∅}, 2 = {∅,{∅}}, and n = {0, …, n−1}
- a structure satisfying every Peano axiom except induction (ℕ plus a copy of ℤ)

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Prove the characteristic property of Kuratowski pairs
- From the recursive definition of addition, prove a + b = b + a by induction
