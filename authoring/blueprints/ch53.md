# Chapter 53: Cauchy's Theorem and Its First Consequences

Directory: `src/ch53-cauchy-theorem/`. State: not started.

**Part V, Complex Analysis.** Complex differentiability turns out to be so rigid that one integral formula controls everything; the Part runs from Goursat to the Riemann mapping theorem and then through the qualifying-exam second layer (harmonic and subharmonic functions, normal families, products, continuation, Picard), with normal families as the complex spiral node of Arzelà–Ascoli.

*Complex quantities first meet the spiral here: locally uniform convergence is the C(K) sup-metric on each compact set. Green's theorem is mentioned only to explain why Goursat avoids assuming f′ continuous.*

## Key examples and counterexamples

- ∫_ℝ e^{−πx²} e^{−2πixξ} dx by shifting the contour
- ∫₀^∞ sin x / x dx via an indented contour
- a non-constant entire function has unbounded range
- the identity theorem fails for smooth real functions (e^{−1/x²})

## Exit checks (C-exercises)
