## Planned sections

1. Spectrum and resolvent: Neumann series, the spectrum is non-empty and compact (Liouville), spectral radius; the right shift and the Volterra operator

4. Riesz–Fredholm theory for I − K: finite-dimensional kernel, closed range, the Fredholm alternative; integral equations u − Ku = f
5. The spectrum of a compact operator: non-zero points are eigenvalues of finite multiplicity accumulating only at 0

7. Self-adjoint operators: ‖T‖ = sup|⟨Tx,x⟩|; the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators; min–max

## Must prove

- the spectrum of a bounded operator on a complex Banach space is non-empty and compact
- compact operators form a closed two-sided ideal; in Hilbert space they are norm limits of finite-rank operators
- Hilbert–Schmidt integral operators are compact
- the Fredholm alternative for I − K
- the spectrum of a compact operator is countable with 0 the only possible accumulation point
- spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators

## Key examples and counterexamples

- right shift on ℓ²: no eigenvalues, spectrum the closed disc
- Volterra operator: compact, quasinilpotent, spectrum {0}
- multiplication by x on L²[0,1]: self-adjoint, not compact, no eigenvalues
- identity on an infinite-dimensional space is not compact
- Green's kernel of −d²/dx² with Dirichlet conditions
