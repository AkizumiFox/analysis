# Chapter 66: Weak Topologies, Tychonoff and Banach–Alaoglu

Directory: `src/ch66-weak-topologies-alaoglu/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Tychonoff sits here, immediately before Banach–Alaoglu, with the diagonal proof for the separable case (decision 8 amended: general topology is the Part VI block topological-spaces-compactness and separation-urysohn-lch, and Tychonoff joins it here because it needs Zorn). Closes the 'moved out of measure theory' block (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak/weak*). Spiral: compactness row, Lᵖ/weak* column.*

**Page budget:** about 60 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 62 (Banach Spaces, Dual Spaces and the Dual of Lᵖ), Chapter 64 (The Hahn–Banach Theorems), Chapter 65 (Baire Category and the Fundamental Theorems for Operators).

## Planned sections

1. Initial topologies; the weak topology σ(X,X*); weak neighbourhoods are unbounded in infinite dimension
2. Weak convergence of sequences: eₙ ⇀ 0 in ℓ², sin(nx) ⇀ 0 in L²; weakly convergent sequences are bounded; weak lower semicontinuity of the norm
3. Mazur's theorem: closed convex sets are weakly closed; convex combinations converge strongly
4. The weak* topology; weak* convergence of measures and approximation of δ
5. Tychonoff's theorem (via Zorn), and the diagonal proof for countable products of compact metric spaces
6. Banach–Alaoglu: the general proof by Tychonoff; the separable case by a diagonal argument; metrizability of the dual ball
7. Reflexivity and separability: bounded sequences in reflexive spaces have weakly convergent subsequences; Lᵖ reflexive (1<p<∞); X* separable ⇒ X separable; c₀, ℓ¹, L¹, L^∞ not reflexive
8. Extreme points and Krein–Milman; why L¹[0,1] is not a dual space

## Must prove

- weakly convergent sequences are bounded, and ‖x‖ ≤ liminf ‖xₙ‖
- Mazur's theorem
- Tychonoff's theorem
- Banach–Alaoglu (general, and separable case by diagonal extraction)
- in a reflexive space every bounded sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence
- X* separable ⇒ X separable
- Lᵖ is reflexive for 1<p<∞

## Key examples and counterexamples

- eₙ ⇀ 0 weakly but not strongly
- c₀ is not reflexive
- in ℓ¹ weak and norm convergence of sequences coincide (Schur, exercise)
- L¹[0,1] is not isometric to any dual space (unit ball has no extreme points)
- δ_{1/n} → δ_0 weak* in C[0,1]* but not in norm

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Prove Banach–Alaoglu for separable X by a diagonal argument, then generally via Tychonoff
- Show that c₀ is not reflexive and that L¹ is not a dual space
