## Planned sections

1. Integral of simple functions; bounded functions on sets of finite measure; the bounded convergence theorem (Arzelà's theorem from riemann-limits as a corollary)
2. Nonnegative functions: the integral as a sup; Fatou's lemma; the monotone convergence theorem; term-by-term integration of series
3. Integrable functions, linearity, absolute continuity of the integral; the dominated convergence theorem
4. Riemann versus Lebesgue: a Riemann-integrable function is Lebesgue integrable with the same integral; Riemann integrable ⇔ bounded and continuous a.e.; improper integrals that are not Lebesgue integrals
5. L¹ as a normed space of classes; completeness (Riesz–Fischer for L¹); every L¹-convergent sequence has an a.e.-convergent subsequence
6. Dense families in L¹(ℝᵈ): simple, step, and continuous compactly supported functions; continuity of translation in L¹
7. Complex-valued functions; the limit-exchange checklist revisited: which Part III ad hoc hypotheses are now theorems

## Must prove

- bounded convergence theorem

- dominated convergence theorem

- L¹ is complete
- L¹ convergence ⇒ a.e. convergent subsequence
- step functions and C_c are dense in L¹(ℝᵈ)
- ‖f(·+h) − f‖₁ → 0

## Key examples and counterexamples

- n·1_(0,1/n) (Fatou strict)
- sin x / x: improper Riemann but not Lebesgue
- a bounded measurable function not a.e. equal to any Riemann-integrable function (GO 8.32)
- Σ integrals of a series of nonnegative functions
