# Chapter 59: Analytic Continuation, Monodromy and Picard's Theorem

Directory: `src/ch59-continuation-and-picard/`. State: not started.

**Part V, Complex Analysis.** Complex differentiability turns out to be so rigid that one integral formula controls everything; the Part runs from Goursat to the Riemann mapping theorem and then through the qualifying-exam second layer (harmonic and subharmonic functions, normal families, products, continuation, Picard), with normal families as the complex spiral node of Arzelà–Ascoli.

*Riemann surfaces, sheaves and covering spaces (CON §IX.4–7) are cut; monodromy is proved directly for paths in a region. The Part V exit exam sits at the end of this chapter.*

## Must prove

- Bloch's theorem (with some constant)
- the little Picard theorem

## Key examples and counterexamples

- √z and log z continued around 0: monodromy fails without simple connectivity
- Σ z^{2ⁿ}: natural boundary
- ζ(s) continued to ℂ∖{1} with the functional equation
- e^z omits exactly one value; an entire function omitting two values is constant
