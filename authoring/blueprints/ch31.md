## Planned sections

1. The questions: what do the solutions of x′ = Ax look like, and how does the solution of x′ = f(t,x) depend on its initial point? Linear systems as the one large class solved in closed form
2. The matrix exponential: e^{A} = Σ Aᵏ/k! in the operator norm; d/dt e^{tA} = Ae^{tA}; e^{A+B} = e^{A}e^{B} when AB = BA, and its failure otherwise
3. Constant-coefficient systems: x(t) = e^{tA}x₀; computing e^{tA} from the Jordan form (companion book); phase portraits in the plane; higher-order scalar equations as systems
4. Time-dependent linear systems: the solution space, fundamental matrices, Liouville's formula det Φ(t) = det Φ(t₀)·exp ∫ tr A; variation of constants (Duhamel)
5. Stability of x′ = Ax: eigenvalues with negative real part give exponential decay; linearization at an equilibrium as a preview
6. Differentiable dependence on initial data: the variational equation; C¹ dependence proved with Gronwall
7. The local flow φ_t of an autonomous equation: the group property φ_{s+t} = φ_s ∘ φ_t, each φ_t a C¹ diffeomorphism onto its image; linear flows e^{tA} as the model
8. Preview of boundary value problems: −u″ = λu with u(0) = u(π) = 0 has non-zero solutions only for λ = k² (forward to compact-operators-spectral and the capstone)

## Must prove

- e^{A} converges absolutely in the operator norm, ‖e^{A}‖ ≤ e^{‖A‖}, and d/dt e^{tA} = Ae^{tA}
- e^{A+B} = e^{A}e^{B} when AB = BA
- the solutions of x′ = A(t)x form an n-dimensional vector space; Liouville's formula
- the variation-of-constants formula for x′ = A(t)x + g(t)
- if every eigenvalue of A has negative real part, then ‖e^{tA}‖ ≤ Ce^{−εt} for some C, ε > 0
- C¹ dependence on initial data, the derivative solving the variational equation
- the group property of the flow of an autonomous C¹ equation

## Key examples and counterexamples

- A = [[0,−1],[1,0]]: e^{tA} is rotation by t
- nilpotent A: e^{tA} is a polynomial in t
- two non-commuting matrices with e^{A+B} ≠ e^{A}e^{B}
- the harmonic oscillator x″ + x = 0 as a planar system
- x′ = x²: the flow is defined only on part of ℝ × ℝ
- −u″ = λu, u(0) = u(π) = 0: eigenvalues k², eigenfunctions sin kx
