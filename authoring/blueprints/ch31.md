# Chapter 31: Compactness, Connectedness and Quotient Spaces

Directory: `src/ch31-compactness-quotients/`. State: not started.

**Part II, Metric Spaces and Several Variables.** Lifts convergence, completeness, compactness and continuity from ℝ to metric spaces and C(K), forced by concrete problems (ODE existence and uniqueness, polynomial approximation, typical continuous functions), then does calculus in ℝⁿ (inverse and implicit functions, the Riemann integral on ℝⁿ) and ends by abstracting open sets into topological spaces.

*Second week of the topology module (decisions.md 8). Local compactness and the one-point compactification go with Urysohn/Tietze at the start of Part IV; Tychonoff (Zorn) goes to Part VI before Alaoglu. Surfaces are illustrative only: no classification, no fundamental group.*

## Planned sections

1. Compactness by open covers in a topological space; closed subsets of compact sets; compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed; continuous images
2. The tube lemma and compactness of finite products (Tychonoff for infinite products deferred to Part VI)
3. Compact to Hausdorff: continuous bijections are homeomorphisms, continuous maps are closed; embeddings
4. Compactness vs sequential compactness: equal for metric spaces (recalled), different in general (informal examples)
5. Connectedness revisited: unions, closures, finite products, components
6. Quotient topology and its universal property; the circle, torus, Möbius band, projective plane and Klein bottle by gluing
7. When is a quotient Hausdorff? Quotients of compact spaces; ℝ/ℚ and the line with two origins

## Must prove

- closed subset of compact is compact; compact subset of Hausdorff is closed
- tube lemma; finite product of compact spaces is compact
- continuous bijection from compact to Hausdorff is a homeomorphism
- continuous image of compact (connected) is compact (connected)
- finite products of connected spaces are connected
- universal property of the quotient topology
- [0,1]/(0∼1) is homeomorphic to S¹

## Key examples and counterexamples

- cofinite ℝ: compact and non-Hausdorff, compact sets need not be closed
- line with two origins: locally Euclidean quotient that is not Hausdorff
- ℝ/ℚ: indiscrete quotient
- [0,1) → S¹, t ↦ e^{2πit}: continuous bijection, not a homeomorphism
- torus as [0,1]²/∼ and as S¹×S¹

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- prove that a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, and show both hypotheses are needed
- prove the torus quotient is homeomorphic to S¹×S¹
- prove the tube lemma and use it for compactness of X×Y
