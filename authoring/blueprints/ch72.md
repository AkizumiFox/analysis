# Chapter 72: Capstone: The Dirichlet Problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian Eigenbasis

Directory: `src/ch72-dirichlet-capstone/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part first settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′ and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Final chapter of the book. Everything is assembled: Hilbert spaces (Lax–Milgram), Sobolev (H¹₀, Poincaré), compactness (Rellich), spectral theory (compact self-adjoint). Evans 6.3 boundary regularity and 6.4 maximum principles are out of scope; interior regularity is outlined only.*

## Must prove

- existence and uniqueness of a weak solution u ∈ H¹₀(Ω) of −Δu = f for f ∈ L²(Ω) (or H^{−1})
- energy estimate ‖u‖_{H¹₀} ≤ C‖f‖
- the Dirichlet solution operator is compact self-adjoint positive on L²(Ω)
- the Dirichlet eigenfunctions form an orthonormal basis of L²(Ω) with eigenvalues tending to ∞
- λ₁ = min of the Rayleigh quotient over H¹₀∖{0}

## Key examples and counterexamples

- the interval (0,π): eigenfunctions sin(kx), λ_k = k²
- the square (0,π)²: products of sines, multiplicities
- the disc: Bessel-function eigenfunctions (statement)
- Weierstrass's example of an energy whose infimum is not attained among smooth functions
