## Planned sections

1. Linear maps as a normed space: the operator norm; invertible maps form an open set and inversion is continuous (Neumann series); linear algebra cited from the companion book
2. The derivative as the best linear approximation; uniqueness; differentiable ⇒ continuous
3. Partial and directional derivatives; the Jacobian matrix; why their existence is not enough
4. The chain rule and its consequences (product rule, gradients)
5. The mean value inequality on convex sets; Df = 0 on a connected open set ⇒ f constant; continuous partials ⇒ differentiable (C¹)
6. Higher derivatives and Cᵏ; symmetry of second derivatives (Schwarz/Clairaut) and its failure without continuity; Taylor's theorem in several variables
7. Local extrema: critical points and the second-derivative test via definiteness of the Hessian

## Must prove

- operator norm is a norm; ‖AB‖ ≤ ‖A‖‖B‖; GL(n) open and A ↦ A⁻¹ continuous
- uniqueness of the derivative; differentiable ⇒ continuous
- chain rule
- mean value inequality
- continuous partials ⇒ differentiable
- symmetry of mixed partials for C²
- Taylor's formula with remainder in several variables
- second-derivative test

## Key examples and counterexamples

- xy/(x²+y²): separately continuous, not continuous
- x²y/(x⁴+y²): all directional derivatives at 0, not continuous
- a differentiable function that is not C¹
- xy(x²−y²)/(x²+y²): unequal mixed partials
- the mean value equality fails for vector-valued maps: t ↦ (cos t, sin t)
