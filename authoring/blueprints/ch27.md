## Planned sections

1. The question: does y' = f(t,y) have a solution when f is merely continuous? Euler polygons, and why we need compactness in C[a,b]
2. Pointwise and uniform equicontinuity; Lipschitz families and families with bounded derivatives; swapping quantifiers (equicontinuity vs continuity of each member)
3. The diagonal argument: extracting a subsequence converging on a countable dense set
4. Arzelà–Ascoli on a compact metric space K; propagation from a dense set; compact subsets of C(K) are the closed, bounded, equicontinuous sets
5. Each hypothesis is needed: sin(nx), xⁿ on [0,1], moving bumps, fₙ = n
6. Peano's existence theorem via Euler polygons; non-uniqueness revisited; hooks forward to Montel (Part V) and Rellich/compact operators (Part VI)

## Must prove

- diagonal extraction lemma
- Arzelà–Ascoli (sequential form): bounded equicontinuous sequence in C(K) has a uniformly convergent subsequence
- equicontinuous + pointwise convergent on dense set ⇒ uniformly convergent
- E ⊆ C(K) compact ⇔ closed, bounded, equicontinuous
- Peano's existence theorem

## Key examples and counterexamples

- sin(nx) on [0,π]: bounded, not equicontinuous
- xⁿ on [0,1]
- moving bump functions
- {f: |f(0)| ≤ 1, Lip(f) ≤ 1} is compact in C[0,1]
- y' = y^{1/3}: Peano gives existence, uniqueness fails

## Exit checks (C-exercises)
