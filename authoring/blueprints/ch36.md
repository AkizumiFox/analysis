# Chapter 36: Applications of Fourier Series

Directory: `src/ch36-fourier-series-applications/`. State: not started.

**Part III, Fourier Analysis in the Riemann Setting.** Fourier series and the Fourier transform on 𝒮(ℝᵈ), built entirely on the Riemann integral: summability, mean-square and pointwise convergence, applications, and (optionally) finite abelian groups and Dirichlet's theorem. The Part ends by listing what the Riemann framework cannot do, and that list motivates Part IV.

*Weyl's criterion uses the characters of ℝ/ℤ concretely; the optional finite-fourier-analysis recasts them in group language (decisions.md 13).*

**Page budget:** about 45 source-book pages.

**Prerequisites (from the DAG):** Chapter 33 (Fourier Series: Origins and Basic Properties), Chapter 34 (Good Kernels, Summability and Fejér's Theorem), Chapter 35 (Mean-Square and Pointwise Convergence).

## Planned sections

1. Wirtinger's inequality and Hurwitz's proof of the isoperimetric inequality, with the equality case
2. The circle ℝ/ℤ and the exponentials e^{2πikx}; Weyl's equidistribution criterion
3. Equidistribution of {nγ} for irrational γ; further sequences as exercises (van der Corput optional)
4. Lacunary series and a continuous nowhere-differentiable function (Weierstrass type, via delayed means)
5. The heat equation on the circle: the heat kernel as a good kernel, smoothing, and the solution of the initial-value problem

## Must prove

- Wirtinger's inequality
- isoperimetric inequality L² ≥ 4πA for C¹ closed curves, with equality iff circle
- Weyl's criterion
- {nγ} is equidistributed iff γ is irrational
- Σ 2^{−nα} e^{i2ⁿx} (0<α<1) is continuous and nowhere differentiable
- heat kernel on the circle is a good kernel and u = f*H_t solves the heat equation

## Key examples and counterexamples

- lacunary series Σ 2^{−nα} e^{i 2ⁿ x}
- {n²γ} (exercise)
- leading digits of 2ⁿ (Benford-type, exercise)
- heat kernel on 𝕋 via its Fourier series

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Prove Weyl's criterion and apply it to {nγ}
- Prove the isoperimetric inequality by Parseval
