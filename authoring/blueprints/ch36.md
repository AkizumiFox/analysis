# Chapter 36: The Fourier Transform on ℝ

Directory: `src/ch36-fourier-transform-line/`. State: not started.

**Part III, Fourier Analysis in the Riemann Setting.** Fourier series and the Fourier transform on 𝒮(ℝᵈ), built entirely on the Riemann integral: summability, mean-square and pointwise convergence, applications, and finite abelian groups. The Part ends by listing what the Riemann framework cannot do, and that list motivates Part IV.

*The Riemann framework is felt here: every exchange of limits needs its own ad hoc hypothesis; record these on the 'what we need' list for riemann-limits.*

## Planned sections

1. Integration over ℝ for functions of moderate decrease; the Riemann-setting rules for exchanging integrals and differentiating under the integral sign, with explicit hypotheses (and a warning example when they fail)
2. The Schwartz space 𝒮(ℝ); the Fourier transform on 𝒮; the Gaussian is its own transform
3. Gaussians as good kernels; Fourier inversion and the Plancherel formula on 𝒮; extension to functions of moderate decrease
4. Convolution on ℝ; the heat equation on the line and the heat kernel; the steady-state problem in the upper half-plane and the Poisson kernel
5. The Poisson summation formula; the theta functional equation; the circle heat kernel by periodization
6. The Heisenberg uncertainty principle
7. (Optional) Weierstrass approximation via the heat kernel, and Fejér's theorem for Fourier integrals

## Must prove

- 𝒮 is closed under ∂, multiplication by x, and the Fourier transform
- (e^{−πx²})^ = e^{−πξ²}
- Fourier inversion on 𝒮
- Plancherel ‖f̂‖₂ = ‖f‖₂ on 𝒮
- (f*g)^ = f̂ ĝ on 𝒮
- Poisson summation for f, f̂ of moderate decrease
- theta functional equation ϑ(s) = s^{−1/2} ϑ(1/s)
- Heisenberg inequality

## Key examples and counterexamples

- heat kernel H_t(x) = (4πt)^{−1/2} e^{−x²/4t}
- Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane
- (1/(1+x²))^ and e^{−2π|ξ|}
- a pair of continuous iterated integrals that disagree (KOR 47–48)

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Prove the theta functional equation by Poisson summation
- Prove Fourier inversion on 𝒮 from the Gaussian good kernel
