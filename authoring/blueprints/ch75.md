# Chapter 75: Interpolation: Riesz–Thorin, Young and Hausdorff–Young

Directory: `src/ch75-interpolation/`. State: not started.

**Part VI, Functional Analysis and Distributions.** The Dirichlet problem forces the move from function spaces to abstract ones: the Part opens with the general topology it needs (topological spaces, products and compactness; separation, Urysohn–Tietze and locally compact spaces), settles what was moved out of measure theory (Lᵖ duality, Riesz–Markov, weak and weak* topologies, with Tychonoff just before Banach–Alaoglu), builds Hahn–Banach, Baire, Hilbert and compact-operator theory, then distributions, 𝒮′, interpolation and Sobolev spaces, and ends with the Lax–Milgram solution of the Dirichlet problem and the Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenbasis.

*Route R5b (roadmap A5): the harmonic-analysis on-ramp, kept short. Placed after tempered-distributions-fourier because Hausdorff–Young completes the Lᵖ story of Plancherel; nothing later in Part VI depends on it, so a reader aiming only at the capstone may postpone it. Cross-links: Phragmén–Lindelöf (Part V) and Marcinkiewicz (Part IV). Calderón–Zygmund theory and the Lᵖ bounds of the Hilbert transform are stated only (branch).*

## Planned sections

1. The question: Plancherel gives ‖f̂‖₂ = ‖f‖₂ and trivially ‖f̂‖_∞ ≤ ‖f‖₁; what happens for 1 < p < 2? Operators bounded at two endpoints
2. Hadamard's three-lines lemma (Phragmén–Lindelöf on a strip, recalled from global-cauchy-theory)
3. The Riesz–Thorin theorem: complex interpolation of Lᵖ norms, on simple functions first, then by density
4. Consequences: Young's convolution inequality ‖f*g‖_r ≤ ‖f‖_p‖g‖_q; Hausdorff–Young on ℝⁿ and on 𝕋; the exponents forced by scaling
5. Real versus complex interpolation: Marcinkiewicz (lebesgue-differentiation) beside Riesz–Thorin
6. The Hilbert transform on L² through Plancherel (the multiplier −i sgn ξ) and the statement of its Lᵖ bounds: the entry to the harmonic-analysis branch

## Must prove

- Hadamard's three-lines lemma
- the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem
- Young's convolution inequality for 1 + 1/r = 1/p + 1/q
- Hausdorff–Young ‖f̂‖_{p′} ≤ ‖f‖_p for 1 ≤ p ≤ 2, on ℝⁿ and on 𝕋
- the Hilbert transform is unitary on L²(ℝ)

## Key examples and counterexamples

- dilated Gaussians force the exponent p′ in Hausdorff–Young
- Hausdorff–Young fails for p > 2 (a function in Lᵖ(𝕋), p > 2, whose coefficients are not in ℓ^{p′})
- the Hilbert transform of 1_[0,1] is (1/π) log|x/(x−1)|: no L^∞ bound

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- Deduce Hausdorff–Young and show by scaling that its exponent cannot be improved
