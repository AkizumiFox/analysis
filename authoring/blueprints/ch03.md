# Blueprint: Chapter 3 — Predicate Logic and the Quantifier Rules

**Directory:** `src/ch03-predicate-logic/` (slug `predicate-logic`). **Part 0, the third of four logic chapters**, main line. Level reference: Huth & Ryan §2.1–2.4 (the need for predicates, terms and formulas, free and bound variables, substitution, the quantifier and equality rules, quantifier equivalences, models and semantic entailment), **depth and coverage only**. Not covered: undecidability, expressiveness, second-order logic, micromodels. No example, formula list or exercise of theirs is reused; our examples come from analysis and arithmetic.

**Purpose.**
- Analysis lives in quantifiers: "for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) there is \( N \) such that for every \( n \ge N \) …". Propositional logic cannot see inside such a sentence (Chapter 2 §03's warning). This chapter extends the language (terms, predicates, quantifiers), the calculus (\( \forall \)I, \( \forall \)E, \( \exists \)I, \( \exists \)E, =I, =E, with their side conditions) and the semantics (models), and, in the optional §09, proves the extended calculus sound.
- Its practical goals are Stage 0's (curriculum (a) and (e)): formalize, negate and **order** quantifiers; know exactly why a \( \delta \) may not depend on an \( x \) introduced later (the eigenvariable condition), and why a witness obtained by \( \exists \)E may not be treated as arbitrary (the fresh-name condition).
- Its centrepiece is decision 2's worked example: "uniformly continuous \( \Rightarrow \) continuous" as a full derivation, and the rule-by-rule reason the converse fails.
- Rhetoric is sketched only ("let \( x \) be arbitrary", "choose \( N \) such that") in each section's "What the rules sound like" paragraph; the full dictionary and the prose register are Chapter 4.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`):
- **Let \( x \) be arbitrary** (\( \forall \)I): a fresh name, about which nothing is assumed.
- **Name the witness** (\( \exists \)E): "choose \( x_0 \) with …", and never generalize over it.
- **Fix, obtain, choose** (the analysis pattern): \( \forall \)I, then \( \forall \)E and \( \exists \)E on the hypothesis, then \( \exists \)I with a term built from what is available.
- **Negate quantifier by quantifier.** Push \( \neg \) inward, flipping \( \forall \leftrightarrow \exists \) and keeping bounds.
- **Refute with a model.** A structure in which the premises are true and the conclusion false.
- **Read the dependency off the order.** A variable may depend on the variables quantified to its left, and only on them.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 1–2. School arithmetic and order facts about ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ in examples, as premises (in derivations they enter as premise lines or as a justification "arith"). Membership \( x \in A \) is used as a binary predicate; say that Chapter 5 develops sets (prose pointer). Functions appear as function symbols (\( f(x) \), \( x^2 \), \( \abs{x} \)); say that Chapter 5 defines functions as sets (prose pointer). No `@ref` beyond Chapters 1–2.

## 01-predicates-and-quantifiers.md — Predicates, Quantifiers and Translation

- **Hook.** An argument that is obviously correct and that propositional logic calls invalid: "every positive real has a square root; 2 is positive; so 2 has a square root" becomes \( p, q \therefore r \) with three unrelated atoms, refuted by a row. The missing structure is *inside* the atoms.
- **Informal definitions** (made exact in §02): predicate (a statement with holes), variable, domain of discourse, the quantifiers \( \forall \) and \( \exists \), with readings.
- **`def-bounded-quantifier`:** \( \forall x \in A\ \varphi \) abbreviates \( \forall x (x \in A \to \varphi) \); \( \exists x \in A\ \varphi \) abbreviates \( \exists x (x \in A \wedge \varphi) \); likewise \( \forall \varepsilon > 0 \), \( \exists n \ge N \). Explain **why** \( \to \) under \( \forall \) and \( \wedge \) under \( \exists \) (the other choice gives nonsense: \( \forall x (x \in A \wedge \varphi) \) says everything is in \( A \)). **One domain per formula:** the book's logic has a single domain; when a sentence mixes kinds of object (\( \varepsilon \in \nR \), \( N, n \in \nN \)), the domain is the larger one (ℝ) and the smaller kind enters as a bound (\( \exists N \in \nN \), \( \forall n \in \nN \)); a sequence \( a_n \) is a function symbol \( a \) whose values off ℕ are never used. Say this once here; every analysis translation in the chapter follows it.
- **Translation, worked** (`exm-translate-limit`): "\( a_n \to L \)", "\( A \) is bounded above", "\( f \) is continuous at \( a \)", "there is no largest prime", "every integer greater than 1 has a prime divisor" — each to symbols, with the domain stated. Analysis sentences written in symbols *after* their prose (STYLE §10).
- **Quantifier order** (`exm-quantifier-order-sum`, the curriculum's example): \( \forall x \exists y\, (x + y = 0) \) true on ℝ, \( \exists y \forall x\, (x + y = 0) \) false; read each aloud as a game (the second player may see the first move). The dependence of \( y \) on \( x \) is the whole difference.
- **Warnings.** "Any" is ambiguous in English ("if any \( x \) satisfies…" is \( \exists \); "any \( x \) satisfies…" is \( \forall \)). Hidden universal quantifiers: a theorem stated "if \( n \) is even then \( n^2 \) is even" means \( \forall n \) (Chapter 4 names this convention). An existential with \( \to \) inside is almost always a mistranslation: \( \exists x (x \in A \to \varphi) \) is true whenever something lies outside \( A \).
- **Quick checks.** Swap the quantifiers in "for every \( n \) there is a prime larger than \( n \)"; is the result true? (STYLE §10 swap check.)
- **What the rules sound like:** none yet; one sentence pointing to §03–§04.
- **Exercises.** A1: translate five sentences; spot the mistranslation in three. B1: formalize "\( A \) has a maximum", "\( f \) is increasing", "\( (a_n) \) is bounded" with domains stated. B2: for each of four ordered-quantifier sentences over ℕ, ℤ or ℝ, decide true or false with a witness or a reason. B3 (example generation): a predicate \( R(x, y) \) on ℕ such that \( \forall x \exists y\, R \) is true and \( \exists y \forall x\, R \) is false; check both. C1: translate "\( f \) is uniformly continuous on \( A \)" and "\( f \) is continuous at every point of \( A \)", and describe in one sentence the only difference. C2: how many essentially different statements arise from \( \forall x \exists y \forall z \) by permuting the three quantifier prefixes over one fixed matrix? Give a matrix over ℤ that separates two of them. (Answer: 4 up to equivalence — the six orders fall into \( \{\exists y \forall x \forall z, \exists y \forall z \forall x\} \), \( \{\forall x \forall z \exists y, \forall z \forall x \exists y\} \), \( \{\forall x \exists y \forall z\} \), \( \{\forall z \exists y \forall x\} \), since adjacent \( \forall \)'s commute (§03); "essentially different" means "not equivalent for every matrix", and each pair is separated by a matrix in two of the variables, e.g. \( y > x \).)

## 02-terms-formulas-substitution.md — Terms, Formulas, Free Variables and Substitution

- **Hook.** "\( x > 2 \)" is not a statement (Chapter 1's pilot non-example); "\( \forall x\, (x^2 \ge 0) \)" is; "\( \forall x\, (x > y) \)" depends on \( y \). The difference is which variables are **free**. And "\( \exists y\, (y \ne x) \)" (true in ℕ for every \( x \)), read with \( y \) put for \( x \), becomes \( \exists y\, (y \ne y) \), false for a silly reason: **capture**. (Not \( \forall y\, (y \ne x) \): it is false for every \( x \) already, so capture changes nothing visible.)
- **Definitions** (inductive, as in Chapter 2 §01; structural induction and recursion carry over — say so, cite `thm-structural-induction` and `thm-structural-recursion` for the pattern, and note the new clauses):
  - `def-term`: variables, constants, \( f(t_1, \dots, t_n) \).
  - `def-first-order-formula`: atomic \( R(t_1, \dots, t_n) \) and \( t_1 = t_2 \), \( \bot \), connectives, \( \forall x\, \varphi \), \( \exists x\, \varphi \). Parse trees with quantifier nodes.
  - `def-free-variable` (by recursion; a quantifier node binds its variable in its subtree), bound occurrence, scope. `def-sentence` (no free variables).
  - `def-substitution` \( \varphi[t/x] \): replace the **free** occurrences of \( x \). `def-free-for`: \( t \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \) if no free occurrence of \( x \) in \( \varphi \) lies in the scope of a quantifier \( \forall y \) or \( \exists y \) with \( y \) a variable of \( t \) (informally: no variable of \( t \) becomes bound). In the text, with a two-line structural induction: if \( x \) is not free in \( \varphi \), then \( \varphi[t/x] = \varphi \) (the passage laws of §05 use this, so it may not be left to an exercise). Renaming bound variables (\( \forall y\, \varphi \) and \( \forall z\, \varphi[z/y] \) for \( z \) not occurring in \( \varphi \)) as the standard way out; stated and used. Its justification: for a renaming at the front, \( \forall y\, \varphi \dashv\vdash \forall z\, \varphi[z/y] \) is a three-line derivation each way once §03's rules exist (show it there; the \( \exists \) version in §04); renaming inside a formula is taken as a convention here, and its semantic justification is an instance of `lem-term-substitution` (optional §09; prose pointer only).
- **Worked examples.** Free and bound occurrences in \( \forall \varepsilon (\varepsilon > 0 \to \exists N \forall n (n \ge N \to \abs{a_n - L} < \varepsilon)) \): which variables are free? (Only \( L \); \( a \) is a function symbol, not a variable, so "free" does not apply to it, though the statement is about \( a \) too.) Substitutions that are fine and one that captures: \( (\exists y\, (y > x))[y + 1 / x] \).
- **Warnings.** The same letter may occur free and bound in one formula (\( P(x) \wedge \forall x\, Q(x) \)); legal, but the book renames to avoid it. Capture changes meaning: exhibit it.
- **Quick checks.** Is \( x \) free in \( \forall y\, (x < y) \to \exists x\, (x = 0) \)? Where? Is \( y + 1 \) free for \( x \) in \( \forall z\, (x < z) \)?
- **Exercises.** A1: free/bound marking for four formulas; sentences or not. B1: compute five substitutions, two of which need renaming first. B2: define the set of free variables by recursion (the clauses), then check it on two formulas. C1: prove by structural induction that if \( t \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \) and \( x \) is free in \( \varphi \), then the free variables of \( \varphi[t/x] \) are those of \( \varphi \) other than \( x \), together with those of \( t \); show by the capture example of the text that the hypothesis "free for" is needed. (The old C1, \( \varphi[t/x] = \varphi \) when \( x \) is not free, is now proved in the text.) C2: show that \( \forall x \exists y\, (x < y) \) and \( \forall y \exists x\, (y < x) \) are the same sentence up to renaming, while \( \forall x \exists y\, (x < y) \) and \( \exists y \forall x\, (x < y) \) are not.

## 03-universal-rules.md — For Every: Let x Be Arbitrary

- **Hook.** To prove \( x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0 \) for every real \( x \), we cannot check every \( x \). We take one we know **nothing** about and prove it for that one. The rule makes "nothing" precise.
- **`def-rules-universal`.**
  - \( \forall \)E: from \( \forall x\, \varphi \), infer \( \varphi[t/x] \) for any term \( t \) free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \).
  - \( \forall \)I: open a box flagged with a **fresh** name \( x_0 \) (occurring in no premise, no open assumption, and not in the conclusion \( \forall x\, \varphi \)); if \( \varphi[x_0/x] \) is the last line of that box, close it and infer \( \forall x\, \varphi \). The side condition is checked at the line where \( \forall x\, \varphi \) is written: \( x_0 \) occurs in no premise, in no assumption of a box still open there, and not in \( \forall x\, \varphi \). Lines outside the box may mention \( x_0 \) (e.g. an earlier \( \forall \)E at \( x_0 \)); that is harmless, and the book says so rather than H&R-style "\( x_0 \) occurs nowhere outside its box", which is stronger than needed. Extend `def-derivation`'s availability rule to flagged boxes: nothing changes except the side condition (state this as one paragraph added to the Chapter 1 definition, not a new definition).
  - Paraphrase: "fresh" is the formal version of "arbitrary". The box keeps the name from leaking.
- **Derivations to show:** \( \forall x \forall y\, R(x, y) \vdash \forall x\, R(x, x) \) (4 lines: \( \forall \)E twice at the same fresh name; not \( \forall x (P(x) \to Q(x)), \forall x\, P(x) \vdash \forall x\, Q(x) \), Huth & Ryan's first \( \forall \) example); `prp-forall-and`: \( \forall x (P(x) \wedge Q(x)) \dashv\vdash \forall x\, P(x) \wedge \forall x\, Q(x) \); \( \forall x \forall y\, R(x, y) \vdash \forall y \forall x\, R(x, y) \); and a one-sentence foreshadowing of `prp-forall-implies-exists`, which needs \( \exists \)I and opens §04. Analysis example written both ways: "for every real \( x \), \( x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0 \)" as a derivation with the arithmetic fact \( \forall y\, (y^2 \ge 0) \) as a premise and \( \forall \)E at the term \( x_0 - 1 \).
- **Vacuous truth for \( \forall \)** (subsection): \( \forall x \in \emptyset\ \varphi \) is true for every \( \varphi \): from the premise \( \forall x\, \neg(x \in \emptyset) \) (what "empty" means; Chapter 5), derive \( \forall x (x \in \emptyset \to \varphi) \) by \( \forall \)E, `prp-vacuous-implication` and \( \forall \)I. The propositional base is Chapter 1's `prp-vacuous-implication`, which Chapter 10 now cites directly; this subsection extends it to \( \forall \) and pays no Chapter 10 pointer. Every real number is an upper bound of \( \emptyset \).
- **Warnings (catalogue entries previewed; the full catalogue is Chapter 4 §06).** Checking instances does not prove \( \forall \) (N1). Generalizing over a name that occurs in an open assumption: from the assumption \( x_0 > 0 \) one may not conclude \( \forall x\, (x > 0) \) — show the derivation and the flagged line.
- **Quick checks.** In a given derivation, may line 6 apply \( \forall \)I to \( y_0 \)? (It occurs in the premise on line 1.)
- **What the rules sound like:** "Let \( x \in \nR \)" / "Fix \( \varepsilon > 0 \)" (\( \forall \)I); "In particular, for \( x = 3 \)…" / "applying the hypothesis to \( x_0 - 1 \)" (\( \forall \)E); Lean `intro x`, `h a` / `specialize`.
- **Exercises.** A1: which lines of three derivations violate the side condition; rule names. B1: derive \( \forall x (P(x) \to Q(x)), \forall x (Q(x) \to R(x)) \vdash \forall x (P(x) \to R(x)) \). B2: derive \( \forall x (P(x) \to Q(x)) \vdash \forall x\, \neg Q(x) \to \forall x\, \neg P(x) \), and say why the converse search stalls (refuted in §08 B3; model: \( D = \{a, b\} \), \( P = \{a\} \), \( Q = \{b\} \)). (Not \( \forall x\, P(x) \vee \forall x\, Q(x) \vdash \forall x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \): Huth & Ryan exercise 2.3.9(l); it stays a text result in §05.) B3: a real-number \( \forall \) proof as a derivation with arithmetic premises (for every real \( x \), \( \abs{x} \ge x \), using the cases of Chapter 1 §03). C1: show that the side condition "not in the conclusion" is needed, by a derivation that violates only it and "proves" a sentence false over ℕ from a premise true over ℕ. (Answer, 4 lines: \( \forall x\, (x \le x) \); box \( y_0 \): \( y_0 \le y_0 \) by \( \forall \)E; "\( \forall x\, (x \le y_0) \)" by \( \forall \)I with \( \varphi = (x \le y_0) \), the violation; \( \exists y \forall x\, (x \le y) \) by \( \exists \)I — "there is a largest natural number".) C2 (**find the error, true conclusion**): the instance check (see Scaffolding); the solution names \( \forall \)I, points to the missing fresh-name box, and gives a *different* polynomial for which checking \( x = 0, 1, 2 \) succeeds and the conclusion fails (e.g. \( x(x - 1)(x - 2) \ge 0 \) fails at \( x = -1 \)).

## 04-existential-rules.md — There Exists: Name a Witness

- **Hook.** Two moves: to **prove** "there is an \( N \)", exhibit one; to **use** "there is a \( k \) with \( n = 2k \)", give it a name and forget where it came from. Chapter 1's evenness arguments did both informally; here they become rules (pay Chapter 1's signposts).
- **`def-rules-existential`.**
  - \( \exists \)I: from \( \varphi[t/x] \), infer \( \exists x\, \varphi \) (\( t \) free for \( x \)).
  - \( \exists \)E: from \( \exists x\, \varphi \) and a box flagged with a fresh \( x_0 \), opening with the assumption \( \varphi[x_0/x] \) and ending with \( \chi \), infer \( \chi \), provided \( x_0 \) does not occur in \( \chi \), in \( \exists x\, \varphi \), in any premise, or in any assumption of a box still open at the line where \( \chi \) is inferred (the box's own assumption is closed by then). The condition "not in \( \exists x\, \varphi \)" is **needed**: without it, \( \forall y \exists x\, R(x, y) \vdash \exists z\, R(z, z) \) (\( \forall \)E at \( x_0 \) gives \( \exists x\, R(x, x_0) \); box \( x_0 \) with assumption \( R(x_0, x_0) \); \( \exists \)I), false in ℤ with \( < \). It also guarantees that \( x_0 \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \).
  - Paraphrase: the box says "whatever the witness is, \( \chi \) follows"; so \( \chi \) must not mention it.
- **`prp-forall-implies-exists`** (opening the section): \( \forall x\, P(x) \vdash \exists x\, P(x) \); remark: this is correct because domains are **non-empty** (§08). Contrast with the bounded case \( \forall x \in \emptyset \) (true) vs \( \exists x \in \emptyset \) (false) — no conflict, since the bound turns into \( \to \) and \( \wedge \).
- **`prp-exists-forall-swap`** (key proof; faded triple full version): \( \exists x \forall y\, R(x, y) \vdash \forall y \exists x\, R(x, y) \), with the "fix, obtain, choose" order visible. **Converse attempt**: the search for \( \forall y \exists x\, R \vdash \exists x \forall y\, R \) stalls exactly at a side condition — show the partial derivation and the line that would break \( \exists \)E or \( \forall \)I. Its refutation by a model is §08 (`exm-counter-model-swap`).
- **Derivations to show:** `prp-exists-and`: \( \exists x (P(x) \wedge Q(x)) \vdash \exists x\, P(x) \wedge \exists x\, Q(x) \) (not conversely); \( \exists x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \dashv\vdash \exists x\, P(x) \vee \exists x\, Q(x) \); \( \exists x\, P(x), \forall x (P(x) \to \neg Q(x)) \vdash \neg\forall x\, Q(x) \) (10 lines; e.g. "some integer is odd, no odd integer is divisible by 4, so not every integer is"; not \( \forall x (P(x) \to Q(x)), \exists x\, P(x) \vdash \exists x\, Q(x) \), Huth & Ryan's first \( \exists \)E example); the arithmetic example "the sum of two even integers is even" as a derivation (two \( \exists \)E boxes, one \( \exists \)I at the term \( k_0 + m_0 \); the step from \( n = 2k_0 \), \( m = 2m_0 \) to \( n + m = 2(k_0 + m_0) \) is one line justified "arith", since =E arrives only in §06 — say so).
- **Warnings.** Treating the witness as arbitrary (N3; show a "proof" that every integer is even from "some integer is even"). Two \( \exists \)E's giving the same name to different witnesses ("let \( n = 2k \) and \( m = 2k \)"): the second name must be fresh — the common error in parity proofs.

## 05-quantifier-laws.md — Negating and Moving Quantifiers

- **Hook.** To prove that a sequence does **not** converge, or that a function is **not** uniformly continuous, we need the negation in a usable form: \( \neg \) pushed all the way in. Mechanical — once the laws are proved.

## 06-equality-and-uniqueness.md — Equality and Unique Existence

- **Hook.** Every calculation \( a = b = c \) is a chain of substitutions; every "the" (the limit, the supremum) hides a uniqueness proof.
- **`def-rules-equality`.** =I: \( t = t \) (no citations). =E: from \( t_1 = t_2 \) and \( \varphi[t_1/x] \), infer \( \varphi[t_2/x] \) (both terms free for \( x \)).
- **`prp-equality-symmetric-transitive`:** derivations of symmetry and transitivity from =I and =E (the choice of \( \varphi \) in each is the point; show it).
- **Calculation chains.** A chain \( t_1 = t_2 = \dots = t_n \) is repeated =E with transitivity; a mixed chain with \( \le \) needs one monotonicity fact per link (say that Chapter 10's ordered-field rules supply them).
- **`def-unique-existence`:** \( \exists! x\, \varphi \) abbreviates \( \exists x (\varphi \wedge \forall y (\varphi[y/x] \to y = x)) \), with \( y \) a variable that does not occur in \( \varphi \) (so it is free for \( x \) and is not captured; say what goes wrong otherwise, e.g. \( \varphi = (x \le y) \)). `prp-unique-existence-use`: it is equivalent to "\( \exists x\, \varphi \)" together with "\( \forall x \forall y (\varphi \wedge \varphi[y/x] \to x = y) \)"; the second is what "uniqueness" proofs prove.
- **Worked examples.** Uniqueness of the additive inverse in a field (from the field axioms as premises, stated as school algebra; Chapter 10 has them as axioms); "the" maximum of a set, if it exists (two maxima are each \( \le \) the other). The proof pattern: "suppose \( y \) also satisfies … then \( y = x \)".
- **Warnings.** Proving uniqueness is not proving existence (and vice versa). Substituting into a position that is not a free occurrence: from \( x = 0 \) and \( \forall x\, (x = x) \), "=E" to \( \forall x\, (0 = x) \) is illegal — the \( x \)'s of \( \forall x\, (x = x) \) are bound, so it is \( \varphi[x/z] \) only for a \( \varphi \) in which \( z \) does not occur, and =E returns it unchanged.
- **Quick checks.** Which formula \( \varphi \) makes =E yield symmetry from \( s = t \) and \( s = s \)?
- **What the rules sound like:** "substituting \( x = 2 \)"; "by the uniqueness of …"; Lean `rw`, `calc`.
- **Exercises.** A1: rules in a calc chain. B1: in a group-like structure (premises given), identity element is unique. B2: \( \exists! x\, (x^2 = 0) \) over ℝ, both halves. (Checked by exhaustive search: none on 1 or 2 elements; on \( \{0, 1, 2\} \), \( R = \{(1, 2), (2, 0), (2, 1)\} \) makes the first true (only \( x = 1 \)) and the second false (every \( y \) has exactly one \( x \)). ## 08-models.md — Models and Counter-models

- **Definitions.** `def-structure` (model): a **non-empty** domain \( D \); an element for each constant, a function \( D^n \to D \) for each function symbol, a set of \( n \)-tuples (a relation) for each predicate symbol; = is interpreted as identity. `def-assignment` \( \ell \) (values for variables) and \( \ell[x \mapsto d] \). The value of a term (recursion). `def-satisfaction` \( \mathfrak{M}, \ell \models \varphi \), clause by clause (Tarski), with \( \forall \) and \( \exists \) via \( \ell[x \mapsto d] \).
