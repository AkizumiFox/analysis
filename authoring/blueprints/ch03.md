# Blueprint: Chapter 3 — Truth, Soundness and Completeness

**Directory:** `src/ch03-semantics-soundness-completeness/` (slug `semantics-soundness-completeness`). **Part 0, the third of five logic chapters**, main line. Level reference: Huth & Ryan §1.3–1.5 (formal language, parse trees, semantics, induction, soundness, completeness, normal forms), **depth and coverage only**; the proofs of soundness and completeness below follow a route of our own (stated per section), and no example, formula or exercise of theirs is reused. **No Horn clauses, no SAT solvers.**

**Purpose.**
- Chapter 1 built a calculus of derivations and ended on a question it could not answer: *how can one show that something is not derivable?* This chapter answers it by giving the formulas a **meaning** (valuations, truth tables, semantic entailment \( \Gamma \models \psi \)) and proving that the calculus matches the meaning **exactly**:
  - **Soundness:** \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \Rightarrow \Gamma \models \psi \). The rules never lead from truth to falsehood; so one refuting row proves non-derivability.
  - **Completeness:** \( \Gamma \models \psi \Rightarrow \Gamma \vdash \psi \). Every truth-table consequence has a derivation; so the rules of Chapter 1 are *enough*.
- To state and prove these, the chapter makes formulas into mathematical objects (an inductive definition, parse trees, unique readability) and introduces **induction**, just in time: on ℕ, course-of-values, and structural induction on formulas. These are the book's first proofs *about* proofs, and the first long proofs the reader meets: they model the Step-roadmap and Claim structure (STYLE §4) at a level where every step can still be checked mechanically.
- It closes with normal forms (CNF, DNF) and adequate sets of connectives: the constructive content of completeness, and a tool Chapter 4 reuses when negating.
- The pilot's §06 ("truth tables summarize the rules", informal soundness) is absorbed and made exact here. Its central idea survives as the aftermath of completeness: *the truth tables are exactly what the rules prove about each connective* (`lem-connective-rows`).

**Signature moves** (`index.md`):
- **Induction on the shape of a formula.** Prove it for atoms; prove it for each connective, assuming it for the parts.
- **Define by recursion on formulas.** One clause per way of building a formula; unique readability makes it well defined.
- **Refute with one row.** A valuation making every premise true and the conclusion false proves \( \Gamma \nvdash \psi \) (by soundness).
- **Induction on the lines of a derivation.** To prove every derivable sequent has a property, show every rule preserves it.
- **Split on an atom.** \( p \vee \neg p \) and \( \vee \)E: prove the goal once assuming \( p \), once assuming \( \neg p \).

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapter 1 (all of it). ℕ = {1, 2, 3, …} with its arithmetic and order as school facts. Informal "for every \( n \)" and "there is a \( k \)" are used freely: say once, in §02, that the rules governing them are Chapter 4's and that nothing here needs more than their everyday reading (prose pointer). No sets beyond "a finite list of formulas" and "the set of atoms of a formula"; say that Chapter 6 treats sets properly (prose pointer). **No forward citations**; Chapter 7's well-ordering is not used.

**Induction, just in time (decision recorded here, flag for the author).**
- The principle of mathematical induction on ℕ is stated as a **basic property of ℕ we take as given**: `ax-induction`. It is a Part 0 axiom in the same sense as the axiom of choice in Chapter 8 (prose pointer; no label, since Chapter 8 comes later). The optional ZF chapter proves it from the set axioms (prose pointer), and Chapter 7 proves its equivalence with well-ordering and adds recursion on ℕ. Chapter 7's blueprint must then **cite** `ax-induction` and `thm-strong-induction` rather than re-derive strong induction.
- `thm-strong-induction` (course-of-values induction) is **proved** from `ax-induction` (apply it to "the property holds for every \( k \le n \)"): the reader's first real proof by induction, and the form soundness needs.
- `thm-structural-induction` is proved from `thm-strong-induction` by inducting on the **position of an entry in a formation sequence** (S2: not on the height, which presupposes that a formula has only one parse tree, i.e. `thm-unique-readability`, proved later from structural induction; that would be circular). `thm-structural-recursion` is proved by strong induction on the **number of symbols**.

## index.md

- **Title:** Truth, Soundness and Completeness.
- **Story.** Chapter 1 could show that a sequent *is* derivable, by writing a derivation, but not that one is not: the failed search for \( P \to Q \vdash Q \to P \) proved nothing. Here the formulas get a meaning, truth under a valuation, and two theorems tie meaning to derivation: whatever can be derived is true in every row where the premises are (soundness), and whatever is true in every such row can be derived (completeness). Along the way formulas become objects we can prove things about, and induction arrives, because both theorems are proved by it. The motivating problem: *prove that no derivation of \( Q \to P \) from \( P \to Q \) exists*.

## 01-formulas-and-parse-trees.md — Formulas as Objects

- **Hook.** Chapter 1 parsed statements by eye. To prove a theorem about **all** derivations, we must first say exactly what a formula is — the same way that to prove a theorem about all integers we need to know what an integer is. A goal theorem stated first: "every derivable sequent is true in every row" needs "every" to range over a precisely defined collection.
- **Definitions.**
  - The alphabet: atoms, \( \bot \), the connectives, parentheses.
  - `def-formula` (inductive definition, clause by clause): (F1) every atom and \( \bot \) is a formula; (F2) if \( \varphi \) is a formula, so is \( \neg\varphi \); (F3) if \( \varphi, \psi \) are formulas, so are \( (\varphi \wedge \psi) \), \( (\varphi \vee \psi) \), \( (\varphi \to \psi) \), \( (\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi) \); (F4) nothing else. Explain (F4) as "the smallest collection closed under (F1)–(F3)", and its practical meaning: every formula has a **formation sequence**, a finite list in which each entry is an atom, \( \bot \), or built from earlier entries.
  - `def-parse-tree` (TikZ trees; the main connective at the root; leaves are atoms or \( \bot \)). `def-subformula` (the formulas at the nodes). `def-height` (the height of the parse tree; atoms and \( \bot \) have height 1). Say in one sentence that "*the* parse tree" and so the height are well defined only once §02's `thm-unique-readability` shows a formula is built in one way only; §02 then re-obtains the height as a recursive definition (`thm-structural-recursion`). Nothing in §02 before that theorem may use the height.
  - The conventions of Chapter 1 (dropping outer parentheses, precedence, right grouping of \( \to \)) are abbreviations for official formulas; state them as a translation, not as part of the definition.
- **Examples.** Three parse trees, from an atom (degenerate case: a one-node tree) to a formula with every connective; a string that is **not** a formula (\( p \wedge \to q \); \( (p \wedge q \)), with the clause that fails. A formula written in Chapter 1's shorthand and its official form.
- **Why this definition.** Fully parenthesized binary clauses are what make parsing unambiguous (the result is §02's `thm-unique-readability`). Show the ambiguity without parentheses: \( p \wedge q \vee r \) has two parse trees and, under a row we exhibit, two different values (forward pointer to §03 for the values; the two trees are enough here).
- **Schemas (one subsection, short).** Chapter 1's \( P, Q \) were placeholders; a derivation written with them is a pattern whose every instance is a derivation. §04 proves the semantic counterpart; the syntactic statement is obvious rule by rule, since each rule is itself stated for arbitrary formulas — say so, and say it is used in the completeness proof.
- **Warning.** A parse tree is not a truth table: the tree is syntax; values come later. And the height counts nodes on the longest branch, not symbols.
- **Quick checks.** Draw the parse tree of \( (\neg p \vee q) \leftrightarrow \neg(r \wedge p) \) and list its subformulas; what is its height? (Answer: height 4; eight subformulas, \( p \) listed once although it occurs twice. Replaces \( \neg(p \to (q \vee \neg p)) \), a near copy of Huth & Ryan exercise 1.3.4(a).)
- **Exercises.** A1: formula or not (five strings), with the failing clause; height and subformulas. B1: parse trees for three formulas from Chapter 1's derivations. B2: translate two official formulas to shorthand and back. C1: how many parse trees does \( p \to q \to r \to s \) have if we forget the grouping convention? (5; list them; count via the Catalan recursion as a remark only.)

## 02-induction.md — Induction on Numbers and on Formulas

- **Hook.** We want to show that *every* formula has some property, but there are infinitely many. The formation sequence suggests the answer: prove it for the starting pieces and show each building step preserves it. This is induction, and it is the tool for everything in this chapter.
- **The principle.** `ax-induction`: if a property holds for 1, and whenever it holds for \( n \) it holds for \( n + 1 \), then it holds for every \( n \in \nN \). Explain its status (a basic property of ℕ taken as given; the optional ZF chapter proves it; Chapter 7 proves it equivalent to well-ordering — prose pointers). Paraphrase: the domino picture, and why it is **not** an infinite derivation (it is one rule licensing one conclusion).
- **First induction proof, worked in full:** \( 1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 3 + \dots + n(n + 1) = \tfrac{n(n + 1)(n + 2)}{3} \) (base \( 2 = 2 \); step: add \( (n + 1)(n + 2) \)). (Not \( 1 + 3 + \dots + (2n - 1) = n^2 \), which is Huth & Ryan exercise 1.4.7(a).) (Logic-flavoured examples wait until structural induction is available.) Show the base case, the inductive step and the conclusion, each labelled, and the rule-level reading: the step is a \( \to \)I box ("assume it for \( n \)").
- **`thm-strong-induction`** (course-of-values): if, for every \( n \), the property holding for all \( k < n \) implies it holds for \( n \), then it holds for all \( n \). Proof from `ax-induction` applied to "holds for every \( k \le n \)". Say why "no separate base case" is not a gap: for \( n = 1 \) there is no \( k < 1 \), so "the property holds for all \( k < 1 \)" is vacuously true (each instance "\( k < 1 \Rightarrow \dots \)" is a vacuous implication, `prp-vacuous-implication`; the rule for "every" itself is Chapter 4's, prose pointer), and the hypothesis hands us \( n = 1 \) outright.
- **`thm-structural-induction`:** if a property holds for every atom and for \( \bot \), and is preserved by each formation clause (F2), (F3), then every formula has it. Proof: given a formula, take a formation sequence ending in it (`def-formula`, (F4)); by strong induction on \( i \), the \( i \)-th entry has the property, since it is an atom or \( \bot \), or is built by (F2) or (F3) from **earlier** entries. (Not by induction on the height: see Induction, just in time.)
- **`lem-parenthesis-count`** (first structural induction, worked in full): (a) every formula has equally many left and right parentheses; (b) every initial segment of a formula has at least as many left as right parentheses, and **strictly** more if the segment is proper and non-empty and the formula has the form \( (\varphi \circ \psi) \); (c) every occurrence of a binary connective in a formula is preceded (within the formula) by strictly more left than right parentheses. Proof by structural induction. (S2: the strict clause alone is not inductive: for \( (\neg(p \wedge q) \wedge r) \) the step needs the inequality for the segment \( \neg(p \) of the \( \neg \)-formula, which the strict clause does not cover; and the depth-one argument below needs (c).)
- **`thm-unique-readability`:** every formula that is not an atom or \( \bot \) is of exactly one of the forms \( \neg\varphi \), \( (\varphi \circ \psi) \), with \( \circ \) and the immediate parts **uniquely** determined. Proof from `lem-parenthesis-count` (the main connective is the unique binary connective at parenthesis depth one: an occurrence inside \( \varphi \) or \( \psi \) has depth at least 2 by (c) plus the outer parenthesis; so \( (\varphi \circ \psi) = (\varphi' \circ' \psi') \) forces the two main connectives to the same position, hence \( \varphi = \varphi' \), \( \circ = \circ' \), \( \psi = \psi' \)). Keep the proof to about 12 lines.
- **`thm-structural-recursion`:** clauses giving a value for atoms and \( \bot \) and a rule for each connective define **exactly one** function on all formulas. Proof: strong induction on the number of symbols (the immediate parts are shorter), with unique readability supplying the uniqueness of the decomposition. Examples of recursive definitions: height, the set of atoms, the number of connectives.
- **Warnings.** The inductive step must work for **every** \( n \), including the first transition (a pointer: Chapter 7 has the famous failure at \( 1 \to 2 \); here give a small one of our own, not the all-equal/horses argument, which is Chapter 7's find-the-error item: "\( 2^n \ge n^2 \) for every \( n \)", with base \( n = 1 \) and the step \( 2^{n+1} = 2 \cdot 2^n \ge 2n^2 \ge (n + 1)^2 \), whose last inequality holds only for \( n \ge 3 \); the claim is false at \( n = 3 \) (\( 8 < 9 \)) and true again from \( n = 4 \) — keep it to four sentences). Structural induction must cover **every** clause, including \( \bot \) and \( \neg \).
- **Quick checks.** In the proof of `thm-strong-induction`, what is the property to which `ax-induction` is applied? Which clause of `def-formula` makes the \( \neg \) case necessary?
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** base case and step for three statements; which induction (ordinary, strong, structural) fits each.
  - **B1:** \( 2^n \ge n + 1 \) for \( n \in \nN \). **B2:** the number of subformulas of \( \varphi \) is at most the number of symbols of \( \varphi \) (structural). **B3:** every formula of height \( h \) has at most \( 2^{h} - 1 \) nodes in its parse tree.
  - **C1:** prove that a proper initial segment of a formula is never a formula (using `lem-parenthesis-count`), and deduce the uniqueness half of `thm-unique-readability` in a second way. **C2 (find the error, true conclusion):** the even-parentheses "proof" by strong induction on the number of symbols (see Scaffolding): atoms and \( \bot \) have none; for \( \neg\varphi \) remove the \( \neg \) and apply the hypothesis to \( \varphi \) (correct); for \( (\varphi \circ \psi) \) remove the **outer pair of parentheses** and apply the hypothesis to the shorter string \( \varphi \circ \psi \) (the error). The solution names that line (the hypothesis is about formulas, and \( \varphi \circ \psi \) is not one: \( p \wedge q \) from \( (p \wedge q) \)), gives a counterexample to the step (the same move applied to the property "no binary connective lies outside all parentheses", true of every formula, would assert it of \( p \wedge q \), where it is false), and repairs the proof by applying the hypothesis to \( \varphi \) and \( \psi \), or by structural induction. (S2: the earlier version, "remove the last symbol", admits no plausible one-error proof: after a final \( ) \) the count would be odd.)

## 03-valuations-and-truth-tables.md — Valuations and Truth Tables

*Migrates:* pilot §06 "Reading a table off the rules", `def-truth-table`, `exm-truth-table-modus-ponens`; pilot §06 exercises A1 and C2 ("the rows of implication are forced").

- **Hook (kept in spirit).** The pilot read each table off the rules; keep that subsection first, as the motivation for the clauses: \( \wedge \)I and \( \wedge \)E force the \( \wedge \) column, \( \bot \)E and `prp-vacuous-implication` force the vacuous rows of \( \to \).
- **Definitions.** `def-valuation`: an assignment of \( \mathrm{T} \) or \( \mathrm{F} \) to each atom. `def-truth-value`: the recursive extension \( v(\varphi) \), one clause per connective, with \( v(\bot) = \mathrm{F} \) — well defined by `thm-structural-recursion` (cite it: this is what §02 was for). `def-truth-table` (kept): the table lists \( v(\varphi) \) for every restriction of \( v \) to the atoms of \( \varphi \) — which is legitimate only by the next lemma.
- **`lem-coincidence`:** if \( v \) and \( w \) agree on \( \mathrm{atoms}(\varphi) \), then \( v(\varphi) = w(\varphi) \). Structural induction, **all cases written** (faded triple, full version). Consequence: a formula with \( n \) atoms has a table of \( 2^n \) rows, and that table determines its value under every valuation.
- **Worked examples.** `exm-truth-table-modus-ponens` (kept); a table built column by column for \( (p \to q) \leftrightarrow (\neg q \to \neg p) \); a formula with three atoms (8 rows) where a shortcut (find the only row that can make it F) saves work — the "work backwards from F" technique, named.
- **Warning.** A truth table checks propositional form only; it cannot see inside an atom ("every \( \varepsilon \) has an \( N \)" is not a row) — kept from the pilot; leads to Chapter 4.
- **Quick checks.** The value of \( (p \vee q) \to \neg r \) under a given valuation, computed down the parse tree. Why does `def-truth-table` need `lem-coincidence`?
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** fill in tables (kept, pilot A1).
  - **B1:** four tables with the "backwards from F" shortcut. **B2 (faded, last step blank):** the count "atom occurrences = binary connectives + 1" for formulas built from atoms with binary connectives only; the \( \to \) case blank; "*Name the move, then complete it.*"
  - **C1:** the rows of implication are forced (kept, pilot C2), now stated exactly: any truth function for \( \to \) that makes \( \to \)E and \( \to \)I truth-preserving is the standard one. Since \( \models \) arrives only in §04, the item states truth-preservation in rows: for \( \to \)E, "whenever \( \varphi \to \psi \) and \( \varphi \) are T in a row, so is \( \psi \)"; for \( \to \)I, "if every row making the formulas \( \Gamma \) and \( \varphi \) true makes \( \psi \) true, then every row making \( \Gamma \) true makes \( \varphi \to \psi \) true". Intended answer: \( \to \)E forces (T, F) ↦ F; \( \to \)I with \( \Gamma \) empty and \( \varphi = \psi = p \) forces (T, T) ↦ T and (F, F) ↦ T; with \( \Gamma = q \), \( \varphi = p \), \( \psi = q \) it forces (F, T) ↦ T. **C2 (find the error, true conclusion):** the all-T valuation claim (see Scaffolding); the solution names the \( \to \) case, the wrong reason, and a formula (\( p \to q \)) on which the stated reason is false though the conclusion holds.

## 04-entailment-and-equivalence.md — Tautologies, Entailment and Equivalence

*Migrates:* pilot §06 "Tautologies and equivalence" (`def-tautology`, `prp-standard-equivalences`), "Using tables to refute" (`def-valid-argument`, `exm-affirming-the-consequent`); pilot exercises B1–B3, C1 (C1, "two connectives are enough", moves to §07).

- **Definitions.** `def-tautology` (kept: tautology, contradiction), `def-satisfiable` (new), `def-valid-argument` (kept label): \( \Gamma \models \psi \) iff every valuation making all of \( \Gamma \) true makes \( \psi \) true; \( \models \psi \) iff \( \psi \) is a tautology. `def-semantic-equivalence`: \( \varphi \equiv \psi \) iff \( \varphi \models \psi \) and \( \psi \models \varphi \).
- **Paraphrase and degenerate cases.** \( \Gamma \) unsatisfiable \( \Rightarrow \) \( \Gamma \models \psi \) for every \( \psi \) (the semantic ex falso; compare `prp-ex-falso`); \( \models \psi \) with \( \Gamma \) empty. Relations: \( \psi \) tautology iff \( \neg\psi \) unsatisfiable; \( \Gamma \models \psi \) iff \( \Gamma, \neg\psi \) unsatisfiable (prove both; they feed §06 and §07).
- **Results.**
  - `prp-standard-equivalences` (kept; proved by tables): contrapositive, De Morgan, distributivity, \( p \to q \equiv \neg p \vee q \), double negation, \( \leftrightarrow \) as two implications, and **commutativity and associativity of \( \wedge \) and \( \vee \)** (S2: §07 needs these to write a conjunction or disjunction of many formulas without caring about order or grouping; say there that a many-term conjunction is grouped to the right, and that De Morgan extends to many terms by induction on the number of terms).
  - `lem-replacement`: if \( \varphi \equiv \varphi' \), then replacing an occurrence of \( \varphi \) as a subformula of \( \chi \) by \( \varphi' \) gives a formula equivalent to \( \chi \). Structural induction on \( \chi \).
  - `lem-substitution`: if \( \models \chi \) and each atom \( p_i \) is replaced **uniformly** by a formula \( \varphi_i \), the result is a tautology; likewise for \( \Gamma \models \psi \). Proof via the valuation \( w(p_i) \coloneqq v(\varphi_i) \) and structural induction. This is the semantic face of "Chapter 1's letters were placeholders".
- **Worked examples.** `exm-affirming-the-consequent` (kept; the refuting row \( p = \mathrm{F}, q = \mathrm{T} \), tied back to Argument B of Chapter 1 §01). A valid three-premise entailment checked by the backwards-from-F method. A non-equivalence: \( (p \to q) \to r \not\equiv p \to (q \to r) \) (pilot B3), with a row.
- **The two questions, side by side.** A small table: \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \) (exists a derivation; shown by exhibiting one; about syntax) vs \( \Gamma \models \psi \) (every row; checked by a table; about meaning). Say that the next two sections prove they coincide, and what each direction buys.
- **Warnings.** \( \models \) is not \( \to \): \( \Gamma \models \psi \) is a statement *about* formulas, \( \varphi \to \psi \) is a formula. (The relation between them, \( \varphi \models \psi \) iff \( \models \varphi \to \psi \), is a B exercise.) Replacement needs equivalence, not mere one-way entailment: replacing \( p \wedge q \) by \( p \) inside \( \neg(\cdot) \) fails (row \( p = \mathrm{T} \), \( q = \mathrm{F} \)). Substitution must be uniform: replacing only the first \( p \) of the tautology \( p \to p \) by \( q \) gives \( q \to p \).
- **Quick checks.** Is \( \{p, \neg p\} \models q \)? Is \( p \to q, \neg p \models \neg q \)? Give the row (\( p = \mathrm{F} \), \( q = \mathrm{T} \)). (Not \( p \vee q \models p \), Huth & Ryan's example after their Definition 1.34.)
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** tautology / satisfiable / contradiction for five formulas; \( \models \) or a refuting row for three sequents.
  - **B1:** valid or not (kept pilot B1: derive if valid — using Chapter 1 — or give a row). **B2:** \( p \leftrightarrow q \equiv (p \wedge q) \vee (\neg p \wedge \neg q) \) by table; say which theorem of this chapter will turn the table into derivations both ways (completeness, §06). (Replaces the pilot's "implication as a disjunction by table": that equivalence is already in `prp-standard-equivalences`, and it is Huth & Ryan exercise 1.4.1.) **B3:** \( \varphi \models \psi \) iff \( \models \varphi \to \psi \); and \( \Gamma, \varphi \models \psi \) iff \( \Gamma \models \varphi \to \psi \) (the semantic deduction theorem).
  - **C1 (faded, last half blank):** `lem-substitution` for a formula language extended by a new binary connective \( \mathbin{|} \) (NAND) with its table; only the atom case given. **C2:** is \( \equiv \) preserved by uniform substitution? Prove it or find a counterexample. (Answer: yes; \( \varphi \equiv \psi \) iff \( \models \varphi \leftrightarrow \psi \), then `lem-substitution`.)

## 05-soundness.md — Soundness

*Migrates:* pilot §06 "Why the table can be trusted" (`thm-soundness`, its Idea), now with a full proof; the pilot's closing remark on completeness becomes the frame of §06.

- **Hook.** Chapter 1's failed search for \( P \to Q \vdash Q \to P \). A row with \( p \to q \) true and \( q \to p \) false exists. Soundness is the theorem that turns this row into a proof of non-derivability.
- **Setting up the induction.** Recall from `def-derivation` (Chapter 1 §02) the **context** \( \Gamma_k \) of line \( k \): the premises and the assumptions of the boxes containing line \( k \). Two combinatorial facts about derivations, proved from the scope rule:
  - `lem-context-inclusion`: (a) if line \( i \) is available at line \( k \), then \( \Gamma_i \subseteq \Gamma_k \) (every box containing \( i \) contains \( k \)); (b) if a closed box from line \( a \) to line \( b \) is available at line \( k \), then \( \Gamma_b = \Gamma_k \) together with the assumption on line \( a \) (the box sits directly in the part containing \( k \), by the scope rule of `def-derivation`; and \( b \) lies in no further box inside it, because every box rule requires the box's last line to be a line of that box itself, as `def-rules-implication` and `def-rules-disjunction` say; check the same wording in `def-rules-negation`, `def-rule-raa` and `def-rules-biconditional`).
- **`thm-soundness`** (kept label; statement upgraded): if \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \) then \( \Gamma \models \psi \). **Key proof.**
  - *Idea with Step roadmap.* ① Prove the stronger claim \( \Gamma_k \models \varphi_k \) for **every** line \( k \) of **every** derivation, where \( \varphi_k \) is the formula on line \( k \). ② Strong induction on \( k \) (`thm-strong-induction`, course-of-values: the cited lines and boxes all come earlier). ③ Case by case on the justification of line \( k \): premises and assumptions (\( \varphi_k \in \Gamma_k \)); R and the one-step rules (\( \wedge \)I/E, \( \to \)E, \( \vee \)I, \( \neg \)E, \( \bot \)E, \( \leftrightarrow \)E: induction hypothesis on the cited lines, contexts included by `lem-context-inclusion` (a), then the truth-table clause); the box rules (\( \to \)I, \( \neg \)I, RAA, \( \vee \)E, \( \leftrightarrow \)I: induction hypothesis on each box's last line, whose context is \( \Gamma_k \) plus the assumption by (b), then the semantic step). ④ The last line of a derivation lies in no box, so its context is the premises. The theorem is proved for derivations in the **primitive** rules; a line citing a derived rule (MT, LEM, …) abbreviates a pasted derivation in primitive rules with its letters replaced by formulas (`def-derived-rule`; §01's schema remark), so \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \) is the same relation either way. Say this in one sentence before Step 1 (S2: otherwise a derivation using LEM, a line citing nothing, falls under no case).
  - Why course-of-values rather than ordinary induction: a rule may cite any earlier line, not just the previous one. Say this is the route; it differs from inducting on the length of the whole derivation by removing its last line, which would need to cut boxes open.
  - *Try it first* on the \( \to \)I case (ten minutes: "the box shows every row making \( \Gamma_k \) and \( \varphi \) true makes \( \psi \) true; what about rows where \( \varphi \) is false?").
  - The proof writes every case in full except those that repeat another's pattern exactly (\( \wedge \)E right after \( \wedge \)E left; \( \leftrightarrow \)E second form), which it names as skipped (STYLE §4: a symmetric half may be skipped).
  - *Before reading on* prompt at the RAA case: "where does this case use that \( v(\neg\varphi) \) is \( \mathrm{F} \) exactly when \( v(\varphi) \) is \( \mathrm{T} \)?"
- **`cor-refuting-row`:** if some valuation makes all of \( \Gamma \) true and \( \psi \) false, then \( \Gamma \nvdash \psi \). Pay Chapter 1's question: \( p \to q \nvdash q \to p \); affirming the consequent is not a rule.
- **`thm-raa-independent`** (**`.optional`**): \( \neg\neg p \vdash p \) and \( \vdash p \vee \neg p \) are **not** derivable from the primitive rules without RAA. Proof by the same induction with a **three-valued** semantics: values \( 0 < \tfrac12 < 1 \); \( \wedge = \min \), \( \vee = \max \), \( a \to b = 1 \) if \( a \le b \), else \( b \); \( \neg a = a \to 0 \); \( a \leftrightarrow b \) as the min of the two implications; \( \bot = 0 \); "\( \Gamma \models_3 \psi \)" means \( \min \Gamma \le v(\psi) \) for every \( v \), with \( \min \) of the empty list \( = 1 \). Every rule except RAA preserves this (the drafter checks each case, and a sidecar script checks all rules over all \( 3^k \) value combinations); at \( v(p) = \tfrac12 \) both \( \neg\neg p \to p \) and \( p \vee \neg p \) take value \( \tfrac12 \). Aftermath: RAA says something the other rules cannot; Chapter 5 §07 tells what, and who objected (prose pointer). **Nothing may cite this result** (STYLE §4).
- **Warnings.** Soundness goes one way: a formula true in the one row you checked is not thereby derivable. And a refuting row must make **every** premise true.
- **Quick checks.** Which case of the proof handles line 5 of the derivation of `prp-contrapositive`? Give a row showing \( p \to r \nvdash (p \vee q) \to r \) (\( p = \mathrm{F} \), \( q = \mathrm{T} \), \( r = \mathrm{F} \)). (Not \( p \vee q \nvdash p \wedge q \): Huth & Ryan exercise 1.4.13(a), and C1's row.)

## 06-completeness.md — Completeness

- **Hook.** Soundness says the rules prove nothing false. Could they be too weak — a tautology with no derivation? Completeness says no. Frame: the pilot's remark ("the converse also holds, with RAA among the rules") becomes a theorem.
- **Proof route (our presentation; Step roadmap in the Idea of `thm-completeness`).**
  - `lem-weakening-cut` (bookkeeping, proved by pasting derivations; `def-derived-rule` made exact): (a) if \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \) then \( \Gamma, \varphi \vdash \psi \); (b) if \( \Gamma \vdash \varphi \) and \( \Gamma, \varphi \vdash \psi \) then \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \).
  - `lem-connective-rows` — *the rules prove their own truth tables.* For all formulas \( \alpha, \beta \): \( \alpha, \beta \vdash \alpha \wedge \beta \); \( \neg\alpha \vdash \neg(\alpha \wedge \beta) \); \( \neg\beta \vdash \neg(\alpha \wedge \beta) \); \( \alpha \vdash \alpha \vee \beta \); \( \beta \vdash \alpha \vee \beta \); \( \neg\alpha, \neg\beta \vdash \neg(\alpha \vee \beta) \); \( \neg\alpha \vdash \alpha \to \beta \); \( \beta \vdash \alpha \to \beta \); \( \alpha, \neg\beta \vdash \neg(\alpha \to \beta) \); the four \( \leftrightarrow \) rows; \( \alpha \vdash \neg\neg\alpha \); \( \vdash \neg\bot \). Each row of each table corresponds to one sequent whose premises are **only the parts it needs**, each as itself or negated (not "literals", which §07 reserves for atoms and their negations). Present as a table: row of the truth table → sequent → the Chapter 1 result or two-line derivation that gives it. **Remark:** none of these needs RAA (check each); RAA enters the completeness proof only in the next-but-one lemma. This is the chapter's exact version of the pilot's slogan "truth tables summarize the rules".
  - `lem-truth-table-line` (**key proof**): let \( p_1, \dots, p_n \) include all atoms of \( \varphi \) and let \( v \) be a valuation. Then \( p_1^{v}, \dots, p_n^{v} \vdash \varphi^{v} \). Structural induction on \( \varphi \): atoms (one line), \( \bot \) (\( \neg\bot \) from `lem-connective-rows`), \( \neg \) (two subcases), binary connectives (four value combinations each, but by `lem-connective-rows` only the needed parts' induction hypotheses are called, then cut by `lem-weakening-cut`). Write the \( \to \) and \( \wedge \) cases in full; the others "as for \( \to \)", with the table of `lem-connective-rows` making it checkable.
  - `lem-atom-splitting`: if \( \Delta, p \vdash \psi \) and \( \Delta, \neg p \vdash \psi \), then \( \Delta \vdash \psi \). Proof: LEM (`prp-excluded-middle`) and \( \vee \)E, with the two derivations pasted into the two boxes. *This is where RAA enters.* *Before reading on* prompt.
  - `thm-completeness`: if \( \Gamma \) is a finite list and \( \Gamma \models \psi \), then \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \). Step roadmap: ① let \( p_1, \dots, p_n \) be the atoms of \( \Gamma \) and \( \psi \); ② **Claim:** for every valuation \( v \), \( \Gamma, p_1^{v}, \dots, p_n^{v} \vdash \psi \) — if \( v \) makes all of \( \Gamma \) true, then \( v(\psi) = \mathrm{T} \) and `lem-truth-table-line` gives \( \psi \); if \( v \) makes some \( \gamma \in \Gamma \) false, it gives \( \neg\gamma \), and \( \neg \)E with \( \bot \)E give \( \psi \); ③ remove the signed atoms one at a time by `lem-atom-splitting`, by induction on the number of atoms removed (formally: for \( j = n, n-1, \dots, 0 \), \( \Gamma, p_1^{v}, \dots, p_j^{v} \vdash \psi \) for every \( v \); the step from \( j \) to \( j - 1 \) applies the case \( j \) to the two valuations that agree with \( v \) except that \( p_j \) is T in one and F in the other. Since ℕ starts at 1, induct on \( m = n - j + 1 \) from 1 to \( n + 1 \). The case \( n = 0 \), no atoms, is the Claim itself.) This route avoids converting \( \Gamma \models \psi \) into one long nested implication; say so in one sentence as the reason for the choice.
  - `cor-derivable-iff-valid`: \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \iff \Gamma \models \psi \) (finite \( \Gamma \)), and so derivability is **decidable** by a finite table check. Remark (prose only): if \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \) is extended to an infinite \( \Gamma \) as "\( \psi \) is derivable from finitely many members of \( \Gamma \)" (a derivation has finitely many premises), the theorem still holds (compactness), not proved here.
- **Worked example.** Run the proof on \( \models (p \to q) \vee (q \to p) \) (compare Chapter 1 §07 C2): the four signed-atom derivations, then the splittings: two on \( q \) (one for each value of \( p \)) and one on \( p \), three uses of `lem-atom-splitting` in two rounds. Show the resulting derivation's outline only (it is long), and say that the proof is a **method**, not a recommendation: a search as in Chapter 1 §07 finds shorter derivations.
- **Aftermath.** Two ways to settle any propositional question: search for a derivation, or check a table; soundness and completeness say they always agree. Point out which half used which rule: completeness needed LEM (hence RAA) exactly once, in `lem-atom-splitting` — cf. the optional `thm-raa-independent` (prose pointer only; nothing cites it).
- **Warnings.** The proof produces derivations of size roughly \( 2^n \) times the formula; completeness is not efficiency. The statement is for finite \( \Gamma \) here.
- **Quick checks.** For \( \varphi = p \to \neg q \) and \( v(p) = \mathrm{T}, v(q) = \mathrm{T} \), what sequent does `lem-truth-table-line` provide? Which row of `lem-connective-rows` is used? (Answer: \( p, q \vdash \neg(p \to \neg q) \); the row \( \alpha, \neg\beta \vdash \neg(\alpha \to \beta) \) with \( \beta = \neg q \), fed by the \( \neg \) case's \( q \vdash \neg\neg q \).)
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** comprehension parts on `lem-truth-table-line` and `thm-completeness`: which rows of `lem-connective-rows` the \( \wedge \) case calls when \( v(\alpha) = \mathrm{F}, v(\beta) = \mathrm{T} \); where RAA is used in the whole completeness proof; the idea of the proof in two sentences; which step fails if the premises are allowed to be infinitely many.
  - **B1:** derive three rows of `lem-connective-rows` not written in the text (e.g. the \( \leftrightarrow \) ones). **B2:** apply the method to \( p, p \to q \models q \) and write the derivation it produces. **B3:** decide by table, then derive by search: \( \neg(p \leftrightarrow q) \vdash \neg p \leftrightarrow q \) (valid; needs RAA for \( \neg p \to q \); 12 lines if two rows of `lem-connective-rows` are cited as derived rules, 22 in primitive rules, so the item says rows may be cited).
  - **C1:** the \( \vee \) and \( \leftrightarrow \) cases of `lem-truth-table-line` in full. **C2 [C2: Exit check: completeness]:** prove `lem-atom-splitting` and step ③ of `thm-completeness` in full. **Marking scheme** (10): pastes both derivations into \( \vee \)E boxes with correct availability (3); LEM cited as a derived rule (1); induction on the number of atoms set up with the right statement (3); the Claim applied to every valuation, not one (2); conclusion (1). Fatal: splitting on an atom for a single valuation only; forgetting that the Claim must hold for all \( 2^n \) valuations before splitting.

## 07-normal-forms.md — Normal Forms and Adequate Connectives

*Migrates:* pilot §06 exercise C1 ("two connectives are enough"), generalized.

- **Hook.** The proof of completeness read a formula off its truth table line by line. Doing the same without derivations gives every formula a standard shape.
- **Definitions.** `def-literal` (an atom or its negation), clause and conjunct (minor), `def-dnf`, `def-cnf`. `def-truth-function` (a table with \( n \) inputs), `def-adequate` (a set of connectives that expresses every truth function).
- **Results.**
  - `thm-dnf`: every formula is equivalent to one in DNF; route: one conjunction of signed atoms per T-row (the \( p^{v} \) of §06), disjoined; the empty disjunction convention for a contradiction (\( \bot \), or \( p \wedge \neg p \) for any atom \( p \)); a formula with no atoms (built from \( \bot \) alone) is equivalent to \( p \vee \neg p \) or to \( p \wedge \neg p \). `def-truth-function` takes \( n \ge 1 \) inputs.
  - `thm-cnf`: every formula is equivalent to one in CNF; route: DNF of \( \neg\varphi \), negate, push \( \neg \) inward by De Morgan and double negation (`prp-standard-equivalences`, `lem-replacement`). A second route by rewriting (eliminate \( \to, \leftrightarrow \); push \( \neg \) in; distribute \( \vee \) over \( \wedge \)) as an "Alternatively," paragraph, with the warning that distribution can blow up the size.
  - `prp-cnf-validity` (**Try it first**): a CNF is a tautology iff every clause contains some atom together with its negation. Dually a DNF is satisfiable iff some conjunct contains no such pair.
  - `thm-adequate-connectives`: \( \{\neg, \wedge\} \), \( \{\neg, \vee\} \), \( \{\to, \bot\} \) are adequate (the construction in the proof of `thm-dnf`, applied to any table, gives \( \{\neg, \wedge, \vee\} \); then rewrite; write the statement our way: Huth & Ryan exercise 1.5.3(a)–(b) has the same sets and the same all-T hint); \( \{\wedge, \vee, \to\} \) is not (the all-T valuation — the §03 C2 fact, now proved correctly).
- **Worked examples.** CNF and DNF of \( (p \to q) \to (\neg q \to r) \) by both routes (it is \( \equiv p \vee q \vee r \): the table route gives a DNF of seven conjuncts and a CNF of one clause, the rewriting route reaches \( p \vee q \vee r \) in a few lines; use this contrast); validity of a four-clause CNF read off at a glance.
- **Warnings.** A CNF is not unique; equivalence, not identity. The table route needs \( 2^n \) rows; the rewriting route can explode on distribution.
- **Quick checks.** Which of three formulas are in CNF? Is \( (p \vee \neg p \vee q) \wedge (q \vee \neg r) \) a tautology?
- **Exercises.**

(Replaces \( \{\neg, \leftrightarrow\} \), which is Huth & Ryan exercise 1.5.3(c).) **C3 [C3: Exit check: deciding sequents]:** for four sequents, decide \( \vdash \) or \( \nvdash \): derive or give a refuting row, citing soundness or completeness as appropriate.
