# Blueprint: Chapter 3 — Predicate Logic and the Quantifier Rules

**Directory:** `src/ch03-predicate-logic/` (slug `predicate-logic`). **Part 0, the third of four logic chapters**, main line. Level reference: Huth & Ryan §2.1–2.4 (the need for predicates, terms and formulas, free and bound variables, substitution, the quantifier and equality rules, quantifier equivalences, models and semantic entailment), **depth and coverage only**. Not covered: undecidability, expressiveness, second-order logic, micromodels. No example, formula list or exercise of theirs is reused; our examples come from analysis and arithmetic.

**Purpose.**
- Analysis lives in quantifiers: "for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) there is \( N \) such that for every \( n \ge N \) …". Propositional logic cannot see inside such a sentence (Chapter 2 §03's warning). This chapter extends the language (terms, predicates, quantifiers), the calculus (\( \forall \)I, \( \forall \)E, \( \exists \)I, \( \exists \)E, =I, =E, with their side conditions) and the semantics (models), and proves the extended calculus sound.
- Its practical goals are Stage 0's (curriculum (a) and (e)): formalize, negate and **order** quantifiers; know exactly why a \( \delta \) may not depend on an \( x \) introduced later (the eigenvariable condition), and why a witness obtained by \( \exists \)E may not be treated as arbitrary (the fresh-name condition).
- Its centrepiece is decision 2's worked example: "uniformly continuous \( \Rightarrow \) continuous" as a full derivation, and the rule-by-rule reason the converse fails.
- Rhetoric is sketched only ("let \( x \) be arbitrary", "choose \( N \) such that") in each section's "What the rules sound like" paragraph; the full dictionary and the prose register are Chapter 4.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`):
- **Let \( x \) be arbitrary** (\( \forall \)I): a fresh name, about which nothing is assumed.
- **Name the witness** (\( \exists \)E): "choose \( x_0 \) with …", and never generalize over it.
- **Fix, obtain, choose** (the analysis pattern): \( \forall \)I, then \( \forall \)E and \( \exists \)E on the hypothesis, then \( \exists \)I with a term built from what is available.
- **Negate quantifier by quantifier.** Push \( \neg \) inward, flipping \( \forall \leftrightarrow \exists \) and keeping bounds.
- **Refute with a model.** A structure in which the premises are true and the conclusion false.
- **Read the dependency off the order.** A variable may depend on the variables quantified to its left, and only on them.

**Allowed prerequisites.** Chapters 1–2. School arithmetic and order facts about ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ in examples, as premises (in derivations they enter as premise lines or as a justification "arith"). Membership \( x \in A \) is used as a binary predicate; say that Chapter 5 develops sets (prose pointer). Functions appear as function symbols (\( f(x) \), \( x^2 \), \( \abs{x} \)); say that Chapter 5 defines functions as sets (prose pointer). No `@ref` beyond Chapters 1–2.

## 01-predicates-and-quantifiers.md — Predicates, Quantifiers and Translation

- **Hook.** An argument that is obviously correct and that propositional logic calls invalid: "every positive real has a square root; 2 is positive; so 2 has a square root" becomes \( p, q \therefore r \) with three unrelated atoms, refuted by a row. The missing structure is *inside* the atoms.
- **Informal definitions** (made exact in §02): predicate (a statement with holes), variable, domain of discourse, the quantifiers \( \forall \) and \( \exists \), with readings.
- **`def-bounded-quantifier`:** \( \forall x \in A\ \varphi \) abbreviates \( \forall x (x \in A \to \varphi) \); \( \exists x \in A\ \varphi \) abbreviates \( \exists x (x \in A \wedge \varphi) \); likewise \( \forall \varepsilon > 0 \), \( \exists n \ge N \). Explain **why** \( \to \) under \( \forall \) and \( \wedge \) under \( \exists \) (the other choice gives nonsense: \( \forall x (x \in A \wedge \varphi) \) says everything is in \( A \)).
- **Translation, worked** (`exm-translate-limit`): "\( a_n \to L \)", "\( A \) is bounded above", "\( f \) is continuous at \( a \)", "there is no largest prime", "every integer greater than 1 has a prime divisor" — each to symbols, with the domain stated. Analysis sentences written in symbols *after* their prose (STYLE §10).
- **Quantifier order** (`exm-quantifier-order-sum`, the curriculum's example): \( \forall x \exists y\, (x + y = 0) \) true on ℝ, \( \exists y \forall x\, (x + y = 0) \) false; read each aloud as a game (the second player may see the first move). The dependence of \( y \) on \( x \) is the whole difference.
- **Warnings.** "Any" is ambiguous in English ("if any \( x \) satisfies…" is \( \exists \); "any \( x \) satisfies…" is \( \forall \)). Hidden universal quantifiers: a theorem stated "if \( n \) is even then \( n^2 \) is even" means \( \forall n \) (Chapter 4 names this convention). An existential with \( \to \) inside is almost always a mistranslation: \( \exists x (x \in A \to \varphi) \) is true whenever something lies outside \( A \).
- **Quick checks.** Swap the quantifiers in "for every \( n \) there is a prime larger than \( n \)"; is the result true? (STYLE §10 swap check.)
- **What the rules sound like:** none yet; one sentence pointing to §03–§04.
- **Exercises.** A1: translate five sentences; spot the mistranslation in three. B1: formalize "\( A \) has a maximum", "\( f \) is increasing", "\( (a_n) \) is bounded" with domains stated. B2: for each of four ordered-quantifier sentences over ℕ, ℤ or ℝ, decide true or false with a witness or a reason. B3 (example generation): a predicate \( R(x, y) \) on ℕ such that \( \forall x \exists y\, R \) is true and \( \exists y \forall x\, R \) is false; check both. C1: translate "\( f \) is uniformly continuous on \( A \)" and "\( f \) is continuous at every point of \( A \)", and describe in one sentence the only difference. C2: how many essentially different statements arise from \( \forall x \exists y \forall z \) by permuting the three quantifier prefixes over one fixed matrix? Give a matrix over ℤ that separates two of them.

## 02-terms-formulas-substitution.md — Terms, Formulas, Free Variables and Substitution

- **Hook.** "\( x > 2 \)" is not a statement (Chapter 1's pilot non-example); "\( \forall x\, (x^2 \ge 0) \)" is; "\( \forall x\, (x > y) \)" depends on \( y \). The difference is which variables are **free**. And "\( \forall y\, (y \ne x) \)", read with \( y \) put for \( x \), becomes false for a silly reason: **capture**.
- **Definitions** (inductive, as in Chapter 2 §01; structural induction and recursion carry over — say so, cite `thm-structural-induction` and `thm-structural-recursion` for the pattern, and note the new clauses):
  - `def-term`: variables, constants, \( f(t_1, \dots, t_n) \).
  - `def-first-order-formula`: atomic \( R(t_1, \dots, t_n) \) and \( t_1 = t_2 \), \( \bot \), connectives, \( \forall x\, \varphi \), \( \exists x\, \varphi \). Parse trees with quantifier nodes.
  - `def-free-variable` (by recursion; a quantifier node binds its variable in its subtree), bound occurrence, scope. `def-sentence` (no free variables).
  - `def-substitution` \( \varphi[t/x] \): replace the **free** occurrences of \( x \). `def-free-for`: \( t \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \) if no variable of \( t \) becomes bound. Renaming bound variables (\( \forall y\, \varphi \) and \( \forall z\, \varphi[z/y] \) for fresh \( z \)) as the standard way out; stated, used, and its semantic justification promised for §08 (prose pointer within the chapter).
- **Worked examples.** Free and bound occurrences in \( \forall \varepsilon (\varepsilon > 0 \to \exists N \forall n (n \ge N \to \abs{a_n - L} < \varepsilon)) \): which variables are free (\( L \), and \( a \) as a function symbol)? Substitutions that are fine and one that captures: \( (\exists y\, (y > x))[y + 1 / x] \).
- **Warnings.** The same letter may occur free and bound in one formula (\( P(x) \wedge \forall x\, Q(x) \)); legal, but the book renames to avoid it. Capture changes meaning: exhibit it.
- **Quick checks.** Is \( x \) free in \( \forall y\, (x < y) \to \exists x\, (x = 0) \)? Where? Is \( y + 1 \) free for \( x \) in \( \forall z\, (x < z) \)?
- **Exercises.** A1: free/bound marking for four formulas; sentences or not. B1: compute five substitutions, two of which need renaming first. B2: define the set of free variables by recursion (the clauses), then check it on two formulas. C1: prove by structural induction that if \( x \) is not free in \( \varphi \) then \( \varphi[t/x] = \varphi \). C2: show that \( \forall x \exists y\, (x < y) \) and \( \forall y \exists x\, (y < x) \) are the same sentence up to renaming, while \( \forall x \exists y\, (x < y) \) and \( \exists y \forall x\, (x < y) \) are not.

## 03-universal-rules.md — For Every: Let x Be Arbitrary

- **Hook.** To prove \( x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0 \) for every real \( x \), we cannot check every \( x \). We take one we know **nothing** about and prove it for that one. The rule makes "nothing" precise.
- **`def-rules-universal`.**
  - \( \forall \)E: from \( \forall x\, \varphi \), infer \( \varphi[t/x] \) for any term \( t \) free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \).
  - \( \forall \)I: open a box flagged with a **fresh** name \( x_0 \) (occurring in no premise, no open assumption, and not in the conclusion \( \forall x\, \varphi \)); if \( \varphi[x_0/x] \) is reached as a line of that box, close it and infer \( \forall x\, \varphi \). Extend `def-derivation`'s availability rule to flagged boxes: nothing changes except the side condition (state this as one paragraph added to the Chapter 1 definition, not a new definition).
  - Paraphrase: "fresh" is the formal version of "arbitrary". The box keeps the name from leaking.
- **Derivations to show:** \( \forall x (P(x) \to Q(x)), \forall x\, P(x) \vdash \forall x\, Q(x) \); `prp-forall-and`: \( \forall x (P(x) \wedge Q(x)) \dashv\vdash \forall x\, P(x) \wedge \forall x\, Q(x) \); \( \forall x \forall y\, R(x, y) \vdash \forall y \forall x\, R(x, y) \); and a one-sentence foreshadowing of `prp-forall-implies-exists`, which needs \( \exists \)I and opens §04. Analysis example written both ways: "for every real \( x \), \( x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0 \)" as a derivation with the arithmetic fact \( \forall y\, (y^2 \ge 0) \) as a premise and \( \forall \)E at the term \( x_0 - 1 \).
- **Vacuous truth for \( \forall \)** (subsection): \( \forall x \in \emptyset\ \varphi \) is true for every \( \varphi \), from `prp-vacuous-implication`. This pays the Chapter 10 pilot's pointer ("Chapter 1 explains why a statement with no instances counts as true" — now this section; see the overview's renumbering list). Every real number is an upper bound of \( \emptyset \).
- **Warnings (catalogue entries previewed; the full catalogue is Chapter 4 §06).** Checking instances does not prove \( \forall \) (N1). Generalizing over a name that occurs in an open assumption: from the assumption \( x_0 > 0 \) one may not conclude \( \forall x\, (x > 0) \) — show the derivation and the flagged line.
- **Quick checks.** In a given derivation, may line 6 apply \( \forall \)I to \( y_0 \)? (It occurs in the premise on line 1.)
- **What the rules sound like:** "Let \( x \in \nR \)" / "Fix \( \varepsilon > 0 \)" (\( \forall \)I); "In particular, for \( x = 3 \)…" / "applying the hypothesis to \( x_0 - 1 \)" (\( \forall \)E); Lean `intro x`, `h a` / `specialize`.
- **Exercises.** A1: which lines of three derivations violate the side condition; rule names. B1: derive \( \forall x (P(x) \to Q(x)), \forall x (Q(x) \to R(x)) \vdash \forall x (P(x) \to R(x)) \). B2: derive \( \forall x\, P(x) \vee \forall x\, Q(x) \vdash \forall x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \), and say why the converse search stalls (refuted in §08). B3: a real-number \( \forall \) proof as a derivation with arithmetic premises (for every real \( x \), \( \abs{x} \ge x \), using the cases of Chapter 1 §03). C1: show that the side condition "not in the conclusion" is needed, by a derivation that violates only it and "proves" a false sentence over ℕ. C2 (**find the error, true conclusion**): the instance check (see Scaffolding); the solution names \( \forall \)I, points to the missing fresh-name box, and gives a *different* polynomial for which checking \( x = 0, 1, 2 \) succeeds and the conclusion fails (e.g. \( x(x - 1)(x - 2) \ge 0 \) fails at \( x = -1 \)).

## 04-existential-rules.md — There Exists: Name a Witness

- **Hook.** Two moves: to **prove** "there is an \( N \)", exhibit one; to **use** "there is a \( k \) with \( n = 2k \)", give it a name and forget where it came from. Chapter 1's evenness arguments did both informally; here they become rules (pay Chapter 1's signposts).
- **`def-rules-existential`.**
  - \( \exists \)I: from \( \varphi[t/x] \), infer \( \exists x\, \varphi \) (\( t \) free for \( x \)).
  - \( \exists \)E: from \( \exists x\, \varphi \) and a box flagged with a fresh \( x_0 \), opening with the assumption \( \varphi[x_0/x] \) and ending with \( \chi \), infer \( \chi \), provided \( x_0 \) does not occur in \( \chi \), in any premise or open assumption.
  - Paraphrase: the box says "whatever the witness is, \( \chi \) follows"; so \( \chi \) must not mention it.
- **`prp-forall-implies-exists`** (opening the section): \( \forall x\, P(x) \vdash \exists x\, P(x) \); remark: this is correct because domains are **non-empty** (§08). Contrast with the bounded case \( \forall x \in \emptyset \) (true) vs \( \exists x \in \emptyset \) (false) — no conflict, since the bound turns into \( \to \) and \( \wedge \).
- **`prp-exists-forall-swap`** (key proof; faded triple full version): \( \exists x \forall y\, R(x, y) \vdash \forall y \exists x\, R(x, y) \), with the "fix, obtain, choose" order visible. **Converse attempt**: the search for \( \forall y \exists x\, R \vdash \exists x \forall y\, R \) stalls exactly at a side condition — show the partial derivation and the line that would break \( \exists \)E or \( \forall \)I. Its refutation by a model is §08 (`exm-counter-model-swap`).
- **Derivations to show:** `prp-exists-and`: \( \exists x (P(x) \wedge Q(x)) \vdash \exists x\, P(x) \wedge \exists x\, Q(x) \) (not conversely); \( \exists x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \dashv\vdash \exists x\, P(x) \vee \exists x\, Q(x) \); \( \forall x (P(x) \to Q(x)), \exists x\, P(x) \vdash \exists x\, Q(x) \); the arithmetic example "the sum of two even integers is even" as a derivation (two \( \exists \)E boxes, one \( \exists \)I at the term \( k_0 + m_0 \)).
- **Warnings.** Treating the witness as arbitrary (N3; show a "proof" that every integer is even from "some integer is even"). Two \( \exists \)E's giving the same name to different witnesses ("let \( n = 2k \) and \( m = 2k \)"): the second name must be fresh — the common error in parity proofs.
- **Quick checks.** In the sum-of-evens derivation, why must the second witness be \( m_0 \), not \( k_0 \)? Is \( \exists x\, P(x) \wedge \exists x\, Q(x) \vdash \exists x (P(x) \wedge Q(x)) \)? (No — say where the search breaks; model in §08.)
- **What the rules sound like:** "Take \( N = 7 \)" / "\( 2k + 1 \) will do" (\( \exists \)I); "Choose \( k \) with \( n = 2k \)" / "let \( x_0 \) be such a point" (\( \exists \)E); Lean `use`, `obtain ⟨x, hx⟩`.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1:** comprehension parts on `prp-exists-forall-swap`: why \( y_0 \) must be introduced before \( \exists \)E is applied (or after — say which orders work); where the side condition of \( \exists \)E is checked; which line breaks in the converse attempt.
  - **B1:** derive \( \neg\exists x\, P(x) \vdash \forall x\, \neg P(x) \) (preview of §05). **B2 (faded, last step blank):** as in Scaffolding. **B3:** "the product of two odd integers is odd" as a derivation, then as three sentences of prose.
  - **C1:** \( \exists x \forall y\, R(x, y), \forall x \forall y (R(x, y) \to R(y, x)) \vdash \forall y \exists x\, R(y, x) \). **C2 (find the error, true conclusion):** see Scaffolding.

## 05-quantifier-laws.md — Negating and Moving Quantifiers

- **Hook.** To prove that a sequence does **not** converge, or that a function is **not** uniformly continuous, we need the negation in a usable form: \( \neg \) pushed all the way in. Mechanical — once the laws are proved.
- **Results with proof routes.**
  - `prp-quantifier-negation` (key proof; **Try it first** on (a)): (a) \( \neg\forall x\, \varphi \vdash \exists x\, \neg\varphi \) — **needs RAA** (the Idea: no witness is visible; assume \( \neg\exists x\, \neg\varphi \) and derive \( \forall x\, \varphi \)); (b) \( \exists x\, \neg\varphi \vdash \neg\forall x\, \varphi \); (c) \( \neg\exists x\, \varphi \dashv\vdash \forall x\, \neg\varphi \) (both without RAA). State which direction is classical; pay the Chapter 10 pilot's pointer ("that law is derived there with RAA").
  - `prp-bounded-quantifier-negation`: \( \neg\forall x \in A\ \varphi \dashv\vdash \exists x \in A\ \neg\varphi \), \( \neg\exists x \in A\ \varphi \dashv\vdash \forall x \in A\ \neg\varphi \): **the bound stays** (\( \neg \forall \varepsilon > 0 \) becomes \( \exists \varepsilon > 0 \), never \( \exists \varepsilon \le 0 \)).
  - `prp-quantifier-distribution`: \( \forall \) over \( \wedge \) (`prp-forall-and`), \( \exists \) over \( \vee \); the one-way laws \( \forall x\, \varphi \vee \forall x\, \psi \vdash \forall x (\varphi \vee \psi) \) and `prp-exists-and`, converses refuted in §08.
  - `prp-quantifier-passage` (\( x \) not free in \( \psi \)): \( \forall x (\psi \to \varphi) \dashv\vdash \psi \to \forall x\, \varphi \); \( \forall x (\varphi \to \psi) \dashv\vdash \exists x\, \varphi \to \psi \); \( \exists x (\varphi \wedge \psi) \dashv\vdash \exists x\, \varphi \wedge \psi \); \( \exists x (\varphi \to \psi) \dashv\vdash \forall x\, \varphi \to \psi \) (the \( \vdash \) direction from right to left needs RAA; say so). The side condition's role, with a failure when \( x \) is free in \( \psi \).
  - `prp-arbitrarily-close-equal`: for real \( a, b \), if \( \abs{a - b} < \varepsilon \) for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \), then \( a = b \) (old Chapter 2 "must prove"). Proof: RAA; negate; \( \varepsilon \coloneqq \abs{a - b} \) — the first "choose \( \varepsilon \) from the data" of the book. Arithmetic facts about \( \abs{\cdot} \) as school algebra, pointer to Chapter 10.
- **The negation procedure** (an `::: {.algorithm}` block): rewrite \( \to \) and \( \leftrightarrow \) if needed; push \( \neg \) through quantifiers (bounded ones keep their bounds) and connectives (De Morgan, \( \neg(\varphi \to \psi) \equiv \varphi \wedge \neg\psi \), double negation); stop at atoms; replace \( \neg(a < b) \) by \( a \ge b \) in ordered domains. Each step cites the law.
- **Worked examples.** Negate: "\( a_n \to L \)"; "\( s = \sup A \)" (in the two-clause form); "\( f \) is uniformly continuous on \( A \)"; "\( f_n \to f \) uniformly on \( A \)" (curriculum exit items — one of them is worked here, the other is the exit check).
- **Warnings.** Negating "\( \forall \varepsilon > 0 \)" as "\( \forall \varepsilon \le 0 \)" (bound negated instead of the quantifier). Negating \( \varphi \to \psi \) as \( \neg\varphi \to \neg\psi \) (N-entry pointer to Chapter 4 §06).
- **Quick checks.** Negate "every non-empty bounded set has a supremum" (with the bound conditions kept as hypotheses).
- **Exercises.**

## 06-equality-and-uniqueness.md — Equality and Unique Existence

- **Hook.** Every calculation \( a = b = c \) is a chain of substitutions; every "the" (the limit, the supremum) hides a uniqueness proof.
- **`def-rules-equality`.** =I: \( t = t \) (no citations). =E: from \( t_1 = t_2 \) and \( \varphi[t_1/x] \), infer \( \varphi[t_2/x] \) (both terms free for \( x \)).
- **`prp-equality-symmetric-transitive`:** derivations of symmetry and transitivity from =I and =E (the choice of \( \varphi \) in each is the point; show it).
- **Calculation chains.** A chain \( t_1 = t_2 = \dots = t_n \) is repeated =E with transitivity; a mixed chain with \( \le \) needs one monotonicity fact per link (say that Chapter 10's ordered-field rules supply them).
- **`def-unique-existence`:** \( \exists! x\, \varphi \) abbreviates \( \exists x (\varphi \wedge \forall y (\varphi[y/x] \to y = x)) \). `prp-unique-existence-use`: it is equivalent to "\( \exists x\, \varphi \)" together with "\( \forall x \forall y (\varphi \wedge \varphi[y/x] \to x = y) \)"; the second is what "uniqueness" proofs prove.
- **Worked examples.** Uniqueness of the additive inverse in a field (from the field axioms as premises, stated as school algebra; Chapter 10 has them as axioms); "the" maximum of a set, if it exists (two maxima are each \( \le \) the other). The proof pattern: "suppose \( y \) also satisfies … then \( y = x \)".
- **Warnings.** Proving uniqueness is not proving existence (and vice versa). Substituting into a position that is not a free occurrence (\( \forall x\, (x = x) \) with \( t_1 = x \)).
- **Quick checks.** Which formula \( \varphi \) makes =E yield symmetry from \( s = t \) and \( s = s \)?
- **What the rules sound like:** "substituting \( x = 2 \)"; "by the uniqueness of …"; Lean `rw`, `calc`.
- **Exercises.** A1: rules in a calc chain. B1: in a group-like structure (premises given), identity element is unique. B2: \( \exists! x\, (x^2 = 0) \) over ℝ, both halves. ## 08-models.md — Models and Counter-models

- **Hook.** §04 and §05 promised that some searches must fail. As in Chapter 2, proving non-derivability needs meaning — now a domain of objects rather than a row of T and F.
- **Definitions.** `def-structure` (model): a **non-empty** domain \( D \); an element for each constant, a function \( D^n \to D \) for each function symbol, a set of \( n \)-tuples (a relation) for each predicate symbol; = is interpreted as identity. `def-assignment` \( \ell \) (values for variables) and \( \ell[x \mapsto d] \). The value of a term (recursion). `def-satisfaction` \( \mathfrak{M}, \ell \models \varphi \), clause by clause (Tarski), with \( \forall \) and \( \exists \) via \( \ell[x \mapsto d] \). For sentences \( \ell \) does not matter (`lem-free-coincidence`, proved in §09 — **order problem:** state and prove `lem-free-coincidence` here, since truth of sentences needs it; §09 uses it). `def-first-order-entailment` \( \Gamma \models \varphi \): every model and assignment satisfying \( \Gamma \) satisfies \( \varphi \).
- **Why non-empty.** `prp-forall-implies-exists` is derivable; in an empty domain it would fail. The book's convention: domains are non-empty; bounded quantifiers handle empty sets.
- **Worked examples.**
  - `exm-counter-model-swap`: \( \forall y \exists x\, R(x, y) \not\models \exists x \forall y\, R(x, y) \) with \( D = \nZ \), \( R(x, y) \) as \( x > y \) — and a two-element model found by hand, to show finite models suffice.
  - Refute the converses of `prp-exists-and` and of the \( \forall \)/\( \vee \) law with small finite models.
  - A model of the sentence "\( < \) is a strict order with no largest element" that is not ℕ (ℚ, or \( \nZ \)).
- **Counter-models are proofs of non-derivability** — but only once soundness is proved (§09). In this section say "\( \not\models \)"; §09 turns each into "\( \nvdash \)".
- **Warnings.** The same sentence can be true in one model and false in another: "\( \forall x \exists y\, (x < y) \)" in ℕ vs in \( \{1, 2, 3\} \). A model must interpret **every** symbol. A "counter-model" that falsifies a premise refutes nothing.
- **Quick checks.** Evaluate \( \forall x \exists y\, (y \cdot y = x) \) in ℝ, in \( [0, \infty) \), in ℂ (with \( \cdot \) the usual products).
- **Exercises.** A1: truth of four sentences in three models. B1: counter-models for three one-way laws of §05. B2 (example generation): a model where \( \forall x \exists y\, R(x, y) \) and \( \forall x\, \neg R(x, x) \) hold and \( D \) has three elements; check. B3: show \( \forall x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \not\models \forall x\, P(x) \vee \forall x\, Q(x) \). C1: a sentence true in every finite model but false in some infinite one (e.g. "if \( f \) is injective then \( f \) is surjective"; state it in the language with one function symbol).
