- Apostol's skeleton: the chapter follows Apostol ch. 6 in breadth and order (monotone functions → bounded variation → total variation → additivity → the variation function → Jordan → continuity → paths → arc length → change of parameter), and closes on the Cantor staircase that pays the AN1.4 hook.

- **No footnote jokes, no historical-anecdote footnotes, no first person** ("we" of the mathematical community in proofs is fine, as in AN1.1; no "I", no persona).

**Purpose.**
- A graph can be continuous and still infinitely long: \( x\sin(1/x) \) on \( [0,1] \) wiggles so often that its inscribed polygons have unbounded length. The chapter names the property that rules this out (**bounded variation**), measures it (**total variation**), and shows that it is exactly "difference of two increasing functions" (**Jordan**).
- It transfers the idea to paths in \( \mathbb{R}^n \): a path is **rectifiable** iff each coordinate has bounded variation; arc length is additive, continuous in the endpoint, independent of the parameter, and is the limit of polygonal lengths as the mesh shrinks.
- It is the definitional base for AN2.10 (Stieltjes integrals with BV integrators), for CA.1 (contour integrals over rectifiable paths and the length estimate), for HA (the Dirichlet–Jordan test) and for MT (BV, absolute continuity, differentiation).
- It does **not** integrate. The Riemann integral is the next chapter (AN1.8). The formulas \( V_a^b(f)=\int_a^b\lvert f'\rvert \) and \( L(\gamma)=\int_a^b\lVert\gamma'\rVert \) for \( C^1 \) data are **promised to AN2.10** and stated in this chapter only as forward pointers in prose.

## Chapter hook and where it is paid

- **Opened (index.md and §01):** AN1.4 built the Cantor set \( C \): closed, uncountable, and the open intervals removed to make it have total length 1. On the removed intervals put a staircase: value 1/2 on the middle third, 1/4 and 3/4 on the next two, and so on. Filling in the points of \( C \) gives an increasing function \( \kappa \) that climbs from 0 to 1, yet is flat on intervals of total length 1. **Question: how long is its graph?** A horizontal run of length 1 and a climb of height 1 suggest anything from \( \sqrt2 \) to 2. The chapter does not yet have the word "length" for a curve that is not a polygon.
- **Paid (§06, `thm-cantor-graph-length`):** the graph is a rectifiable path of length exactly **2**: as long as the "staircase" path that goes right 1 and up 1 with no diagonal at all. All of the climb happens over \( C \).
- **In-chapter hook:** §01 shows that the polygons inscribed in the graph of \( x\sin(1/x) \) have unbounded length; §05 (`cor-graph-rectifiable`) turns this into "the graph is not rectifiable", using the definition built in between.
- **Hooks opened here, paid later (prose pointers, not dependencies):**
  - AN2.10: \( V_a^b(f)=\int_a^b\lvert f'\rvert \) and \( L(\gamma)=\int_a^b\lVert\gamma'\rVert \) for \( C^1 \) data; the Stieltjes integral \( \int_0^1 d\kappa = 1 \) carried entirely by \( C \).
  - MT (differentiation chapter, MT.9–MT.10): \( \kappa'=0 \) off \( C \), yet \( \kappa \) is not constant; the length formula \( \int\sqrt{1+\kappa'^2} \) "sees" only 1 of the 2. What is missing is absolute continuity.
  - AN2.15: the gallery collects \( \kappa \), \( x^2\sin(1/x^2) \) and the non-rectifiable space-filling curve.

**Named moves** (index.md):
1. **Insert a point, then split.** Adding a point to a partition never decreases a variation sum or a polygonal length (triangle inequality), so to bound a sup over partitions of \( [a,c] \) one may assume the partition contains any chosen point \( b \), and then split at \( b \). This is the chapter's **one new move**; it gets the faded triple.
2. **A witness partition.** To show unbounded variation, choose partition points at peaks and troughs; to bound a length from below, choose one good partition.
3. **Coordinates squeeze the norm.** \( \lvert x_j\rvert\le\lVert x\rVert\le\sum_j\lvert x_j\rvert \) transfers every one-variable statement about variation to paths in \( \mathbb{R}^n \).
4. **Subtract the accumulated variation.** \( v_f-f \) is increasing because the variation so far absorbs every decrease of \( f \).
5. **Every fine partition is nearly as good as one good partition.** Uniform continuity bounds what adding the finitely many points of a chosen partition can gain.

## 01-variation-sums.md — Wiggling, partitions and variation sums (≤ 3,000)

- **Hook.** The Cantor-staircase question, stated with a picture (TikZ: stages 1–3 of the staircase). Promise the answer in §06.
- **Brief Review box (AN1.5, monotone functions).** An increasing \( f \) on \( [a,b] \) has one-sided limits at every point, every discontinuity is a jump, and there are at most countably many. Cite the AN1.5 labels; no proofs.
- **Partitions.** `def-partition` (a **finite** set containing \( a \) and \( b \), listed increasingly), `def-refinement` (\( P\subseteq Q \)), common refinement \( P\cup Q \). Minor definitions: STYLE steps 4, 7, 8 only. `def-mesh` is introduced in §05, where it is first used. Note in one sentence that AN1.8 reuses these.
- **The polygon in a graph.** For \( f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R} \) and \( P \), the polygon through \( (x_k,f(x_k)) \) has length \( \sum_k\sqrt{(\Delta x_k)^2+(\Delta f_k)^2} \), and
  \[ \max\Bigl(b-a,\ \textstyle\sum_k\lvert\Delta f_k\rvert\Bigr)\ \le\ \sum_k\sqrt{(\Delta x_k)^2+(\Delta f_k)^2}\ \le\ (b-a)+\sum_k\lvert\Delta f_k\rvert . \]
  So the horizontal part is harmless; the polygons stay bounded exactly when the sums \( \sum\lvert\Delta f_k\rvert \) do. This motivates `def-variation-sum` (\( \operatorname{Var}(f;P) \)). The display is proved in one line (\( \max(\lvert u\rvert,\lvert w\rvert)\le\sqrt{u^2+w^2}\le\lvert u\rvert+\lvert w\rvert \)).
- **`lem-variation-refinement`.** If \( P\subseteq Q \) then \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le\operatorname{Var}(f;Q) \). Proof: by induction on \( \lvert Q\setminus P\rvert \), adding one point \( y\in(x_{k-1},x_k) \) replaces \( \lvert f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})\rvert \) by \( \lvert f(y)-f(x_{k-1})\rvert+\lvert f(x_k)-f(y)\rvert \), which is at least as large by the triangle inequality. First statement of move 1 ("insert a point"); name it here.
- **Failure: `exm-xsin-unbounded-variation`.** \( f(x)=x\sin(1/x) \), \( f(0)=0 \), on \( [0,1] \): continuous (AN1.5; sin from the F4.7 borrowed box), and \( \{\operatorname{Var}(f;P)\} \) is unbounded.
  - **Scratch Work** (first witness-partition argument): where is \( \lvert\sin(1/x)\rvert=1 \)? At \( 1/x=(k+\tfrac12)\pi \). There \( f=\pm x \) with alternating signs, so consecutive differences are sums, not differences.
  - **Proof.** Let \( t_k=1/((k+\frac12)\pi) \) for \( k\ge1 \); then \( f(t_k)=(-1)^kt_k \), and \( 0<t_N<\dots<t_1<1 \). With \( P=\{0,t_N,\dots,t_1,1\} \), \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\ge\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(t_k+t_{k+1})\ge\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}t_k\ge\frac1\pi\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac1{k+1} \), which is unbounded in \( N \).
  - **Brief Review box (harmonic sums):** \( \sum_{k=1}^{2^m}\frac1k\ge1+\frac m2 \) by grouping \( 2^{j-1}<k\le2^j \) (each block contributes \( \ge\frac12 \)); finite sums only, no series. Cite AN1.2 instead if it carries this (coordinator notes).
  - Consequence in words: the polygons inscribed in this continuous graph have unbounded length. Whether the graph "has infinite length" needs a definition of length: §05.
- **`exm-dirichlet-not-bv` (short).** \( 1_{\mathbb{Q}} \) on \( [0,1] \): alternate rational and irrational points (density, AN1.1); \( m \) intervals give \( \operatorname{Var}=m \).
- **Warning.** Two partitions need not be comparable (\( \{0,\frac12,1\} \) and \( \{0,\frac13,1\} \)); compare each with \( P\cup Q \).
- **Quick check.** For an increasing \( f \), what is \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P) \)? Answer: \( f(b)-f(a) \) for every \( P \) (telescoping, every term non-negative).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** \( f(x)=\lvert x\rvert \) on \( [-2,1] \), \( P=\{-2,-1,0,1\} \): compute \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P) \). *Answer:* \( 1+1+1=3 \).
  - **A2.** Which of \( \{0,\frac14,\frac12,1\} \), \( \{0,\frac13,1\} \), \( \{0,\frac12,\frac34,1\} \) refine \( \{0,\frac12,1\} \)? *Answer:* the first and third.
  - **B1.** Prove \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\ge\lvert f(b)-f(a)\rvert \) for every \( P \), with equality for monotone \( f \). Give a non-monotone \( f \) with equality for some \( P \). *Answer:* telescoping plus the triangle inequality; \( f(x)=x^2 \) on \( [-1,1] \), \( P=\{-1,1\} \) gives \( 0=\lvert f(1)-f(-1)\rvert \).
  - **B2.** \( g(x)=x^2\sin(1/x^2) \), \( g(0)=0 \), on \( [0,1] \) (`exm-x2sin-inv-sq-not-bv`): show \( g \) is differentiable at every point of \( [0,1] \) (one-sided at the ends) and that its variation sums are unbounded. *Answer:* \( \lvert g(h)/h\rvert\le\lvert h\rvert \) gives \( g'(0)=0 \); witness points \( x_k=((k+\frac12)\pi)^{-1/2} \), \( g(x_k)=(-1)^k/((k+\frac12)\pi) \), and the same harmonic estimate as the text.
  - **C1.** Let \( f \) be increasing on \( [a,b] \), discontinuous at \( c_1<\dots<c_m \) in \( (a,b) \). Show \( \sum_i\bigl(f(c_i^+)-f(c_i^-)\bigr)\le f(b)-f(a) \). Deduce that for each \( n\ge1 \) there are fewer than \( n(f(b)-f(a))+1 \) jumps of size \( \ge 1/n \). *Answer:* choose \( a\le u_1<c_1<w_1\le u_2<c_2<\dots<c_m<w_m\le b \). Since \( f \) is increasing, \( f(c_i^+)-f(c_i^-)\le f(w_i)-f(u_i) \), and \( \sum_i(f(w_i)-f(u_i))\le f(b)-f(a) \) because the intervals \( [u_i,w_i] \) do not overlap and \( f \) increases across the gaps between them. If there were \( N \) jumps of size \( \ge1/n \), then \( N/n\le f(b)-f(a) \). The solution says this reproves AN1.5's countability (pointer).
  - **C2.** Find \( f \) continuous on \( [0,1] \), with bounded variation sums on \( [\delta,1] \) for every \( \delta>0 \), but unbounded sums on \( [0,1] \). *Answer:* `exm-xsin-unbounded-variation`; on \( [\delta,1] \) it has a bounded derivative (\( \lvert f'\rvert\le1+1/\delta \)) and §02's `cor-bounded-derivative-bv` applies; for this exercise, a direct MVT bound on each term suffices.

## 02-bounded-variation.md — Functions of bounded variation (≤ 3,500)

- **Situate.** §01 found continuous functions whose variation sums are unbounded. Name the functions for which they are not.
- **`def-bounded-variation`** (major notion of the chapter: full explain template, all eleven steps).
  - Hook: the naive notion that fails is "continuous, so the graph has finite length"; §01's \( x\sin(1/x) \) shown failing.
  - Slogan: *the value of \( f \) travels only a finite total distance up and down.*
  - Definition: \( f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R} \) is **of bounded variation** if **there exists** \( M \) such that \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le M \) **for every** partition \( P \). In symbols: \( \exists M\ \forall P\in\mathcal{P}[a,b]:\ \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le M \).
  - Examples: monotone (`thm-monotone-bv`), Lipschitz (`thm-lipschitz-bv`), a step function with one jump; degenerate: a constant (all sums 0).
  - Non-example by minimal change: \( x^2\sin(1/x) \) is BV (`exm-x2sin-bv`), \( x\sin(1/x) \) is not: one factor of \( x \) less.
  - Why this definition: dropping "for every \( P \)" to "for each \( P \) some \( M \)" says nothing (Quick check below).
  - Warning, payoff: below.
- **`thm-monotone-bv`.** A monotone \( f \) on \( [a,b] \) is BV, and \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)=\lvert f(b)-f(a)\rvert \) for every \( P \). (§01 Quick check.)
- **`thm-lipschitz-bv`.** If \( \lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\le K\lvert x-y\rvert \) on \( [a,b] \), then \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le K(b-a) \) for every \( P \).
- **`cor-bounded-derivative-bv`.** If \( f \) is continuous on \( [a,b] \), differentiable on \( (a,b) \) and \( \lvert f'\rvert\le K \) there, then \( f \) is BV with bound \( K(b-a) \). Route: AN1.7 MVT gives the Lipschitz bound.
- **`exm-x2sin-bv`.** \( f(x)=x^2\sin(1/x) \), \( f(0)=0 \): \( f'(x)=2x\sin(1/x)-\cos(1/x) \) for \( x\ne0 \), \( f'(0)=0 \), \( \lvert f'\rvert\le3 \) on \( [0,1] \); BV with bound 3. It is not monotone on any \( [0,\delta] \) (§05 C3 proves this); BV does not mean "piecewise monotone".
- **`thm-bv-bounded`.** BV on \( [a,b] \) ⇒ bounded: \( \lvert f(x)\rvert\le\lvert f(a)\rvert+M \). Route: the partition \( \{a,x,b\} \) (or \( \{a,b\} \) if \( x\in\{a,b\} \)). Corollary in words: \( 1/x \) (with any value at 0) is not BV on \( [0,1] \).
- **`thm-bv-algebra`.** If \( f,g \) are BV on \( [a,b] \) and \( c\in\mathbb{R} \), then \( f+g \), \( cf \), \( fg \) are BV; if moreover \( \lvert f(x)\rvert\ge m>0 \) for all \( x \), then \( 1/f \) is BV. Bounds: \( \operatorname{Var}(fg;P)\le A\operatorname{Var}(g;P)+B\operatorname{Var}(f;P) \) with \( A=\sup\lvert f\rvert \), \( B=\sup\lvert g\rvert \) (finite by `thm-bv-bounded`); \( \operatorname{Var}(1/f;P)\le m^{-2}\operatorname{Var}(f;P) \). Proof: termwise, from \( f(y)g(y)-f(x)g(x)=f(y)(g(y)-g(x))+g(x)(f(y)-f(x)) \) and \( \frac1{f(y)}-\frac1{f(x)}=\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{f(x)f(y)} \).
  - Remark: \( \mathrm{BV}[a,b] \) is a vector space and closed under products (an algebra).
- **`thm-lipschitz-composition-bv`.** If \( f \) is BV on \( [a,b] \) and \( g \) is Lipschitz with constant \( K \) on an interval containing \( f([a,b]) \), then \( g\circ f \) is BV with bound \( K\cdot M \). Examples: \( \lvert f\rvert \), \( \max(f,0) \), \( f^2 \) (\( g(y)=y^2 \) is Lipschitz on the bounded range).
- **Weird example `exm-sqrt-composition-not-bv`.** \( h(x)=x^2\sin^2(1/x) \), \( h(0)=0 \), is BV on \( [0,1] \) (\( h'(x)=2x\sin^2(1/x)-\sin(2/x) \), \( \lvert h'\rvert\le3 \), \( h'(0)=0 \)), and \( \sqrt{\phantom{x}} \) is increasing, hence BV on \( [0,1] \); but \( \sqrt{h(x)}=\lvert x\sin(1/x)\rvert \) is not BV (same witness points as §01, now the values are \( t_k \) at peaks and 0 at \( 1/(k\pi) \)). The outer function \( \sqrt{\phantom{x}} \) is not Lipschitz near 0, which is exactly the hypothesis of `thm-lipschitz-composition-bv` that fails.
- **Weird example `exm-step-infinitely-many-jumps`.** \( f(0)=0 \), \( f(x)=1/n \) for \( x\in(1/(n+1),1/n] \), \( n\ge1 \). Increasing, so BV (\( \operatorname{Var}=1 \) for every \( P \)), with a jump at every \( 1/n \), \( n\ge2 \): BV allows infinitely many discontinuities.
- **Warning.** "BV" and "continuous" are independent: a step function is BV and discontinuous; \( x\sin(1/x) \) is continuous and not BV. Also "differentiable" does not give BV (`exm-x2sin-inv-sq-not-bv`, §01 B2): the derivative must be **bounded**.
- **Quick check (quantifier swap, STYLE §10).** Compare "\( \exists M\ \forall P \): \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le M \)" with "\( \forall P\ \exists M \): \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le M \)". *Answer:* the second holds for every function (each sum is a finite number), so it carries no information.
- **Exercises.**

## 03-total-variation.md — Total variation (≤ 3,500)

- **Lead-in.** A bound \( M \) is not unique; the best one is a sup.
- **`def-total-variation`.** For \( f \) BV on \( [a,b] \), \( V_a^b(f)=\sup\{\operatorname{Var}(f;P):P\in\mathcal{P}[a,b]\} \); it exists (non-empty, bounded above; AN1.1). Conventions: \( V_a^a(f)=0 \); for \( f \) not BV write \( V_a^b(f)=\infty \) as notation only.
  - Examples: monotone: \( \lvert f(b)-f(a)\rvert \); `exm-point-indicator-variation`: \( 1_{\{c\}} \) with \( a<c<b \) has \( V_a^b=2 \) (upper bound: at most two non-zero terms, each \( \le1 \)); `exm-step-infinitely-many-jumps`: 1.
- **`thm-variation-additive`** (Apostol 6.11; key proof 1). Let \( a<c<b \). Then \( f \) is BV on \( [a,b] \) iff it is BV on \( [a,c] \) and on \( [c,b] \), and then \( V_a^b(f)=V_a^c(f)+V_c^b(f) \).
  - *Try it first (ten minutes): one inequality is easy. Which one, and what stops the other?*
  - **Scratch Work** (first appearance of move 1): a partition \( P \) of \( [a,b] \) need not contain \( c \), so it does not split. But \( P\cup\{c\} \) does split, and by `lem-variation-refinement` its sum is at least as big. For "≥", glue a partition of \( [a,c] \) to one of \( [c,b] \).
  - **Proof.** (≤) For \( P\in\mathcal{P}[a,b] \), \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le\operatorname{Var}(f;P\cup\{c\})=\operatorname{Var}(f;P_1)+\operatorname{Var}(f;P_2)\le V_a^c+V_c^b \), with \( P_1=(P\cup\{c\})\cap[a,c] \), \( P_2 \) likewise; take the sup. (≥) For \( P_1\in\mathcal{P}[a,c] \), \( P_2\in\mathcal{P}[c,b] \), \( P_1\cup P_2\in\mathcal{P}[a,b] \) and \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P_1)+\operatorname{Var}(f;P_2)=\operatorname{Var}(f;P_1\cup P_2)\le V_a^b \); take sups over \( P_1 \), then \( P_2 \) (name: equality by two inequalities, from AN1.1). The BV equivalence comes out of the same two estimates.
  - Name move 1 formally here; faded triple instance 1.
- **`cor-variation-subinterval`.** \( [c,d]\subseteq[a,b] \) ⇒ \( V_c^d(f)\le V_a^b(f) \); a BV function is BV on every subinterval.
- **`thm-piecewise-monotone-variation`.** If \( a=c_0<\dots<c_m=b \) and \( f \) is monotone on each \( [c_{i-1},c_i] \), then \( V_a^b(f)=\sum_i\lvert f(c_i)-f(c_{i-1})\rvert \). Route: `thm-variation-additive` by induction plus `thm-monotone-bv`. Worked example: \( x^3-3x \) on \( [-2,2] \) gives \( 4+4+4=12 \) (pieces at \( \pm1 \)), against the crude 36 of §02 A2.
- **`thm-variation-seminorm`.** For BV \( f,g \) and \( c\in\mathbb{R} \): \( V_a^b(f+g)\le V_a^b(f)+V_a^b(g) \); \( V_a^b(cf)=\lvert c\rvert V_a^b(f) \); \( V_a^b(f)=0 \) iff \( f \) is constant. Remark: \( \lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BV}}=\lvert f(a)\rvert+V_a^b(f) \) is a norm on \( \mathrm{BV}[a,b] \) (C exercise); it reappears in MT and FA.
- **`def-variation-function`, `thm-variation-function-increasing`.** \( v_f(x)=V_a^x(f) \). Then \( v_f \) is increasing, and for \( a\le x<y\le b \): \( v_f(y)-v_f(x)=V_x^y(f)\ge\lvert f(y)-f(x)\rvert \). Route: additivity with \( c=x \) and the partition \( \{x,y\} \).
- **Weird example (table row).** \( x^2\sin(1/x) \): \( V_0^1\le3 \) is finite, yet no finite list of monotone pieces exists, so `thm-piecewise-monotone-variation` cannot compute it; the total variation of a BV function need not be a finite sum of rises and falls.
- **Warning.** \( V_a^b(f)\ne\lvert f(b)-f(a)\rvert \) in general; and \( V(fg)\ne V(f)V(g) \) (take \( f=g=x \) on \( [-1,1] \): \( V(f)V(g)=4 \), \( V(x^2)=2 \)).
- **Quick check.** For \( 1_{\{1/2\}} \) on \( [0,1] \), compute \( V_0^{1/2} \), \( V_{1/2}^1 \), \( V_0^1 \). *Answer:* 1, 1, 2, as additivity demands.
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Compute \( V \): \( \lvert x\rvert \) on \( [-2,1] \); \( x^2 \) on \( [-1,2] \); \( 1_{\mathbb{Z}} \) on \( [0,\frac52] \). *Answer:* 3; \( 1+4=5 \); 5.
  - **A2.** Explain why the partition \( \{-2,-1,1,2\} \) already achieves \( V_{-2}^2(x^3-3x) \). *Answer:* it contains every turning point; refining it adds only monotone stretches, which telescope.
  - **B1.** Prove the first claim of `thm-variation-seminorm`, and find \( f,g \) with strict inequality. *Answer:* \( f(x)=x \), \( g(x)=-x \): \( V(f+g)=0<2(b-a) \).
  - **B2.** Prove \( V_a^b(f)=0 \) iff \( f \) is constant.
  - **B3.** Suppose \( f \) is BV on \( [a,c] \) for every \( c<b \) and \( S=\sup_{c<b}V_a^c(f)<\infty \). Show \( f \) is BV on \( [a,b] \). *Answer:* for \( P\in\mathcal{P}[a,b] \) with last interior point \( x_{m-1} \), \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\le S+\lvert f(b)-f(x_{m-1})\rvert\le 2S+\lvert f(a)\rvert+\lvert f(b)\rvert \), using \( \lvert f(x)\rvert\le\lvert f(a)\rvert+S \) on \( [a,x_{m-1}] \) (`thm-bv-bounded`).
  - **C1 (exit check).** Prove `thm-variation-additive` in full. **Marking scheme** (10): 2 for reducing "≤" to partitions containing \( c \) and citing the refinement lemma; 2 for splitting \( P\cup\{c\} \) and bounding each half by its sup; 2 for the "≥" gluing; 2 for taking the two sups in a legitimate order (fix \( P_2 \), sup over \( P_1 \), then sup over \( P_2 \)); 2 for the BV equivalence. Fatal: assuming every partition of \( [a,b] \) contains \( c \); taking "sup of a sum = sum of sups" without the two-step argument.
  - **C2.** Show \( V_a^b(\max(f,g))\le V_a^b(f)+V_a^b(g) \). *Answer:* \( \max(f,g)=\frac12(f+g+\lvert f-g\rvert) \), \( V(\lvert h\rvert)\le V(h) \) (`thm-lipschitz-composition-bv` with \( K=1 \), or directly), then `thm-variation-seminorm`.
  - **C3.** Show \( \lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BV}}=\lvert f(a)\rvert+V_a^b(f) \) is a norm on \( \mathrm{BV}[a,b] \) and \( \sup\lvert f\rvert\le\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BV}} \). *Answer:* triangle inequality from `thm-variation-seminorm`; definiteness: \( \lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{BV}}=0 \) ⇒ constant with \( f(a)=0 \); the sup bound from `thm-bv-bounded`'s route.

## 04-jordan-decomposition.md — The Jordan decomposition and continuity (≤ 4,000)

- **Lead-in.** Monotone ⇒ BV, and differences of BV functions are BV, so every difference of two increasing functions is BV. The converse is the structure theorem.
- **`thm-jordan-decomposition`** (Apostol 6.13). \( f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R} \) is BV iff \( f=g-h \) with \( g,h \) increasing on \( [a,b] \). One may take \( g=v_f \), \( h=v_f-f \); and \( g,h \) can be chosen **strictly** increasing (add \( x \) to both).
  - Idea (move 4, named): \( v_f \) has already paid for every drop of \( f \), so subtracting \( f \) leaves something that never drops.
  - Proof: (⇐) `thm-monotone-bv`, `thm-bv-algebra`. (⇒) For \( x<y \), \( (v_f-f)(y)-(v_f-f)(x)=V_x^y(f)-(f(y)-f(x))\ge0 \) by `thm-variation-function-increasing`.
- **`def-positive-negative-variation`.** \( v_f^{+}=\frac12(v_f+f-f(a)) \), \( v_f^{-}=\frac12(v_f-f+f(a)) \). Then \( v_f^{\pm} \) are increasing, vanish at \( a \), \( f=f(a)+v_f^{+}-v_f^{-} \), \( v_f=v_f^{+}+v_f^{-} \). Paraphrase: total rise and total fall so far. (Pugh §6.10's formulation; Apostol uses only \( v_f \).)
- **`thm-jordan-minimal`.** If \( f=g-h \) with \( g,h \) increasing, then for \( a\le x<y\le b \): \( v_f^{+}(y)-v_f^{+}(x)\le g(y)-g(x) \) and \( v_f^{-}(y)-v_f^{-}(x)\le h(y)-h(x) \). Proof: \( V_x^y(f)\le(g(y)-g(x))+(h(y)-h(x)) \) termwise on any partition of \( [x,y] \), and \( f(y)-f(x)=(g(y)-g(x))-(h(y)-h(x)) \); average the two. Remark: the decomposition is never unique (add any increasing function to both); \( (v_f^+,v_f^-) \) is the least one.
- **`cor-bv-one-sided-limits`.** A BV function has one-sided limits at every point of \( [a,b] \) (one-sided at the ends); every discontinuity is of the first kind (removable or a jump, in AN1.5's classification); there are at most countably many. Route: Jordan, AN1.5 for each of \( g,h \), F2.3 (union of two countable sets).
  - Consequence in words: the Dirichlet function, \( \sin(1/x) \) and anything with a discontinuity of the second kind is not BV.
  - Forward pointer (prose): monotone functions are Riemann integrable (AN1.8), hence so are BV functions; AN1.8 records this as a remark citing this corollary's route.
- **`thm-variation-continuity`** (Apostol 6.14; key proof 2). Let \( f \) be BV on \( [a,b] \) and \( c\in[a,b] \). Then \( v_f \) is right-continuous at \( c \) (for \( c<b \)) iff \( f \) is, and left-continuous at \( c \) (for \( c>a \)) iff \( f \) is. In particular \( v_f \) is continuous at \( c \) iff \( f \) is.
  - **Scratch Work.** (⇒) is the inequality \( \lvert f(x)-f(c)\rvert\le\lvert v_f(x)-v_f(c)\rvert \). For (⇐), right side: we need \( V_c^{x}(f) \) small for \( x \) just right of \( c \). Pick a partition of \( [c,b] \) whose sum is within ε of \( V_c^b \), refine it so its first point \( x_1 \) is within δ of \( c \). Then the first term is \( <ε \), the rest is \( \le V_{x_1}^b \), so \( V_c^{x_1}=V_c^b-V_{x_1}^b<2ε \). Monotonicity of \( v_f \) handles every \( x\in(c,x_1] \).
  - **Proof** (right side; the left side is symmetric, say so). Quantifier order on the page: fix \( \varepsilon>0 \); δ from right-continuity of \( f \) (depends on ε); \( P \) from the sup (depends on ε); \( x_1\in(c,c+\delta) \) inserted into \( P \) (depends on both, legitimate by `lem-variation-refinement`). Conclude \( 0\le v_f(x)-v_f(c)\le V_c^{x_1}(f)<2\varepsilon \) for \( c<x\le x_1 \).
  - Aftermath: the jump of \( v_f \) at \( c \) equals the jump of \( \lvert f\rvert \)-type quantity: \( v_f(c^+)-v_f(c)=\lvert f(c^+)-f(c)\rvert \) (C3, not used later).
- **`cor-continuous-bv-jordan`.** A continuous BV function is the difference of two continuous (even strictly) increasing functions: \( v_f+x \) and \( v_f-f+x \). This is the form AN2.10 and CA.1 use.
- **Warning.** \( v_f \) continuous does **not** follow from "\( f \) continuous" unless \( f \) is BV (\( x\sin(1/x) \) has \( v_f\equiv\infty \) on \( (0,1] \)); and a Jordan pair for a continuous \( f \) need not be continuous: for \( a<c<b \), \( g=v_f+1_{(c,b]} \) and \( h=g-f \) are increasing with \( f=g-h \), and both jump at \( c \). Continuity of the pair is a choice, made in the corollary.
- **Quick check.** For \( f(x)=-x \) on \( [0,1] \), find \( v_f \), \( v_f^{+} \), \( v_f^{-} \). *Answer:* \( x \), 0, \( x \).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Write \( x^2 \) on \( [-1,1] \) as a difference of increasing functions; compute \( v_f^{\pm} \). *Answer:* \( g=x^2 \) on \( [0,1] \), \( 0 \) on \( [-1,0] \); \( h=-x^2 \) on \( [-1,0] \), \( 0 \) on \( [0,1] \). \( v_f^{+}=g \), \( v_f^{-}=h+1 \) (i.e. \( 1-x^2 \) on \( [-1,0] \), 1 on \( [0,1] \)).
  - **A2.** Where is \( v_f \) discontinuous for `exm-step-infinitely-many-jumps`? *Answer:* at each \( 1/n \), \( n\ge2 \) (right-discontinuous), by `thm-variation-continuity`.
  - **B1.** Prove `cor-bv-one-sided-limits` in full.
  - **B2.** Prove that \( g,h \) can be taken strictly increasing, and continuous if \( f \) is.
  - **B3 (faded triple, instance 2: last step blank).** Define \( p(f;P)=\sum_k\max(f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1}),0) \) and \( p_a^b(f)=\sup_Pp(f;P) \). Show \( p_a^b=p_a^c+p_c^b \) for \( a<c<b \). The solution's text is given through "\( p(f;P)\le p(f;P\cup\{c\})= p(f;P_1)+p(f;P_2) \)"; the reader supplies the gluing half. *Name the move, then complete it.* (Needs the refinement inequality for \( p \): \( \max(u+w,0)\le\max(u,0)+\max(w,0) \); state it in the problem.)

  - **C1 (exit check).** Prove `thm-variation-continuity` (right side). **Marking scheme** (10): 2 for (⇒) via \( \lvert f(x)-f(c)\rvert\le v_f(x)-v_f(c) \); 2 for δ from continuity with dependence on ε stated; 2 for a near-optimal partition of \( [c,b] \) refined by a point \( x_1\in(c,c+\delta) \), citing the refinement lemma; 2 for \( V_c^{x_1}=V_c^b-V_{x_1}^b \) via additivity; 2 for extending from \( x_1 \) to all \( x\in(c,x_1] \) by monotonicity. Fatal: bounding \( V_c^x \) by a single term; δ depending on \( x \).
  - **C2 (find the error; true conclusion).** "Claim: if \( f \) is continuous and BV on \( [a,b] \), then \( v_f \) is continuous. *Proof:* \( v_f(x)-v_f(c)=V_c^x(f)=\sup_P\sum_k\lvert\Delta_kf\rvert\le\sup_P\, m_P\cdot\max_k\lvert\Delta_kf\rvert \), and \( \max_k\lvert\Delta_kf\rvert\to0 \) as \( x\to c^+ \) by continuity, so \( v_f(x)-v_f(c)\to0 \)." *Answer:* the conclusion is true (`thm-variation-continuity`); the illegal step is the last one: \( m_P \) (the number of intervals) is unbounded over \( P \), so the sup of the products need not tend to 0. ## 05-rectifiable-paths.md — Paths, rectifiability and arc length (≤ 5,000)

- **Situate.** Return to the graph of §01 and to curves in the plane and space. A curve is given by a parametrization; its length should be the sup of inscribed polygons.
- **`def-path`.** A **path** in \( \mathbb{R}^n \) is a continuous \( \gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^n \); its **trace** is \( \gamma([a,b]) \); coordinates \( \gamma=(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_n) \). Continuity: AN1.5/AN1.6 componentwise, norm from F4.8. (Apostol 6.9.)
- **`def-polygonal-length`, `lem-polygonal-refinement`.** \( \Lambda(\gamma;P) \); refinement does not decrease it (triangle inequality in \( \mathbb{R}^n \), F4.8). Move 1 again, one sentence.
- **`def-rectifiable`** (major notion: full template, compact). \( \gamma \) is **rectifiable** if \( \{\Lambda(\gamma;P)\} \) is bounded above; then its **length** is \( L(\gamma)=\sup_P\Lambda(\gamma;P) \). Slogan: *length is what every inscribed polygon falls short of.* Examples: a segment \( \gamma(t)=p+t(q-p) \) on \( [0,1] \), \( L=\lVert q-p\rVert \) (every polygon is the segment); a polygonal path; the graph of \( x^2 \). Degenerate: a constant path, \( L=0 \). Non-example by minimal change: the graph of \( x\sin(1/x) \) (below). Warning: length belongs to the path, not the trace (`exm-back-and-forth-segment`).
- **`thm-rectifiable-iff-components-bv`** (Apostol 6.17). \( \gamma \) is rectifiable iff every \( \gamma_j \) is BV on \( [a,b] \), and then \( V_a^b(\gamma_j)\le L(\gamma)\le\sum_{j=1}^nV_a^b(\gamma_j) \). Route: move 3, \( \lvert x_j\rvert\le\lVert x\rVert\le\sum_j\lvert x_j\rvert \), applied term by term, then sups. Name move 3.
- **`cor-graph-rectifiable`.** For continuous \( f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R} \), the graph path \( t\mapsto(t,f(t)) \) is rectifiable iff \( f \) is BV, and \( \max(b-a,V_a^b(f))\le L\le(b-a)+V_a^b(f) \). **Pays the §01 in-chapter hook:** the graph of \( x\sin(1/x) \) on \( [0,1] \) is not rectifiable; the graph of \( x^2\sin(1/x) \) is.
- **`thm-lipschitz-path-rectifiable`.** If each \( \gamma_j \) is Lipschitz with constant \( K_j \) (e.g. continuous on \( [a,b] \), differentiable on \( (a,b) \) with \( \lvert\gamma_j'\rvert\le K_j \), by AN1.7), then \( \gamma \) is rectifiable and \( L(\gamma)\le(\sum_jK_j)(b-a) \). Remark: B2 sharpens this to \( L\le K(b-a) \) when \( \lVert\gamma'\rVert\le K \); AN2.10 proves \( L=\int_a^b\lVert\gamma'\rVert \) for \( C^1 \) paths (**promise**).
- **`thm-arc-length-additive`** (Apostol 6.18). For \( a<c<b \): \( L(\gamma;[a,b])=L(\gamma;[a,c])+L(\gamma;[c,b]) \), each side finite iff the others are. Proof: as `thm-variation-additive`, with \( \Lambda \) for \( \operatorname{Var} \) and `lem-polygonal-refinement` for `lem-variation-refinement`; write it in four lines (not "same as before": S2a forbids resting on an exercise, and the text must carry the proof).
- **`thm-arc-length-function`** (Apostol 6.19). For rectifiable \( \gamma \), \( s_\gamma(t)=L(\gamma;[a,t]) \) is increasing and continuous on \( [a,b] \). Route: increasing by additivity; for \( t_0<t \), \( s_\gamma(t)-s_\gamma(t_0)\le\sum_j\bigl(v_{\gamma_j}(t)-v_{\gamma_j}(t_0)\bigr) \) by `thm-rectifiable-iff-components-bv` on \( [t_0,t] \) and `thm-variation-additive`; each \( v_{\gamma_j} \) is continuous by `thm-variation-continuity` because \( \gamma_j \) is continuous. Same on the left.
- **`def-mesh`, `thm-length-mesh-limit`** (key proof 3; not in Apostol's main text; PMA-style and needed by CA.1). Let \( \gamma \) be a path. If \( \gamma \) is rectifiable, then for every \( \varepsilon>0 \) there is \( \delta>0 \) such that \( \Lambda(\gamma;P)>L(\gamma)-\varepsilon \) for every \( P \) with \( \lVert P\rVert<\delta \). If \( \gamma \) is not rectifiable, then for every \( M \) there is \( \delta>0 \) with \( \Lambda(\gamma;P)>M \) whenever \( \lVert P\rVert<\delta \).
  - **Scratch Work** (move 5, first appearance). Fix a partition \( P_0 \) with \( m \) points and \( \Lambda(P_0)>L-\varepsilon/2 \). For a fine \( P \), insert the points of \( P_0 \) one at a time. Each insertion into an interval of length \( <\delta \) can raise \( \Lambda \) by at most twice the largest jump of \( \gamma \) over a distance \( <\delta \). So we want that jump below \( \varepsilon/(4m) \): uniform continuity gives δ, and δ depends on ε and on \( P_0 \) (through \( m \)), **not on \( P \)**.
  - **Proof.** As in the scratch work, with uniform continuity of each \( \gamma_j \) on \( [a,b] \) (AN1.6, Heine–Cantor) and \( \lVert\cdot\rVert\le\sum_j\lvert\cdot_j\rvert \) giving: \( \lvert u-w\rvert<\delta \) ⇒ \( \lVert\gamma(u)-\gamma(w)\rVert<\eta \), \( \eta=\varepsilon/(4m) \). Inserting \( p \) into \( [t',t''] \) changes \( \Lambda \) by \( \lVert\gamma(t')-\gamma(p)\rVert+\lVert\gamma(p)-\gamma(t'')\rVert-\lVert\gamma(t'')-\gamma(t')\rVert<2\eta \). After at most \( m \) insertions, \( \Lambda(P)>\Lambda(P\cup P_0)-2m\eta\ge\Lambda(P_0)-\varepsilon/2>L-\varepsilon \). The non-rectifiable case: the same with \( P_0 \) chosen so \( \Lambda(P_0)>M+1 \) and \( \eta=1/(2m) \).
  - **`cor-variation-mesh-limit`.** For continuous BV \( f \) on \( [a,b] \), \( \operatorname{Var}(f;P)\to V_a^b(f) \) as \( \lVert P\rVert\to0 \) (the case \( n=1 \)). Warning: continuity is needed—for \( 1_{\{c\}} \), partitions avoiding \( c \) have \( \operatorname{Var}=0 \) at every mesh, while \( V=2 \).
- **`def-reparametrization`, `thm-length-reparametrization`** (Apostol 6.12, 6.20). If \( \varphi:[c,d]\to[a,b] \) is continuous, strictly monotone and onto, then \( \gamma\circ\varphi \) is a path, rectifiable iff \( \gamma \) is, and \( L(\gamma\circ\varphi)=L(\gamma) \). Route: \( \varphi \) maps partitions of \( [c,d] \) bijectively onto partitions of \( [a,b] \) (order reversed if decreasing), preserving each polygonal length. "Onto" plus strict monotonicity give the bijection; continuity is needed only to keep \( \gamma\circ\varphi \) a path.
- **`exm-back-and-forth-segment`.** \( \gamma(t)=(\lvert t\rvert,0) \) on \( [-1,1] \): trace \( [0,1]\times\{0\} \), \( L(\gamma)=2 \). The parameter change \( t\mapsto t^2 \) from \( [-1,1] \) is not monotone, and length is not preserved; length is a property of the path.
- **`thm-arc-length-parametrization`.** Let \( \gamma \) be rectifiable with \( L=L(\gamma) \), and constant on no non-degenerate subinterval of \( [a,b] \). Then \( s_\gamma:[a,b]\to[0,L] \) is a strictly increasing continuous bijection with continuous inverse, and \( \sigma=\gamma\circ s_\gamma^{-1} \) satisfies \( L(\sigma;[u,v])=v-u \) for \( 0\le u<v\le L \). Route: strict monotonicity—if \( s_\gamma(x)=s_\gamma(y) \), \( x<y \), then \( L(\gamma;[x,y])=0 \), so \( \lVert\gamma(t)-\gamma(x)\rVert\le L(\gamma;[x,y])=0 \) for \( t\in[x,y] \), contradiction; onto by the IVT and continuous inverse (AN1.6); then `thm-length-reparametrization` on \( [u,v] \) and additivity. Remark (prose): differentiability of \( \sigma \) ("unit speed") needs AN2.10 and is used in AM.

## 06-cantor-staircase.md — The Cantor staircase (≤ 4,000)

- **Brief Review box (AN1.4, Cantor set).** \( C_0=[0,1] \); \( C_n \) is obtained from \( C_{n-1} \) by removing the open middle third of each of its \( 2^{n-1} \) closed intervals; \( C_n \) is a union of \( 2^n \) disjoint closed intervals of length \( 3^{-n} \); \( C=\bigcap_nC_n \). The removed open intervals ("gaps") through stage \( n \) number \( 2^n-1 \) and have total length \( \sum_{k=1}^n2^{k-1}3^{-k}=1-(2/3)^n \) (finite geometric sum). Endpoints of gaps lie in \( C \). Cite the AN1.4 labels.
- **`def-cantor-function`.** List the gaps through stage \( n \) from left to right, \( J_{n,1},\dots,J_{n,2^n-1} \), and set \( \kappa_0=j/2^n \) on \( J_{n,j} \). Then for \( x\in[0,1] \), \( \kappa(x)=\sup\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{\kappa_0(t):t\in[0,1]\setminus C,\ t\le x\}\bigr) \).
- **`lem-cantor-gap-values`.** \( \kappa_0 \) is well defined: the gap \( J_{n,j} \) is \( J_{n+1,2j} \) in the next list, so its value \( j/2^n=2j/2^{n+1} \) does not depend on the stage. And \( \kappa_0 \) is increasing on \( [0,1]\setminus C \). Route: induction; between consecutive gaps of stage \( n \) (and before the first, after the last) lies exactly one interval of \( C_n \), and stage \( n+1 \) inserts one gap in each.
- **`lem-monotone-dense-range-continuous`.** An increasing function on an interval whose range is dense in an interval \( [\alpha,\beta] \) and contained in it is continuous. Route: AN1.5 one-sided limits; a jump \( f(c^-)<f(c^+) \) would leave the open interval \( (f(c^-),f(c^+)) \) with at most one value of \( f \) (namely \( f(c) \)), contradicting density. Endpoints likewise. (General; AN2.15 reuses it.)
- **`thm-cantor-function`.** \( \kappa \) is increasing and continuous on \( [0,1] \), \( \kappa(0)=0 \), \( \kappa(1)=1 \), \( \kappa=j/2^n \) on \( J_{n,j} \), and \( \kappa([0,1])=[0,1] \). Route: increasing from the sup definition and `lem-cantor-gap-values`; agrees with \( \kappa_0 \) off \( C \); \( \kappa(1)=\sup\{j/2^n\}=1 \) (Archimedes, AN1.1); range contains \( 0,1 \) and all \( j/2^n \), dense (choose \( n \) with \( 2^{-n}<y'-y \)); `lem-monotone-dense-range-continuous`; onto by the IVT (AN1.6). Total variation \( V_0^1(\kappa)=1 \) (`thm-monotone-bv`).
- **`lem-cantor-endpoint-values`.** If \( [l,r] \) is the \( j \)-th interval of \( C_n \) from the left (\( 1\le j\le2^n \)), then \( \kappa(l)=(j-1)/2^n \) and \( \kappa(r)=j/2^n \). Route: \( l=0 \) or \( l \) is the right end of \( J_{n,j-1} \); \( r=1 \) or \( r \) is the left end of \( J_{n,j} \); continuity of \( \kappa \) and its constant value on the gap.
- **`thm-cantor-graph-length`** (pays the chapter hook). The graph path \( \Gamma(t)=(t,\kappa(t)) \), \( t\in[0,1] \), is rectifiable and \( L(\Gamma)=2 \).
  - *Try it first (ten minutes): show \( L(\Gamma)\le2 \) for any increasing continuous function from \( (0,0) \) to \( (1,1) \).*
  - Idea: upper bound from \( \lVert(u,w)\rVert\le\lvert u\rvert+\lvert w\rvert \) and telescoping both coordinates (move 3); lower bound from one good partition per stage (move 2): the endpoints of the \( 2^n \) intervals of \( C_n \). Over each gap \( \Gamma \) is horizontal, contributing exactly the gap's length; over each interval of \( C_n \) it rises \( 2^{-n} \), contributing at least \( 2^{-n} \).
  - Proof. (≤) `cor-graph-rectifiable`: \( L\le1+V_0^1(\kappa)=2 \). (≥) With \( P_n \) the endpoints of the intervals of \( C_n \): \( \Lambda(\Gamma;P_n)\ge\bigl(1-(2/3)^n\bigr)+2^n\cdot2^{-n}=2-(2/3)^n \), using `lem-cantor-endpoint-values`. So \( L\ge2-(2/3)^n \) for all \( n \), hence \( L\ge2 \) (\( (2/3)^n \) can be made smaller than any ε: AN1.1/AN1.2).
  - Aftermath (prose). The graph is as long as the right-then-up staircase; the "diagonal" never happens. All of the rise, and half of the length, sits over \( C \).
- **Closing hooks (prose only; these are forward pointers, no dependency).**
  - *What the length formula misses.* On every gap \( \kappa'=0 \), so \( \sqrt{1+\kappa'^2}=1 \) there; any recipe for length that ignored a set as thin as \( C \) would answer 1, not 2. AN2.10 proves \( L=\int\sqrt{1+f'^2} \) for \( C^1 \) functions; MT (differentiation chapter) names the hypothesis that \( \kappa \) lacks: absolute continuity.
  - *All of the measure on \( C \).* AN2.10: \( \int_0^1 d\kappa=1 \), although \( \kappa \) is constant off \( C \).
