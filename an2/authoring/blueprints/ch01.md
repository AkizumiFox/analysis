## Must prove

- the metric axioms for d₁, d₂, d_∞ on ℝⁿ and for the sup metric on C[a,b]
- open balls are open; arbitrary unions / finite intersections of open sets are open
- F is closed iff it contains the limits of its convergent sequences
- closure = smallest closed superset = set of limits of sequences
- U ⊆ Y is open in the subspace Y iff U = V ∩ Y with V open in X
- equivalence of ε-δ, sequential and open-preimage continuity
- composition of continuous maps is continuous; Lipschitz ⇒ uniformly continuous ⇒ continuous
- d is continuous on X×X

## Key examples and counterexamples

- discrete metric: every set open and closed
- closure of an open ball need not be the closed ball (discrete metric)
- sup vs L¹ metric on C[0,1]: a sequence converging in one and not the other
- ℓ^∞ is not separable (uncountably many 0-1 sequences at mutual distance 1)
- (0,1) and ℝ are homeomorphic
- x ↦ x² continuous but not uniformly continuous on ℝ

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- prove that closure of A equals the set of limits of sequences in A, both inclusions
- negate 'f: X→Y is uniformly continuous' and exhibit a continuous non-uniformly-continuous map between metric spaces
- show two metrics generate the same open sets iff the identity is a homeomorphism
