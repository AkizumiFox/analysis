# Blueprint: AN2.10 — The Riemann–Stieltjes Integral

**Directory:** `an2/src/ch03-riemann-stieltjes-integral/`. **AN2, chapter 3** (position 3 of `an2/authoring/design/layout-apostol.md`), main line. State: S1 blueprint written; S2a not started. Binding plan: math-atlas `spec/08-map-v2.md` §C3 (row 3), §C5 (AN2 column), §C6 (AN2.10 = former CL.2), §E1.

**Purpose.**
- AN1.8 integrated f against length: Δxₖ. Physics, probability and number theory want to integrate against a *distribution* that may carry point masses: Δαₖ = α(xₖ) − α(xₖ₋₁) for an integrator α that may jump. One integral then holds both Σ f(pᵢ)mᵢ and ∫ f ρ dx.
- The chapter builds that integral on Apostol's definition (limit along refinements), develops it for increasing integrators by upper and lower sums (the spiral of AN1.8's Darboux theory), proves integration by parts and the reduction formulas, extends everything to integrators of bounded variation through AN2.9's Jordan decomposition, and says exactly when the integral fails to exist (shared one-sided discontinuities).
- It closes with the first notion of measure theory, **sets of measure zero**, and a **full proof of Lebesgue's criterion** for Riemann integrability. The last subsection lists what the Riemann integral still cannot do; MT (via HA.9) pays that hook.
- Not here: Euler's summation formula (Apostol 7.10), the integral as a function of its interval and the second mean-value theorem (Apostol 7.19–7.22), parameter integrals (Apostol 7.23–7.25). They go to AN2.11 (see coordinator notes on the spec's section numbers). Infinite series are not yet available (AN1.3 is the next chapter): countably many jumps appear only as limits of finite sums in one C exercise.

## Failure-motivated definitions

- **`def-rs-integral`**: the Riemann sum Σ f(tₖ)Δxₖ cannot see a point mass (a point has length zero). Replace Δxₖ by the mass Δαₖ of the piece. Second failure: with the mesh-limit definition, the most natural jump example (`exm-opposite-jumps`, previewed) has no value; with the refinement definition it does. State both failures in the explain template's step 2.
- **`def-upper-lower-stieltjes-sums`**: the general definition gives no method for proving existence. For increasing α the weights Δαₖ ≥ 0, so sup and inf squeeze S(P, f, α) as in AN1.8; for a non-monotone α the "upper" sum can lie below the "lower" one (Quick check in §2).
- **`def-measure-zero`**: "finitely many discontinuities" (AN1.8) is too small and "countably many" is the wrong scale (H3). A set is negligible when it can be covered by open intervals of arbitrarily small total length.

## Named moves (index.md, "Named moves")

- **Refine to a common partition.** Two "eventually" statements along refinements hold together on P′ ∪ P″. (§1)
- **Summation by parts.** Σ α(tₖ)(f(xₖ) − f(xₖ₋₁)) = [fα] − Σ f(xₖ)(α(tₖ₊₁) − α(tₖ)): move the difference from one factor to the other. (§3; reused in AN1.3 and AN2.13)
- **Reduce BV to increasing.** Write α = V − (V − α) and prove the statement for each increasing piece. (§4)
- **Good/bad split.** Put the trouble in finitely many short intervals; on the rest, the oscillation is small. (§4, §6)
- **Count the bad intervals.** If U − L < η and each bad interval has oscillation ≥ ε, their total weight is < η/ε. (§6)

## index.md

- **Story.** A rod of length 2 has density 1 and carries point masses at x = 1/2 and x = 2. Its mass is ∫ρ plus a sum; its centre of mass is another integral plus another sum. The Riemann integral of AN1.8 sees only lengths, and a point has length zero. Measure each piece by its *mass* instead: α(x) = mass of [0, x]. Then mass = ∫ dα and moment = ∫ x dα, one formula for both (H1, paid in §3).
  - The same integral turns sums into integrals (∫ f d⌊x⌋ = Σ f(n), used for Euler summation in AN2.11) and expectations of random variables into integrals against distribution functions (prose pointer to PR.1).
  - The chapter ends with the question the Riemann integral could not answer in AN1.8: *exactly which* functions are integrable. The answer (Lebesgue's criterion) needs the first notion of measure theory, and it shows what Riemann's theory still lacks.
- **What you need.** AN2.9 (variation, Jordan decomposition, the Cantor function), AN1.8 (Darboux sums and criterion, FTC). Also AN1.4 (Heine–Borel, the Cantor set), AN1.6 (uniform continuity), F2.3 (countable sets).

## 01-integrators-with-jumps.md — Integrators with jumps

- **Opening frame.** AN1.8 weights each piece of [a, b] by its length. Recall `thm-darboux-criterion` in one clause. Question: how do we weight by mass?
- **Hook (H1): `exm-rod-with-point-masses`.** Rod [0, 2], density 1, point mass 2 at 1/2, point mass 1 at 2. Mass function α(x) = x + 2·[x ≥ 1/2] + [x ≥ 2] (Iverson brackets introduced in one clause, or use indicators). Riemann sums with Δxₖ miss the 3 units carried by points. With Δαₖ, a piece containing 1/2 carries mass ≥ 2. The computation of mass 5 and centre 1 is promised for §3.
- **`def-rs-sum` and `def-rs-integral`** (major notion; full explain template, STYLE §3).
  - Partition P = {x₀ < ⋯ < xₙ} of [a, b], tags tₖ ∈ [xₖ₋₁, xₖ], S(P, f, α) = Σ f(tₖ)Δαₖ. No boundedness or monotonicity assumed for f or α.
  - f ∈ 𝓡(α) on [a, b] with value A if: ∀ε > 0 ∃P_ε ∀P ⊇ P_ε ∀ tags: |S(P, f, α) − A| < ε. Give the symbolic form (STYLE §10).
  - Slogan: *A is what the sums settle to once the partition contains P_ε.*
  - Failure motivation (step 2 of the template): (i) Δxₖ cannot see a point mass; (ii) preview: with "mesh → 0" the simplest example with jumps on both sides of a point has no value (`exm-opposite-jumps`, §4). That is why Apostol's refinement limit is used. **Hook H2**: one sentence, "§4 shows the two limits agree whenever α is continuous".
  - α(x) = x: the sums are AN1.8's Riemann sums, but the limit is taken differently; that the classes agree is `cor-riemann-condition-agrees` (§2). Until then the reader does not identify them.
- **`thm-rs-unique`.** The value A is unique. Proof: given A, A′ with P_ε, P′_ε, the common refinement P_ε ∪ P′_ε gives |A − A′| < 2ε. Name the move **refine to a common partition**.
- **Quick check.** If α is constant on [a, b], what is ∫ f dα, and for which f? (Every f; value 0, since every Δαₖ = 0.)
- **`thm-step-integrator`** (Apostol 7.8–7.9, in the case f continuous at the jumps). Let a ≤ c₁ < ⋯ < c_m ≤ b and let α be constant on each open interval between consecutive points of {a, c₁, …, c_m, b}. Let f be continuous at each cⱼ (one-sided at a and b). Then f ∈ 𝓡(α) and
  ∫_a^b f dα = Σⱼ f(cⱼ)(α(cⱼ+) − α(cⱼ−)), with the conventions α(a−) := α(a), α(b+) := α(b).
  - **Scratch Work (block 1).** One jump at c ∈ (a, b) first. If c ∈ P, only the two pieces touching c have Δα ≠ 0: [xₖ₋₁, c] carries α(c) − α(c−), [c, xₖ₊₁] carries α(c+) − α(c). Their tags lie within δ of c if both neighbours do. So put c and c ± δ into P_ε; refinements keep them. Choose δ from continuity of f at c with ε/(|J₁| + |J₂| + 1).
  - Proof: write it for m jumps with δ smaller than half the least gap between jump points.
  - Remark: only the side of the jump matters; the general version (f and α not discontinuous from the same side) is `thm-rs-necessary-condition`'s positive half and `exr-integrators-with-jumps-c1`.
- **`exm-floor-integrator`.** ∫₀³ x² d⌊x⌋ = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14 (jump at the right endpoint 3 counts; jump at 0 does not, since α(0−) := α(0)). Point out the endpoint convention with a warning.
- **Warning.** A jump of α at the left endpoint a contributes nothing, at b it contributes α(b) − α(b−): the convention is forced by the definition, since only Δαₖ inside [a, b] enter. Witness: ∫₀¹ 1 d(1_{[0,1]}) = α(1) − α(0) = 0, while the "jump at 0" is 1 if one looks outside the interval.
- **H1, first payment.** The point-mass part of the rod: with α_pt = 2·1_{[1/2,2]} + 1_{\{2\}}, ∫₀² x dα_pt = 2·(1/2) + 1·2 = 3. The density part waits for linearity and reduction (§3).
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1** (parts): (a) write the definition of f ∈ 𝓡(α) in symbols; (b) true/false: if α is constant then every f is in 𝓡(α) with integral 0 (true); (c) for f continuous and α = 1_{[0,1]} on [−1, 1], compute ∫ f dα (f(0)); (d) true/false: the definition allows the tags to be chosen after P (true; "∀ tags" sits inside "∀P ⊇ P_ε").
  - **B1.** ∫₀^{5/2} (x³ − x) d⌊x⌋. Answer: f(1) + f(2) = 0 + 6 = 6. Hence the jump at 1 contributes nothing although α jumps there.
  - **B2.** α = 0 on [0, 1), 3 on [1, 2), 5 at 2; ∫₀² eˣ dα = 3e + 2e².
  - **B3.** Prove `thm-rs-unique` in your own words, naming the move. (Answer: common refinement, 2ε.)
  - **C1** (one-sided jumps). α constant on [a, c] and on (c, b], with a jump only from the right at c; f continuous from the right at c (possibly discontinuous from the left). Prove ∫ f dα = f(c)(α(c+) − α(c)). Answer: with c ∈ P the left piece has Δα = 0, the right piece has tag in [c, c + δ]. Hint ladder: rung 1 "which Δαₖ can be non-zero once c ∈ P?"; rung 2 "only Δα on [c, xₖ₊₁]".
  - **C2** (telescoping and downward jumps). (a) Show that for *every* α, ∫_a^b 1 dα exists and equals α(b) − α(a). (b) α = 1 on [0, 1), α(1) = 0 (a downward jump at the right endpoint), f continuous: show ∫₀¹ f dα = −f(1). (c) Revisit after §3: with α(x) = x on [0, 1), α(1) = 0, ∫₀¹ x dα = 1/2 − 1 = −1/2. (Answers: (a) every sum telescopes; (b) `thm-step-integrator` with a negative jump; (c) linearity in α plus reduction.)

## 02-increasing-integrators.md — Increasing integrators and Riemann's condition

- **Opening frame (spiral, STYLE §10).** AN1.8 proved integrability by squeezing sums between U and L. Recall `thm-darboux-criterion` with `@ref`. What changes: Δxₖ becomes Δαₖ, which is ≥ 0 only if α is increasing; what survives: refinement monotonicity and the ε-criterion. Standing assumption for the section: α increasing on [a, b].
- **`def-upper-lower-stieltjes-sums`** (failure-motivated: the definition gives no existence method). Mₖ, mₖ sup/inf of f on [xₖ₋₁, xₖ], f bounded. U(P, f, α) = Σ MₖΔαₖ, L similarly.
- **Quick check.** Why must α be increasing for L ≤ S ≤ U? Give α decreasing with U < L. (α(x) = −x, f(x) = x on [0, 1], P = {0, 1}: U = 1·(−1) = −1, L = 0.)
- **`lem-stieltjes-refinement`.** P ⊆ P′ ⇒ L(P) ≤ L(P′) ≤ U(P′) ≤ U(P). Proof: add one point; Δα splits into two non-negative parts. Cite AN1.8's `lem-refinement` and say the proof is that proof with Δx replaced by Δα.
- **`cor-lower-below-upper`.** L(P₁) ≤ U(P₂) for all P₁, P₂ (common refinement).
- **`def-upper-lower-stieltjes-integrals`.** \overline{∫} = inf_P U, \underline{∫} = sup_P L; \underline{∫} ≤ \overline{∫}.
- **`exm-x-dx2-sums`.** f(x) = x, α(x) = x² on [0, 1], uniform Pₙ. Δαₖ = (2k − 1)/n²; U = n(n + 1)(4n − 1)/(6n³) → 2/3; L → 2/3; U − L = (1/n)(α(1) − α(0)) = 1/n. Remark: 2/3 = ∫₀¹ x·2x dx, explained by `thm-rs-reduction-riemann` in §3.
- **`thm-riemann-condition`** (Apostol 7.11–7.13; **key proof**). Let α be increasing and f bounded on [a, b]. TFAE: (i) f ∈ 𝓡(α); (ii) ∀ε ∃P_ε ∀P ⊇ P_ε: U(P) − L(P) < ε; (iii) ∀ε ∃P: U(P) − L(P) < ε; (iv) \underline{∫} = \overline{∫}. In that case ∫ f dα equals the common value.
  - Idea: S is squeezed between L and U; conversely tags can push S to within ε of U and of L.
  - (i) ⇒ (ii): if α(b) = α(a) everything is 0. Otherwise choose tags tₖ, tₖ′ with f(tₖ) > Mₖ − ε/(α(b) − α(a)), f(tₖ′) < mₖ + ε/(α(b) − α(a)); then U − L < S(P, t) − S(P, t′) + 2ε < 4ε for P ⊇ P_ε.
  - (ii) ⇔ (iii) by `lem-stieltjes-refinement`; (iii) ⇒ (iv) since \overline{∫} − \underline{∫} ≤ U − L; (iv) ⇒ (i): with A the common value, L ≤ A ≤ U and L ≤ S ≤ U, so |S − A| ≤ U − L < ε for P ⊇ P_ε (P_ε from (iii) and refinement).
  - *Self-explain the step "if α(b) = α(a) everything is 0": which sums vanish and why?*
- **`cor-riemann-condition-agrees`.** With α(x) = x, f ∈ 𝓡(x) if and only if f is Riemann–Darboux integrable in the sense of AN1.8, with the same value. Proof: (iv) is AN1.8's `thm-darboux-criterion`. From now on 𝓡 = 𝓡(x). This closes the remark in §1.
- **`thm-continuous-rs-integrable`** (Apostol 7.16, case α increasing; PMA 6.8). f continuous, α increasing ⇒ f ∈ 𝓡(α). Proof: `thm-heine-cantor`; ‖P‖ < δ gives U − L ≤ ε(α(b) − α(a)). Remark: it is the *mesh* that is used, but only on the f side.
- **`thm-rs-comparison`.** f, g ∈ 𝓡(α), f ≤ g ⇒ ∫ f dα ≤ ∫ g dα. (Linearity in f is in §3; here prove comparison directly from sums: S(P, f) ≤ S(P, g) for the same tags.)
- **`thm-rs-abs-product`** (Apostol 7.14). f ∈ 𝓡(α) ⇒ |f| ∈ 𝓡(α), |∫ f dα| ≤ ∫ |f| dα; f² ∈ 𝓡(α). Proof: sup|f| − inf|f| ≤ Mₖ − mₖ on each piece; for f², Mₖ(f²) − mₖ(f²) ≤ 2K(Mₖ − mₖ), K = sup|f|. The product fg waits for linearity (exercise in §3).
- **`thm-rs-first-mvt`** (Apostol 7.18, first form). α increasing, f ∈ 𝓡(α), m ≤ f ≤ M ⇒ ∫ f dα = c(α(b) − α(a)) with c ∈ [m, M]; if f is continuous, c = f(x₀) for some x₀ ∈ [a, b] (`thm-ivt`).

## 03-properties-and-integration-by-parts.md — Properties, integration by parts, and reduction

- **Opening frame.** The algebra of the integral, for an *arbitrary* integrator α; then the theorem that makes Stieltjes integration more than a generalization: the roles of f and α can be exchanged.
- **`thm-rs-cauchy-criterion`.** f ∈ 𝓡(α) ⇔ ∀ε ∃P_ε ∀P′, P″ ⊇ P_ε ∀ tags: |S(P′) − S(P″)| < ε. Proof of ⇐: nested P_{1/n}; S(P_{1/n}) is Cauchy (`thm-cauchy-convergent`); its limit is A.
- **`thm-rs-linear`** (Apostol 7.4). Linear in f and in α: ∫(c₁f + c₂g) dα and ∫ f d(c₁α + c₂β). Proof: sums are linear; common refinement.
- **`thm-rs-additive`** (Apostol 7.4, 7.12). If f ∈ 𝓡(α) on [a, c] and on [c, b] then on [a, b], and the integrals add. Conventions ∫_a^a = 0, ∫_b^a = −∫_a^b.

## 04-integrators-of-bounded-variation.md — Integrators of bounded variation

- **Opening frame (spiral).** Recall AN2.9's `thm-jordan-decomposition` and `thm-variation-function-continuity`. Every BV α is a difference of increasing functions, so §2's machinery applies piece by piece. Name the move **reduce BV to increasing**.
- **`thm-rs-bv-to-variation`** (Apostol 7.15). α BV on [a, b], V(x) = V_a^x(α), f bounded. If f ∈ 𝓡(α) then f ∈ 𝓡(V) and f ∈ 𝓡(V − α).
  - Idea: on a fine partition, ΔVₖ ≈ |Δαₖ|; choosing tags according to the sign of Δαₖ turns Σ (f(tₖ) − f(tₖ′))Δαₖ into ≈ Σ (Mₖ − mₖ)|Δαₖ|.
  - Proof sketch (by `thm-riemann-condition` for the increasing V, it suffices to make U(P, f, V) − L(P, f, V) small): |f| ≤ K. Take P ⊇ P_ε with V(b) < Σ|Δαₖ| + ε; refinement keeps this, since Σ|Δαₖ| only grows. Split Σ(Mₖ − mₖ)ΔVₖ = Σ(Mₖ − mₖ)(ΔVₖ − |Δαₖ|) + Σ(Mₖ − mₖ)|Δαₖ|. The first sum is ≤ 2K·Σ(ΔVₖ − |Δαₖ|) < 2Kε (each term ≥ 0). For the second, choose tags with (f(tₖ) − f(tₖ′))·sign(Δαₖ) > Mₖ − mₖ − ε; then it is < |S(P, t) − S(P, t′)| + εV(b) < 2ε + εV(b). Total < ε(2K + 2 + V(b)). Then V − α is increasing and f ∈ 𝓡(V − α) by linearity in α.

## 05-oscillation-and-measure-zero.md — Oscillation and sets of measure zero

- **Opening frame (H3).** Which bounded functions are Riemann integrable? AN1.8: continuous, monotone, finitely many discontinuities. Thomae (`exm-thomae-function`): countably many discontinuities, integrable. Dirichlet: discontinuous everywhere, not. The Cantor indicator (§6): uncountably many, integrable. Cardinality is the wrong scale; length is the right one. From here on α(x) = x (the Stieltjes analogue is the MT hook, §6).
- **`def-oscillation`.** Ω_f(I) = sup_I f − inf_I f; ω_f(x) = lim_{h→0+} Ω_f((x − h, x + h) ∩ [a, b]) (a decreasing limit, exists by `thm-monotone-convergence` or as an infimum).
- **`thm-oscillation-continuity`.** f continuous at x ⇔ ω_f(x) = 0.
- **`lem-oscillation-closed`.** D_f(ε) = {ω_f ≥ ε} is closed, hence compact (`thm-heine-borel`). D_f = ∪ₙ D_f(1/n).
- **Quick check.** ω at 0 of sign(x); of sin(1/x) (f(0) = 0); of x sin(1/x). (2; 2; 0.) Thomae at p/q in lowest terms: 1/q.
- **`def-measure-zero`** (major notion, failure-motivated, explain template). E ⊆ ℝ has measure zero if ∀ε > 0 there are open intervals I₁, I₂, … (finitely or countably many) covering E with Σ_{k≤n} ℓ(Iₖ) < ε for every n. Say in one clause that after AN1.3 this reads Σ ℓ(Iₖ) ≤ ε; the partial-sum form avoids series here.
  - Slogan: *a null set can be hidden under intervals of total length as small as you like.*
  - Non-example by minimal change: [0, 1] (`cor-interval-not-null`).
- **`thm-countable-measure-zero`.** Countable sets are null: cover qₖ by an interval of length ε/2^{k+1}. Choice is not needed (the intervals are explicit).
- **`thm-cantor-measure-zero`.** The Cantor set (`exm-cantor-set`) is null: stage n is 2ⁿ closed intervals of length 3^{−n}; enlarge each to an open interval of length 2·3^{−n}; total 2(2/3)ⁿ.
- **`thm-union-measure-zero`.** A countable union of null sets is null: cover Eⱼ with total < ε/2^{j+1}; the family is countable (F2.3: ℕ × ℕ countable); any finite partial sum is < ε. Flag countable choice in one clause (choosing a cover for each j).
- **`lem-interval-cover-length`.** If finitely many open intervals cover [a, b] (a < b), their lengths add to more than b − a. Proof by induction on the number of intervals (pick the interval containing a, its right end, recurse). **`cor-interval-not-null`**: by `thm-heine-borel`, any countable cover has a finite subcover, so the partial sums exceed b − a.
- **`lem-compact-null-finite-cover`.** A compact null set can be covered by finitely many open intervals of total length < ε.
- **Warning.** Null is not nowhere dense: ℚ ∩ [0, 1] is null and dense. Nowhere dense is not null: `exm-fat-cantor-set`.
- **`exm-fat-cantor-set`** (construction in text; non-nullness in `exr-oscillation-and-measure-zero-c2`). Remove the open middle interval of length 1/4 from [0, 1]; at stage n remove from each of the 2^{n−1} remaining intervals the open middle interval of length 4^{−n}. Removed through stage n: (1/2)(1 − 2^{−n}) < 1/2. Remaining intervals at stage n have length (1/2 + 2^{−n−1})/2ⁿ > 4^{−n−1}, so the construction continues. K = intersection: compact, contains no interval.

## 06-lebesgues-criterion.md — Lebesgue's criterion and what Riemann lacks

- **Opening frame.** §5 built the scale. Now the answer to AN1.8's question.
- **`thm-lebesgue-criterion`** (Apostol 7.26; **key proof**). Let f be bounded on [a, b]. Then f ∈ 𝓡 on [a, b] if and only if D_f has measure zero.
  - (⇒) *Try it first (ten minutes): if U(P) − L(P) is tiny, what can you say about the total length of the pieces on which f oscillates by at least 1/n?*
    - **Scratch Work (block 4): count the bad intervals.** Pieces Iₖ with Ω_f(Iₖ) ≥ 1/n satisfy (1/n)Σ_bad ℓ(Iₖ) ≤ Σ_bad Ωₖℓ(Iₖ) ≤ U − L. So U − L < ε/n forces Σ_bad ℓ < ε.
    - Proof: fix n, ε; choose P with U − L < ε/(2n). A point of D_f(1/n) in the interior of a piece makes the piece bad (Ω ≥ ω ≥ 1/n); the open interiors of bad pieces have total length < ε/2; the finitely many partition points are covered by intervals of total length < ε/2. So D_f(1/n) is null; D_f = ∪ D_f(1/n) is null by `thm-union-measure-zero`.
    - Name the move **count the bad intervals**.
  - (⇐) Idea: D_f(ε) is compact and null, so finitely many short intervals hide it (good/bad split, second full use). Off them, ω < ε everywhere, and compactness gives a partition with small oscillation on each piece.
    - Proof: |f| ≤ K. Given ε, by `lem-oscillation-closed` and `lem-compact-null-finite-cover` cover D_f(ε) by finitely many open intervals of total length < ε; their union meets [a, b] in finitely many intervals of total length < ε (bad set B). [a, b] ∖ B is a finite union of closed intervals Gᵢ on which ω_f < ε at every point; each x ∈ Gᵢ has an open neighbourhood Nₓ with Ω_f(Nₓ ∩ Gᵢ) < ε; a Lebesgue number for this cover of compact Gᵢ (or a direct finite subcover argument) gives a partition of Gᵢ with Ω < ε on each piece. P = all endpoints. U − L ≤ 2K·ε + ε(b − a).
    - *Before reading on: where is it used that D_f(ε) is closed, and what would go wrong with D_f itself?* (D_f need not be closed, e.g. Thomae: D_f = ℚ ∩ [a, b]; a null set that is not compact need not be hidden by *finitely* many intervals.)
- **`cor-lebesgue-algebra`.** f, g ∈ 𝓡 ⇒ f + g, fg, |f|, max(f, g), and 1/f (if |f| ≥ c > 0) are in 𝓡 (D_{fg} ⊆ D_f ∪ D_g). Monotone f ∈ 𝓡 (`thm-monotone-countable-discontinuities`). Compare with AN1.8's hand proofs in one sentence.
- **`cor-lebesgue-composition`.** f ∈ 𝓡 with values in [c, d], φ continuous on [c, d] ⇒ φ∘f ∈ 𝓡 (D_{φ∘f} ⊆ D_f).
- **`exm-thomae-lebesgue`.** Thomae: D = ℚ ∩ (0, 1], null; integrable (value 0 from L = 0).
- **`exm-cantor-indicator`.** 1_C: continuous off C (C closed, complement open), discontinuous on C (C has empty interior); D = C null; integrable, integral 0 (every piece meets the complement, so L = 0 and U → 0 by integrability).
- **`exm-composition-fails`** (weird). K fat Cantor, φ(x) = dist(x, K) continuous, f = 1_{(0,1]} on [0, 1] (φ takes values in [0, 1/2]): f∘φ = 1_{[0,1]∖K}, discontinuous at every point of K, not null (§5 C2): not integrable. Integrable ∘ continuous fails although continuous ∘ integrable holds.
- **Warning.** Changing an integrable function on a countable set can destroy integrability: 0 changed on ℚ is Dirichlet. "Countable" is not "finite" here; Lebesgue's criterion is about where f is *discontinuous*, not where it differs from something nice.
- **`## What the Riemann integral still lacks` (H4).** Prose plus one example, no proofs beyond the example:
  - **Limits.** `exm-dirichlet-as-limit`: q₁, q₂, … an enumeration of ℚ ∩ [0, 1]; fₙ = 1_{\{q₁,…,qₙ\}} ∈ 𝓡 with ∫ fₙ = 0, fₙ ↑ 1_ℚ ∉ 𝓡. Monotone, bounded, pointwise: still no limit theorem. (AN1.9 will prove the uniform-limit theorem; uniform convergence fails here.)
  - **Completeness.** With d(f, g) = ∫|f − g|, 𝓡 is not complete (statement; the fat Cantor approximants are the witness, proved in MT).
  - **Bounded derivatives.** There is a differentiable F on [0, 1] with F′ bounded and F′ ∉ 𝓡 (Volterra; construction in the optional AN2.15, prose pointer): FTC part 2 cannot even be stated for it.
  - **Integrators as measures.** Every increasing right-continuous α defines a "length" of [c, d] as α(d) − α(c); Lebesgue–Stieltjes theory extends it to all Borel sets (MT.2 revisits this chapter; PR.1 uses it for distribution functions). There is no Lebesgue criterion for 𝓡(α) in this book until then.
  - The repair: measure the sets {f > t} instead of chopping the domain. HA.9 opens MT with exactly this list. One sentence: the gauge integral (optional AN2.14) is another repair that keeps Riemann sums.
