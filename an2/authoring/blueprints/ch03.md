# Chapter 26: Compactness and Connectedness in Metric Spaces

Directory: `src/ch26-compactness-connectedness/`. State: not started.

**Part II, Metric Spaces and Several Variables.** Lifts convergence, completeness, compactness and continuity from ℝ to metric spaces and C(K), forced by concrete problems (ODE existence and uniqueness, polynomial approximation, typical continuous functions), then does calculus in ℝⁿ: inverse and implicit functions, linear systems with the matrix exponential and the flow, and the Riemann integral on ℝⁿ. General topology waits for Part VI, where it is used; Part II ends with Gate II.

*Cover-compactness is defined here directly for metric spaces; the topological version returns in topological-spaces-compactness (Part VI, spiral). KF §11 is written after its topological §9–10: use its metric content only. Norm equivalence uses compactness of the unit sphere in ℝⁿ and a basis (cite companion LA book for bases). The metric Urysohn and Tietze section (route R2) is all that Part IV needs from topology (Lusin, C_c density in ℝᵈ); the normal-space versions wait for separation-urysohn-lch in Part VI.*

## Planned sections

1. Sequential compactness; compact ⇒ closed and bounded; Bolzano–Weierstrass and Heine–Borel in ℝⁿ
2. Open covers, the Lebesgue number lemma, total boundedness; compact ⇔ sequentially compact ⇔ complete and totally bounded
3. Continuous images of compact sets: extreme values, uniform continuity (the metric version, the gate to the rest of the Part), continuous bijections from compact spaces are homeomorphisms
4. Finite versus infinite dimensions: all norms on a finite-dimensional space are equivalent; the unit ball of ℓ² is not compact; closed and bounded ≠ compact
5. The Cantor set as a compact, perfect, totally disconnected, nowhere dense set; the Peano curve as a uniform limit
6. Connectedness and path-connectedness; connected subsets of ℝ are intervals; continuous images; the general intermediate value theorem
7. Separating closed sets in a metric space: the Urysohn function d(x,A)/(d(x,A)+d(x,B)); the Tietze extension theorem for metric spaces (by uniform approximation, using completeness of C_b); continuous partitions of unity subordinate to a finite open cover of a compact set
8. Local to global: open connected subsets of ℝⁿ are path-connected; a locally constant function on a connected space is constant; the topologist's sine curve

## Must prove

- compact ⇔ sequentially compact ⇔ complete + totally bounded (metric spaces)
- Lebesgue number lemma
- Heine–Borel in ℝⁿ
- continuous image of compact is compact; EVT on compact metric spaces
- continuous on compact ⇒ uniformly continuous (metric), by sequences and by Lebesgue number
- continuous bijection from compact metric space to metric space is a homeomorphism
- all norms on ℝⁿ are equivalent
- unit ball of ℓ² is not compact
- path-connected ⇒ connected; connected subsets of ℝ are intervals
- open connected subsets of ℝⁿ are path-connected
- metric Urysohn lemma: disjoint closed A, B ⊆ X are separated by a continuous f: X → [0,1]
- Tietze extension theorem for a closed subset of a metric space (bounded, then real-valued)

## Key examples and counterexamples

- eₙ in ℓ²: bounded, no convergent subsequence
- closed bounded non-compact: ℕ with discrete metric, closed unit ball of C[0,1]
- Cantor set
- Peano curve [0,1] → [0,1]² (continuous surjection; cannot be a homeomorphism)
- topologist's sine curve: connected, not path-connected
- graphs y=0 and y=1/x: disjoint closed sets at distance 0
- Tietze fails for non-closed subsets: sin(1/x) on (0,1]

## Exit checks (C-exercises)

- prove uniform continuity on compact metric spaces twice: by sequences and contradiction, and by the Lebesgue number
- unit ball of ℓ² is not compact; prove the finite-dimensional contrast via norm equivalence
- prove [0,1] and [0,1]² are not homeomorphic using connectedness
