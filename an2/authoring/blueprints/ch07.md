## 01-radius-of-convergence.md — Power series and the disc of convergence

Budget 3,500. Apostol 9.14; PMA 3.38–3.40; Abbott 6.5; Pugh 4.2.

- **Hook.** The chapter hook. Then: what *is* the set where a power series converges? On ℝ one would guess "an interval"; the honest answer lives in ℂ.
- **`def-power-series`.** Σ_{n≥0} aₙ(z − c)ⁿ with aₙ, c ∈ ℂ, z ∈ ℂ; the series *at* z. Real power series are the case aₙ, c, z real. Everything in §1–§3 is stated for c = 0 and transferred by z ↦ z − c in one sentence.
- **`lem-power-series-comparison`.** If Σ aₙz₀ⁿ converges (or merely: its terms are bounded), then Σ aₙzⁿ converges absolutely for every |z| < |z₀|. Proof: |aₙz₀ⁿ| ≤ M, so |aₙzⁿ| ≤ M(|z|/|z₀|)ⁿ, geometric comparison (AN1.3).
- **Failure first.** Three series on the unit circle (`exm-boundary-behaviour`): Σzⁿ diverges at every |z| = 1 (terms do not tend to 0); Σzⁿ/n² converges absolutely at every |z| = 1; Σzⁿ/n diverges at z = 1, converges at z = −1 (alternating), and (stated now, proved in C1) at every other point of the circle. Same disc, three behaviours on its edge. So the right description is: a disc where convergence is absolute, a complement where the terms are unbounded, and an edge about which nothing general can be said.
- **`def-radius-of-convergence`.** R = sup{|z| : (aₙzⁿ) is bounded} ∈ [0, ∞]. Read back: the lemma gives absolute convergence inside, and outside the terms are unbounded so the series diverges.
- **`thm-cauchy-hadamard`.** R = 1/limsup |aₙ|^{1/n}, with 1/0 = ∞ and 1/∞ = 0.
  - *Scratch Work (first of four).* For |z| < R we want a geometric bound; limsup only controls |aₙ|^{1/n} eventually and only by something strictly bigger than the limsup. So insert ρ with |z| < ρ < R: eventually |aₙ|^{1/n} < 1/ρ, hence |aₙzⁿ| < (|z|/ρ)ⁿ. For |z| > R, limsup > 1/|z| gives infinitely many n with |aₙ|^{1/n} > 1/|z|, i.e. |aₙzⁿ| > 1.
  - *Proof:* the two halves as in the scratch work, written cleanly, citing AN1.2's characterization of limsup (eventually below anything larger; infinitely often above anything smaller).
- **`cor-ratio-radius`.** If |aₙ₊₁/aₙ| → L ∈ [0, ∞], then R = 1/L. Proof by AN1.3's ratio test applied to |aₙzⁿ|, or from AN1.2's lim inf ratio ≤ lim inf root ≤ lim sup root ≤ lim sup ratio. Warning: the ratio may have no limit while the root does (B1).
- **`exm-radius-extremes`.** Σ zⁿ/n! (R = ∞), Σ n! zⁿ (R = 0: converges only at 0), Σ zⁿ (R = 1).
- **`thm-power-series-uniform-compact`.** For 0 ≤ r < R the series converges uniformly on the closed disc |z| ≤ r. Proof: M-test (AN1.9) with Mₙ = |aₙ|rⁿ, summable by Cauchy–Hadamard at the real point r.
- **Named move 2, "Shrink to a closed subdisc",** stated after the proof.
- **`cor-power-series-continuous`.** The sum is continuous on the open disc |z| < R. Proof: a point z₀ with |z₀| < R lies in |z| ≤ r with r = (|z₀| + R)/2 (or |z₀| + 1 if R = ∞); uniform limit of the continuous partial sums (AN1.9; see S2a item 2 on the domain).
- **Warning.** Uniform convergence on the *open* disc can fail: Σzⁿ on |z| < 1 is not uniformly Cauchy (sup over the disc of |zⁿ| is 1 for every n).
- **Quick checks.**
  - Radius of Σ 2ⁿz³ⁿ? (Answer: 2^{−1/3}; root test on the coefficients: limsup of |b_k|^{1/k} with b_{3n} = 2ⁿ is 2^{1/3}.)
  - Swap the quantifiers: "for every z in the disc the series converges" vs "uniformly on the disc". Which holds for Σzⁿ on |z| < 1? (Only the first.)
- **Exercises** (solutions complete in the source; these are the intended answers).
  - **A1.** Radius of Σ n²zⁿ/3ⁿ. *(3.)*
  - **A2.** Σ zⁿ/(n 2ⁿ): radius, and behaviour at z = 2 and z = −2. *(R = 2; diverges at 2 (harmonic), converges at −2 (alternating).)*
  - **B1.** aₙ = 2ⁿ for n even, aₙ = 3^{−n} for n odd. Show the ratio |aₙ₊₁/aₙ| has no limit (it takes values tending to 0 and to ∞) while R = 1/2.
  - **B2.** If Σ aₙzⁿ has radius R, the radius of Σ aₙz²ⁿ is √R (with √0 = 0, √∞ = ∞). *(By `def-radius-of-convergence` applied to w = z²: the terms aₙ(z²)ⁿ are bounded for |z|² < R and unbounded for |z|² > R.)*
  - **C1.** For |z| = 1, z ≠ 1, prove Σ zⁿ/n converges (Dirichlet test, AN1.3: |Σ_{k≤n} zᵏ| ≤ 2/|1 − z|). Then show the convergence is not uniform on {|z| = 1, z ≠ 1}. *(If it were, the partial sums would be uniformly Cauchy there; letting z → 1 along the circle in a fixed block Σ_{n<k≤m} zᵏ/k gives the block of the harmonic series ≤ ε, contradicting divergence at 1.)*

## 02-termwise-differentiation.md — Differentiating and integrating term by term

Budget 3,000. Apostol 9.14 (Thm 9.23 neighbourhood), 9.18; PMA 8.1; Abbott 6.5; Ross §26.

- **Opening.** AN1.9 gave a termwise differentiation theorem for real series under uniform convergence of the derivatives. Power series deserve better: the derived series has the same radius, and differentiation works in ℂ (AN1.7's complex derivative).
- **`lem-derived-series-radius`.** Σ n aₙ zⁿ⁻¹ and Σ aₙ zⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) have the same radius as Σ aₙzⁿ. Proof: n^{1/n} → 1 (AN1.2) and Cauchy–Hadamard; the index shift does not change limsup.
- **`thm-power-series-differentiable`.** f(z) = Σ aₙzⁿ is complex differentiable at every |z| < R with f′(z) = Σ n aₙ zⁿ⁻¹. Real case: the same, for real series on (−R, R).
  - *Scratch Work (second of four).* We need |f(z+h) − f(z) − h g(z)| = o(|h|). Termwise this is Σ aₙ[(z+h)ⁿ − zⁿ − n zⁿ⁻¹h]. Expand binomially: the bracket is Σ_{k≥2} C(n,k) zⁿ⁻ᵏ hᵏ, which starts at h². Pull out |h|²: use C(n,k) ≤ C(n,2)·C(n−2,k−2) for 2 ≤ k ≤ n, so the rest sums to (|z| + |h|)ⁿ⁻² by the binomial theorem (F4.6). With |z| + |h| ≤ ρ < R the bound is C(n,2)ρⁿ⁻²|h|², and Σ |aₙ|C(n,2)ρⁿ⁻² converges by applying the lemma twice.
  - *Proof:* fix ρ with |z| < ρ < R; for 0 < |h| ≤ ρ − |z|, |f(z+h) − f(z) − h g(z)| ≤ K|h|² with K = Σ n(n−1)/2 |aₙ| ρⁿ⁻²; divide by |h|.
  - Remark: the real case also follows from AN1.9's theorem via named move 2; the direct proof is shorter and covers ℂ.
- **`cor-power-series-smooth`.** f has derivatives of every order on |z| < R, each given by the termwise series (induction with the lemma).
- **`cor-taylor-coefficients`.** aₙ = f⁽ⁿ⁾(0)/n!. So a function has at most one power-series expansion about a point.
- **`thm-power-series-identity`.** If f(z) = Σ aₙzⁿ (R > 0) vanishes at the points of a sequence zₖ → 0 with zₖ ≠ 0, then every aₙ = 0. Proof sketch: a₀ = f(0) = lim f(zₖ) = 0 by continuity; then f(z)/z = Σ aₙ₊₁zⁿ has the same radius and vanishes at the zₖ; induction. (Left partly to C1, the proof skeleton in the text.)
- **`thm-power-series-integrate`.** For real series and |x| < R: ∫₀ˣ Σ aₙtⁿ dt = Σ aₙxⁿ⁺¹/(n+1). Proof: the right side has radius R (lemma) and derivative f (theorem); FTC (AN1.8). Alternative via uniform convergence on [0, x] (AN1.9).
- **`exm-sums-from-geometric`** (worked example). From 1/(1−x) = Σxⁿ: Σ n xⁿ = x/(1−x)², and L(x) ≔ Σ_{n≥1} xⁿ/n is the antiderivative of 1/(1−x) vanishing at 0. L is named and left unidentified; §4 (`exm-log-series`) proves L(x) = −log(1−x). This is a small hook paid two sections later.
- **`exm-continuous-to-boundary-not-differentiable`** (weird-table row). f(x) = Σ xⁿ/n² converges uniformly on [−1, 1] (M-test), so it is continuous there; on (−1, 1), f′(x) = L(x)/x, which is unbounded as x → 1⁻ (L(x) ≥ Σ_{n≤N} xⁿ/n → H_N). The derived series has the same radius but not the same boundary behaviour.
- **Quick checks.**
  - f(x) = Σ x²ⁿ/(n+1). What is f⁽¹⁰⁾(0)? *(10!/6, from a₁₀ = 1/6.)*
  - True or false: if Σ aₙxⁿ converges at x = R, so does Σ n aₙ xⁿ⁻¹. *(False: Σ xⁿ/n² at x = 1.)*
- **Exercises.**
  - **A1.** Σ n²xⁿ for |x| < 1. *(x(1+x)/(1−x)³.)*

## 03-algebra-of-power-series.md — Products, substitution, reciprocals, and re-centering

Budget 3,500. Apostol 9.15–9.17; PMA 8.3–8.4; Pugh 4.6*; Tao II 4.4.

- **Opening question.** Is the product, composite or reciprocal of power series again a power series? And is a power series about 0 also a power series about every other point of its disc? Yes to all four. The product is AN1.3's Cauchy product; the other three are one idea: write a double series that converges absolutely and rearrange it (AN2.12).
- **`thm-power-series-product`.** If f = Σaₙzⁿ, g = Σbₙzⁿ have radii ≥ R, then fg = Σcₙzⁿ with cₙ = Σ_{k≤n} aₖbₙ₋ₖ, radius ≥ R. Proof: Cauchy product of absolutely convergent series (AN1.3, Mertens). Example where the radius grows: (1 − z)·Σzⁿ = 1 (C1).
- **`thm-power-series-substitution`.** Let f(w) = Σaₙwⁿ have radius R and g(z) = Σbₘzᵐ have radius r > 0. If 0 < ρ ≤ r and Σ|bₘ|ρᵐ < R, then for |z| < ρ, f(g(z)) = Σ_k cₖzᵏ, where cₖ is obtained by expanding each g(z)ⁿ as a power series (`thm-power-series-product`, n times) and collecting the coefficient of zᵏ. (All hypotheses; the condition Σ|bₘ|ρᵐ < R holds for small ρ exactly when |b₀| < R.)
  - Proof sketch: write g(z)ⁿ = Σ_k b^{(n)}_k zᵏ. Then |b^{(n)}_k| is dominated by the coefficients of (Σ|bₘ|zᵐ)ⁿ, so Σ_n Σ_k |aₙ||b^{(n)}_k||z|ᵏ ≤ Σ_n |aₙ| (Σ_m |bₘ||z|ᵐ)ⁿ < ∞. Absolutely convergent double series: sum by columns (Σ_n aₙ g(z)ⁿ = f(g(z))) or by rows (Σ_k cₖzᵏ). AN2.12's rearrangement theorem.
- **`cor-power-series-reciprocal`.** If f(0) ≠ 0, then 1/f equals a power series on some disc about 0. Proof: f = a₀(1 − w) with w(z) = 1 − f(z)/a₀, w(0) = 0; substitute w into Σwⁿ (radius 1) with ρ small. Computation: coefficients by solving (Σaₙzⁿ)(Σdₙzⁿ) = 1 recursively.
- **`thm-power-series-recentre`.** If f(z) = Σaₙzⁿ has radius R and |c| < R, then f(z) = Σ_k (f⁽ᵏ⁾(c)/k!)(z − c)ᵏ for |z − c| < R − |c|.
  - Proof sketch: zⁿ = ((z − c) + c)ⁿ = Σ_k C(n,k)cⁿ⁻ᵏ(z − c)ᵏ; the double series Σ_n Σ_k |aₙ|C(n,k)|c|ⁿ⁻ᵏ|z − c|ᵏ = Σ_n |aₙ|(|c| + |z − c|)ⁿ is finite when |c| + |z − c| < R; rearrange; the coefficients of (z − c)ᵏ are f⁽ᵏ⁾(c)/k! by `cor-taylor-coefficients` applied about c.
- **`def-analytic`** (failure-motivated: postponed in its full force to §6). A function on an open set U ⊆ ℝ (or ℂ) is **analytic** if about every point of U it equals a power series with positive radius. Read back: `thm-power-series-recentre` says *a power series is analytic in its disc*. §6 shows C^∞ does not imply analytic.
- **`exm-recentred-disc-escapes`** (worked; weird-table candidate). Σzⁿ = 1/(1 − z), R = 1. Re-centred at c = −1/2: 1/(1 − z) = Σ (z + 1/2)ᵏ/(3/2)ᵏ⁺¹, radius 3/2, larger than the guaranteed R − |c| = 1/2. The new disc pokes out of the old one: the function "knows" it continues past |z| = 1 except near z = 1. One sentence pointing to CA (analytic continuation), no more.

## 06-taylor-series.md — Taylor series: when a function equals its expansion

Budget 5,000. Apostol 9.19–9.20 (Thms 9.28–9.30), 9.21 (open interval); Pugh 3.1 (nonanalytic smooth function); Abbott 6.6; Spivak 20; Ross §31 (revisited). Closing section: R group, closing table, List of Results.

- **Opening.** AN1.7 proved Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder and AN1.8 the integral remainder (recall both statements, with labels, in one display each). §2 showed a power series is the Taylor series of its sum. The converse question: given a C^∞ function, does its Taylor series converge, and to it?
- **`def-taylor-series`.** For f ∈ C^∞ near c, T_{f,c}(x) ≔ Σ f⁽ⁿ⁾(c)(x − c)ⁿ/n!. Three things can happen: it converges to f near c; it converges, but not to f; it has radius 0 (mention only: every real sequence is the derivative sequence at 0 of some C^∞ function — stated without proof, pointer to AN2.15).
- **`thm-taylor-derivative-bound`** (Apostol 9.28 in our form). If f ∈ C^∞ on an interval I ∋ c and there are A, B with |f⁽ⁿ⁾(x)| ≤ A Bⁿ for all x ∈ I and n, then f = T_{f,c} on I. Proof: Lagrange remainder ≤ A Bⁿ⁺¹|x − c|ⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)! → 0 (AN1.2: Mⁿ/n! → 0). Examples: E, cos, sin about any c (a re-derivation, as a sanity check; they were defined by their series).
- **`exm-flat-function`** (failure that motivates `def-analytic`'s force; weird-table row). f(x) = e^{−1/x²} for x ≠ 0, f(0) = 0.
  - *Scratch Work (fourth of four).* Differentiate twice by hand: f′ = (2/x³)f, f″ = (4/x⁶ − 6/x⁴)f. Guess f⁽ⁿ⁾(x) = Pₙ(1/x)f(x) for x ≠ 0 with Pₙ a polynomial; the induction step is the product rule. At 0 the difference quotient is (1/h)Pₙ(1/h)e^{−1/h²}, and u^k e^{−u²} → 0 as |u| → ∞ is `cor-exp-beats-powers` in disguise.
