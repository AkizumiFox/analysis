# AN1.9: Sequences and Series of Functions

Directory: `src/ch19-sequences-of-functions/`. State: not started.

**Part I, Analysis on the Real Line.** Starting from the least upper bound axiom alone, Part I builds ε-N and ε-δ fluency on ℝ through sequences, series, the topology of the line, continuity, the derivative, the Riemann–Darboux integral, uniform convergence and power series, because each classical theorem of calculus (IVT, EVT, MVT, FTC, term-by-term differentiation) fails or is unprovable without completeness; it closes by proving that the forms of completeness are equivalent.

*Stage S1 exit check on pointwise limits lives here. Drafter: the answer is 'yes, such a sequence exists' for bounded limits, e.g. continuous fₙ decreasing to the indicator of a fat Cantor set (discontinuous on a set of positive length, so not Riemann integrable by Lebesgue's criterion); contrast with the Dirichlet function, which is not a pointwise limit of continuous functions (Baire class 2, mention only). The Weierstrass approximation theorem (ABB §6.7) is NOT here: it goes to Part II with Stone–Weierstrass (curriculum S2). Arzelà–Ascoli and equicontinuity (PMA 7.19–7.25) are Part II. Named skill: the checklist for exchanging limits.*

## Must prove

- uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous
- Cauchy criterion for uniform convergence
- ∫ lim fₙ = lim ∫ fₙ under uniform convergence (and the limit is integrable)
- term-by-term differentiation theorem
- Weierstrass M-test
- Dini's theorem
- Σ 2⁻ⁿ-type construction of a continuous nowhere-differentiable function (at least continuity by M-test, nondifferentiability sketched)

## Key examples and counterexamples

- xⁿ on [0,1]: continuous functions, discontinuous limit
- n²x(1−x)ⁿ: ∫ lim ≠ lim ∫
- sin(nx)/√n: uniform limit 0 but derivatives diverge
- Σ sin(nx)/n: pointwise but not uniformly convergent near 0
- Weierstrass or Takagi nowhere-differentiable function
