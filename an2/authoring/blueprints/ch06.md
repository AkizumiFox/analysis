## 0. Purpose

- A sum of continuous functions can be discontinuous. The chapter opens on that fact (a trigonometric series whose partial sums are continuous and whose sum jumps) and on a plausible "proof" that it cannot happen. Finding the false line forces the chapter's central definition, **uniform convergence**, as the hypothesis that the proof silently used.
- It then answers, for each operation of analysis (limit at a point, continuity, integral, derivative, infinite sum), the question *when may this operation be exchanged with a limit of functions?* The answers are collected at the end as one checklist, the chapter's Named move.
- It recasts uniform convergence as convergence in the **sup metric** and proves that B(S), C_b(S) and C(K) are complete metric spaces. These completeness results moved here from AN2.2 (spec/08 C2); they are what AN2.4 (Picard), AN2.6 (Stone–Weierstrass, Baire) and AN2.8 (Tietze) stand on.
- It does **not** do power series (AN1.10), equicontinuity and Arzelà–Ascoli (AN2.5), Weierstrass approximation (AN2.6, Bernstein proof first), Tauberian theorems (AN2.13), the space-filling curve in full (AN2.15, optional) or Lebesgue's convergence theorems (MT).

## 4. Notation added (for NOTATION.md via coordinator note 3)

- \( f_n \to f \) pointwise on S; \( f_n \rightrightarrows f \) uniformly on S (`\rightrightarrows`), always with the set named.
- \( \norm{f}_S \coloneqq \sup_{x \in S} \abs{f(x)} \), written \( \norm{f}_\infty \) when S is fixed. The book's `\norm{\cdot}_\infty` is reserved for this sup norm until MT, where the essential sup gets `\esssup`.
- \( B(S) \): bounded real functions on a non-empty set S; \( C_b(S) \): bounded continuous functions on a metric space S; \( C(K) \) for compact K (already in NOTATION). Metric \( d_\infty(f,g) = \norm{f - g}_\infty \).
- Real-valued only. A remark in §4 says that everything holds verbatim for complex-valued (or ℝᵐ-valued) functions with |·| the modulus (norm); AN1.10 and CA use that.

### §1 `01-pointwise-limits.md` — What pointwise limits lose (Apostol 9.1–9.2) — budget 2,200

**Opening frame.** Sequences of numbers (AN1.2) and series of numbers (AN1.3) recalled in one sentence each with `@thm-cauchy-criterion`; what changes: each term is now a function, and we ask what properties survive the limit.

**The hook.** The square-wave series \( \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k \ge 0} \frac{\sin((2k+1)x)}{2k+1} \): partial sums plotted (figure: S₁, S₃, S₁₀, S₄₀ on [−π, π], overshoot visible near 0). Stated: its sum is 1 on (0, π), −1 on (−π, 0), 0 at 0 (proof in HA). Then the **claimed theorem** `exm-continuous-sum-claim`: "a convergent series of continuous functions has a continuous sum", with a four-line proof that looks right (it fixes x, picks N from convergence at x, then uses continuity of the partial sum S_N near x, and quietly reuses the same N at nearby points y). Text: "the conclusion is false; one line of the proof is wrong; by the end of §3 you will be able to say which". (This is the chapter's false-conclusion find-the-error item; the reader is asked to try now, solution in §3.)

- `def-pointwise-convergence`: \( f_n, f \colon S \to \nR \); \( f_n \to f \) pointwise on S iff for every x ∈ S, \( f_n(x) \to f(x) \). In symbols: ∀x ∈ S ∀ε > 0 ∃N ∀n ≥ N: |f_n(x) − f(x)| < ε. Emphasis: N = N(x, ε).
- `def-series-of-functions-pointwise`: Σ f_k converges pointwise iff the partial sums do.

**Four witnesses** (each an example with its own label; all on bounded intervals, all algebraic):
- `exm-pointwise-loses-continuity`: \( g_n(x) = \frac{nx}{1 + n\abs{x}} \) on [−1, 1]; limit sgn x. Continuous terms, discontinuous limit.
- `exm-pointwise-loses-integral`: the tent \( h_n \) with base [0, 2/n], peak n at 1/n, zero elsewhere on [0,1]. h_n → 0 pointwise (at 0 always 0; at x > 0, zero once 2/n < x); ∫₀¹ h_n = 1 for all n. So lim ∫ ≠ ∫ lim.
- `exm-pointwise-loses-derivative`: \( k_n(x) = \frac{x}{1 + n x^2} \) on [−1,1]; k_n → 0 (indeed |k_n| ≤ 1/(2√n) by AM–GM), but k_n′(0) = 1 for all n while the limit has derivative 0. Derivatives converge pointwise to a function that is 1 at 0 and 0 elsewhere.
- `exm-pointwise-loses-boundedness`: on (0, 1], \( m_n(x) = \min(n, 1/x) \): each bounded, limit 1/x unbounded.

`rem-weird-examples-table-an1-9`: this chapter adds rows to the pathological-examples table started in F4.7 (closed in AN2.15). The table is printed in §7; §1 lists the four rows above.

**Quick checks.** QC1: x ↦ xⁿ on [0,1]: find the pointwise limit and say which property it loses. (Continuity; limit is 0 on [0,1), 1 at 1.) QC2: for g_n at x = 0.01, find the least N with |g_n(x) − 1| < 0.1 for n ≥ N; and at x = 0.001. (|g_n − 1| = 1/(1 + nx) < 0.1 iff nx > 9: N = 901 and N = 9001; N grows as x → 0.)

**Exercises.**
- A1 `exr-pointwise-limits-a1`: pointwise limits of (a) x/n on ℝ, (b) \( \frac{n^2 x}{1 + n^3 x^2} \) on [0,1], (c) \( \frac{1}{1 + x^{2n}} \) on ℝ. Answers: (a) 0; (b) 0 (for x > 0, ≤ n²x/(n³x²) = 1/(nx)); (c) 1 on (−1,1), 1/2 at ±1, 0 for |x| > 1.
- A2 `exr-pointwise-limits-a2`: is a pointwise limit of increasing functions increasing? of convex functions convex? of polynomials of degree ≤ 3 a polynomial of degree ≤ 3? Answers: yes (non-strictly), yes, yes (convergence at 4 points determines coefficients via Lagrange interpolation, a linear invertible map; then convergence everywhere).
- B1 `exr-pointwise-limits-b1`: construct continuous f_n on [0,1] with f_n → 0 pointwise but ∫ f_n → ∞. Answer: tent with base [0, 2/n], peak n² at 1/n (area n).
- B2 `exr-pointwise-limits-b2`: let f_n be continuous on [0,1] with f_n → f pointwise. Show f need not attain a maximum. Answer: f_n(x) = x for x ≤ 1 − 1/n, linear down to 0 at 1; limit x on [0,1), 0 at 1; sup 1 not attained.
- C1 `exr-pointwise-limits-c1`: let f_n → f pointwise on ℝ with each f_n continuous. Show {x : f(x) ≥ c} need not be closed, but show it is a countable intersection of countable unions of closed sets. Answer: {f ≥ c} = ⋂_k ⋃_N ⋂_{n ≥ N} {f_n ≥ c − 1/k}; inner sets closed, intersection closed. Witness for non-closed: g_n and c = 1 gives (0,1]. (Pointer: AN2.6 Baire class.)

### §2 `02-uniform-convergence.md` — Uniform convergence (Apostol 9.3, 9.5) — budget 2,600

**Failure-motivated definition.** Re-read the claimed proof of §1: it needs one N that serves x and every nearby y. Ask for one N that serves every point of S. That is the definition.

- `def-uniform-convergence`: \( f_n \rightrightarrows f \) on S iff ∀ε > 0 ∃N ∀n ≥ N ∀x ∈ S: |f_n(x) − f(x)| < ε. Symbols beside prose; the only change from pointwise is the position of ∀x.
- `prp-uniform-sup-criterion`: f_n ⇉ f on S iff \( \sup_{x \in S} \abs{f_n(x) - f(x)} \to 0 \) (sup possibly +∞ for small n). Proof: two lines each way; note < ε for all x gives sup ≤ ε, hence the "≤ ε versus < ε" remark.
- `prp-uniform-implies-pointwise` and restriction: uniform on S ⇒ uniform on every subset, and pointwise; uniform on S₁ and S₂ ⇒ uniform on S₁ ∪ S₂ (max of two N).
- **Named move: Witness point** (`index.md`). To show non-uniformity, find x_n ∈ S with |f_n(x_n) − f(x_n)| ≥ c > 0 for infinitely many n; then the sup does not tend to 0. First use: g_n at x_n = 1/n gives |g_n − 1| = 1/2.
- `exm-uniform-on-subsets`: g_n ⇉ sgn on [δ, 1] for every δ > 0 (sup = 1/(1 + nδ)), not on (0,1]. Lesson: uniformity is a property of the pair (sequence, set). Same for xⁿ on [0, a], a < 1.
- `exm-uniform-tests`: k_n ⇉ 0 on ℝ (sup = 1/(2√n)); h_n not uniform (sup = n).
- `thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion` (Apostol 9.5): (f_n) converges uniformly on S (to some f) iff ∀ε ∃N ∀m, n ≥ N ∀x ∈ S: |f_n(x) − f_m(x)| < ε. **Key step:** define f pointwise by `@thm-cauchy-criterion`; let m → ∞ in |f_n(x) − f_m(x)| < ε to get ≤ ε for all x (limit of strict inequality is non-strict). **Scratch Work** (first appearance of "let m → ∞ inside an inequality that holds for all m ≥ N"): set apart, then the clean proof.
- `rem-uniform-vector-valued`: same definitions for ℂ- or ℝᵐ-valued functions.

**Quick checks.** QC1 (STYLE §10 quantifier swap): write the definition with "∀x ∈ S" moved before "∃N"; which notion is it? (Pointwise.) What would "∃N ∀n ≥ N ∀x ∀ε" mean? (f_n(x) = f(x) for all x once n ≥ N: the sequence is eventually equal to f.) QC2: if f_n ⇉ f and g_n ⇉ g on S, does f_n + g_n ⇉ f + g? f_n g_n ⇉ fg? (Yes; no in general: f_n = g_n = x + 1/n on ℝ, product error 2x/n + 1/n²; yes if all bounded — exercise B2.)

**Exercises.**
- A1 `exr-uniform-convergence-a1`: decide uniformity of A1-§1's (b) on [0,1] and on [δ,1]. Answer: at x = n^{−3/2}: value n²·n^{−3/2}/(1 + 1) = √n/2 → ∞: not uniform on [0,1]; on [δ,1] bounded by 1/(nδ) → 0: uniform.
- A2 `exr-uniform-convergence-a2` (comprehension, `thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion`): where in the proof is completeness of ℝ used, and why does the final inequality become ≤? Answer: to define f(x) = lim f_n(x); because a limit of terms < ε is only ≤ ε.
- B1 `exr-uniform-convergence-b1`: show f_n(x) = \( \sqrt{x^2 + 1/n} \) ⇉ |x| on ℝ. Answer: 0 ≤ √(x² + 1/n) − |x| = (1/n)/(√(x² + 1/n) + |x|) ≤ 1/√n.
- B2 `exr-uniform-convergence-b2`: if f_n ⇉ f, g_n ⇉ g on S and f, g are bounded, then f_n g_n ⇉ fg. Answer: f_n g_n − fg = (f_n − f)g_n + f(g_n − g), and g_n eventually bounded by ‖g‖ + 1.
- B3 `exr-uniform-convergence-b3`: if f_n ⇉ f on S and each f_n is bounded, then f is bounded and the f_n are uniformly bounded. Answer: take N for ε = 1; |f| ≤ |f_N| + 1; then sup over the finitely many n < N and ‖f‖ + 1 for n ≥ N.
- C1 `exr-uniform-convergence-c1`: let φ: ℝ → ℝ be uniformly continuous and f_n ⇉ f on S. Show φ∘f_n ⇉ φ∘f; give a continuous φ for which this fails. Answer: δ from uniform continuity; φ(t) = t², f_n(x) = x + 1/n on ℝ (error 2x/n + 1/n², unbounded).

### §3 `03-continuity-and-double-limits.md` — Uniform limits, continuity, and interchanging limits (Apostol 9.4, 9.12; PMA 7.11–7.13) — budget 3,600

**Opening frame.** Recall `@thm-continuity-sequential` (AN1.5) and uniform continuity (AN1.6); "what survives".

- `thm-uniform-limit-continuous` (**key proof 1**): S a metric space, f_n: S → ℝ continuous at p ∈ S, f_n ⇉ f on S. Then f is continuous at p. Corollary: continuous everywhere if each f_n is. **Scratch Work** (first appearance of the **3ε argument**, the chapter's new named move): split |f(x) − f(p)| ≤ |f(x) − f_N(x)| + |f_N(x) − f_N(p)| + |f_N(p) − f(p)|; first and third by uniformity (a single N for both x and p, which is exactly what pointwise lacks), middle by continuity of f_N at p. Then the clean proof. "*Before reading on: where is uniformity used, and could N depend on x?*" (one self-explanation prompt; STYLE §10).
- **Diagnosis of the claim** `exm-continuous-sum-claim` solved: the failing line is "the same N works at y", which is uniform convergence near x. Witness to the illegal step: g_n at x = 0. Hook paid (first part): the square-wave series cannot converge uniformly on any interval containing 0, by `thm-uniform-limit-continuous` contrapositive.
- `cor-nonuniform-test`: continuous terms + discontinuous limit ⇒ not uniform. Remark: the converse fails (h_n has continuous limit 0 but is not uniform).
- `thm-moore-osgood` (Apostol 9.12 / PMA 7.11): S a metric space, p a limit point of S (p need not lie in S), f_n ⇉ f on S \ {p}, and lim_{x→p} f_n(x) = A_n exists for each n. Then (A_n) converges and lim_{x→p} f(x) exists and equals lim A_n; that is, lim_{x→p} lim_n f_n(x) = lim_n lim_{x→p} f_n(x). Proof route: |A_n − A_m| ≤ sup|f_n − f_m| (let x → p in |f_n(x) − f_m(x)| < ε), so (A_n) is Cauchy, converges to A by `@thm-cauchy-criterion`; then 3ε. Second appearance of the 3ε argument, **no Scratch Work** (fading).
- `exm-double-limit-fails`: \( a_{m,n} = \frac{m}{m+n} \): iterated limits 1 and 0. Recast as f_n(x) = 1/(1 + n x) at x = 1/m → 0: not uniform. Ties to the double sequences of AN2.12 (see-pointer).
- `thm-dini` (PMA 7.13): K ⊆ ℝ compact (main line: K = [a,b] and compact subsets of ℝ; remark: any compact metric space, same proof, see AN2.3), f_n: K → ℝ continuous, f_n(x) ≥ f_{n+1}(x) for all x, n, and f_n → f pointwise with f continuous. Then f_n ⇉ f. Proof route (sequential, uses `@thm-bolzano-weierstrass`): put g_n = f_n − f ≥ 0, decreasing; if not uniform, pick c > 0 and x_n with g_n(x_n) ≥ c; a subsequence x_{n_j} → x ∈ K; for fixed m and n_j ≥ m, g_m(x_{n_j}) ≥ g_{n_j}(x_{n_j}) ≥ c, so g_m(x) ≥ c by continuity of g_m; contradiction with g_m(x) → 0.
- `rem-dini-hypotheses` (STYLE §10 counterexample per hypothesis): K compact dropped: xⁿ on [0,1); f continuous dropped: xⁿ on [0,1]; monotone dropped: moving tent of height 1, base [0, 2/n], on [0,1]; f_n continuous dropped: indicator of (0, 1/n) on [0,1].

**Proof ordering item** `exr-continuity-double-limits-a3` (STYLE §11.4; one per new move): shuffled 8 lines of `thm-uniform-limit-continuous`, plus 2 distractors: "choose N such that |f_N(x) − f(x)| < ε/3 (N depending on x)"; "choose δ such that |f_n(x) − f_n(p)| < ε/3 for all n". Answer: an admissible order; distractor 1 breaks the requirement that the same N serve x and p (N must be fixed before x is chosen); distractor 2 asks for a δ uniform in n, which is equicontinuity (AN2.5) and is not given.

**Faded triple for the 3ε argument** (STYLE §11.4): (1) `thm-uniform-limit-continuous` in full; (2) `exr-continuity-double-limits-b2` with the last step blank; (3) `exr-continuity-double-limits-c2` with the last half blank. Prompts "*Name the move, then complete it.*"

**Quick checks.** QC1: can bounded continuous functions on (0,1) converge uniformly to 1/x? (No: a uniform limit of bounded functions is bounded, §2 B3.) And if "bounded" is dropped? (Yes: f_n(x) = 1/x + 1/n.) QC2: Dini with "increasing" in place of "decreasing"? (Same, apply to −f_n.)

**Exercises.**
- A1 `exr-continuity-double-limits-a1`: use `cor-nonuniform-test` to show xⁿ is not uniform on [0,1]; then show it is not uniform on [0,1) either (where the limit 0 is continuous) by a witness point. Answer: x_n = 2^{−1/n} gives x_nⁿ = 1/2.

### §4 `04-function-spaces.md` — The sup metric: B(S), C_b(S) and C(K) are complete (Apostol 9.5 recast; PMA 7.14–7.15; Pugh 4.1; moved from AN2.2) — budget 2,800

### §5 `05-integration-and-differentiation.md` — Integrals and derivatives of limits (Apostol 9.8–9.10; PMA 7.16–7.17) — budget 3,400

**Opening frame.** The tent h_n and k_n of §1. Two questions, two different answers: integration commutes with uniform limits; differentiation needs uniform convergence of the derivatives.

- `thm-uniform-limit-integral`: f_n Riemann integrable on [a,b], f_n ⇉ f on [a,b]. Then f is Riemann integrable and ∫_a^b f_n → ∫_a^b f; indeed \( \abs{\int f_n - \int f} \le (b - a)\norm{f_n - f}_\infty \). Route: with η_n = ‖f − f_n‖_∞, f is bounded (§2 B3) and for every partition P, U(f,P) − L(f,P) ≤ U(f_n,P) − L(f_n,P) + 2η_n(b − a) (sup and inf on each subinterval move by at most η_n); `@thm-riemann-criterion`; then `@prp-integral-abs`.
- `cor-uniform-limit-antiderivative`: under the same hypotheses, \( F_n(x) = \int_a^x f_n \rightrightarrows F(x) = \int_a^x f \) on [a,b].
- `rem-integral-rs`: the same proof works for Riemann–Stieltjes integrals with increasing integrator α, error (α(b) − α(a))‖f_n − f‖ (Apostol 9.8); exercise C2, see AN2.10.
- `thm-arzela-bounded-convergence` (Apostol 9.9; **stated without proof**, labelled as such per STYLE §4 optional results and cited by no proof): f_n Riemann integrable on [a,b], |f_n(x)| ≤ M for all n and x, f_n → f pointwise and f Riemann integrable. Then ∫f_n → ∫f. Remark: tents h_n show "bounded" cannot be dropped; §7's fat-Cantor example shows "f Riemann integrable" cannot be dropped; MT proves the dominated convergence theorem, which contains this.
- `thm-uniform-limit-derivative` (**key proof 3**; PMA 7.17 form, Apostol 9.10 under weaker hypotheses): f_n differentiable on [a,b] (one-sided at endpoints), (f_n(x₀)) converges for some x₀ ∈ [a,b], and (f_n′) converges uniformly on [a,b]. Then (f_n) converges uniformly on [a,b] to a differentiable f with f′(x) = lim f_n′(x) for all x. Route: (i) MVT (`@thm-mean-value`) applied to f_n − f_m: |(f_n − f_m)(x) − (f_n − f_m)(t)| ≤ |x − t| ‖f_n′ − f_m′‖_∞; with t = x₀ this gives uniform Cauchy, so f_n ⇉ f (`thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion`); (ii) fix x, put φ_n(t) = (f_n(t) − f_n(x))/(t − x) on [a,b] \ {x}; (i) shows φ_n is uniformly Cauchy there; lim_{t→x} φ_n(t) = f_n′(x); `thm-moore-osgood` exchanges the limits. Self-explanation prompt: "*Self-explain step (ii): why is the uniform Cauchy bound for φ_n exactly the MVT estimate divided by |t − x|?*"
- `rem-derivative-hypotheses`: k_n (§1): f_n ⇉ 0 but f_n′(0) = 1; f_n′ converge pointwise, not uniformly. Uniform convergence of f_n alone says nothing about f_n′: f_n = (1/n)·Z(n² x) where Z is the 1-periodic zigzag of slope ±1 (distance to the nearest integer); ‖f_n‖ ≤ 1/(2n) yet f_n has slope ±n wherever differentiable. Convergence at one point is needed: f_n = n (constant) has f_n′ = 0 ⇉ 0.
- `cor-derivative-c1-version`: easier version with continuous f_n′ by FTC (`@thm-ftc-evaluation`, `cor-uniform-limit-antiderivative`): f_n(x) = f_n(x₀) + ∫_{x₀}^x f_n′. Given first as **worked example** (two lines), so the reader sees why the general theorem needs MVT instead.
- `exm-differentiate-a-series-preview`: the function \( F(x) = \sum_{k \ge 1} x^k/k^2 \) on [−1, 1]: term-by-term derivative Σ x^{k−1}/k converges uniformly on [−a, a] for a < 1 (M-test of §6 is not yet available: done via `thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion` and geometric tails); so F′ = Σ x^{k−1}/k on (−1,1). ### §6 `06-series-of-functions.md` — Series of functions: the M-test and its refinements (Apostol 9.6, 9.7 pointer, 9.11) — budget 3,000

**Opening frame.** A series of functions is the sequence of its partial sums; every theorem of §§2–5 applies. What is new: tests that check uniformity term by term.

- `def-uniform-convergence-series`: Σ f_k converges uniformly on S iff S_n = Σ_{k≤n} f_k does.
- `thm-series-uniform-cauchy`: Σ f_k converges uniformly on S iff ∀ε ∃N ∀n > m ≥ N ∀x: |Σ_{k=m+1}^n f_k(x)| < ε. (From `thm-uniform-cauchy-criterion`.) Corollary: then f_k ⇉ 0 on S (necessary condition).
- `thm-weierstrass-m-test`: |f_k(x)| ≤ M_k for all x ∈ S and Σ M_k < ∞ ⇒ Σ f_k converges uniformly (and absolutely) on S. **Scratch Work** (first appearance of "bound a uniform tail by a numerical tail": the **Telescoping-tail move**, Named move): |Σ_{m<k≤n} f_k(x)| ≤ Σ_{m<k≤n} M_k, which does not depend on x; `@thm-series-cauchy-criterion` on Σ M_k.
- `thm-term-by-term`: the three exchange theorems for series, as corollaries of `thm-uniform-limit-continuous`, `thm-uniform-limit-integral`, `thm-uniform-limit-derivative` (stated with full hypotheses; one-line proofs).
- `exm-takagi-continuous` (worked; pays AN1.7 hook in part): Z(x) = distance from x to the nearest integer; \( T(x) = \sum_{k \ge 0} 2^{-k} Z(2^k x) \). Each term continuous with |term| ≤ 2^{−k−1}; M-test ⇒ T continuous on ℝ. Graph (figure: T₁, T₃, T₈). Nondifferentiability: `exr-function-series-c2`. AN2.6 and AN2.15 revisit.
- `exm-space-filling-pointer` (Apostol 9.7): one paragraph and a figure: a continuous map of [0,1] onto [0,1]² can be built as a pair of M-test series; its continuity is exercise B3; surjectivity is in AN2.15 (optional; see-pointer only).
- `thm-dirichlet-test-uniform` (Apostol 9.11): on S, let the partial sums of Σ a_k(x) be uniformly bounded (|Σ_{k≤n} a_k(x)| ≤ A for all n, x), and let b_k(x) ≥ b_{k+1}(x) ≥ 0 with b_k ⇉ 0 on S. Then Σ a_k b_k converges uniformly on S. Route: `@lem-summation-by-parts` pointwise; the bound 2A·b_{m+1}(x) is uniform. Second appearance of the telescoping-tail move, no Scratch Work.
- `thm-abel-test-uniform`: Σ a_k converges uniformly on S (a_k may be constants), b_k(x) monotone in k for each x and uniformly bounded. Then Σ a_k b_k converges uniformly. Route: summation by parts with the uniform Cauchy tails of Σ a_k. Used by AN2.13 (Abel's limit theorem, with b_k(x) = xᵏ on [0,1]).

### §7 `07-modes-of-convergence.md` — Modes of convergence and the exchange checklist (Apostol 9.13; PMA 7 exercises) — budget 2,900

**Opening frame.** Three ways a sequence of functions can approach a limit have appeared (pointwise, uniform, and the integral distance hinted at in §4). Mean convergence completes Apostol's list; then the chapter closes.

- `def-mean-convergence`: for Riemann-integrable f_n, f on [a,b]: f_n → f in mean (order 1) iff ∫|f_n − f| → 0; in mean square iff ∫|f_n − f|² → 0 (Apostol 9.13). Remark: mean square is the convergence of HA (Parseval) and of L² in MT.
- `prp-uniform-implies-mean`: f_n ⇉ f on [a,b] ⇒ mean and mean-square convergence (bounds (b − a)η_n and (b − a)η_n²).
- `exm-typewriter`: indicators of [j/2^m, (j+1)/2^m], j = 0, …, 2^m − 1, listed in order: mean (and mean-square) convergence to 0 (∫ = 2^{−m}), pointwise convergence at **no** point of [0,1]. Mean does not imply pointwise.
- `exm-tent-not-mean`: h_n of §1: pointwise to 0, ∫|h_n| = 1: pointwise does not imply mean. With `thm-arzela-bounded-convergence`: for uniformly bounded sequences with integrable limit, pointwise implies mean.
- `exm-mean-distance-incomplete` witness (promised in §4): f_n(x) = min(n, x^{−1/2}) on (0,1], f_n(0) = n: continuous, d_1-Cauchy (∫|f_n − f_m| ≤ ∫₀^{1/m²} x^{−1/2} = 2/m for n > m), but no continuous f has ∫|f_n − f| → 0 (on each [δ,1] f must equal x^{−1/2}, unbounded near 0). Hook to MT.
- `exm-fat-cantor-limit` (answers the old exit check): let C be the fat Cantor set of `@exm-fat-cantor-set` and f_n(x) = max(0, 1 − n·d(x, C)). Then f_n is continuous (`@prp-distance-to-set`), f_n ≥ f_{n+1}, and f_n → 1_C pointwise (C closed). 1_C is not Riemann integrable: every subinterval meets [0,1] \ C (empty interior), so L(1_C, P) = 0; the subintervals of P that miss C lie inside the removed open intervals (each such closed subinterval lies in one of them, being connected), so their total length is at most the total removed length r < 1, and U(1_C, P) ≥ 1 − r. So continuous functions can decrease to a non-integrable limit; Dini does not apply (limit discontinuous); Arzelà's integrability hypothesis is needed.
- `rem-dirichlet-double-limit`: the Dirichlet function is a pointwise limit of pointwise limits of continuous functions but not a pointwise limit of continuous functions (statement only; the proof uses Baire, AN2.6). Uses `exr-pointwise-limits-c1`'s pattern.
- `tbl-modes-of-convergence` (STYLE §10 "four modes" witness table): rows pointwise, uniform, mean, mean square; entries "implies" or a named witness (typewriter; tent; tent with base [0, 2/n] and height √n: ∫h = 1/√n → 0 but ∫h² = 2/3, so mean without mean square; uniform ⇒ all on [a,b]).
